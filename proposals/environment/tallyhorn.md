# Tallyhorn sports fan app: live games, where to watch and fantasy

**Proposed PR title** — [Environment Proposal] [Media & entertainment + Games & play] Tallyhorn sports fan app: live games, where to watch and fantasy

## 1. App and domains

- **Authors / GitHub handles (required):** @byshail
- **Reference app or website / URL (required):** Tallyhorn, an independently branded synthetic sports fan app for one invented basketball league, seen from one fan's account: live games, where to watch, spoiler mode and a fantasy team. ESPN is the reference for scores, schedules, team pages and score alerts. The NBA app and NBA League Pass are the references for where a game is televised and for blackouts by home market and national broadcast. NFL Fantasy and ESPN Fantasy are the references for weekly lineups with a per-player lock, waiver claims processed in priority order and trade offers between managers. [ESPN Fan Support](https://support.espn.com/hc/en-us) · [NBA League Pass Blackout Guide](https://support.watch.nba.com/hc/en-us/articles/115002481154) · [NFL Fantasy](https://fantasy.nfl.com/)
- **Primary domain (required):** Media & entertainment
- **Secondary domains (optional):** Games & play

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Tallyhorn is a synthetic sports fan app for one invented basketball league, and the only proposal with live game state, TV blackouts, spoiler mode and a fantasy team with lineup locks, waivers and trades. Games change state only through Infra events. The fan's region and the services they hold decide where a game can be watched, and a per-team spoiler mode decides which results reads withhold. The same request has a different right answer for different fans, because it depends on where they live, what they pay for, which results they have not seen yet and the rules they set for their fantasy team.

### Three example requests

The seeded account belongs to Ines, a fan of the Port Alder Herons in the Corvane League. She lives in the Port Alder region, uses the `America/Los_Angeles` time zone and holds two services: Cordline TV, a live TV bundle that carries the national channel Arcline Sports but not the Herons' regional channel, Alder Sports Network; and Corvane Pass, the league's out-of-market package. Spoiler mode is on for the Herons, and every Herons game is marked seen except Tuesday's. She manages Alder Street Hoopers in the eight-team Mossgate fantasy league, where Bram manages Glass Cleaners. Fantasy week 7 runs from Monday 30 November to Sunday 6 December 2026. Times are Ines's. All people, teams, channels and services are fictional.

### Workflows

| Workflow | Entities and transitions | Confirm, modify, cancel and error paths |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W1 League, schedule and live games | A league of 12 teams, each with a home region and a venue. Players have positions (`G`, `F`, `C`) and an injury status (`active`, `questionable`, `doubtful` or `out`). A game has `starts_at`, a venue, broadcast feeds, a status, a live score with period and clock, and a box score once final. Status moves `scheduled` → `in_progress` → `final`, or `scheduled` → `postponed`. A new start time for a scheduled or postponed game makes it `rescheduled`, which keeps `original_starts_at` and then starts like a scheduled game. Status, scores and injury status change only through Infra events, so a game whose `starts_at` has passed without a start event still reads `scheduled`. Team records are derived from final games. Games before the 2026–27 season keep a final score without a box score, so reads return `box_score: null` for them, and player season averages and fantasy points count only 2026–27 games. | Read-only for the agent. An unknown ID returns `not_found`. An event out of order, such as a score for a scheduled game or a start for a final one, fails with `invalid_transition` and changes nothing. Reads give times in UTC and in the account's time zone. |
| W2 Where to watch | A region is one of the 12 teams' home regions, one domestic region with no team, or `rg-international`. A channel is `national` (for example Arcline Sports), `regional` (one per team, carried only in listed regions) or `league_pass` (Corvane Pass). A service, such as Cordline TV, carries listed channels in listed regions. Each game lists feeds as `{channel_id, feed: home, away or national, exclusive}`. Three fixed rules decide availability. A regional feed is available only in its channel's regions. An exclusive national feed blacks out the regional feeds and Corvane Pass in every domestic region (`national_exclusive`). Corvane Pass blacks a game out in both teams' home regions (`in_market`). In `rg-international` only Corvane Pass is available, with no blackouts. The account records its region and held services. | `tallyhorn.watch.options` returns, for each game, every available channel with the services that carry it in the region and whether each is `held`, every blacked-out channel with its reason, and `watchable_with_held_services`. A postponed game returns no options, and a final one returns `ended`. An optional `region_id` answers for another region without changing the account. `tallyhorn.account.update` changes the region or services. An unknown region or service returns `not_found`. Tallyhorn sells nothing: a held service is only a record. |
| W3 Account, spoiler mode, alerts and notices | The account holds a time zone, a region, held services, favorite teams and players, and a spoiler setting per team (`enabled`, `since`). Seen games are a set of game IDs. A game is hidden when spoiler mode is on for either team, the game started at or after `since`, it is `in_progress` or `final`, and it is not seen. For a hidden game, game and schedule reads withhold the score, period, clock, winner and box score. Team records, player averages and game logs, and fantasy points leave it out and report how many games they left out. A stored `game_final` notice withholds its score on read. No `close_game` notice is written for a game that is hidden when the score event arrives, whichever team the rule follows. A read with `include_hidden: true` returns everything, marked `hidden`. An alert rule has a kind (`game_start`, `close_game` with a point margin, or `game_final`) and a team. It is `active` until deleted and fires at most once per game. | `tallyhorn.spoilers.set` turns spoiler mode on, setting `since` to the current time, or off. `tallyhorn.games.mark_seen` reveals one game, and a game that has not started returns `not_started`. Reads never mark a game seen or change a setting. Alert rules can be created, updated and deleted. A second active rule of the same kind for the same team returns `duplicate_alert`. An unknown IANA time zone returns `invalid_time_zone`. |
| W4 Fantasy roster, lineup and injuries | The fantasy league has eight teams, each with a manager and at most 12 players. Fantasy week *n* runs from Monday 00:00 to Sunday 24:00 league time (`America/Chicago`), and a game belongs to the week of its current `starts_at`, so a reschedule can move it to another week. A week reads `upcoming`, `current` or `complete` from `ctx.virtual_now`. A weekly lineup (for example `lu-alder-street-w07`) has seven starting slots (`G1`, `G2`, `F1`, `F2`, `C`, `UTIL1`, `UTIL2`), and every other rostered player is on the bench. A week's lineup that was never saved reads as the previous week's lineup, carried forward when read, and is stored on its first save, so a new week needs no write. A player who leaves a roster by trade, drop or waiver claim is removed from that team's current and later lineups, and past weeks keep him. An unrostered player is on `waivers` or a `free_agent`. A player's lock time in a week is the earliest `starts_at` among his team's games that week that are not postponed. He is locked once `ctx.virtual_now` reaches it or once one of those games is `in_progress` or `final`. Lock is derived when read or checked and is never stored. A postponement before tip-off moves the lock to his next game that week, and a player with no game left that week is never locked. A team's weekly points are its starters' fantasy points from final games that week, using the league's points table. | `tallyhorn.fantasy.lineup.set` takes the lineup as `id` and slot assignments as `patch`, and a displaced player goes to the bench. Moving a locked player into or out of a starting slot returns `player_locked`. A player not on the roster returns `not_on_roster`, a slot his positions do not fit returns `ineligible_slot`, one player in two slots returns `invalid_lineup`, another manager's lineup returns `forbidden`, and a `complete` week's lineup returns `week_closed`. `tallyhorn.fantasy.roster.add` adds a free agent at once, with an optional drop. A 13th player returns `roster_full`, and a rostered or waiver player returns `player_unavailable`. `tallyhorn.fantasy.roster.drop` puts a player on waivers, and a locked starter cannot be dropped. Starting a questionable or out player is allowed, because the fan's rules are not App rules. An account with no fantasy team gets `not_found` from every fantasy operation. |
| W5 Waivers and trades | A waiver claim names a player on waivers, an optional drop, a rank among the account's claims and a `run_at`: the first scheduled run after the claim was made. The league runs waivers on Wednesdays at 03:00 league time (`America/Chicago`). Claims move `pending` → `won`, `lost`, `invalid` or `cancelled`. The waiver order lists the eight teams. A trade offer has a proposer team, a recipient team, the players each side gives, optional drops and `expires_at`. `tallyhorn.fantasy.trades.propose` takes an optional `expires_at` that defaults to 48 hours after the offer is made, and `tallyhorn.fantasy.trade_offer` carries its own. It moves `pending` → `completed`, `rejected`, `cancelled` or `failed`, and reads show `expired` once `ctx.virtual_now` reaches `expires_at` while it is pending. A completed trade moves every player to the other team's bench at once. | A run happens only through `tallyhorn.fantasy.waivers.process`, so claims stay `pending` after their `run_at` until it arrives. The run repeats one step: the highest team in the order with a valid pending claim gets its best-ranked claim and moves to the end of the order. A claim for a player already taken is `lost`. A claim whose drop player has left the roster, or that would take the roster past 12, is `invalid`. Unclaimed players dropped before `run_at` become free agents. Claims can be re-ranked or cancelled while pending, and otherwise return `not_pending`. A second claim for the same player returns `duplicate_claim`. Accepting an offer needs every player still on his roster (`not_on_roster`), none locked in a starting slot (`player_locked`) and both rosters at 12 or fewer after drops (`roster_full`). When the agent accepts and a check fails, the call returns the error and the offer stays pending. When another manager accepts by event and a check fails, the offer becomes `failed` with that reason. Responding to an expired offer returns `offer_expired`, and responding to the account's own offer returns `forbidden`. Counter-offers are out of scope. |

## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** None in the first version. All five workflows complete inside Tallyhorn. The task supplies the fan's own rules, such as "never start anyone listed questionable", and their plans in the user context. Tallyhorn sends no messages and receives none.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** MCP only in the first version, with 32 agent operations. There are 17 read operations: `tallyhorn.league.get`, `tallyhorn.teams.get`, `tallyhorn.players.search`, `tallyhorn.players.get`, `tallyhorn.games.list`, `tallyhorn.games.get`, `tallyhorn.watch.options`, `tallyhorn.services.list`, `tallyhorn.account.get`, `tallyhorn.alerts.list`, `tallyhorn.notices.list`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.league.get`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.roster.get`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.lineup.get`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.matchup.get`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.claims.list` and `tallyhorn.fantasy.trades.list`. There are 15 write operations: `tallyhorn.account.update`, `tallyhorn.spoilers.set`, `tallyhorn.games.mark_seen`, `tallyhorn.alerts.create`, `tallyhorn.alerts.update`, `tallyhorn.alerts.delete`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.lineup.set`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.roster.add`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.roster.drop`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.claims.create`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.claims.update`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.claims.cancel`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.trades.propose`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.trades.respond` and `tallyhorn.fantasy.trades.cancel`. The acting person is the bound account's `owner_person_id` from `binding.json`, never an argument, and the App acts for the fantasy team that person manages. Writes that change an existing object take it as `id` and the fields to change as `patch`; `tallyhorn.games.mark_seen`, `tallyhorn.alerts.delete`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.roster.drop`, `tallyhorn.fantasy.claims.cancel` and `tallyhorn.fantasy.trades.cancel` take only `id`. A task can therefore narrow tools, objects and fields with the `constraints` in `policies.json`, which Core checks directly, for example allowing `tallyhorn.fantasy.lineup.set` only on `lu-alder-street-w07` with fields `F1` and `F2`. App errors are `not_found`, `not_started`, `invalid_time_zone`, `duplicate_alert`, `not_on_roster`, `ineligible_slot`, `invalid_lineup`, `player_locked`, `week_closed`, `roster_full`, `player_unavailable`, `duplicate_claim`, `not_pending`, `offer_expired` and `forbidden`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Infra alone uses `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the runtime's `env.advance_time`. They are not on the agent connection, and Core returns `permission_denied` if the agent calls one. There is no GUI and no reviewer console.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** League data is App state loaded from the run fixture: teams, players and injury statuses, venues, regions, channels, services, and games with their feeds, scores and box scores. Account state is bound to one account instance: the account's settings, spoiler settings, seen games, alert rules and notices, and the fantasy league as that account sees it, with all eight rosters and lineups, the waiver order, the account's claims and the offers involving its team. A run loads both, plus the virtual clock. The seed schema accepts any history from 2025-01-01, including final games with box scores, postponed and rescheduled games, injury histories, earlier lineups and weekly results, completed trades and waiver results. Seed validation rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, a player on two fantasy rosters, a lineup slot holding a player who is not on that roster or does not fit the slot, box-score lines for a player on neither team, box-score points that do not add up to the team's score, a final game without a score and timestamps without a UTC offset (`invalid_seed`). Each write returns a receipt with the object ID and Core's new version. Tallyhorn keeps no per-object version numbers. Every write takes Core's `expected_version` (`{epoch, revision}`) for the App instance, and every committed write, applied event or `env.advance_time` raises the revision, so during a live game the agent reads again before it writes. Repeating a committed `request_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`. The same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`, and a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`. A failed call leaves data and version unchanged, and reads never change business data. Five states are derived from `ctx.virtual_now` when read or checked and are never stored: lineup lock, offer expiry, a claim's `run_at` having passed, the fantasy week's status and local start times. Time passing writes nothing. Game status and scores, injury status, waiver results and other managers' trade decisions change only through events. Records dated after `ctx.virtual_now` do not exist yet for any tool: an injury update, notice, claim, trade, lineup save or seen mark before its own time is absent from lists and counts and `not_found` by ID. A game whose recorded start or final is after the clock reads as it stood at the clock, without the later score. Nothing is written when the clock reaches them, so a seed that runs past its clock, such as the evaluation dataset, can load at any point in its window. Scheduled starts stay visible. Reset (`reset_scope: app_instance`) restores the loaded league, account and clock without touching other Apps. Evaluator records come from App activity rows, Core audit rows and the supplied agent trace, joined on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Each activity row records the sequence number, virtual time, actor (`agent` or `event`), action, object and the replaced value. There is one row per committed write or applied event, and none for a replay, a rejected call or a time-driven change. Infra takes the snapshot before reset.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Infra applies nine scenario events, and the runner controls their timing. `tallyhorn.game.start` moves a scheduled or rescheduled game to `in_progress`. `tallyhorn.game.score` updates the score, period and clock of a game in progress. `tallyhorn.game.final` sets the final score and the box score, from which fantasy points are computed. `tallyhorn.game.postpone` sets `postponed`. `tallyhorn.game.schedule_update` changes the start time, venue or feeds, and a new start time makes the game `rescheduled`. `tallyhorn.player.injury_update` sets a player's status, note and optional expected return. `tallyhorn.fantasy.trade_offer` adds another manager's offer to the account's team under an `offer_id` the task chooses. `tallyhorn.fantasy.trade_response` accepts or rejects an offer the account's team made. `tallyhorn.fantasy.waivers.process` runs waivers for one `run_at` and carries the other managers' claims in its payload, because those claims are private to their managers. Events name seeded records by ID. An unknown game, player or team fails with `target_not_found`. An event out of order, or a waiver run that is not yet due or was already processed, fails with `invalid_transition`, and an `offer_id` already in use fails with `duplicate_id`. Replaying the `event_id` of a committed event changes nothing.

  **How events find records the agent creates.** Offers the agent makes get generated IDs, so `tallyhorn.fantasy.trade_response` names its offer by `trade_ref: {proposer_team_id, recipient_team_id, player_ids}`. It matches pending, unexpired offers from the proposer to the recipient that include every listed player on either side, and `player_ids` may be empty. Waiver claims need no key, because a run processes every pending claim with its `run_at`. If no offer matches, the event fails with `target_not_found`. If more than one matches, it fails with `ambiguous_target`. A failed event changes nothing and stores no receipt. `env.apply_event` returns `applied: false` with the failure code, and Core's audit records a `deny` row with that code as `reason`. The task can retry with the same `event_id`, the current `expected_version` and an `at` timestamp at or after the current virtual time, for example once the agent has made the offer. A committed acceptance whose trade checks fail marks the offer `failed` with `not_on_roster`, `player_locked` or `roster_full`, leaves both rosters unchanged and writes a notice. The agent's own tools never take these keys: they use IDs returned by its reads and writes.

  Tallyhorn writes in-app notices, read with `tallyhorn.notices.list`, for score alerts that match an active rule, injury updates to players on the account's fantasy roster or among its favorite players, schedule changes (postponement, new time, venue or feeds) to favorite teams' games, incoming trade offers, responses to the account's offers and the account's waiver results. Every notice comes from an event. None is written for the agent's own writes, such as a trade it accepts, and none for time passing alone, such as an offer expiring. Notices about a hidden game follow the spoiler rules in W3. Tallyhorn sends no cross-app notifications: `publishes` and `receives` are empty.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Node.js 18+ and the public runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` revision `7a05aa9`. The account's `account_id` and the owner's `person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`. Other fantasy managers carry an optional `person_id`, so a task that also uses Contacts or Messages gives Bram the same ID there. Tallyhorn keeps no contact details. Fixtures ship as UTF-8 JSON. The first version has no logos, photos, video or audio (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the reference profile's 64 assets). The package runs on its own with only Core: `dependencies.required_apps` is empty, and it reads nothing outside its own folder. Task authors keep grading judgments, such as whether a reply reveals a hidden result, in the evaluator. [Runtime](../../runtime/README.md) · [App contribution guide](../../docs/app-contribution.md)
