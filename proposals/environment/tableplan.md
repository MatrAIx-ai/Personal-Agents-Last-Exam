# Environment Track proposal

**Proposed PR title:** `[Environment Proposal] [Food & dining] TablePlan`

TablePlan is an independently branded, synthetic advance-catering customer portal. This is a design proposal, not an implemented or validated App. It targets public runtime 1.6.0 / Core API 1.2.0. Template and evidence baseline: repository commit `25d559778696dff849bb88fcca03f325185f2599`.

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable catering portal where a personal agent can compare current menu offers, prepare a group order, obtain an itemized quote, confirm or revise that order before its cutoff, and reconcile delivery and final invoice records. Its central business object is a versioned commitment to feed a specified number of people at a specified place and time. A changed headcount requires a new quote; changing a draft does not change an existing commitment.

V1 has one fictional caterer per run, one bound customer account, 4–40 attendees per order, six configurable menu packages, one currency (USD), one service area and explicit delivery windows. Multiple occasions and historical orders remain independently accessible. Five business views cover the menu, occasion/draft editor, quote comparison and confirmation, orders, and order detail with delivery, invoice and notice history. All screens use the same business operations as MCP.

| Workflow | Preconditions → action → observable result | Failure or control |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Prepare and compare | Read current packages and service terms; create an occasion draft with headcount, address, delivery window, package and vendor-facing instructions; request quotes for alternatives. Each quote preserves a full itemized total, servings, declared dietary properties, terms version and expiry. | A draft or quote creates no order. Invalid quantities, unsupported delivery areas and unavailable packages fail visibly; absent dietary information remains unknown. |
| Confirm | Submit an unexpired quote for an occasion with no active order. Atomically create one confirmed order with the exact quoted contents and total. | Expired quote, changed applicable terms, lost capacity or stale state rejects without creating an order. Replaying the same successful request returns the same receipt. |
| Amend | Before the current change cutoff, quote an amendment against the current order revision, compare old and new contents/totals, then explicitly apply it. Retain the order ID and append an immutable revision. | Quoting leaves the old order in force. A rejected amendment leaves it unchanged. Editing the same candidate invalidates its previous quote; creating a separate alternative does not. Stale amendments cannot overwrite a later change. |
| Cancel | Before the change cutoff, explicitly cancel a confirmed order. Retain its history, release capacity and record zero remaining order obligation under v1's free-before-cutoff rule. | At or after cutoff cancellation is unavailable; no late-fee or refund workflow is simulated. Repeated cancellation has no additional effect. A new order for the same occasion requires a new quote. |
| Follow and reconcile | Infra advances fulfillment and may update an ETA. The customer reads the current status and history; a delivery event records actual delivery, and an invoice event records the final itemization for the fulfilled revision. | ETA is not proof of delivery; delivery is not proof of payment. An invoice with mismatching items, quantities, fees, tax or total is rejected; conflicting duplicate invoices also reject. A cancelled order cannot be dispatched or delivered. |

### Business rules and task independence

- **Quantity and pricing.** One order selects one menu package and one supported preparation option for the entire group; it supplies exactly one complete meal per attendee, for 4–40 attendees. Mixed menus or per-person options require a later scope extension. Quotes disclose the package's minimum headcount; no quantity is silently rounded up. Each service window has a capacity measured in meals, not order count. Prices are integer cents: subtotal = headcount × (package unit price + selected option surcharge); tax = subtotal × the window's disclosed tax rate in integer basis points, rounded half up once to a cent; total = subtotal + tax + fixed delivery fee. Delivery is not taxed in this synthetic policy; there are no tips or hidden fees. Capacity updates cannot reduce a window below its existing active commitments. An amendment releases the old meal allocation and reserves the new one in the same transaction; failure preserves both the original allocation and order.
- **Independent candidates and quotes.** An occasion has stable candidate IDs. `draft.save` creates or revises one candidate; each candidate contains the complete proposed order, and its revisions remain private and readable. `quotes.create(candidate_id, candidate_revision, order_id?)` stores an immutable quote ID with that configuration snapshot, the account/occasion, package and window-terms revisions, and, for amendments, the current order revision. Creating candidate B and its quote does not invalidate candidate A or A's quote. Editing A invalidates A's earlier quotes. Quotes remain readable after expiry or invalidation, with a reason. A quote lasts 30 virtual minutes, capped by its applicable cutoff. Quotes reserve no capacity.
- **Quote validity and commitment.** Confirmation uses the booking cutoff; amendment uses the change cutoff. Commit requires the cited candidate revision, package revision and window-terms revision to still be current, an unexpired quote, a valid order state/revision, and enough current capacity. Any update to the quoted package (price, declaration, option or availability) invalidates its outstanding quotes; unrelated package updates do not. Any terms change for the quoted window invalidates its quotes. Capacity is checked at commit even with a current quote. Refreshing the Core version does not bypass these business checks. Committed orders retain the exact accepted package/declarations and price despite later catalog changes. A voluntary amendment gets a fresh quote under current prices for the whole order and shows the old/new total and difference; unrelated quotes cannot overwrite it.
- **Vendor acceptance.** For this bounded simulator, a valid quote represents an offer the fictional vendor commits to when the customer confirms and all commit-time checks pass. There is no separate pending vendor-approval queue. This intentionally simplifies the reference service's submitted/accepted distinction. A returned confirmation receipt is the vendor commitment in this App; merely reading or requesting a quote is not.
- **Time and deadlines.** All business time uses `ctx.virtual_now`; Infra sets the clock and an applied event's `at`. Each window has `booking_cutoff <= change_cutoff < delivery_window.start`. Equality is closed: `now >= cutoff` rejects the corresponding new commit. A terms event may shorten or extend either cutoff, provided the resulting cutoffs are both future times and preserve this ordering; it includes a visible reason and a window terms revision. It invalidates outstanding window quotes but does not invalidate existing commitments. At the same virtual timestamp, Core serializes events and customer operations: only an operation evaluated before a new terms revision can commit under the old terms; subsequent attempts must reread/requote. Reads derive action availability without mutating state. Clock advancement never dispatches or delivers food.
- **Order lifecycle.** Orders move `confirmed → preparing → dispatched → delivered`; only confirmed orders before the current change cutoff may be amended or cancelled. Infra can start preparation only at or after the current change cutoff. Once preparing, the order is permanently locked against customer amendments/cancellation: a later terms extension never reopens it. A still-confirmed order can become editable again after a valid extension; state and the current cutoff jointly determine eligibility. Reads return allowed actions and denial reasons. Occasion date, delivery window and address are fixed while an order is active; changing them requires cancelling and making a new candidate/quote. ETA updates are allowed only for preparing/dispatched orders and change no commitment or occasion time. Terminal orders reject further fulfillment changes. The vendor neither cancels accepted orders nor substitutes food in v1.
- **Dietary statements.** Packages expose versioned ingredient/allergen declarations, supported preparation options and explicit cross-contact/unknown statements. These are fictional vendor assertions, not medical judgments. Free-text instructions are retained verbatim as customer requests, not automatically promoted into a vendor guarantee. Selecting a declared preparation option preserves its stated scope on the quote and order. Tasks decide whether those facts satisfy a person's needs; a generic tag does not establish that every dietary requirement is met.
- **Privacy and authority.** Saving a candidate or generating a deterministic quote is private account preparation: neither operation exposes instructions to the merchant. The first merchant disclosure occurs only on a successful `orders.confirm` or `orders.amend`, atomically with commitment. These operations disclose the committed quantity, package/option, address, window and instruction text. Failed writes, quote reads and later editing/deleting private draft text disclose nothing. Each successful commit preserves a disclosure record containing exact disclosed fields/text, merchant recipient ID, order/revision, virtual timestamp, request ID and Core commit version; later amendments cannot erase it. Task evaluators join this history to supplied full tool-call/result traces and Core outcomes, distinguish denied attempts from actual disclosure, and count an idempotent replay as no new disclosure. Preserve snapshots before reset; Core audit alone does not retain arbitrary text. The App does not ingest private chats. Budget authorization and dietary suitability remain task judgments, not hard-coded business gates.
- **Duplicates and financial records.** An occasion has at most one active order (confirmed, preparing or dispatched). A delivered occasion is complete and cannot be reordered; a new real gathering requires a new occasion. A cancelled occasion may be rebooked with a new quote. Different occasion IDs can still represent mistaken duplicate real-world bookings, which tasks inspect across orders. Confirmation records a simulated obligation; amendment replaces it. No card charge, refund or paid status is claimed. Exactly one final invoice can be issued, only for a delivered order and its final committed revision; until then its invoice is pending. It must match the accepted package/option, headcount, unit amounts, delivery fee, tax and total, and reference the delivery record. A repeat with the same invoice ID and identical contents under a fresh event ID is a business no-op; another invoice ID for that order or conflicting contents is rejected. No correction/dispute workflow is included. Reconciliation means reading and comparing the commitment, delivery and invoice, not resolving a discrepant bill.

### Reusable tasks and overlap

Potential tasks include updating a gathering after extra guests confirm, preserving a dietary requirement without forwarding its private explanation, choosing an authorized alternative before a shortened cutoff, and reporting a delivery delay without changing the event time. Another task can prefer a different menu or permit a larger spend under identical commercial conditions.

The local [Lena spring gathering task](../task/lena-spring-gathering.md) provides a concrete motivating use case. Its $224/$268/$236 alternatives, ten guests and $240 authorization would be scenario data, not hard-coded rules. TablePlan would supply the vendor-order portion only. The complete task still requires its Mail, Messages, Calendar, session and grading integration; no compatibility or adoption by its author is claimed.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** Plan 13 Agent tools: `catalog.get`, `occasions.list`, `draft.save`, `quotes.create`, `quotes.get`, `orders.list`, `orders.get`, `orders.confirm`, `orders.amend`, `orders.cancel`, `invoices.get`, `notices.list`, and `notices.read`, under the `tableplan` namespace. `occasions.list` returns paginated occasions and full private candidate snapshots/revisions (optionally filtered by occasion/candidate ID), allowing drafts to be reopened. `catalog.get` returns current windows/capacity/terms and packages; optional package/version or window/terms-version selectors retrieve retained historical versions. Order reads include revision, disclosure and fulfillment history plus allowed actions/denial reasons; `quotes.get` returns the immutable snapshot and current validity reason. `invoices.get` distinguishes pending from an issued invoice. Quote creation is a write; quote retrieval is a read. Every other get/list operation is read-only; draft save, confirm, amend, cancel and notice-read marking are writes. The GUI uses the public Web Host and Agent bridge. Core supplies Infra-only seed/reset/snapshot, clock and event application. Merchant updates never become Agent tools or actor overrides. Account identity comes from the trusted launch configuration.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Core isolates runs and bound accounts and owns persistence and atomic commits. App records include occasions/drafts, menu and terms revisions, immutable quotes, orders and revisions, fulfillment events, invoices and notices. Every write carries an observed Core epoch/revision and a retry-stable request ID. Rejected writes leave business records unchanged; reads and denial evidence use the existing Core audit/rollout trace. After an unknown transport result the client rereads state or replays the identical request, including its original expected version and request ID, rather than creating a second order. After a known stale/invalid rejection, a revised request uses a new request ID. Stored receipts describe the original action; reread the order to learn its current status. Reset reloads the baseline and advances the epoch; snapshot exports the current state. No unsupported snapshot-restore API is assumed.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Infra owns four declared event types: `tableplan.catalog_changed`, `tableplan.terms_changed`, `tableplan.fulfillment_changed` and `tableplan.invoice_issued`. They carry stable event IDs, represented times, target IDs and expected source revisions; duplicates are idempotent and inconsistent/out-of-order transitions reject. A terms-change payload identifies a service window, old terms revision, new cutoffs and reason. A fulfillment payload identifies an order, transition or ETA change, and actual delivery time only for delivery. Core provides the event envelope; examples and effects are specified below. The implementation will validate their domain payloads against its schemas. Catalog events update a complete package revision or a window capacity, leaving existing committed contents intact; reducing capacity below commitments rejects. Task scenarios own scheduling. Relevant successful mutations append typed local notices atomically; notices are not Mail deliveries. Failed/replayed actions do not append duplicate notices. No Messaging publish/receive channel is declared in v1.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Required runtime: public PALE 1.6.0, Core API 1.2.0, contribution contract 0.4.0, Node 18+ and the public Web Host. App-only business tests require no neighboring App. A package-local registry will load `apps/tableplan/` without changing the reference assembly. Reuse the public schema validator and Core/Web patterns under MIT; do not import another App's private code. All business data are JSON/text; structured invoices are available through tools and GUI, without PDF/OCR or media assets. Store UTC-offset timestamps and the service IANA zone; dates displayed to the customer use that zone. Pin the exact runtime and browser versions in delivery evidence.

### Infra event examples

These are proposed event objects for `env.apply_event`; the caller also supplies the current Core `expected_version`. They are independent examples with the stated seeds, not a single executable timeline. IDs must belong to the bound account/run. `expected_*_revision` fields are business revisions in addition to the Core version. `at` is never a wall-clock callback. Seed and event schemas will be checked in the implementation.

1. With package `standard` at revision 2, replace its catalog definition. The new revision invalidates outstanding quotes for this package, while preserving existing orders. Unknown option references or negative prices reject atomically.

```json
{"event_id":"tp-catalog-1","at":"2026-04-10T10:00:00-04:00","operation":"tableplan.catalog_changed","payload":{"kind":"package_replace","package_id":"standard","expected_package_revision":2,"package":{"name":"Standard group meal","unit_price_cents":2200,"minimum_headcount":4,"available":true,"ingredients":["rice","beans","vegetables"],"contains":[],"may_contain":["sesame"],"declaration_status":"declared","options":[{"id":"standard-prep","surcharge_cents":0,"statement":"Standard preparation; shared kitchen."}]}}}
```

The alternative catalog payload is `kind: capacity_set`, with `window_id`, `expected_capacity_revision` and `capacity_meals`. It only changes that window's capacity and its capacity revision; commit still rechecks availability. It rejects values below current commitments and does not invalidate quotes solely because capacity changed.

2. With `dinner-16` terms revision 3 and delivery starting April 16 at 17:00, shorten both cutoffs. Reads now show the earlier deadline; old window quotes invalidate. New cutoffs at/before the event time or out of order reject. If any affected order is already preparing, a later extension still cannot unlock it.

```json
{"event_id":"tp-terms-1","at":"2026-04-13T10:00:00-04:00","operation":"tableplan.terms_changed","payload":{"window_id":"dinner-16","expected_terms_revision":3,"booking_cutoff":"2026-04-14T12:00:00-04:00","change_cutoff":"2026-04-14T12:00:00-04:00","reason":"Kitchen staffing changed."}}
```

3. With `order-1` dispatched, commitment revision 2 and fulfillment revision 3, record delivery and advance its fulfillment revision. `kind: status` also supports `preparing` and `dispatched` under the lifecycle guards; `kind: eta` instead takes `estimated_delivery_at` and a reason and leaves status unchanged. All variants use the expected commitment and fulfillment revisions. Delivery time is the event's `at`, so backdated or out-of-sequence delivery cannot invent an earlier completion.

```json
{"event_id":"tp-delivery-1","at":"2026-04-16T17:43:00-04:00","operation":"tableplan.fulfillment_changed","payload":{"order_id":"order-1","expected_order_revision":2,"expected_fulfillment_revision":3,"kind":"status","status":"delivered","delivery_id":"delivery-1"}}
```

4. For a delivered order whose committed totals match this example (ten meals at $20, 8% tax on subtotal, $20 delivery), issue its unique invoice. Changed line quantities with the same total, premature issuance or a second invoice ID reject. Identical business duplicates create no additional notice or invoice.

```json
{"event_id":"tp-invoice-1","at":"2026-04-16T18:00:00-04:00","operation":"tableplan.invoice_issued","payload":{"invoice_id":"invoice-1","order_id":"order-1","order_revision":2,"delivery_id":"delivery-1","package_id":"standard","package_revision":2,"option_id":"standard-prep","headcount":10,"unit_price_cents":2000,"option_surcharge_cents":0,"subtotal_cents":20000,"tax_rate_bps":800,"tax_cents":1600,"delivery_fee_cents":2000,"total_cents":23600}}
```

### Historical-data and validation plan

Use `America/New_York` for the dataset coverage calculation. Count represented record times, including order/revision `created_at`, quote `issued_at`, notice occurrence time, fulfillment occurrence time and invoice `issued_at`; never use file modification dates. Report source files/hashes, total records, per-source and globally deduplicated distinct dates, earliest/latest dates, monthly counts, real/synthetic partitions, invalid dates and gaps. Include meaningful records at both window boundaries and throughout the period, without claiming daily activity or a 300-date minimum. Public workflow captures remain provenance references and are not counted as customer activity. Public menu/service fact records use their observed date and are counted only in the distinct real-source partition; the report distinguishes these from synthetic business events. Seasonal menus, price revisions, occasional cancellations and differing group sizes provide variation instead of copying four identical orders into each month. If public menu facts cannot be obtained lawfully, report the gap for scope review rather than presenting workflow screenshots as a completed real-data anchor.

The eight smoke fixtures cover ordinary confirmation; headcount/menu amendment; shortened cutoff; stale quote/capacity conflict (including an old quote submitted with a fresh Core version); dietary unknowns and private versus disclosed instruction revisions; cancellation/rebooking and a post-preparation deadline extension; delayed delivery with pending/duplicate/mismatched invoices; and multiple occasions/account isolation. They do not replace the full dataset. A seed selector loads only records known by a scenario's initial virtual time; later events enter through Infra. Historical quotes, menus and order revisions remain readable without exposing future catalog changes.

Validation will inspect intermediate revisions as well as final state, particularly the exact total committed at each amendment and all vendor-facing instructions. Agent smoke trials use two recorded model configurations on ordinary and changed-cutoff cases, retaining failed attempts and distinguishing interface faults from reasoning faults. These are environment usability checks, not Task difficulty qualification. No tests, screenshots, datasets or agent trials are represented as completed by this proposal.
