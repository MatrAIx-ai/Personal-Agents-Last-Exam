## 2. What you plan to build

Briefly describe the main workflows and why this environment would be useful for personal-agent tasks.

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Tallymark is a resettable credit and identity-protection hub for one person. It holds the person's report at each of three bureaus, a freeze at each one, fraud alerts, disputes, an identity-theft report that blocks fraudulent items, and monitoring notices. Creditors, bureaus and breached companies act only through events the task schedules.

A person's credit file decides whether they get a loan, an apartment or a good rate, and looking after it is tedious work most people put off. That makes it a good job for a personal agent, and much of it starts without the owner asking. Some examples, not Task Track submissions:

- A monitoring notice says someone applied for a card in the owner's name. The agent checks all three reports, places a fraud alert and files an identity-theft report for the inquiry.
- A company the owner uses reports a data breach. The agent freezes the owner's credit at all three bureaus.
- The owner is buying a car on Thursday and the dealer's lender pulls Keystone. The agent lifts the Keystone freeze for those few days only.
- An account the owner always paid on time shows as late at one bureau and drags their credit down. The agent finds which bureau reports it and disputes it there.

These are easy to get subtly wrong. Almost every action works at one bureau only and has a time limit: a freeze at one bureau does nothing at the other two, a lift that is too long or at the wrong bureau leaves the file open, and a dispute has a 30-day clock. The freeze PIN works like a password and should never end up in an email. Fake breach-alert emails are also a common scam lure ([ESET, April 2026](https://www.welivesecurity.com/en/scams/data-breach-alert-might-be-trap/)), so the agent should check what the bureaus actually show before acting on one. Tallymark never labels anything as fraudulent and holds no task answer; it records what happened at each bureau and when.

Simplifications: one person per instance, already signed in; no credit score, since tasks act on the items behind a score (accounts, late payments, inquiries); money in integer cents. Time limits are seed settings loosely based on the FCRA. By default a freeze stops only credit applications, because the FCRA exempts purposes such as account reviews, prescreening and tenant screening.

1. **Reports and history** (read-only). `bureau.list`, `report.get` and `item.get` return each bureau's accounts (tradelines, each with balance and monthly payment status), inquiries and personal-information variants with their revisions; `as_of` rebuilds an earlier report.
2. **Freezes and lifts** (freeze `none | removed -> active -> removed`; lift `scheduled -> in_effect -> ended`, or `replaced`, `ended_early`, `cancelled`). `freeze.place` issues a new 10-digit PIN each time a freeze is placed. `freeze.lift` with the PIN opens one bureau from `start` to `end` (no past start, at most 30 days), taking effect up to 60 minutes after the request; a new lift replaces the current one and `freeze.refreeze` ends it early. `freeze.remove` needs the PIN. Bureaus never affect each other.
3. **Fraud alerts** (`active -> superseded | removed | expired`). `fraud_alert.place` adds an initial (1 year) or extended (7 years) alert at one bureau, which refers it to the other two. An extended alert needs a filed identity-theft report and supersedes an initial one. `fraud_alert.remove` acts at one bureau only. While an alert is active, a new account opens only if the creditor verified the applicant.
4. **Disputes** (`open -> resolved | withdrawn | superseded_by_block`). `dispute.open` disputes one item at one bureau with a reason and statement, due in 30 days. `dispute.add_info` extends the due date once by 15 days; `dispute.withdraw` cancels. The outcome (`deleted`, `updated`, `verified`, `dismissed`) arrives by event; with none by the due date the item is deleted (`deleted_unverified`).
5. **Identity-theft report and blocks** (report `filed -> withdrawn`; block `pending -> effective`, or `declined`, `cancelled`, `released`, `rescinded`). `idreport.file` lists the items the person did not authorize, and each gets a block that hides it 4 days later. `idreport.amend` adds or removes items; `idreport.withdraw` cancels pending blocks and releases effective ones. A bureau can decline or rescind a block by event.
6. **Monitoring notices and settings**. Events write notices (`new_inquiry`, `blocked_access`, `new_account` and `address_change` at monitored bureaus; `breach_exposure`, `dispute_result` and `block_update` always). If email copies are on, each notice also goes to Mail. `notice.list`, `profile.get` and `preferences.update` (monitoring per bureau, email copies) cover the rest. The agent's own actions write no notices, and neither do outcomes reached only by time (a lift ending, an alert expiring, a block taking effect, `deleted_unverified`); those show on reads.

Checked against the proposal list on 2026-11-22: no Environment proposal models a credit file, a freeze or identity theft. #182 Brindle Card is one issuer's card account with no credit file; #39 Lintel and #170 Homegrid own rental and utility applications, and Tallymark owns only the credit pull such an application triggers.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** MCP over stdio on the public runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` commit `a4bce02`. 22 Agent tools prefixed `tallymark.` (9 reads, 13 writes); the names are proposals. By workflow: (1) `bureau.list`, `report.get`, `item.get`; (2) `freeze.get`, `freeze.place`, `freeze.lift`, `freeze.refreeze`, `freeze.remove`; (3) `fraud_alert.list`, `fraud_alert.place`, `fraud_alert.remove`; (4) `dispute.list`, `dispute.open`, `dispute.add_info`, `dispute.withdraw`; (5) `idreport.list`, `idreport.file`, `idreport.amend`, `idreport.withdraw`; (6) `notice.list`, `profile.get`, `preferences.update`. The Agent acts as the bound person. No Agent tool returns the full SSN, full account numbers, stored PINs or the access log, except that `freeze.place` returns the PIN it issues. Infra-only operations: `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the host's `env.advance_time`; they are off the Agent connection and Core returns `permission_denied`. No GUI in v1; reviewers use `env.snapshot`.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is kept per run and App instance. Data has three parts: platform data (bureaus, organizations, rule settings), the person's account (profile, files with item revisions, freezes, lifts, alerts, disputes, reports, blocks, notices) and run records (access log, activity rows, email outbox), which only `env.snapshot` returns. `env.load_seed` loads any history with past timestamps and a `virtual_now`; broken references or overlapping lifts fail with `invalid_seed`. Every write takes `request_id` and `expected_version` and returns `status: committed`, the object ID and new version. A repeated identical request returns the first receipt, so a retried `freeze.place` gives the same PIN; a reused ID with different input fails with `idempotency_conflict`; a stale version, including one caused by an event, fails with `stale_version`; nothing is applied partially. Reads change nothing. `env.reset` restores this App's seed and clock only (`reset_scope: app_instance`); emails already in Mail stay. A snapshot is evidence, not a restore.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Seven events through `env.apply_event`: `tallymark.inquiry.request` (a company pulls one bureau; allowed or denied by the freeze and lift at that moment), `tallymark.account.open_attempt` (an application in the person's name: `refused_frozen`, `refused_alert` or `opened`), `tallymark.tradeline.post` (a new or revised account), `tallymark.personal_info.post` (a new address, phone or name), `tallymark.dispute.resolve` (the bureau's outcome), `tallymark.block.decide` (decline or rescind a block) and `tallymark.breach.exposure` (a breach record; no freeze or alert changes). Example input: `{"event_id":"ev-tm-101","at":"2026-12-03T10:05:00-08:00","operation":"tallymark.inquiry.request","payload":{"pull_id":"pull-0031","bureau":"meridian","requester_id":"org-orchardlane","purpose":"credit","kind":"hard"}}`. Meridian is frozen, so the pull is denied, nothing posts on the report, and one access-log row and one `blocked_access` notice are written. Second example: `{"event_id":"ev-tm-120","at":"2026-12-05T16:00:00-08:00","operation":"tallymark.breach.exposure","payload":{"breach_id":"brc-0002","organization_id":"org-pinecrest","discovered_on":"2026-11-28","data_kinds":["ssn","birth_date","address"]}}`. Each event is evaluated at its `at` (`ctx.virtual_now`). Lifts, alerts, disputes and blocks store due times, so `env.advance_time` fires no timers. A replayed `event_id` has no second effect; an event dated before the App's time or naming an unknown or closed object fails with `invalid_event` and changes neither data nor clock.

  Messaging: publishes `channel.email`; receives none. With email copies on, each notice goes into an outbox that the package's own sender publishes through Messaging (Infra supplies the trusted client and binding), following the send-only `examples/workflows/delivery-to-mail` example. Output payload for the denied pull (the `data` of the wire message), which passes Mail's payload schema `urn:pale:email-payload:1` at `a4bce02`: `{"channel":"channel.email","recipient":{"kind":"person_ref","id":"person-maren"},"source_object":{"app_id":"tallymark","account_id":"tallymark-maren","object_type":"notice","object_id":"ntc-0014"},"content":{"title":"Meridian blocked a request for your credit file","body":{"format":"text/plain","text":"On 3 December 2026 at 10:05, Orchard Lane Power asked Meridian for your credit file for a credit application. Your Meridian freeze was in place, so the file was not released."},"asset_ids":[]},"extensions":[]}`. The sender commit and the Mail commit are separate; a rejected delivery does not roll back the local notice.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for bureaus (`northline`, `keystone`, `meridian`), organizations, items (`<account_ref>.<bureau>` for tradelines, `inq-NNNN` for inquiries, `pi-NNNN` for personal-information variants), freezes, lifts, alerts, disputes, reports, blocks and notices. `binding.json` maps `account_id` and `owner_person_id`, so the person has one ID in Tallymark and Mail. Organizations are not Contacts records. All data is UTF-8 JSON text with an empty asset catalog, so no asset can go missing; an unknown ID returns `not_found`. The package runs on its own with only its files and the pinned shared runtime (release 1.6.0): `dependencies.required_apps` is empty, and Mail 1.3.0 is supplied with `--with` only for the integration check.
