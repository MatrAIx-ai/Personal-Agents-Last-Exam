## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable restaurant delivery app for one account holder, who orders prepared dishes from nearby restaurants for themselves or for people they look after: browsing restaurants and menus with hours, delivery areas, fees and restaurant-declared allergens; building a single-restaurant cart from structured options; checking out as soon as possible or for a scheduled time with an all-in total; following the order as the restaurant accepts and prepares it and a courier delivers it, including rejections, unavailable items and delays; cancelling with the charge the stage allows; and reporting missing or wrong items and rating orders afterwards. The App records menus, declarations, totals, statuses and refunds; it does not judge whether a dish suits a person, rank options or say what the right order is. No third-party code, brand assets, real menus or real payment data.

**Simulated policies and simplifications.** Time is the App's own virtual clock (Core keeps one per App), in one time zone per instance, set by the seed (America/New_York in the fixtures); money is USD in integer cents, and every computed amount is rounded to the nearest cent, half up.

- One account holder per App instance, with saved addresses and masked cards. Ordering for someone else means ordering from this account; Plateway keeps no household grants and no profile of what any person eats. A cart line may carry an optional `label` of up to 40 characters, shown on the receipt; it is never sent to the restaurant and cannot change a dish.
- Each address belongs to one delivery zone; each restaurant serves a listed set of zones, with a fixed travel time per zone. The delivery estimate is the restaurant's preparation minutes plus that travel time.
- A restaurant is open during its weekly hours unless a `restaurant.status_changed` event has paused it; a pause lasts until an event reopens it. Dishes have a name, a short description listing the main ingredients, a price, an availability flag and option groups (required or optional, with minimum and maximum selections); each option has an ID and a price change. Dishes are changed only through these options; there are no free-text instructions.
- Allergens are declared by the restaurant, per dish and per option, as `contains` or `may_contain` from the nine major US food allergens (milk, egg, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame). Plateway shows the declarations and does not verify them. A dish or option with no declaration returns empty lists, which means none declared, not allergen-free.
- The all-in total is simulated policy: subtotal (dish price plus option changes, times quantity), the restaurant's delivery fee, a service fee of 12% of the subtotal, a small-order fee of $2.49 when the subtotal is below $12.00, tax, and a tip in cents chosen at checkout. Tax on a line is 8% of that line's subtotal, rounded half up; the order's tax is the sum of its line taxes, so receipts and line refunds reconcile to the cent.
- The card is charged once, for the total, when the order is placed; a card marked as declining or expired fails with `payment_failed` and no order is created. Every refund is a record against that charge, returned to the same card; there are no credits or promotions. The receipt keeps the original lines, prices, fees, tax, tip and charge as booked; a removed line is marked, not deleted.
- Delivery is as soon as possible, or scheduled for a time on a 15-minute boundary from 45 minutes through 7 days ahead. The release time is the delivery time minus the current estimate; it must be strictly after the App's current time and within the restaurant's weekly hours, otherwise `delivery_time_unavailable`. A pause does not block scheduling. A scheduled order is released to the restaurant at its release time, when current hours, pause status and line availability are checked again.
- A submitted order that the restaurant does not accept within 20 minutes of becoming `submitted` is cancelled with a full refund (cancellation reason `not_accepted`).
- Cancellation by the account holder is free while the order is `scheduled` or `submitted`. While `preparing`, it refunds only the delivery fee and the tip as originally charged; the amount kept is the original charge less prior refunds and this cancellation refund. After pickup an order cannot be cancelled.
- Within 48 hours of delivery, the account holder may report delivered lines as `missing` or `wrong`, once per line; each reported line is refunded its subtotal plus its tax, automatically and in full. A report names a non-empty list of distinct lines, and every line is checked before any refund, so one unknown, removed or already reported line, or a line named twice, rejects the whole request. Within 30 days of delivery, each order may be rated once, 1–5 stars with optional text of up to 500 characters. A restaurant's aggregate rating is seed data and is not recomputed.

**Workflows.**

1. **Browse and check out.** Search restaurants that serve an address, read hours, status, fees, estimates and menus with declared allergens, build a cart from valid options, preview the all-in total for a delivery time, and place the order.
2. **Order lifecycle and disruptions.** Scheduled release, acceptance, preparation, pickup and delivery arrive through events and timers; the restaurant may reject the order, mark a line unavailable or fail to accept in time, and the courier may be delayed.
3. **Cancellation.** Preview the charge and refund for the order's current stage, then confirm.
4. **After delivery.** Report missing or wrong lines for a refund, and rate the order.

Rules:

- The cart belongs to the account and holds lines from one restaurant; adding a dish from another restaurant returns `different_restaurant`. Lines keep their option IDs and quantity (1–10); prices are always read from the current menu, so a price change shows in the cart and in the next preview.
- `order.item_unavailable` removes one line from a `submitted` or `preparing` order and refunds its subtotal plus its tax; the fees and tip stay charged unless the removal cancels the order. The event for a line already removed commits with no second refund.
- Refunds on an order never exceed its charge. A full-refund cancellation (while `scheduled` or `submitted`, by the restaurant, by the acceptance limit, at release, or when the last remaining line is lost) refunds the original charge less refunds already posted; an account-holder cancellation while `preparing` refunds only the delivery fee and the tip. A cancelled or delivered order accepts no further status events; `cancelled` and `delivered` orders stay readable.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** Ordering food for a household depends on whose meal it is, what that person can eat, what they ordered and said before, and what the account holder allowed the agent to spend. The App exposes reusable behavior, not one task's answer. Examples (separate Task proposals): order lunch for a spouse within a per-person all-in cap from searchable past orders and feedback, as Task #79 describes; order dinner for a child with a tree-nut allergy where a dish's own declaration is clean but an option or a `may_contain` declaration rules it out; schedule a family order for an evening whose usual restaurant is paused, and recover when a line becomes unavailable.

**Boundaries.** Grocery delivery, shopper substitutions and replacement rules belong to FreshCart (#37); here an unavailable dish is removed and refunded, never substituted. Group orders, participants and shared payments belong to Tablewise (#40), and dine-in reservations to Sitting (#41); both exclude delivery. Parcel redirects and pickup points belong to Parcel Delivery Manager (#101); here the address is fixed at checkout. Independent restaurant listings and reviews belong to Cornerpost (#66); Plateway keeps only an aggregate rating and the ratings of its own delivered orders. Task #79 plans to reach a delivery service through a VitaBench 2.0 bridge; it could use Plateway instead for restaurant and menu reads, explicit dish options, all-in totals, ordering, receipts and event-driven closures and statuses, while the Task supplies its attributed conversations and keeps recipient attribution and its session bridge. Proposal list checked on 2026-11-19: no other restaurant delivery, takeout or meal-ordering proposal. The `delivery` App ID on `main` belongs to a send-only example fixture, so this App uses `plateway`.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 16 Agent-facing MCP operations. Names below omit the App prefix; `tools.json` registers each as `plateway.<name>` (for example `plateway.orders.get`), because Core accepts only tool names that start with the App ID. Read (7): `account.get` (addresses with zones, masked cards and the App's current time), `restaurants.search` (by text and cuisine, for an address; returns only restaurants serving its zone, with status, estimate, fees and aggregate rating), `restaurants.get` (hours, status, fees and menu with prices, availability and declared allergens), `menu_items.get` (option groups, options, price changes and option allergens), `cart.get` (lines with current prices), `orders.list` (by status, restaurant and date range, newest first), `orders.get` (lines, labels, timeline, estimate, itemized receipt, refunds and rating). Preview, read-only (2): `checkout.preview`, `orders.cancel.preview`. Write (7): `cart.add_item`, `cart.update_item`, `cart.remove_item`, `orders.place`, `orders.cancel.confirm`, `orders.rate`, `orders.report_issue`. The Agent acts as the bound account holder set by Infra, never through an argument.

  `checkout.preview` takes an address, a card, `asap` or a scheduled time, and a tip, and returns the lines, every fee, the tax, the tip, the total, the estimate or scheduled time and the release time; `orders.cancel.preview` returns the amounts kept and refunded for the order's stage. Previews store nothing. A preview runs the same business checks as its confirm, including card expiry but not the simulated decline, which only an attempted charge reveals, and returns that error instead of a token; otherwise the token is a digest of the actor, the action, its business arguments (excluding `token`, `request_id` and `expected_version`), the run ID, the loaded seed's `seed_id` and the business changes the confirm would commit: for checkout, the restaurant, each line with its options, label and prices, every fee, the tax, the tip, the total, the card, the address and the delivery and release times (for as soon as possible, `asap` and the estimate in minutes, so a clock move alone does not stale the token); for cancellation, the order and its charge, the stage, each amount kept and refunded and the refund card. `orders.place` and `orders.cancel.confirm` carry the token, restate the arguments and Core's current `expected_version` like any write; the App recomputes the changes from current state, returns that check's error if the recomputation fails and `stale_preview` if the changes differ from the token; otherwise placement attempts the simulated charge, and on success the charge, order, emptied cart and notice commit together, while a decline returns `payment_failed` and preserves business state; a matching cancellation commits its state change, refund and notice together. For a fresh `request_id`, Core checks `expected_version` before invoking the write handler, so a confirm that carries the version seen at preview after another commit returns `stale_version`; `stale_preview` is for a confirm with the current version and a token whose changes no longer match. No user approval event is needed: the Agent acts as the account holder, and any spending limit belongs to the Task; a Task that must rule out purchases loads `policies` whose profile omits `plateway.orders.place`, and Core then returns `permission_denied`. Infra-only operations: `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status` and `env.validate_bundle`; Core returns `permission_denied` when the Agent calls them. App errors: `not_found`, `cart_empty`, `different_restaurant`, `invalid_option`, `item_unavailable`, `restaurant_closed`, `outside_delivery_area`, `delivery_time_unavailable`, `payment_failed`, `stale_preview`, `invalid_state`, `window_closed`, `duplicate_request`, `invalid_arguments` for a report that names a line twice, and `invalid_event` for an event that names an unknown restaurant, dish, order or line; Core adds `idempotency_conflict`, `stale_version`, `permission_denied`, `invalid_arguments` for input that fails a tool or event schema, and `invalid_event` for an event Core itself cannot apply, such as a fresh one dated before the App's current time.

  Checkout checks, in order: a non-empty cart (`cart_empty`), every line's dish available and options valid (`item_unavailable`, `invalid_option`), the address in a served zone (`outside_delivery_area`), the delivery time (`restaurant_closed` for as soon as possible while closed or paused; `delivery_time_unavailable` for a scheduled time outside the rules), then the card (`payment_failed` for an expired card at preview, and for an expired or declining card at placement). Cancellation returns `invalid_state` for `picked_up`, `delivered` and `cancelled` orders. `orders.report_issue` names a non-empty list of distinct delivered lines, each with a kind (`missing` or `wrong`); an empty list fails the input schema, and the App rejects a list that names a line twice, even with different kinds, both as `invalid_arguments`; it returns `invalid_state` before delivery and `window_closed` after 48 hours, then checks every line before recording any refund: `not_found` for a line not on the order, `invalid_state` for a line removed before delivery and `duplicate_request` for a line already reported. `orders.rate` returns `invalid_state` before delivery, `window_closed` after 30 days and `duplicate_request` for a second rating.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is per run and account, loaded by `env.load_seed` and validated (unique IDs, cart lines from one restaurant, every order's charge equal to its recomputed total, refunds never above the charge). The seed schema requires a `seed_id`, and Task authors give each distinct seed its own; preview tokens include it, so a token whose seed ID differs from the loaded seed's fails the freshness check, and reset restores the loaded seed ID. Every Agent write carries `request_id` and `expected_version`. Within an epoch, after access and schema checks, Core returns the stored receipt (marked duplicate) for an exact repeat of a committed write (the same arguments, including `expected_version`) or event (the same event and bundle), and `idempotency_conflict` for a write with the same ID and changed arguments; a fresh ID with an old epoch or revision, including one from before a reset, gets `stale_version`. A call that Core rejects preserves business data, version and notices and appends a denial audit row; after a lost reply, the caller retries the original request unchanged. Reset is Core's new epoch: Core restores the loaded seed and the virtual time loaded with it (the task's `virtual_now` in `env.load_seed`) and clears receipts and prior audit rows, recording the reset; seeded records and their scheduled work return to their loaded values. Reset covers this App instance only; notices already delivered to Mail stay there.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Events use Core's envelope via `env.apply_event`: `restaurant.status_changed`, `menu_item.availability_changed`, `menu_item.price_changed`, `order.accepted`, `order.rejected`, `order.item_unavailable`, `order.picked_up`, `order.delayed`, `order.delivered`, `clock.advanced` (empty payload; moves time only). Core sets the clock when a seed is loaded and restores it at reset; during a scenario it moves only through committed events. For each event Plateway applies timers due before its time in a stable order (due time, then order ID), then the event, then timers due at that time; a timer started by another timer (a release starts the 20-minute limit at its own due time) runs in the same pass if it is due. Timers: scheduled release and the 20-minute acceptance limit. Separate events with the same `at` apply in Infra's call order, and each runs the timers due at that time after its own action, so an `order.accepted` at exactly the 20-minute mark wins only when it is the first event at that time; a scenario that accepts a scheduled order at its exact release time sends `clock.advanced` at that time first. Order IDs created during a run are sequential (the next order after `o-21` is `o-22`, and lines, refunds and notices follow the same rule, repeated after reset), so a scenario can name the next order the Agent will place. An event that names a restaurant, dish, order or line that does not exist is rejected by the App as `invalid_event`; Core then preserves business data, notices, version and virtual time and appends a denial audit row. A late event skips its own action: order events apply only to an order in a matching state; it is still committed, so the clock moves to its `at` (which may equal the current time) and due timers run and may change records. For a schema-valid event with an unseen ID and the current version, Core rejects an `at` earlier than the App's current time as `invalid_event`. Notices, each written once per occurrence to the account holder: `notice.order_placed` (with the receipt) on placement, `notice.order_accepted` on acceptance, `notice.order_delayed` on a delay, `notice.order_delivered` on delivery, `notice.item_refunded` for each line refunded on its own (an unavailable line, a line removed at release or a reported line) and `notice.order_cancelled` once per cancellation, with that cancellation's refund; pickup writes none. Each notice is one log entry; seeded notice entries are history, and the adapter publishes only entries appended in the current epoch; an Infra-run sender adapter (package code) reads the log through `env.snapshot` and calls `router.publish` when the account holder has a Mail account bound in the run and an Infra Messaging grant for `channel.email`; otherwise entries stay in the log. Messages keep titles within 200 characters, bodies within 20,000 and message IDs within 100 (Mail stores the ID as a thread ID of at most 100 characters); the placed-order email carries the itemized receipt when it fits, otherwise the order ID, the total and a pointer to `plateway.orders.get`. A delivery rejected before dispatch (for example `incompatible_contract`) creates no Mail record; a rejection by Mail or an unknown acknowledgement halts the router for reconciliation; none of these rolls back Plateway. Example notice as a full wire message (`notice.order_delivered`): `{"specversion":"1.0","id":"e2-n-6","source":"urn:pale:app:plateway:account:plateway-p-1","type":"org.pale.mail.notification.v1","time":"2026-12-04T12:41:00-05:00","datacontenttype":"application/json","dataschema":"urn:pale:email-payload:1","data":{"channel":"channel.email","recipient":{"kind":"person_ref","id":"p-1"},"source_object":{"app_id":"plateway","account_id":"plateway-p-1","object_type":"order","object_id":"o-21"},"content":{"title":"Order delivered","body":{"format":"text/plain","text":"Order o-21 from Basil Row was delivered at 12:41. Total charged: $27.84."},"asset_ids":[]},"extensions":[]}}`. The adapter sets the message `id` and the publish `request_id` from the App epoch returned by `env.snapshot` and the notice-log entry: after a committed publish, a sequential, identical retry returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate` while the same router session is unhalted and the Mail binding and epoch are unchanged; an `unknown` response keeps the run halted; the integration check records Mail's account and epoch from its snapshot at router setup and, after `unknown`, queries `env.event_status` when an event ID is available and Mail is still in that epoch, verifying the receipt and the inbox record, and otherwise records the outcome as indeterminate; a notice written after a reset is a new message.

- T11 [W3] A cancel token taken while `submitted` and confirmed with the current version after `order.accepted` → `stale_preview`; after `order.item_unavailable` changes the amounts → `stale_preview`; [MCP] an identical retry of a committed cancellation → the stored receipt.
