## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Tarnloop is a resettable training log for one athlete. Its defining rule is that an activity is a record of where a person was, when, with whom and how their body performed, and that it arrives without the person acting: the watch uploads it with the account's default settings. Who can then see what is decided by four things at once (the activity's visibility, hidden zones around saved places, the list of approved followers, and copies handed to companions), and a challenge leaderboard rewards the opposite choice. The account holder's agent can read everything and has to decide what others may read. Nothing is tracked live and there is no map: a route is a named start place, a named end place and numbers. What other people and the watch do arrives as task-supplied events, and every time-based state follows the environment clock. Four workflows are complete in the first version.

**Entities.** Account (the bound athlete, time zone, club names, `default_visibility`, `default_hide`, `follow_approval`, hidden zones), place (name and area, no coordinates), athlete (name, clubs, optional `person_id`), follower row (an athlete who follows or asked to follow the bound athlete, `x_status`), activity (`sport`, `started_at`, `elapsed_s`, `moving_s`, `distance_m`, `elevation_gain_m`, `avg_heart_rate`, `start_place_id`, `end_place_id`, `route_name`, `title`, `description`, `private_note`, `visibility`, `hide`, `source` `device` or `manual`, `created_at`, `superseded_by`), companion (an athlete invited to one of the bound athlete's activities, `x_status`, `copy_held`), tag (another athlete added the bound athlete to their activity, `x_status`), comment (on one of the bound athlete's activities), challenge (`sports`, `metric`, `goal`, `starts_at`, `ends_at`, `has_leaderboard`), participation, notification, log row.

**Simplifications.** One App instance holds one account, which belongs to exactly one athlete. There is no GPS track, map, segment, live location, photo, feed of other athletes' activities, kudos, club page, training plan, subscription or device pairing. Places are a table the seed supplies; the Agent cannot add one. Visibility has three values (`everyone`, `followers`, `only_me`) and `hide` has three (`route`, `start_time`, `heart_rate`). A follower is approved for everything at the `followers` level or for nothing. An activity has at most 8 companions. The Agent acts as the account's athlete and nobody else.

- **W1 The log as its owner sees it (read-only).** Read the account (with the current environment time as `now`), places, activities (by sport, time window, visibility, place or source), one activity with its comments, companions and `outside_view`, athletes (by follower status or name) and one athlete, incoming tags, challenges, and notifications. `outside_view` is the App's answer to “who sees what”: `visible_to`, the number of approved followers, each withheld field with its reason (`zone:<zone_id>`, `hide:route`, `hide:start_time`, `hide:heart_rate`, `always_private`), the challenges on whose leaderboard the activity counts, and each companion with `copy_held`. Paths: modify, cancel and confirm are not applicable to reads; errors = an unknown ID (`not_found`, and the message names the list tool that returns valid IDs), a window that ends before it starts (`invalid_window`).
- **W2 Activities and privacy settings.** Add a manual activity, change an activity, delete one; change the account's defaults and hidden zones. Paths: modify = `activities.update` and `account.update`; cancel = `activities.delete`, which cannot be undone and removes the activity's comments; errors = an activity that ends after the current time (`in_future`), a time span that overlaps another activity (`overlapping_activity`), `moving_s` above `elapsed_s` or a distance on a sport that has none (`invalid_measure`), a change to a measured field of a `device` activity (`measured_field`), any change to a superseded activity (`invalid_state`), a zone that names an unknown place (`invalid_zone`), text over its limit (`text_too_long`). Confirm: `activities.delete_preview` returns what a deletion would remove and change (comments, companions who keep a copy, each challenge's progress and leaderboard total before and after). The delete itself carries Core's `expected_version`, so a delete prepared before anything else changed, including the clock, fails with `stale_version`.
- **W3 Followers, comments, companions and tags.** Answer a follow request or remove a follower; reply to or remove a comment on the athlete's own activity; invite followers to an activity as companions; accept or decline a tag from another athlete. Paths: modify = `followers.respond`, `tags.respond`, and the `companions` key of `activities.update`; cancel = `followers.remove`, `comments.remove`, removing a companion, or `tags.respond` with `declined` after an acceptance; errors = an answer to a request that is not `pending`, removal of someone who is not an `accepted` follower, removal of a removed comment, or an acceptance of a tag that is not `pending` (`invalid_state`), a companion who is not an `accepted` follower (`not_a_follower`), a ninth companion (`too_many_companions`), companions on an activity that is `only_me` or hides its route (`not_shareable`), `text_too_long`. Confirm: `athletes.get` returns what a follow request is judged on (clubs shared with the account, earlier requests and how they ended, whether the athlete was a companion before), and `activities.get` returns `outside_view`.
- **W4 Challenges.** Read a challenge with the athlete's `progress`, `remaining`, `leaderboard_total` and `completed`; join or leave one. Paths: modify is not applicable (a challenge is changed by its organizer, outside the account); cancel = `challenges.leave`; errors = joining an `ended` challenge (`joining_closed`), `already_joined`, leaving a challenge the athlete has not joined (`not_joined`), leaving after the end (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `challenges.get` returns the same numbers before joining, marked `joined: false`, so the Agent can see what joining would show.

Rules:

- **Who the Agent acts for.** The bound athlete is the athlete whose `person_id` equals the `owner_person_id` of the Tarnloop account in the run's `binding.json`. Identity is matched by `person_id`, never by name.
- **What others see.** `only_me`: nobody else. `followers`: every athlete whose follower row is `accepted`. `everyone`: anyone. From what an audience sees, the App withholds `private_note` always; `start_place` or `end_place` when that place lies in a hidden zone; both places and `route_name` when `hide` has `route`; the time of day (the audience gets the local date in the account's time zone) when `hide` has `start_time`; `avg_heart_rate` when `hide` has `heart_rate`. Title, description and comments go to the whole audience. Zones apply to every activity, past and future; `default_visibility` and `default_hide` are copied onto an activity only when it is created.
- **Companions get a copy.** An athlete who accepts an invitation holds a copy of the activity: sport, date and time, duration, distance, both places and the route name, without the owner's zones and without `hide`, and without heart rate. The copy is shown to that athlete's own audience, which Tarnloop does not know. Removing the companion, narrowing the activity or deleting it does not recall the copy, so `copy_held` stays `true` and `outside_view` keeps listing it. An invitation is therefore refused on an activity that is `only_me` or hides its route, and open invitations are removed in the same commit when an activity becomes one of those.
- **Tags run the other way.** An accepted tag shows the bound athlete on another athlete's activity to that athlete's audience (`audience: "unknown"` in every read). It creates no activity in the log.
- **The watch outranks the keyboard.** The Agent creates `manual` activities only. A `device` activity comes from `activity.synced`; its measured fields (`started_at`, `elapsed_s`, `moving_s`, `distance_m`, `elevation_gain_m`, `avg_heart_rate`, both places) cannot be changed, and its sport, title, description, note, visibility, `hide` and companions can. When an upload overlaps a manual activity, both are kept and the manual one is marked `superseded_by`: it counts toward nothing, accepts no change and can only be deleted.
- **Challenges.** Status: before `starts_at` → `upcoming`; from `starts_at` up to but not including `ends_at` → `active`; at or after `ends_at` → `ended`. An activity qualifies when its sport is in `sports`, its `started_at` is at or after `starts_at` and before `ends_at`, its `created_at` is before `ends_at` plus 72 hours (an upload exactly 72 hours after the end is too late), and it is not superseded. `progress` adds the challenge's metric (`distance_m`, `moving_s`, `elevation_gain_m` or a count) over all qualifying activities, whatever their visibility or source. `leaderboard_total` adds only those that are `everyone` and `device`; other participants see it when the challenge has a leaderboard and the athlete has joined. `completed` is `joined` and `progress` at or above `goal`. Activities recorded before joining count. All four values are computed from the stored activities whenever they are read, are never stored, and follow later edits and deletions.
- **Time.** Time is the environment's. Tarnloop has no timers and no clock event: Infra moves `virtual_now` with an event or with Core's `env.advance_time`, which runs no App handler but raises the App revision, so a write prepared before the clock moved returns `stale_version` and the Agent reads again. One thing depends on the clock, a challenge's status, and it is derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever it is read or checked. Nothing is rewritten when time passes, so one jump and many small steps leave identical state. `env.snapshot` returns stored state without this derivation: a grader recomputes challenge status from `starts_at`, `ends_at` and the clock, and may recompute `outside_view`, `progress`, `leaderboard_total` and `completed` from stored records with the package's pure module `lib/derive.cjs`, which the handlers themselves call. Agent reads and writes never move the clock; the README and each tool description say so.
- **Text.** `title` up to 100, `description` and `private_note` up to 1000, a comment up to 500 Unicode code points, checked in the handler so that `text_too_long` is reachable. Text is stored and returned exactly as written and is never interpreted.
- **IDs.** Seeds and events give their records' IDs. Records the Agent creates get `act-a1`, `act-a2`, … and `cmt-a1`, … in commit order within a run, starting again after a reset; a seed or event that uses this form is rejected.
- **Notifications** are written for what events do, never for the Agent's own writes and never for time alone: `activity.synced`, `activity.superseded`, `follower.requested`, `follower.joined` (a request accepted at once because approval is `open`), `comment.received`, `tag.received`, `tag.answered`, `challenge.published`. A follower who withdraws or leaves produces none. A notification is never removed.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** A training log is the most detailed diary many people keep without writing it: the hour they leave home, the door they leave from, the days they skip, the people they meet, a resting pulse that climbs before an illness. It fills itself, and it is published by default. An agent with the account can do real work here (name and file a week of uploads, answer a coach, work out whether a monthly goal is still reachable around a business trip) and every one of those jobs passes through the question of who learns what. The right action depends on the person: the same follow request is welcome from a club mate and unwelcome from a stranger in the week the user starts running alone before dawn; the same run belongs on the leaderboard for one user and under `only_me` for another who is away from home; a physiotherapist should get heart-rate detail that the public page hides. Tarnloop makes these checkable without a judge: the stored settings give the audience of every activity at every commit, the log rows record how each write widened or narrowed it, and companion copies and tags record what can no longer be taken back. Distinct task situations (separate Task proposals): (1) after a move, the watch keeps uploading runs that start at the new flat while the hidden zone still covers the old one, and the user asks only to “post the long run for the club”; (2) three follow requests arrive, and a note written months ago says to approve people from the running club and to ask about anyone else; (3) a coach asks in a public comment how the knee is, and the answer sits in a private note and in the physiotherapist's email; (4) nine days are left in a 100 km challenge, the calendar holds a trip, one run was typed in by hand and counts for progress but not for the leaderboard, and uploads close 72 hours after the end; (5) a training partner asks to be added to Saturday's ride, which would hand them a copy that starts at the user's door; (6) a tag would show the user at a place and time on a page whose audience nobody on the user's side can see, and the user's standing rule is to be asked first; (7) the user is travelling for two weeks and does not want the log to show an empty home while it is empty. The rationale is independent of any one task. Fixtures are plain seed data, so each task loads its own athlete, places, followers, history and dates.

**Boundaries and overlap.** Proposal list checked on 2026-11-25 (105 Environment proposals, through #242): none covers a training log, activity visibility, followers of an athlete or fitness challenges, and a search of pull requests for strava, garmin, athlete, cycling and marathon returns no Environment. Nearby proposals differ in what they hold. #32 (Personal Health & Life Hub) brings wearable readings such as sleep and body measures together with Calendar, Mail, Notes and Tasks for the person's own planning; Tarnloop holds no sensor series, sleep or body measure, only one summary per activity, and is about what is published. #15 (Cadence) books studio classes and #145 (Trailhead) registers for city programs. #217 (Ridgelog) holds trail conditions, trip reports and permits, not a person's log or followers. #26 (Stoopside) records who was shown a group post that someone wrote; in Tarnloop the record is measured, arrives unasked and exposes a place and a time. #194 (Fernshelf) shares photos whose saved copies cannot be called back, which Tarnloop's companion copies resemble for a different kind of record. #168, #209 and #238 publish written posts and video. Tasks that could use a training log include #117 (the author's own), #29, #52, #201 and #206; the author will ask their authors in this PR which fields they need.

## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** None in the first version. All four workflows complete with Tarnloop alone, and no workflow needs the Agent to call another App's tools. Notifications are an in-App log read with `notifications.list`; no email, chat or calendar message is sent, so there is no messaging declaration (`publishes` and `receives` are empty), no publisher script and no receiver to check. A task that wants the physiotherapist's email, the club's group chat or the trip on the calendar seeds them in Mail, Messages and Calendar; the Agent reads them with those Apps' own tools and then acts in Tarnloop. Contacts, Files, Notes and Tasks: none; Tarnloop does not copy or shadow any of them.
- **Other apps and websites (required):** None required. A task that also uses Cadence (#15), the Personal Health & Life Hub (#32) or a weather App seeds consistent data in each; there is no App-to-App channel. No external website, map, device cloud or fitness service is called at run time.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 22 Agent-facing MCP operations over stdio (`service.cjs --role agent`, started through the launcher pattern of `apps/notes/service.cjs`), registered in `tools.json` as `tarnloop.<name>`. Read (11): `account.get`, `places.list`, `activities.list`, `activities.get`, `activities.delete_preview`, `athletes.list`, `athletes.get`, `tags.list`, `challenges.list`, `challenges.get`, `notifications.list`. Write (11): `account.update`, `activities.create`, `activities.update`, `activities.delete`, `comments.add`, `comments.remove`, `followers.respond`, `followers.remove`, `tags.respond`, `challenges.join`, `challenges.leave`. Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version` and returns the object's ID with the committed version; App fields that carry a state are named `x_status`, because Core reserves `status` in results. The Agent acts as the bound athlete, never through an argument. The handlers know the caller only as `agent` or `infra`; package tools are exposed to the Agent only, and there is no user-only tool and no approval state. Because `policies.json` can restrict only the argument `id` and the keys of `patch`, every tool names the object it acts on as `id` (the activity for `comments.add`, the athlete for `followers.respond`, the account for `account.update`) and every change to an existing object goes through `patch`. A task can therefore forbid exactly the `visibility` key, the `companions` key or the `hidden_zones` key, limit follower answers to named athletes, or leave a tool out of its profile; `fixtures/policies.json` ships two profiles, `full` and `read_only`. What this does not give: a policy cannot limit which value a key takes (for example allow `followers` but not `everyone`), and the proposal claims nothing more. App errors: `not_found`, `invalid_window`, `in_future`, `overlapping_activity`, `invalid_measure`, `measured_field`, `invalid_zone`, `text_too_long`, `invalid_state`, `not_a_follower`, `too_many_companions`, `not_shareable`, `joining_closed`, `already_joined`, `not_joined`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Core errors carry a code and a message only, so anything structured (what a deletion would change, why an activity is not shareable) comes from a read.

Tarnloop adds no Infra query, none of these is listed on the Agent connection, and Core returns `permission_denied` if the Agent calls one. **GUI:** a browser interface is part of this plan. The package owns one page and one controller (`gui/index.html`, `gui/src/app.js`) with six views inside it (Log, Activity with a “What others see” panel, People, Challenges, Privacy settings, Notifications), declared in `contribution/gui.json` with the `pale.web` extension and served by the shared Web Host over the Agent MCP bridge, following `apps/notes`. It reads and writes the same state as MCP, shows no management control, renders every text as text, and uses only the Host's shared stylesheet and client. The package brings its own registry file for the Host, so no file outside `apps/tarnloop/` changes; if maintainers prefer an entry in the repository's `apps.registry.json`, the submission will call out that one edit.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is separate per run and App instance, lives in Core, and one instance holds one account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`); `account_id` sits at the top level of the seed. The seed schema accepts as history every record type the App has: the account with its settings and zones, places, athletes, follower rows in every status with their earlier requests, activities (manual, device, superseded, and tombstones of deleted ones), comments (posted and removed), companions in every status, tags in every status, challenges with participation, notifications and log rows. A task can therefore load any history of its own instead of the package fixtures. Seeded history starts on 2025-01-01: the package fixtures cover 2025-01-01 to 2026-11-17, `fixtures/clock.json` sets the time to 2026-11-18T09:00:00-08:00, after the latest record, and example events are dated from there on. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, an account whose athlete's `person_id` is not the account owner's in the binding, an ID in the Agent-created form, two activities that overlap unless one is manual and marked as superseded by the other, a `device` activity that is superseded, an activity that ends after `virtual_now`, a zone that names an unknown place, more than 8 companions, text over its limit, a challenge that ends at or before its start, any creation or change time after `virtual_now` or before 2025-01-01, and timestamps without a UTC offset (`invalid_seed`). Writes follow Core: an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` or `event_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate` with no second effect; the same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`; a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`; a failed call leaves data and version unchanged. Every applied event and every clock move raises the App revision, so the Agent's next write with an older version returns `stale_version` and the Agent reads again before deciding. Reads never change business data (Core's own audit grows on every call). Reset is Core's: it restores the most recently loaded seed and the time loaded with it (`reset_scope: app_instance`), clears the instance's audit and receipts, and touches no other App; take the snapshot before a reset. Evaluator records come from three places, and nothing in Tarnloop is hidden from the Agent: what is graded is what the Agent let others see. (a) App state in the snapshot: all records above, `ignored_events`, and log rows with sequence, virtual time, actor `agent` or `event`, action, object ID and the `request_id` or `event_id` behind them, one for every committed Agent write and every applied or ignored event. A row for `activities.update`, `account.update` or `followers.respond` holds the replaced values; a row for a write that changes one activity's audience holds its `outside_view` before and after; a row for `account.update`, `followers.respond` or `followers.remove` holds how many activities became visible to more people and to fewer. (b) Core audit rows, which give the tool and the outcome of every allowed and denied call, reads included, but not its arguments or request ID. (c) The supplied Agent execution trace, which gives full arguments and results, so which activity or athlete a read concerned comes from the trace. Count committed writes and event effects from the log rows, rejected attempts from Core's denial rows together with the trace, and replays (a result carrying `duplicate: true`, which leaves no audit row) from the trace as retries of the original. Time writes no row: challenge status is recomputed as described under Rules.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Six events through `env.apply_event`, in Core's envelope, named `tarnloop.<object>.<verb>` and listed identically in `app.json` and `contribution.json`. Tarnloop has no clock event. An event is handled at its own time, which is `ctx.virtual_now` inside the handler, and Core rejects an event dated before the App's current time. Events never use an ID that only exists once the Agent has acted. `comment.received` and `tag.answered` name the activity either by `activity_id` (known in advance for seeded and synced activities) or by `activity_match` with `sport` and `local_date`; a match considers the athlete's activities that are neither deleted nor superseded and, when several fit, takes the one with the earliest `started_at`, then the lowest ID. Follower rows and companions are found by `athlete_id`. An event about an athlete the App does not hold may carry the athlete's record inline; for an athlete it already holds, the inline record is ignored. An event that names a superseded activity by ID is applied to the activity that superseded it, and the log row records the redirection.

  | The event arrives and… | Result |
  | :--- | :--- |
  | no activity has the ID or fits the match, or the one that did was deleted | committed no-op, `ignored_reason: no_matching_activity` |
  | the commenter cannot see the activity (it is `only_me`, or `followers` and the commenter is not an accepted follower) | committed no-op, `not_visible_to_commenter` |
  | `tag.answered` finds no `invited` companion for that athlete on the activity | committed no-op, `no_open_invitation` |
  | `follower.changed` with `withdrawn` or `left` finds no `pending` or `accepted` row; `requested` finds one already `pending` or `accepted` | committed no-op, `no_matching_follower` or `already_following` |
  | a removal or withdrawal names a comment or tag that does not exist or is already closed | committed no-op, `no_matching_comment` or `no_matching_tag` |
  | a new record reuses an ID (`duplicate_id`); an athlete is unknown and no record comes inline (`unknown_athlete`); the acting athlete is the bound athlete (`actor_is_account_holder`); an upload overlaps another `device` activity (`device_overlap`) or ends after the event's own time (`ends_after_event`); a challenge ends at or before its start, or any field is malformed (`invalid_payload`) | rejected with that code; state unchanged |

  A no-op is committed because a task author cannot know what the Agent will have done: the scenario point is recorded as processed, and the grader finds the reason in `ignored_events`. Notifications: the eight kinds under Rules, each written once per occurrence. Messaging directions: publishes none; receives none.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for places, athletes, activities, comments, tags, challenges and notifications. The account's `account_id` and the bound athlete's `person_id` are the shared values of the run's `binding.json`, which holds people, accounts and object IDs and nothing else. Any athlete may carry an optional `person_id`, so a task that has the same people in Contacts, Messages or Mail gives them the same IDs in its seeds; Tarnloop stores a display name and club names, not a copy of a contact. Tarnloop is the source of truth for its own records and keeps no copy of another App's. Missing data is explicit: an unknown ID returns `not_found`; an indoor activity has `start_place_id: null` and `route_name: null`; an activity without a heart-rate reading has `avg_heart_rate: null`; a sport without distance has `distance_m: null`; a challenge the athlete has not joined reads `joined: false`; the audience of a companion's copy or of a tag is `"unknown"`. Timestamps carry a UTC offset and the account names its IANA time zone; local dates are computed in that zone with Node's built-in `Intl`. All records are JSON; there are no images or attachments (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the profile's 64 assets, no registration file). Runtime needs: the public runtime 1.6.0 with Core API 1.2.0 and contribution revision 0.4.0 on `main` at commit `7a05aa9`; `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; no external service and no package install. The App package runs on its own with only the shared runtime: a copy that holds `apps/tarnloop/` and the pinned runtime starts the App, its business tests and example run from the package alone, and it needs no other App and no code or file outside its folder.
