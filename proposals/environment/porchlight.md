## 2. What you plan to build

Briefly describe the main workflows and why this environment would be useful for personal-agent tasks.

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable family portal of a home-care agency, seen from the account of one family member who looks after one client, usually an older parent. Caregivers visit the client's home on a weekly pattern, and a payer authorizes a number of hours per week. The family's agent reads the schedule, changes or cancels visits, answers the agency's substitute offers and checks the recorded hours. The portal checks each action against the agency's policy and knows nothing about the family's wishes or budget, which is where tasks come from.

Workflows:

1. **Schedule and hours (read-only):** list visits, read the caregiver roster, read a week's hours against the authorization, read the agency's policy.
2. **Changing visits:** cancel a visit, shorten it, or ask the agency to move it, extend it or add a visit; withdraw such a change request. The agency's decision arrives as an Infra event, and the agency can also cancel, retime, reassign or add visits itself.
3. **Substitutes:** when the scheduled caregiver cannot come, the agency offers a substitute with a reply deadline; the family accepts or declines. The family can mark a caregiver as preferred or as not to be sent.
4. **Recorded hours:** after each visit the agency records check-in and check-out; the family can dispute a record within a time limit. The agency's decision arrives as an Infra event.
5. **Notices:** Porchlight records five notices for the family member: substitute offered, change request decided, visit changed by the agency, dispute decided and authorization changed. When Mail is enabled, the host delivers them to the family member's inbox (Section 3).

Business rules that make the right action depend on the person:

- **Time.** A visit stores its start and end as instants. Every period given in hours is elapsed time, and a limit is met when the current time is not later than it. A visit belongs to the local date and the week in which it starts, in the agency's IANA time zone, which the seed sets; a week runs Monday to Sunday. Lengths are whole minutes and amounts are whole cents; the seed's hourly rate must be a multiple of 4 cents. Two visits of the client that are not cancelled may not overlap; a visit that ends at the instant another starts does not overlap it. The values used in this section (24 hours, 2 hours, 7 days) are the policy values of the shipped seeds, and the change-request limit uses the same notice hours.
- **Notice decides what a change costs.** The notice limit is 24 hours before a visit's start. Up to that limit, a cancelled visit costs nothing. After it, a cancellation is late: the family is charged a fee, at the agency's hourly rate, for the visit's length as it stood at the notice limit, so shortening first does not reduce it. Instead of cancelling late, the family can shorten the visit by ending it earlier, to no less than the agency's minimum visit of 2 hours; it then runs and is billed for the shortened length. For example, with a rate of $34.00, a 4-hour visit cancelled 10 hours before its start costs the family $136.00, and the same visit shortened to 2 hours runs as a 2-hour visit. Cancelling a visit that is waiting for a substitute or is unfilled is never late, and a visit that was created, moved or retimed after its notice limit had already passed can also be cancelled without a fee.
- **Change requests need the agency.** Moving a visit, extending its end or adding a visit is a change request, made no later than 24 hours before the earlier of the old and new start times, and the agency approves or declines it. A request still undecided at that earlier start time lapses. A visit with a pending request cannot be shortened, and it has at most one pending request. A substitute offer or an agency change to the visit closes a pending request as `superseded`; the family can ask again if the limit has not passed.
- **Hours beyond the authorization are private pay.** Visits that are scheduled or waiting for a substitute count toward their week at their scheduled length, and completed visits at their billed minutes; cancelled and unfilled visits and caregiver no-shows do not count. A week outside the authorization's dates has no authorized hours. Hours above the authorization are billed to the family at the hourly rate, and so are late-cancellation fees, which never use authorized hours. For example, with 20 authorized hours and 18 counted, adding a 3-hour visit puts 1 hour on private pay, $34.00.
- **The family agrees to private pay only when it acts.** Agreed minutes belong to the object that carries them: a change request that is `pending` or `approved`, or an `accepted` offer, as long as its visit is not cancelled. A week's agreed minutes are their sum. A change request or an offer acceptance needs an explicit `accept_private_pay` flag from a `representative` when it would raise the week's minutes above the authorization, counting pending requests, by more than the week's agreed minutes; it then stores the shortfall as its own agreed minutes. An action that does not raise the week's minutes never needs the flag. A late cancellation also needs a `representative`.
- **Private pay can arise without agreement,** whenever the agency or the payer acts: a lowered authorization, a visit that runs over, a visit the agency adds, reassigns or offers again. `porchlight.week.get` reports the smaller of the minutes above the authorization and the agreed minutes as agreed, and the rest as not agreed, whatever caused it.
- **A substitute offer does not check the family's marks.** A mark is the family's own scheduling note; the agency does not see it. An offer, an approved request or an agency change can name a caregiver marked as not to be sent; the portal shows the mark wherever that caregiver appears and still lets the family accept. An offer must be answered by its deadline; after that it is expired and the visit is unfilled until the agency offers or assigns someone else. An unfilled visit whose start has passed is final and is not billed.
- **Recorded time is billed in quarter hours, rounded up,** which is this agency's stated billing rule. The billed time is the rounded time between check-in and check-out; it is not clipped to the schedule and has no minimum. A visit recorded from 09:05 to 12:10 is 185 minutes and is billed 195. A record can be disputed once, until the end of the 7th local day after the day on which the record was created. It becomes final when that window has ended and no dispute on it is open.
- **Who is acting matters.** The seed gives the account one of two roles. A `representative` can do everything. A `scheduler` can change visits and answer offers but cannot agree to private pay, cancel late, or open or withdraw a dispute.

These rules are illustrative and are stated in full by `porchlight.policy.get`; they do not describe any real agency, payer or law. A mark is a scheduling preference only: complaints and safety incidents about caregivers are out of scope.

Entities: family account and its role, client, agency policy (time zone, notice hours, minimum visit, hourly rate, dispute days), caregiver roster (name, languages, skills) with the family's mark and note, authorization (weekly hours and the dates it covers), visit (start, end, caregiver, billing fields and history), change request, substitute offer, time record, dispute and outbound notice.

Statuses. Visit: `scheduled`, `awaiting_substitute`, `unfilled`, `cancelled` (by the family, with a `late` flag, or by the agency), `completed` or `caregiver_no_show`. `cancelled`, `completed` and `caregiver_no_show` are final, and so is an `unfilled` visit once its start has passed. Change request: `pending` → `approved` or `declined` by the agency's decision, `withdrawn` by the family, `superseded` when the visit is changed in another way, or `lapsed`. Offer: `pending` → `accepted` or `declined` by the family, `withdrawn` when the agency changes the visit or the family cancels it, or `expired`. Time record: `recorded` → `final`. Dispute: `open` → `upheld` or `rejected`, or `withdrawn`.

Stored and derived. Four statuses are derived and never stored: `lapsed`, `expired`, `unfilled` and `final`. One exported function computes them from the stored record and the current time, and every tool and event handler calls it, so reads change nothing and graders compute the same status. A `pending` change request reads `lapsed` after its limit. A `pending` offer reads `expired` after its deadline. A visit is stored as `awaiting_substitute` from the moment its caregiver is withdrawn, and reads `unfilled` whenever it has no pending offer. A `recorded` time record reads `final` as described above. A snapshot and a seed hold only stored statuses. A visit whose start has passed stays `scheduled` until the agency records it. For a completed visit, the time record's billed minutes replace the scheduled length in billing and in the week.

Reusable value: tasks with different stories can use these rules. Examples: a substitute offer that names the caregiver a parent asked never to see again; a clinic appointment that moves into the end of tomorrow's visit, where shortening is right and cancelling is not; an extra visit after a fall that would exceed the authorized hours, where the answer depends on what this family can pay; a sibling's account that may reschedule but not commit money; a record that bills four hours for a visit a parent says ended early. Task #72 has a weekday aide and a weekend agency that appear only as members of a family chat, and Task #115 has a caregiver who appears in a recurring calendar entry. The App holds no rubric and no task answer.

Related work, checked against the full text of all 185 open proposals on 2026-11-23: none implements recurring care visits, authorized hours, substitute offers or recorded hours. #173 (staff scheduling from one hourly employee's account) is the opposite side of a schedule: it shares a weekly-hours read but no write workflow, and it excludes time clocks and timesheets. #85 (family group roles, including an aide role, and a pinned care note) holds no schedule. #63 (pharmacy, with a caregiver's authorization to collect medicines), #14 (patient portal) and #5 (one-off home repair bookings) do not cover care visits. #15 and #145 have late-cancel fees and expiring offers for class and program bookings, which are similar mechanisms in a different setting.
