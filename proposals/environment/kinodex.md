# Kinodex film and TV guide, watch diary and where to watch

**Proposed PR title** — [Environment Proposal] [Media & entertainment] Kinodex film and TV guide, watch diary and where to watch

## 1. App and domains

- **Authors / GitHub handles (required):** @byshail
- **Reference app or website / URL (required):** Kinodex is a fictional film and TV guide used by one person. IMDb is the reference for titles, editions, credits, certifications, parental guides and the contributor correction flow. Letterboxd is the reference for the watch diary, ratings, reviews with spoiler flags and lists. JustWatch is the reference for where a title can be streamed, rented or bought on the services a person has, and for availability alerts. [IMDb Help](https://help.imdb.com/) · [Letterboxd FAQ](https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/) · [JustWatch](https://www.justwatch.com/)
- **Primary domain (required):** Media & entertainment
- **Secondary domains (optional):** None

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Kinodex lets a personal agent answer "what should we watch, where, and is it OK for us" from facts about the person rather than from a single streaming service. It sits above the services and plays nothing. Each title has editions, credits, regional certifications and a versioned parental guide whose items carry a severity and a spoiler flag. Offers say which invented service carries a title in which region, as a subscription, rental, purchase or free with ads, and until when. The person keeps a watchlist, a diary of what they watched, when and with whom, ratings in their own style, reviews, lists, alerts and corrections they submitted. The same request has a different right answer for different people, because it depends on which services they pay for, where they live, what they have already seen and how they usually rate.

### Three example requests

The seeded account belongs to Dana, who lives in the US region and subscribes to two of the six invented services, Brightreel and Northcast. Her partner Jules and their daughter Mira, who is 9, appear in her diary as `watched_with`. Dana rates in half stars, has never given 5 stars and averages 3.4. All people, titles, services and prices are fictional.

| User request | What the user and agent encounter | What a reviewer can check |

It omits the barn setting and what happens to the child. It says the film leaves Brightreel at midnight and costs $3.99 to rent afterwards. No diary entry, rating or watchlist change is made. |

### Workflows

| Workflow | Entities and transitions | Confirm, modify, cancel and error paths |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W1 Titles, credits and guides | Title (`film` or `series`), season, episode and edition (for example theatrical or extended, with its runtime). Person and credit (role, character, billing order). Regional release dates and certifications. A parental guide has five categories (violence, language, sexual content, frightening scenes, substances), each with a severity of `none`, `mild`, `moderate` or `severe`, a spoiler-safe category summary and items that carry a `spoiler` flag. The guide is versioned, and each version records its reason (seed, published update or approved correction). | Read-only. An unknown ID returns `not_found`. `kinodex.guide.get` returns the current version by default and earlier versions on request. Reads accept `hide_spoilers: true`, which retains category severity and the safe summary, and withholds spoiler items and the synopses of episodes after the bound person's furthest logged episode of that series. |
| W2 Where to watch | A service has regions. An offer links a title or edition to a service and region, with a type (`subscription`, `rent`, `buy` or `free_with_ads`), a price for rent and buy, a quality label and an `available_from` and optional `available_until`. Availability is derived from the virtual clock: `upcoming`, `available`, `leaving_soon` (within 7 days of `available_until`) or `ended`. The account records the person's region and subscribed services. | `kinodex.offers.search` can be limited to "on my services" in the account's region. `kinodex.services.update` changes the subscribed services or region with `expected_version`. An unknown service or region returns `not_found`. Kinodex does not sell or start anything: a subscription here is only a record of what the person pays for elsewhere. |
| W3 Watchlist, diary, ratings and lists | The watchlist is one versioned, ordered list. A diary entry records the title or episodes, edition, `watched_on`, `rewatch`, `watched_with` (`person_id`s), an optional rating in half stars from 0.5 to 5, and an optional review with `contains_spoilers` and visibility (`private`, `friends` or `public`). A custom list has a name, a description, visibility and a versioned, ordered set of titles. | The watchlist and lists are replaced as a whole with `expected_version`. Diary entries can be created, edited and deleted. A `watched_on` date in the future, or before the title's earliest release in any region, returns `invalid_date`. A rating that is not a half-star step returns `invalid_rating`. Logging an episode that does not belong to the named series returns `not_found`. Deleting a diary entry removes its rating and review together and returns what it removed. |
| W4 Alerts | An alert rule has a kind (`available_on_my_services`, `price_below` for rent or buy, or `new_season`), a target title, an optional threshold and an optional `expires_at`. Its status is `active` → `triggered`, or `expired` when the virtual clock reaches `expires_at` without an earlier match, or `deleted`. Each rule fires once. For `available_on_my_services`, a match requires a currently available subscription offer in the account’s region on a service the person subscribes to. Rentals, purchases and `free_with_ads` offers are excluded, including ad-supported offers on subscribed services. Upcoming, ended and out-of-region offers are excluded as well. | A `new_season` rule matches only a season added to its target series after rule creation. Existing seasons cannot satisfy it at creation. Only active, unexpired rules accept updates. Updating a triggered or expired rule returns `not_active` and preserves its status and history. An offer-based rule already satisfied at creation returns `already_true` with the matching offer, so the agent tells the person instead of waiting. A second active rule of the same kind for the same title returns `duplicate_alert`. A `price_below` threshold on a subscription-only title returns `invalid_threshold`. |
| W5 Corrections | A correction names a title, a field (release date, runtime, credit, certification or a parental-guide item), the proposed value and an evidence note. Its status is `submitted` → `approved`, `declined` or `needs_info`. An `approved` correction updates the field and, for a guide item, adds a guide version. A `needs_info` correction can be amended once and returns to `submitted`. | The agent can submit, amend and withdraw corrections. An account can have one open correction per title and field, and a second returns `already_submitted`. Withdrawing a decided correction returns `already_decided`. A proposed value equal to the current value returns `no_change`. |

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** MCP only in the first version, with 32 agent operations. There are 18 read operations: `kinodex.titles.search`, `kinodex.titles.get`, `kinodex.episodes.list`, `kinodex.people.get`, `kinodex.credits.list`, `kinodex.guide.get`, `kinodex.offers.search`, `kinodex.offers.for_title`, `kinodex.services.list`, `kinodex.account.get`, `kinodex.watchlist.get`, `kinodex.diary.list`, `kinodex.lists.list`, `kinodex.lists.get`, `kinodex.reviews.list`, `kinodex.alerts.list`, `kinodex.corrections.list` and `kinodex.notices.list`. There are 14 write operations: `kinodex.services.update`, `kinodex.watchlist.save`, `kinodex.diary.log`, `kinodex.diary.update`, `kinodex.diary.delete`, `kinodex.lists.create`, `kinodex.lists.save`, `kinodex.lists.delete`, `kinodex.alerts.create`, `kinodex.alerts.update`, `kinodex.alerts.delete`, `kinodex.corrections.submit`, `kinodex.corrections.amend` and `kinodex.corrections.withdraw`. The acting person is the bound account's `owner_person_id` from `binding.json`, never an argument. Writes that change an existing object take it as `id` and the fields to change as `patch`, so a task can narrow tools, objects and fields with the `constraints` in `policies.json`, which Core checks directly. App errors are `not_found`, `invalid_date`, `invalid_rating`, `invalid_threshold`, `already_true`, `duplicate_alert`, `not_active`, `already_submitted`, `already_decided` and `no_change`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Infra alone uses `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the runtime's `env.advance_time`. They are not on the agent connection, and Core returns `permission_denied` if the agent calls one. There is no reviewer console or GUI.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Titles, editions, people, credits, certifications, parental guides, services, offers and other people's public reviews are App state loaded from the run fixture. The account's region, subscribed services, watchlist, diary, lists, alerts, corrections and notices are account state bound to one account instance. A run loads both, plus the virtual clock. The seed schema accepts any history, including years of diary entries, a correction awaiting a decision or an alert that already fired. Seed validation rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, episodes outside their series, offers whose `available_until` precedes `available_from`, diary dates before a title's release, ratings off the half-star scale and timestamps without a UTC offset (`invalid_seed`). Each write returns a receipt with the object ID and new version. Repeating a committed `request_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`. The same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`, and a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`. A failed call leaves data and version unchanged, and reads never change business data. Availability, `leaving_soon` and alert expiry are stored as due times and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever they are read or checked, so Kinodex needs no timers. Events that change an offer, title or season can trigger a rule. A successful `kinodex.services.update` that changes the account’s region or subscriptions also reevaluates affected active, unexpired rules against the updated account in the same write. Each newly satisfied rule becomes `triggered` and stores the matching offer, the cause `services.update` and the write’s `request_id`. This write emits no notice. Once triggered, the rule stays triggered on retries and later qualifying events. The seven-day leaving-soon window is available in offer searches. Infra moves the clock with an event or `env.advance_time`. Reset (`reset_scope: app_instance`) restores the loaded catalog, account and clock without touching other Apps. Evaluator records come from App activity rows, Core audit rows and the supplied agent trace, joined on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Each activity row records the sequence number, virtual time, actor (`agent` or `event`), action, object and the replaced value. There is one row per committed write or applied event, and none for a replay, a rejected call or a time-driven change. Infra takes the snapshot before reset.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Infra applies four scenario events, and the runner controls their timing. `offer.publish` adds or changes an offer, for example a new rental price or an earlier `available_until`. `offer.end` ends an offer at the event time. `title.update` adds a season or episodes, changes a release date or publishes a new parental-guide version. `correction.decision` decides a correction the agent submitted. After each committed event, Kinodex checks the active, unexpired alert rules it affects at the event’s virtual time, marks matching rules `triggered` and writes a notice.

  **How events find records the agent creates.** Corrections are created by the agent during the run, so their IDs are not predictable. `correction.decision` names its correction by `{title_id, field}`, which matches the account's one open correction for that title and field, and carries the decision (`approved`, `declined` or `needs_info`) and an optional note. The other three events name seeded records by ID. If an event's target does not exist, or no open correction matches, the event fails with `target_not_found` and changes nothing. A failed event stores no receipt. `env.apply_event` returns `applied: false` with the failure code, and Core's audit records a `deny` row with that `reason`. The task can retry with the same `event_id`, the current `expected_version` and an `at` timestamp at or after the current virtual time. Replaying the `event_id` of a committed event changes nothing. The outcome of a committed event is stored in account state: a decided correction keeps its decision, note and decision time, and a fired alert keeps the offer or title change that triggered it. `kinodex.corrections.list` and `kinodex.alerts.list` show them to the agent, and the evaluator reads them from the App state snapshot. Core's `env.apply_event` itself returns only a commit receipt.

  Kinodex writes in-app notices, read with `kinodex.notices.list`, for an alert triggered by an Infra event, a decided correction and a new guide version for a title on the watchlist. It writes none for the agent's own writes and none for time passing alone. It sends no cross-app notifications: `publishes` and `receives` are empty.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Node.js 18+ and the public Core runtime, reference revision `a4bce02`. The account's `account_id` and the owner's `person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`, and `watched_with` uses the same `person_id`s, so a task that also uses Contacts gives the same people the same IDs. Kinodex keeps no contact details. Fixtures ship as UTF-8 JSON. The first version has no posters, stills or trailers (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the reference profile's 64 assets). Synopses, guide items and reviews are short original text. The package runs on its own with only Core: `dependencies.required_apps` is empty, and it reads nothing outside its own folder. Task authors keep grading judgments, such as whether ratings match a person's style, in the evaluator. [Runtime](../../runtime/README.md) · [App contribution guide](../../docs/app-contribution.md)
