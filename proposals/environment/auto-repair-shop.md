## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable customer portal of one repair and collision shop, seen from one customer's account. The shop may not start a repair line until the customer has authorized that exact line, every estimate has an expiry, and authorizing a line makes the customer liable for whatever no insurer or warranty pays. So the portal can accept a step that is wrong for this person: work released before the insurer approved it, a paid repair that a prior repair's warranty would have covered, a balance paid that a remittance was about to cover, or a car left to collect storage fees during a trip. The environment records what the customer authorized, on which estimate revision and at what time, and what that cost.

Scope of one App instance:

- One customer account at one fictional shop, with one to three vehicles and their service history at this shop.
- The service advisor, the technicians, parts suppliers and third-party payers (an insurer, a service-contract administrator, a manufacturer for recall work) are not accounts. What they do arrives as task-supplied events.
- A repair order is one visit of one vehicle. It is `open`, then `ready`, then `released`. While it is open, status reasons such as `awaiting_estimate`, `awaiting_authorization` and `waiting_parts` are derived from its lines and are not stored states.
- Money does not move: a payment is a record. All amounts are integer cents in one currency per instance, and line amounts are task data. The App computes only the sums and the three fees named below.
- A day is a calendar day in the shop's time zone, set by the fixture.

Four workflows are complete end to end in the first version:

1. **W1 Vehicle, history and shop rules (read only).** Read the account's vehicles, past repair orders with their lines, invoices and repair-warranty terms, the people the customer has authorized to pick up a vehicle, and the shop's policy record: estimate validity, the diagnostic fee and its waiver threshold, the restocking percentage for special-order parts, free storage days and the daily storage rate, repair-warranty months and miles, business hours and the liability statement of the shop agreement.
2. **W2 Appointments.** Search drop-off slots by service type; book, reschedule or cancel an appointment for one vehicle, with the customer's stated concerns and whether the customer waits or leaves the car. An appointment is `booked`, then `checked_in`, `cancelled` or `no_show`. A slot has a capacity per service category; a vehicle has at most one `booked` appointment; a change after the slot has started is refused. Check-in is physical, so it arrives as an event and opens the repair order with the recorded mileage.
3. **W3 Estimates, coverage and authorization.** An estimate arrives as an event after diagnosis. It is an immutable revision (`initial`, `supplement` or `reissue`) with an expiry and lines for labor, parts, sublet work and fees. A part line carries its grade (`oem`, `aftermarket`, `recycled`), and two lines can be alternatives of each other.
   - The agent previews a set of decisions and then submits them. Each decision names one line of one estimate revision: `authorize`, `decline`, `hold`, or `revoke` for a line that was authorized and has not started. `hold` is an explicit recorded answer ("do not start; decision pending"), distinct from silence.
   - A line moves `pending → held | authorized | declined`, and `authorized → in_progress → completed` by events. Work on a line that is not `authorized` never starts; the repair order reads `awaiting_authorization`.
   - A supplement found during the repair needs its own authorization. An earlier authorization never extends to it, and no decision can cover lines that do not exist yet.
   - A payer's written decision on a line (`pending`, `approved` with an amount, or `denied` with a reason) is shown as the shop received it, with the payer's own reference such as a claim number. The customer's share of a line is its amount minus the coverage approved by all payers, capped at the line amount; pending or denied coverage counts as zero.
   - For a line that repeats a repair this shop did before, the agent can request coverage under that repair's warranty, naming the earlier repair-order line. The App checks only the months and miles; the shop's decision arrives as an event.
   - The return of replaced parts can be requested only in the submission that authorizes the part line.
4. **W4 Completion, invoice, payment and pickup.** Events report parts status and arrival dates, the promised completion time and line progress. When the work is finished the repair order becomes `ready` and the App issues the final invoice from the completed lines and fees. An order whose lines were all declined also becomes `ready` and is invoiced for the fees alone.
   - The ledger keeps charges, each payer's approved and received amounts, the customer's payments, the customer's share, the amount the customer still owes (including storage accrued so far) and the outstanding balance. Before the invoice, the same sums are computed over the authorized lines and marked as an estimate basis.
   - A deposit or counter payment and a payer's remittance arrive as events; the agent can also submit a customer payment from a saved synthetic payment method. A customer payment is accepted up to the outstanding balance, including money a payer has approved and not yet remitted. A later remittance then leaves a credit. A submitted payment is final for the agent: a refund arrives only as an event.
   - The agent sets, changes or clears a pickup time inside business hours and names who collects the car: the account holder or an authorized person on file. Release is physical and arrives as an event; it is refused while the customer still owes anything. A payer's approved amount may still be outstanding at release.
   - If a payer later pays less or reverses, the customer's share on a `released` order rises and stays payable.

The App computes three fees from the policy record, and `authorization.preview` shows each before the agent commits:

- **Diagnostic fee.** Charged on a diagnosis visit; waived at completion when the completed repair lines reach the waiver threshold.
- **Restocking fee.** A percentage of a special-order part's amount when its line is revoked after the part was ordered.
- **Storage.** The ready day and the free days after it cost nothing; each calendar day started after that is charged at the daily rate, through the release day. Reads show the amount accrued so far as a projection and count it in what the customer owes. It is posted to the ledger in the same commit as a customer payment or a release that succeeds.

The planned Web interface has six views: garage (vehicles, service history and shop rules); appointments; repair order (status, estimate revisions, lines with coverage and parts); decision review (the preview of a set of decisions, its submission, and warranty requests); invoice and ledger (with payment and pickup); and activity (notices and receipts). Every policy value and every effect of a proposed decision that the MCP interface returns is also visible in the UI. The browser and MCP call the same business handlers on the same stored state.

### Semantics to validate

- **Line-exact authorization.** Every decision carries the estimate ID and line ID. A decision on a line of a superseded revision is refused (`superseded_estimate`). `authorize` on an expired estimate is refused (`estimate_expired`); `hold` and `decline` are still accepted. Authorizing both lines of an alternative pair, or one whose alternative is already authorized, is refused (`alternative_conflict`). A line that is `in_progress` or `completed` accepts no decision (`invalid_state`). A declined or held line can be authorized later while its estimate is valid and the order is open. A reissue replaces only the lines of the named revision that are not yet authorized; lines already authorized keep their revision and their state. The return of replaced parts named for a line that was authorized in an earlier submission is refused (`too_late`).
- **Liability.** Authorizing a line whose coverage is `pending` is accepted. The receipt states the customer's share at that moment and the amount still exposed if the payer denies it. The App never judges whether an authorization fits the customer's own limits.
- **Preview is a read.** It stores nothing and returns the state version it was computed from, the per-line effect, the customer's share now, the exposure on pending coverage, fees waived or triggered, alternative conflicts and estimate validity.
- **Time.** Expiry, the appointment cutoff, the warranty window and storage are evaluated against `ctx.virtual_now` when a write or an event is applied. Reads derive them without changing stored records. Nothing changes state by time alone, so the App needs no scheduler and no expiry events. The time a shop asked for an answer by is information; an overdue request is flagged and has no automatic effect.
- **Writes.** Every write carries `request_id` and `expected_version` from its read or reviewed preview. An intervening commit or a later clock causes `stale_version`. An identical retry within an epoch returns the original result with `duplicate: true`; the same ID with changed arguments causes `idempotency_conflict`. A committed write returns `status: committed`, the new `version` and an App receipt ID visible in the activity list. A refused write changes nothing. A Web form keeps the version it observed when it opened, so a stale form shows the conflict instead of overwriting, and an unchanged form that is sent again reuses its request ID.
- **Missing data.** A line with no coverage record is the customer's to pay. A part with no known arrival date reads `eta: null`, and the promised completion time reads `null` instead of a guess. An estimate with no expiry uses the policy default.

The 12 App errors, in the order of the checks; a call that fits several returns the first: `not_found`, `invalid_state`, `slot_unavailable`, `outside_hours`, `too_late`, `superseded_estimate`, `estimate_expired`, `alternative_conflict`, `warranty_expired`, `already_requested`, `nothing_due`, `amount_exceeds_balance`.

### Why it matters for personal agents

The repair is the same for everyone; the right answer is not. It depends on the instruction this person gave ("release only what the insurer has approved, and keep my share within my deductible"), on what was repaired here before and is still under warranty, on what they declined last time, on the deposit they already paid, on when they need the car, and on who may collect it. Four distinct task situations, each seeded from its own history:

- A collision repair where the shop asks to start a supplement while the insurer's decision on those lines is pending in one fixture and approved in the paired one. The right answer is `hold` in one and `authorize` in the other.
- A brake visit where the estimate lists six lines, the person's rule is "safety items only, under a set amount", and the rear pads were replaced here five months ago.
- A car that is `ready` while the person is away: a second insurer remittance is pending, storage starts in two days, and a named family member may collect it.
- A special-order part on backorder with an aftermarket alternative that the insurer prices lower; the person needs the car before a trip, and revoking the ordered part costs a restocking fee.

These illustrate reusable capabilities. A separate Task contribution defines the persona, the event schedule and the rubric.

- **State and lifecycle (required):** One App instance binds one customer account and run. App data separates the shop's catalog and policy record, the account's vehicles and history, and run records: appointments, repair orders, estimate revisions, line decisions with their receipts, coverage records, parts status, invoices, ledger entries, notices and activity receipts. Core owns the clock, epoch and revision, request and event receipts and audit; the App adds no clock or deduplication store.
  - `env.load_seed` loads the account, its vehicles and history, the slot calendar and any in-flight state: a booked appointment, an open order at any stage (awaiting an estimate, mid-repair with a supplement, or `ready` with a balance), with their coverage and ledger records. A task can therefore start mid-repair. The seed validator checks references, line states against the order state, and ledger sums.
  - A seed whose records are dated after the current clock loads and stays readable; writes and events are refused until the clock reaches the latest record.
  - `env.reset` restores the loaded baseline of this App instance (`reset_scope: app_instance`) without touching other Apps. No restore operation beyond the snapshot is promised.
  - Writes and events commit atomically: a submission that names five lines changes five or none. Reads never mutate business state. The browser keeps no business store: MCP and the Web interface read and write the same stored state.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Six Infra operations use the App prefix; task scenarios decide when they occur, and each uses `ctx.virtual_now` (`event.at` during application).
  - `visit.resolve`: an appointment is checked in or missed, or a vehicle arrives without one (a tow-in); check-in opens the repair order with the mileage.
  - `estimate.issue`: an initial estimate, a supplement or a reissue with its lines and expiry; a reissue supersedes a named revision.
  - `coverage.update`: a payer's decisions on named lines as the shop received them, including the shop's own answer to a warranty request.
  - `work.update`: lines started or completed, parts status and arrival date, the promised completion time, and `ready`, which issues the final invoice. Starting a line that is not `authorized` is refused.
  - `payment.post`: a payer's remittance, a counter payment or deposit, a refund or a reversal, posted to the ledger.
  - `vehicle.release`: the vehicle is handed to a named person and the order closes; refused while the customer still owes anything.

  An event that breaks a rule is rejected as `invalid_event` and changes nothing. An identical event retry within an epoch returns Core's original receipt without a second notice. Each applied event writes one notice to the activity list. The App sends no cross-App message. Example envelopes, amounts in cents:

  In this fixture, `evt-0001` checks the car in and opens `ro-2041`. `evt-0003` issues a two-line estimate that expires on March 18. `evt-0004` records the insurer's approval of the labor line in full and no decision yet on the part.
