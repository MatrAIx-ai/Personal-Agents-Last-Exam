## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable browser App and MCP business interface for one fictional mobile carrier. A household has three existing mobile lines. An agent can inspect usage, compare three household plans, schedule or cancel a plan change, manage two types of international data pass, and verify the resulting charges and service status. Five complete workflows share one authoritative business state.

| Workflow | First-version behavior | Observable outcome |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Account and usage inspection | Read the household, line owners, actor role, current billing cycle, active and pending plans, domestic shared-pool usage, per-line hotspot usage, roaming balances, and recent activity. | Account and line records identify whose service is affected and which allowances apply now. |
| Plan comparison | Compare three fixed plans, including household monthly price, shared domestic data, per-line hotspot ceiling, included roaming destinations and allowances, and next-cycle effective date. | A comparison exposes all decision-relevant terms and the impact on all three lines. |
| Plan change | Preview a next-cycle household plan change; confirm or abandon it; inspect its receipt; cancel or replace the change before it takes effect. | The active plan remains unchanged until the boundary; exactly one pending change can exist. |
| Roaming-pass management | Preview and confirm a pass for one line, a supported destination set, and a future UTC start time. Read its status; reschedule or cancel it before activation; reject overlapping passes on the same line. | The pass is scheduled, active, expired, or cancelled with explicit coverage, validity, remaining data, and charge status. |
| Charges and notifications | Inspect a simple carrier charge ledger, monthly statements, itemized pass charges, and in-App notices for usage thresholds, pending changes, activation, and expiry. | Every simulated charge has a source record; service state, receipts, and notices agree. |

**First-version scale.** One carrier; one household and three lines per fixture; three plan definitions; two pass products; six destinations (Canada, Mexico, France, Germany, Japan, and South Korea); five principal browser views corresponding to the workflows above; and six deterministic fixture snapshots. The two pass products provide different durations and data allowances. Their coverage sets, fixed prices, and all plan entitlements will be published in the versioned catalog during M1. Task authors can replace household history and usage through the seed schema without changing the App implementation.

**Business rules to agree in design review.**

- Each household uses one plan for all three lines. Plan changes take effect only at the next billing-cycle boundary. There is no mid-cycle upgrade or proration in v1. A cancellation of a pending change retains the active plan. Replacing a pending change requires a new preview and confirmation.
- The domestic pool is shared. Hotspot usage also consumes that pool and is subject to a per-line ceiling. Roaming balances are separate. Every usage record identifies its line, type, amount, and occurrence time; units and rounding are documented. Exhaustion changes the displayed service allowance/status and generates a notice; it never silently purchases extra data.
- A roaming pass belongs to exactly one line and has a half-open validity interval `[start, end)` in UTC. It consumes included roaming data first, then eligible active-pass data, using a documented deterministic order. Usage outside coverage or available balances records the limitation without an automatic purchase or unlisted overage fee.
- Passes may be modified or cancelled only before activation. Rescheduling is an atomic previewed update that rechecks overlap and coverage. Active or expired passes cannot be cancelled or refunded in v1; the App explains this before confirmation and returns a visible error for invalid requests. Buying an overlapping pass is rejected without a charge or partial mutation.
- Scheduled passes incur no charge until activation. Activation creates one fixed simulated carrier charge. The billing boundary records the next month's fixed household charge and starts the new cycle; no money moves through a bank or payment processor. Statements itemize these records without adding a second charge. Taxes, discounts, device financing, and payment collection are excluded.
- All consequential writes use preview/confirm/abandon with an explicit target, current version, effective time, terms, and total cost. A preview is not a completed change. Stale confirmations fail without the requested business mutation. Repeating the same confirmed request returns its existing receipt; a reused request ID with different input is rejected. Core commits and version handling are reused where available.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** The same request to reduce a bill can require different choices depending on a household member's study needs, hotspot reliance, or upcoming travel. A pass bought for the wrong family member, a cheaper plan that starts after departure, or a previously scheduled downgrade can undermine an otherwise plausible plan. A task could ask an agent to preserve a child's connectivity while adapting a parent's roaming arrangements after a trip changes. The App supplies discoverable terms and consequences; task authors supply personal history, instructions, authorization, event timing, and grading. It will not embed a preferred plan, a task solution, or hidden user preferences.

[Homegrid #170](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/170) covers household utility and fixed home-internet service orders, equipment, outages, and billing; SignalDesk instead models mobile lines, household data allowances, next-cycle mobile-plan changes, and destination-specific roaming passes. It does not recreate fixed-line service installation, water/electricity accounts, or equipment returns. These are related proposals, not runtime dependencies. ## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** None required for v1. All confirmations and notices are available inside SignalDesk. A later optional Mail connection could deliver plan and pass confirmations using Mail's public receive contract. A task could let its agent read travel dates from Calendar, but SignalDesk will not read Calendar automatically or maintain a duplicate calendar. Neither connection is an approved v1 deliverable.
- **Other apps and websites (required):** None in v1. Public carrier help pages are design references only. Airline/hotel environments could provide travel context through a future task assembly; no live carrier, telecom network, payment service, or external account is required.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** Propose `apps/signaldesk/`, subject to maintainer location review, with versioned business schemas, fixtures, tests, and documented MCP operations. Reads cover account/line details, usage, the catalog, comparisons, pending changes, passes, charges, receipts, and notices. Writes cover previews, confirmations, abandonment, and valid modification/cancellation paths. Platform management uses the public runtime's separate Infra connection for seed/history loading, event injection, reset, and snapshots; Agent connections must neither discover nor invoke those operations. The local browser UI will use an App-owned loopback bridge to the same Agent-role MCP interface and run instance, with trusted launch-time account binding. It will not edit state files or expose management endpoints. Browser workflow tests will drive that real bridge. Official hosted Web/CUA registration, if needed, is a platform integration question to resolve with maintainers; the public stdio verifier alone does not establish hosted CUA support.

- **State and lifecycle (required):** Separate immutable catalog data from household/account records and run-local state. Each account and line has a stable synthetic ID; all records, previews, passes, usage entries, statements, and notices resolve within the authorized account. An owner may manage all household lines; a member may inspect only their own line and the terms applicable to it, and cannot mutate the household plan or buy a pass. Core policies and App business checks enforce the documented roles. Account access is distinct from the task's current authorization to act; previews and API availability do not create consent. Independent runs must not share mutable records. Load fixtures/history through the seed contract; use Core's committed state and revision checks for writes. A failed action leaves its business state unchanged, and concurrent confirmations cannot overwrite a newer plan/pass choice. Reset reconstructs the loaded baseline, including the business clock, usage, pending changes, pass statuses, charges, notices, and App deduplication records. Export evidence before reset. Infra snapshots are inspection evidence; arbitrary snapshot restoration or whole-agent reset is not promised.

- **Events and notifications (required):** Declare App events for monotonic business-time advancement, usage posting, and an external account update that changes or cancels a pending plan/pass on behalf of an authorized simulated account user. Tasks/Infra choose dates, sequence, and actors; Agent tools cannot advance time. For example, a proposed `advance_business_time` event could carry `{"now":"2027-02-01T00:00:00Z"}`, and a `post_usage` event could identify `{"line_id":"line-02","kind":"domestic_data","amount_mb":250,"occurred_at":"2027-02-01T00:10:00Z"}`. These are proposed App payloads, not new shared-runtime APIs. The App processes due business boundaries chronologically before the next observation/action, invalidating outdated previews. Same-time ordering, out-of-order usage rejection, and duplicate-event handling will be fixed and tested in M1. A jump across several boundaries must equal sequential replay. This App-local clock does not implement a global scheduler or Agent wake-ups. In-App notices expose stable IDs, type, affected line/account, effective time, and source-record references; a pass activation notice links to its pass and charge. No outbound or inbound cross-App messaging is declared in v1.

- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Use synthetic `person_id`, `account_id`, `line_id`, `plan_id`, `pass_id`, and receipt references with a binding fixture. The task assembly supplies trusted identity/account bindings. Initial/history records, catalog data, events, and statements are small UTF-8 JSON; the GUI presents accessible HTML/text and original CSS. No attachment bytes, product photographs, maps, OCR, or copied carrier assets are needed. Required platform dependency: the public [Core runtime](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/main/runtime/README.md), using the public reference profile documented on `main`. Pin an exact repository commit and compatible contracts at M1 and record them in delivery evidence; do not target a floating version during verification. No other App is required. Implementation will follow the [App delivery guide](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/main/docs/app-contribution.md), [integration guide](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/main/docs/app-integrations.md), and [architecture boundaries](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/main/docs/environment-architecture.md). Reuse shared contracts and relevant SDK helpers without copying Core, Mail, or Calendar implementations. A future Mail integration requires separate scope review, exact receiver-contract links, configured grants, and receiver-state evidence.
