# Kitewatch video platform: subscriptions, watch history, explainable feed and supervised viewing

**Proposed PR title** — [Environment Proposal] [Media & entertainment + Social & communities] Kitewatch video platform: subscriptions, watch history, explainable feed and supervised viewing

## 1. App and domains

- **Authors / GitHub handles (required):** @byshail
- **Reference app or website / URL (required):** Kitewatch, an independently branded synthetic user-generated video platform seen from one viewer's account, with a supervised child account the viewer manages. YouTube is the interaction reference for channels, subscriptions with the notification levels all, personalized and none, watch history with pause, delete and auto-delete, "Not interested" and "Don't recommend channel" feedback, Watch Later and playlists, comments and replies, and the supervised experience a parent sets up for a child. [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/) · [Watch history](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/95725) · [Supervised experiences](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13877231)
- **Primary domain (required):** Media & entertainment
- **Secondary domains (optional):** Social & communities

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Kitewatch is the viewer's side of a user-generated video platform, which no existing proposal covers: channel subscriptions with notice levels, a watch history the person can pause and prune, a home feed built by a written rule that gives a reason for every item, playlists, the person's own comments and a supervised child account the parent manages. Channels publish videos as metadata with a duration, chapters and a timed transcript, and uploads, premieres and deletions arrive as Infra events. The agent watches nothing. Viewing on the person's devices arrives as `watch.record` events, and reading a video, its chapters or its transcript on the person's behalf adds no history. The same request has a different right answer for each person, because it depends on whose viewing is in the history, which channels they follow and how closely, what they have told the feed and what a parent has allowed.

### Workflows

| Workflow | Entities and transitions | Confirm, modify, cancel and error paths |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W1 Channels, videos and transcripts | A channel has a name, a handle and topics. A video has its channel, title, description, `duration_s`, topics, a `content_level` of `all`, `9+`, `13+` or `18+`, a `kind` of `upload` or `premiere`, `published_at` and, for a premiere, `premiere_at`. Chapters are `{start_s, title}`. A transcript is a list of timed segments `{start_s, end_s, text}`. A premiere is `upcoming` until `premiere_at` and `available` afterwards, derived from `ctx.virtual_now`. `video.delete` makes a video `deleted`. | Read-only. An unknown ID returns `not_found`. `kitewatch.videos.transcript` accepts a `query` (case-insensitive word match) and a time window, and returns each matching segment with the chapter it falls in. A video without a transcript returns `no_transcript`, and one without chapters returns an empty list. A deleted video keeps its title and channel, but its chapters and transcript return `video_unavailable`. |
| W2 Subscriptions and notices | A subscription links the account to a channel with a `notify_level` of `all`, `personalized` or `none` and records `subscribed_at`. It goes from subscribed to removed. A notice is a stored record of kind `new_video` or `comment_reply`. When `video.publish` adds a video to a subscribed channel, `all` writes a notice and `none` writes none. `personalized` writes one only when the account has at least two visible history rows from that channel in the 30 days before the event's `at`. | The agent can subscribe, change the level and unsubscribe. A second subscription to the same channel returns `already_subscribed`, and an unknown channel returns `not_found`. A premiere gets one notice when `video.publish` announces it and none when it starts. |
| W3 Watch history and its controls | A history row records the video, device, `watched_at` and `watched_s`. Rows for the owner come only from the seed and from `watch.record` events. The account has `history_paused` with `paused_since`, and `auto_delete_months` (`3`, `18`, `36` or `null` for off). A row is visible until the agent deletes it or auto-delete hides it. A row is hidden from every read and rule once `watched_at` falls before `virtual_now` minus `auto_delete_months`. This is derived on read, and time passing writes nothing. While history is paused, `watch.record` for the owner commits but adds no row. | `kitewatch.history.delete` removes one row. `kitewatch.history.delete_range` removes the owner's visible rows with `from ≤ watched_at < to` and returns their IDs and count. `kitewatch.history.list` with the same range shows them beforehand, and a deletion cannot be undone within the run. A `from` that is not before `to` returns `invalid_range`. `kitewatch.account.update` pauses or resumes history or changes the auto-delete period. Resuming does not restore views made while paused. A change of period removes the rows already past the stricter of the old and new cutoffs and returns how many, so lengthening the period never brings hidden rows back. |
| W4 Home feed and feedback | Feedback is `not_interested` on a video or `dont_recommend_channel` on a channel, and goes from recorded to deleted. `kitewatch.feed.get` computes the feed from current state and `ctx.virtual_now` and stores nothing. The rule starts from available videos and drops videos marked `not_interested`, channels marked `dont_recommend_channel` (subscribed or not) and videos with a visible row covering at least 90% of their duration. Each remaining video takes the first reason that applies, in this order: `continue_watching` (a visible row under 90% in the last 14 days), `new_from_subscription` (published by a subscribed channel in the last 14 days), `watched_channel` (at least two visible rows from its channel in the last 90 days) and `shared_topic` (one of its topics on at least three visible rows in the last 90 days). The last three reasons also require publication in the last 30 days. In every window a premiere counts as published at `premiere_at`. Other videos are left out. Items sort by reason, then by latest row for `continue_watching` or newest publication otherwise, then by `video_id`, up to 30 items. Each item carries `reason` and `reason_detail` with the channel or topic, the count and the window. | The agent can record and delete feedback. A duplicate returns `already_recorded`, and an unknown target returns `not_found`. Reading the feed changes nothing. Deleted rows and new feedback change the next read, which is how a reviewer checks a cleanup. |
| W5 Watch Later and playlists | Watch Later is a system playlist that always exists and is always private. A named playlist has a name, a description, a `privacy` of `private`, `unlisted` or `public` and an ordered item list, and each item records `added_at`. A playlist goes from created through updated to deleted. | `kitewatch.playlists.update` with `patch.video_ids` replaces the whole ordered list. Kept items retain `added_at`, and new items take the current virtual time. A repeated video returns `duplicate_item`. Adding a deleted video returns `video_unavailable`, and a premiere can be saved before it starts. Renaming Watch Later, changing its privacy or deleting it returns `system_playlist`. A video deleted after it was saved stays in the list and reads as `deleted`. |
| W6 Supervised child account | A supervised account belongs to a child's `person_id` from `binding.json`, and the bound owner manages it. Its settings are `content_level` (`all`, `9+` or `13+`), `approved_only`, `approved_channel_ids` and `blocked_channel_ids`. A video is visible to the child when its level is at or below the setting, its channel is not blocked and, in approved-only mode, its channel is approved. A block wins over an approval. `watch.record` for the child stores a row whose `outcome` is `watched`, or `blocked` with the reason `blocked_channel`, `above_level` or `not_approved`, evaluated at the event's `at`. A `blocked` row records no watch time. | The owner reads the settings, the child's rows with `kitewatch.supervision.history` and per-video visibility with `kitewatch.supervision.check`. The owner searches as the child with `as_supervised_id` and changes settings with `kitewatch.supervision.update`. `18+` returns `invalid_level`, and an unknown channel returns `not_found`. New settings apply from the next read or event, and earlier rows keep their outcome. |
| W7 The person's comments | A comment has a video, an author, text, a posted time and an optional parent, and replies sit flat under a top-level comment. The owner's comments go from posted to deleted. Other people's replies arrive through `comment.reply`, and each reply to the owner's comment writes a `comment_reply` notice. | `kitewatch.comments.post` and `kitewatch.comments.delete`. Empty text or text over 10,000 characters returns `invalid_text`. A deleted or upcoming video returns `video_unavailable`. Deleting another person's comment returns `forbidden`. Deleting a top-level comment removes its replies from thread reads and returns how many it removed. Editing is not offered, so the agent deletes and reposts. |

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** MCP only in the first version, with 32 agent operations. There are 18 read operations: `kitewatch.account.get`, `kitewatch.channels.search`, `kitewatch.channels.get`, `kitewatch.videos.search`, `kitewatch.videos.get`, `kitewatch.videos.transcript`, `kitewatch.subscriptions.list`, `kitewatch.notices.list`, `kitewatch.history.list`, `kitewatch.feed.get`, `kitewatch.feedback.list`, `kitewatch.playlists.list`, `kitewatch.playlists.get`, `kitewatch.supervision.get`, `kitewatch.supervision.history`, `kitewatch.supervision.check`, `kitewatch.comments.list` and `kitewatch.comments.thread`. There are 14 write operations: `kitewatch.account.update`, `kitewatch.history.delete`, `kitewatch.history.delete_range`, `kitewatch.subscriptions.create`, `kitewatch.subscriptions.update`, `kitewatch.subscriptions.delete`, `kitewatch.feedback.create`, `kitewatch.feedback.delete`, `kitewatch.playlists.create`, `kitewatch.playlists.update`, `kitewatch.playlists.delete`, `kitewatch.supervision.update`, `kitewatch.comments.post` and `kitewatch.comments.delete`. The acting person is the bound account's `owner_person_id` from `binding.json`, never an argument. Writes that change an existing object take it as `id` and the fields to change as `patch`, and deletions take `{id}`. Account-level writes (`kitewatch.account.update` and `kitewatch.history.delete_range`) take the account's ID, and supervision writes take the supervised account's ID. A task can therefore use `constraints.ids` in `policies.json` to allow `kitewatch.supervision.update` for one supervised account only, and `constraints.fields` to allow a playlist's `video_ids` but not its `privacy`. Core checks both directly. App errors are `not_found`, `video_unavailable`, `no_transcript`, `already_subscribed`, `already_recorded`, `invalid_range`, `invalid_level`, `duplicate_item`, `system_playlist`, `invalid_text` and `forbidden`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Infra alone uses `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the runtime's `env.advance_time`. They are not on the agent connection, and Core returns `permission_denied` if the agent calls one.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Channels, videos, chapters, transcripts and other people's comments are App state loaded from the run fixture. The account settings, subscriptions, notices, history rows, feedback, playlists, the owner's comments and each supervised account with its settings and rows are account state bound to one account instance. A run loads both, plus the virtual clock. The bound owner must be the parent. A seed that binds a supervised child as owner is rejected. The seed schema accepts any history, including paused history, a supervised account with blocked rows or a comment with replies. Seed validation returns `invalid_seed` for the following: duplicate IDs, dangling references, chapters out of order or past the duration, overlapping transcript segments, `watched_s` beyond the duration and rows before a video was available. It also rejects owner rows dated after `paused_since` on a paused account, rows already past the auto-delete cutoff and timestamps without a UTC offset. Every write takes Core's `expected_version` (the App instance's `{epoch, revision}`) and a `request_id`, and returns a receipt with the object ID and new version. Kitewatch keeps no per-object version. Repeating a committed `request_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`. The same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`, and a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`. A failed call leaves data and version unchanged, and reads never change business data. Deletions report what they removed: row IDs for `kitewatch.history.delete_range`, the number of replies for `kitewatch.comments.delete` and the number of rows for a `kitewatch.account.update` that changes the auto-delete period. Three kinds of state are derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever they are read and are never stored: premiere availability, the auto-delete cutoff and the feed's 14-, 30- and 90-day windows. Kitewatch therefore needs no timers. Infra moves the clock with an event or `env.advance_time`, which writes no row and no notice. Reset (`reset_scope: app_instance`) restores the loaded catalog, account and clock without touching other Apps. Evaluator records come from App activity rows, Core audit rows and the supplied agent trace, joined on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Each activity row records the sequence number, virtual time, actor (`agent` or `event`), action, object and replaced value. For `watch.record` it also records the outcome: `recorded`, `history_paused`, `watched` or `blocked`. There is one row per committed write or applied event, and none for a replay, a rejected call or a time-driven change. Infra takes the snapshot before reset.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Infra applies five scenario events, and the runner controls their timing. `video.publish` adds an upload or a premiere with its chapters and transcript, sets `published_at` to the event's `at` and writes subscription notices by the W2 rule. `video.update` changes a video's title, description, topics, content level, chapters or transcript. `video.delete` marks a video `deleted`. Its title and channel stay readable, while chapters, the transcript, new playlist additions, comments and later `watch.record` events return `video_unavailable`. Existing history rows, playlist items and notices keep pointing at it, and the feed and the child's view exclude it. `watch.record` records viewing on a named device by the owner or a supervised child, by the W3 and W6 rules, and the row's `watched_at` is the payload's `started_at`. `comment.reply` adds another person's reply. An unknown channel, video or viewer fails the event with `not_found`. A `video.publish` whose `video_id` already exists fails with `duplicate_video`. A watch longer than its video, or one that ends after the event's `at`, fails with `invalid_watch`. A video that is deleted or not yet premiered fails `watch.record` with `video_unavailable`.

  **How events find records the agent creates.** Comments are the only records an event must find after the agent creates them. `comment.reply` names its parent either by `parent_comment_id` (seeded comments only) or by `parent_ref: {video_id, author_person_id}`. The ref matches that person's one comment on that video that is not deleted. If nothing matches, the event fails with `target_not_found`. If more than one comment matches, it fails with `ambiguous_target`. A failed event changes nothing and stores no receipt. `env.apply_event` returns `applied: false` with the code, and Core's audit records a `deny` row with that `reason`. The task can retry with the same `event_id`, the current `expected_version` and an `at` at or after the current virtual time. Replaying the `event_id` of a committed event changes nothing. The other events name videos and channels by the IDs the task wrote into the seed or into an earlier `video.publish`, and name viewers by `person_id`. The agent's own tools never take lookup keys.

  Kitewatch writes in-app notices, read with `kitewatch.notices.list`, for a new video on a subscribed channel by the W2 rule and for a reply to the owner's comment. It writes none for the agent's own writes, for a child's blocked row, for deletions or for time passing alone. It sends no cross-app notifications: `publishes` and `receives` are empty.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Node.js 18+ and the public runtime 1.6.0 with Core API 1.2.0, reference revision `main` at `7a05aa9`. The account's `account_id` and the owner's `person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`. The child is a person in the same binding, and the supervised account stores only that `person_id` and a display name. A task that also uses Contacts gives the same people the same IDs, and Kitewatch keeps no contact details. Fixtures ship as UTF-8 JSON. There are no video files, thumbnails or audio (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the reference profile's 64 assets), and transcripts are text segments in the seed. The package runs on its own with only Core: `dependencies.required_apps` is empty, and it reads nothing outside its own folder. Task authors keep grading judgments, such as whether a reply's timestamps are close enough, in the evaluator. - **Resources and total effort (required):** Estimated 11–13 person-days. The package runs locally in Node. Seed storage is reported from the generated JSON, and about 350 KB of it is transcript text. No generation, storage or compute costs are expected.
- **Scope boundary and open questions (required):** The first version covers video metadata, viewing records, the feed rule and account controls. MCP only, with no GUI and no reviewer console. The following are outside the proposed scope: video files, audio, thumbnails and playback; live streams and live chat; ads, memberships and purchases; uploads by the person and all creator tools, which CutRoom covers; likes, dislikes and search history; comment editing, mentions and moderation; machine-learned ranking; and email or push delivery of notices. Deferred to a later version are the child's own subscriptions, playlists and comments, a preview of the child's feed, teen supervision, and creating or removing supervised accounts, which are seeded in the first version. Three open questions are listed with the planned default for each, to be settled in review and fixed in the README and tests. First, should `dont_recommend_channel` hide a subscribed channel from the home feed? The plan is yes: feedback wins in the feed, and notices still follow the subscription level. Second, should a child's attempt to open a restricted video be stored as a `blocked` row the parent can read, or should the event be refused? The plan is to store the row and write no notice. Third, is "two visible rows in 30 days" the right `personalized` threshold? The plan is to keep it as a documented constant so task authors can predict notices.
