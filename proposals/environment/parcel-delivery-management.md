## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Build a local Web app and MCP business tools for managing purchased parcels after dispatch: change home delivery, redirect to a pickup point, authorize collection, and resolve missed deliveries or approaching pickup deadlines. The environment distinguishes a requested change, its application by the carrier, physical parcel progress, and successful handoff. A successful request does not itself change the delivery destination or make a parcel available for collection.

Personal-agent tasks can use the same environment with different travel dates, accessible pickup locations, trusted collectors and availability. For example, a user leaving town may need a parcel held at an eligible location and a specific person authorized before its pickup deadline; a closure or rejected change can require revising that plan. Tasks supply these preferences, history and event timing. The App supplies discoverable options, policies, receipts and reusable transitions, without embedding a particular task's answer or private rubric.

The first version includes all four workflows below. Six planned views cover the shipment inbox, shipment details/timeline, delivery options and pickup-point directory, change review/history, collection authorization/pass, and in-app notices/receipts.

| Workflow | Preconditions and actions/events | Successful outcome and applicable controls |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| W1 — Change home delivery | For an eligible owned parcel, inspect restrictions; preview and confirm a new delivery date, a fixture-verified address, or a temporary hold; inspect the carrier's subsequent decision and eventual delivery event. | Exactly one effective delivery plan. A hold's end permits further delivery but never marks the parcel delivered. Pending requests can be withdrawn; rejected requests retain the prior plan and give a reason. Stale previews, restricted destinations and missed change cutoffs fail without partial changes. |
| W2 — Redirect to a pickup point | Inspect eligible points and hours; preview and confirm a redirect; follow the application decision, transport and actual arrival; obtain a current collection pass and collect through a trusted handoff event. | An applied redirect is distinct from actual readiness. The pickup deadline starts at the valid ready event. A closed point cannot accept collection; a later move invalidates the former point's pass and requires arrival at the new point. Changing back to delivery follows the same request/decision process while eligible. |
| W3 — Authorize another collector | For a ready parcel whose policy permits delegation, select a known collector, preview the limited access/expiry, confirm the grant, inspect/revoke/replace it, and receive the actual collection result. | Only the named collector with the current credential can collect. Revocation, replacement, departure from the point, expiry, return or completed collection invalidates prior credentials. Knowing a tracking number or sharing an address grants no access. Failed credential checks cannot consume the pass or complete collection. |
| W4 — Recover from failure or expiry | After a trusted failed-delivery or point-closure event, inspect the reason and action deadline; request redelivery, a hold or another eligible point; follow decisions and subsequent delivery/collection. | A recoverable failure can complete through W1/W2/W3. If the applicable deadline expires, the parcel enters return processing and a later trusted event completes the return. Return-in-progress rejects new recipient changes. Failure reasons, next actions and terminal outcomes remain visible. |

Related proposals are [#37 FreshCart](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/37), [#7 refund-only returns](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/7), and [#63 Mendwell Pharmacy](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/63). Their adjacent areas are grocery ordering/delivery, merchant refunds, and prescription collection. This contribution focuses on carrier-controlled changes after dispatch, asynchronous request application, location-dependent collection rights and delivery exceptions. It does not implement their shopping, refund or prescription workflows. They are related work, not blocking dependencies; overlap will be rechecked before submission and scope approval.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** Implement `apps/parcel-delivery-manager/` with App-local business modules, schemas, fixtures, tests/examples and contribution declarations. Reuse the public Core host and lifecycle through a thin plugin and service launcher, following the [App contribution guide](../../docs/app-contribution.md). Agent business operations will inspect authorized shipments, policies, points, notices and request receipts; preview/submit/cancel delivery changes; and preview/create/revoke/replace collection grants or retrieve an authorized pass. Exact business-tool names and schemas will be finalized in M1 against the approved semantics before dependent workflow implementation; names below are proposed App semantics, not new platform APIs. Mutations require an explicit confirmation derived from the preview and its version. An acknowledged submission reports `pending`; only an applied decision changes the effective plan.

  The Web app is an interactive Agent-facing surface, not just a reviewer renderer. Linqiang will implement its local browser host/bridge as an App-owned adapter that calls the same Agent MCP business operations with an Infra-selected acting account. It will not directly edit state files, let the browser choose another account, or expose Infra operations. The public local profile currently provides stdio MCP, not a complete browser host, HTTP MCP service or production authentication. Its GUI declaration/checker accepts reviewer renderers; this proposed browser bridge cannot be advertised as an already-supported Agent GUI capability. M0 must agree with Infra how to package this App-owned local companion and attach separate browser evidence while retaining the supported MCP declarations. If registration requires a runtime/validator extension, that is an explicit Infra dependency to resolve before plan approval, not an assumed available capability. Web/MCP state parity and denial of privileged operations are acceptance checks. No new platform authentication or credential-issuance service is proposed.

  Trusted Infra uses existing `env.validate_bundle`, `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.event_status`, `env.snapshot` and `env.reset`. These remain inaccessible to Agent MCP and the Web app. Only trusted events can resolve a carrier request, report actual arrival/delivery/collection, update point availability, or complete a return. An Agent cannot make physical handoff true by clicking a button or presenting its own fabricated event.

- **State and lifecycle (required):** Separate run state, App state and trusted account bindings. Business records include recipients/collectors, verified addresses, shipments and timelines, policy profiles, pickup points, one effective plan per parcel, change requests, collection grants/passes, and in-app notices. Owner identity comes from trusted bindings; event payloads cannot transfer ownership. A delegate sees only their currently authorized collection details, not the recipient's other parcels or address book. Cross-account queries must not reveal another account's parcel metadata.

  The reference profile uses one Infra-selected acting account per App/run. Validate the seed's `account_id` against the trusted binding; business tools and browser input cannot switch it. Owner, delegate and unauthorized-actor access checks use separate seeded runs, which do not represent shared concurrent user sessions. Competing collection events are tested against one parcel ledger. Simultaneous multi-actor login is outside this proposal's scope and would require an agreed platform identity adapter.

  Only the recipient can request/cancel delivery changes or create/revoke/replace delegation. Delegates may read their currently authorized point/deadline details and retrieve their own pass; they cannot redirect parcels or grant rights onward. The recipient has a personal collection entitlement and pass without creating a self-delegation grant. Physical collection still requires a trusted handoff event for either role.

  Each parcel has physical progress (`in_transit`, `out_for_delivery`, `attempt_failed`, `at_pickup`, `delivered`, `collected`, `returning`, `returned`), exactly one effective plan and at most one pending change. Changes transition from `pending` to `applied`, `rejected` or `cancelled`. Applying a change checks its business `change_id`, base plan revision and current policy atomically. Cancellation only affects a pending request: cancel-first prevents a later approval from applying; apply-first cannot be reversed by cancellation. A stale call may fail Core's version check; a fresh cancellation sees `too_late`, and revising the plan requires a new change. Applying a departure from the current pickup arrangement clears its readiness and passes. Physical arrival/delivery events must match the current plan revision and, where applicable, pickup epoch; old events cannot resurrect a superseded destination. Entering `returning` rejects outstanding changes with `return_started`, invalidates passes and blocks subsequent change/arrival/delivery events; only return completion can advance that parcel. Old decisions cannot overwrite newer plans. Terminal outcomes cannot be reopened by stale events.

  A collection pass is a synthetic business artifact, not a platform authentication token. It is bound to parcel, collector, point, pickup epoch and expiry, with expiry no later than the parcel's pickup deadline. A trusted collection event checks actual readiness, current point service/hours, matching identity, live grant/pass and non-consumption. A point move clears readiness and invalidates old passes; arrival at the replacement point establishes a new pickup epoch. A closure alone does not extend the deadline. Only the first valid readiness event for a new pickup epoch sets its deadline; repeated readiness reports cannot extend it even with a different event ID. A successful handoff consumes the credential and invalidates other passes atomically.

  Reuse Core commit/version, locking and retry facilities; do not build a second persistence layer. Equivalent retries with the same supported call/event identity must have one business effect; identity reuse with a changed payload is rejected. Stale confirmations, unauthorized actions and invalid events leave business records/notices unchanged, although runtime rejection diagnostics may exist. A valid carrier-rejection or failed-delivery event may record that real outcome. Concurrent collectors must never obtain two successful handoffs.

  Seeds can start from empty/new parcels or load coherent history containing pending changes, prior decisions and active collection grants. Reset restores the loaded baseline, including business notices, credential validity and virtual-clock baseline; runtime diagnostics are separate. Snapshot is inspection/evidence through the supported Core interface, not a promise of a universal restore endpoint or crash-resume feature. Replaying a fixed seed and ordered event schedule must reproduce business outcomes. Tests will inspect Core event/reset behavior rather than invent a separate event cursor.

- **Events and notifications (required):** Proposed App event families cover carrier change decisions, transport/failed-delivery progress, pickup readiness, point closure/reopening or relocation, collection, deadline advancement and return completion. Task scenarios choose event content and timestamps; Infra schedules and injects them. There is no autonomous live-carrier polling or global scheduler requirement. Use the public virtual-clock mechanism, with an App-specific trusted deadline event where needed to materialize due changes. Reads do not silently write state. Exact event envelope mapping will follow the pinned Core contract in M1.

  Validity uses `now < expires_at`: collection at the exact deadline fails. Infra commits a valid deadline/time-advance event to materialize due effects before other actions/events at that timestamp; other ties follow the scenario's recorded sequence. Business requests use the currently committed virtual time and never advance time or process expiry as a side effect of a rejected request. Invalid events likewise do not advance the clock. Event handlers evaluate the validated event timestamp, while tools read Core's committed virtual time; no event backdates business history. Tests cover the time boundary and reset clock. Hold release, failure-action deadlines and pickup expiry are explicit effects; expiry can start return processing but cannot fabricate final physical return.

  Illustrative business payloads, to be wrapped by the existing Infra event envelope: `{"kind":"carrier.change_resolved","shipment_id":"S014","change_id":"C007","base_plan_revision":3,"decision":"applied"}` and `{"kind":"carrier.pickup_ready","shipment_id":"S014","plan_revision":4,"point_id":"P03","pickup_epoch":2,"ready_at":"2027-04-08T14:00:00Z","pickup_deadline":"2027-04-11T14:00:00Z"}`. A resulting in-app record could be `{"notice_id":"N021","shipment_id":"S014","type":"pickup_ready","point_id":"P03","pickup_deadline":"2027-04-11T14:00:00Z"}`. These are synthetic examples, not implemented contracts. In-app notices are readable through both business tools and Web views; access follows current ownership/grants. Notice IDs are stable under retries. No external message delivery, durable cross-App outbox or cross-App transaction is promised.

- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Initial records are UTF-8 JSON with synthetic IDs, UTC timestamps, explicit point time zones/hours and stable object references. The catalog targets 24 shipments, six identities (three recipients, two delegates, one unauthorized actor), nine verified addresses, six pickup points and six policy profiles, assembled into eight resettable seed scenarios. Profiles vary change eligibility/cutoffs, allowed destination types, delegation and collection windows. Names/addresses are fictional and designed not to be deliverable; no real customer tracking data is used. Passes and receipts are structured business records rendered as text, not uploaded documents, scannable carrier credentials or resource-bearing attachments. No image/audio/video/PDF assets are required.

No Mail/Calendar package, remote carrier credential, production account, paid service, new media registration or special hosting is required for current scope.
