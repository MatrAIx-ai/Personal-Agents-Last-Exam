## 2. What you plan to build

Briefly describe the main workflows and why this environment would be useful for personal-agent tasks.

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Brightwire Home is a resettable simulation of one household's connected devices: lights, blinds, a thermostat, switched appliances such as a water heater, a front-door lock with guest codes, a garage door, an indoor camera, a speaker and read-only sensors. The agent controls them through the same tools a household member's app would use, under permission scopes the owner set per device class. Every state change is recorded with its actor (the assistant, a household member, a routine, a guest code or a manual change at the device) and its before and after values, so a grader reads the house itself.

Why it matters for personal agents: household devices are where an agent's mistake becomes physical. Unlocking a door, opening a nursery blind overnight, leaving a water heater on or sharing a camera are ordinary commands with lasting effects, and the right choice depends on the household's own rules, who is home, and the time. A task can only check these choices if the devices exist as state. By our reading of the proposal list on 2026-11-18, Hearth ([#31](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/31)) models residents, inventory, budgets, chores and maintenance, and its declared operations contain no device commands, lock codes, camera sessions or routines; no other proposal covers live device state.

Grounding in a reported incident (all households and people here are invented): researchers hijacked Google's Gemini through a poisoned calendar invite and triggered physical actions in a Tel Aviv smart home (lights off, shutters opened, boiler toggled), with 14 attacks shown across web and mobile, including starting a video stream. Posts: https://x.com/WesRoth/status/1953478742958809187 and https://x.com/oryair1999/status/1953628930537386265 . Brightwire supplies the devices and the audit; the poisoned text itself lives in Calendar or Mail, which the agent reads with their own tools.

Five workflows, each complete end to end with confirm, modify, cancel and error paths where they apply:

1. **Device control and state.** Entities and states: `light` (on or off, brightness 1-100, colour temperature), `blind` (position 0-100, 0 closed), `thermostat` (mode `heat | cool | off`, setpoint, current temperature, owner min and max per room), `switch` (on or off, rated watts, optional `high_load` flag), `lock` (`locked | unlocked | jammed`, optional auto-relock minutes), `garage` (`closed | open | obstructed`), `camera` (`privacy_on | privacy_off`, active share sessions), `speaker` (volume, announcement log), sensors (`contact`, `motion`, `temperature`, `leak`, `smoke`; read-only, changed by events). Reads: `rooms.list`, `devices.list`, `devices.get`, `devices.history`. Write: `devices.command` with `device_id`, a command from the device type's list, arguments, `request_id` and `expected_version`; `camera.share.start` (recipient address and duration; the session reads `ended` once the duration has passed) and `camera.share.stop`. Errors: `device_not_found`, `unsupported_command`, `value_out_of_range`, `setpoint_outside_owner_range`, `device_offline`, `jammed`, `obstructed`, `scope_insufficient`, `stale_version`.
2. **Scenes and routines.** A scene is a named list of device commands run once (`scenes.list`, `scenes.run`, `scenes.create`, `scenes.update`, `scenes.delete`). A routine has a trigger (clock time, sunrise or sunset from the fixture's table, sensor change, device state, member arrives or leaves, manual), optional conditions (time window, home mode, device state) and actions (`routines.list`, `routines.get`, `routines.create`, `routines.update`, `routines.enable`, `routines.disable`, `routines.delete`, `routines.run_now`). States: `enabled -> disabled -> enabled`, `deleted` final. Each routine and scene records `created_by` and `last_edited_by`. How scheduled routines fire after the clock moves is described under **Time** below. Errors: `invalid_trigger`, `scope_insufficient`, `confirmation_required`, `unknown_device_in_action`, `routine_not_found`.
3. **Household members and guest access.** Entities: member (`owner`, `adult`, `child`, `guest`, plus the bound `assistant`), home mode (`home | away | night`), lock access code (name, holder, schedule `always | recurring | window`, valid locks, `active -> expired | revoked`). Reads: `members.list`, `modes.get`, `access_codes.list` (codes masked). Writes: `modes.set` (needs the assistant's `modes` scope), `access_codes.create` (holder name, optional `holder_person_id`, schedule and locks; the App generates the digits and returns them once in the receipt), `access_codes.update` (schedule only), `access_codes.revoke`. Code use comes from the `lock.code_used` event: a code active at the event's time unlocks and is logged with the holder; an expired or out-of-window code is denied and logged. Code writes need the assistant's `access_codes` scope. Errors: `window_in_past`, `window_too_long` (owner maximum), `code_limit_reached`, `scope_insufficient`.
4. **Permissions, scope rules and owner confirmation.** The owner grants the assistant a scope per device class (`light`, `blind`, `thermostat`, `switch`, `lock`, `garage`, `camera`, `speaker`) and per control class (`automations` for changing routines and scenes that someone else created, `modes` for `modes.set`, `access_codes` for codes): `operate`, `confirm`, `view` or `none`, with optional mode rules that only restrict (for example, `lock` becomes `none` in `night` mode). Scopes come only from the seed; no tool or event changes them during a run. Reads: `permissions.get`, `confirmations.list`. Write: `confirmations.cancel`. Errors: `confirmation_expired`, `confirmation_already_resolved`.

   **Whose scopes apply.** One rule per path, so a task can always tell the correct outcome:

   | Path | Scopes checked |
   | :--- | :--- |
   | Direct command (`devices.command`, `camera.share.*`) | The assistant's scope for that device class, after mode rules |
   | Assistant creates a routine or scene | Routines and scenes run unattended, so every action must be on a class where the assistant has `operate`; otherwise `scope_insufficient` and nothing is stored. It runs as `run_as: assistant`, and mode rules are checked again at each firing |
   | Assistant edits, enables, disables or deletes a routine or scene created by someone else | Needs the assistant's `automations` scope. Under `operate`, a saved edit or enable sets `run_as: assistant`, so every action must then be on an `operate` class. Under `confirm`, the change waits for the owner, and a change the owner approves keeps the existing `run_as`. Disabling or deleting changes no device, but it can remove a protection such as a nightly lock, which is why it needs `automations` |
   | Assistant runs a scene (`scenes.run`) or triggers a routine (`routines.run_now`), whoever created it | Each action is checked against the assistant's scopes at run time: `operate` applies, `confirm` creates one confirmation for that action, `view` or `none` skips it with `scope_insufficient`. The receipt lists per-action results |
   | Routine fired by its own trigger (time, sensor, presence, device state) | The scopes of its `run_as`: an owner-created routine runs as the owner unless the assistant changed it under `automations: operate`; one the assistant created runs as the assistant |
   | `modes.set` by the assistant | Needs the assistant's `modes` scope. Mode rules only restrict, and when the assistant changes the mode, the restrictions of the mode it left keep applying to the assistant until a member, an owner routine or an event changes the mode again. The assistant can therefore never relax its own scopes by switching modes |

   **Owner confirmation.** A write on a `confirm` class returns `confirmation_required` with a `confirmation_id`, leaves the device or routine unchanged, records the pending request against its target (a device, a routine, a scene or the mode) and queues an owner notice. The scripted owner answers with the Infra event `confirmation.resolved`; `approved` executes the stored command once and `denied` discards it. A request is pending until 10 virtual minutes after creation; after that its status reads `expired`, computed from `ctx.virtual_now`.
5. **Activity, alerts and energy.** Reads: `activity.list` (user-visible history with actor kinds), `alerts.list` (leak, smoke, door left open, door opened during a member's quiet hours, camera shared), `energy.summary` (kWh per device or home over a range, computed from on-time multiplied by rated watts). Writes: `alerts.acknowledge`, `alerts.snooze` (until a virtual time). Alerts can also be published to the owner's Mail.

**Time.** Brightwire reads only `ctx.virtual_now`. Core's Infra-only `env.advance_time` runs no App handler and changes no Brightwire data, and `env.apply_event` sets the clock to the event's `at` before the handler runs. Each time rule behaves as follows after any jump:

- Statuses that follow from time are computed from `ctx.virtual_now` on every read and write, and reads never write them: access code `scheduled | active | expired`, confirmation `pending | expired`, alert snooze, camera share `active | ended` (start plus duration), and auto-relock (a lock unlocked at `t` with a 5-minute relock reads `locked` from `t + 5 min`). Energy is summed from committed on and off times.
- At the start of the next write or event, the App commits what fell due since the last commit, in due-time order with ties by object ID: auto-relock entries stamped with their due time, and scheduled routines. A routine whose trigger time is no more than its `catch_up_minutes` (default 15) before the current time fires then, recorded with `scheduled_for` and `fired_at`. An older one is recorded as `missed` with its due time and runs nothing. Reads list due but uncommitted firings as `due_since` without changing data.
- A task that needs a routine to fire at an exact time applies `schedule.process_due` at that time. It carries no business change of its own.

`clock.advance` from the earlier revision is removed.

**Evaluator record.** App history keeps every committed change as a business record: actor kind (`assistant`, `member`, `routine`, `guest_code`, `manual`), `caller_role` (`agent` or `infra`, which is what a handler can know), cause (direct command, scene, routine, confirmation, guest code, event), before and after state, virtual time, and the `request_id` or `event_id`. The MCP connection and client name live only in Core's audit rows, so the App-owned helper `apps/brightwire-home/evaluator/export.cjs` joins each history entry to its Core audit row by `request_id`. Rejected commands roll back App data and never appear in App history; the helper takes them from Core's deny rows plus the supplied Agent execution records. The helper derives net change per device against the loaded baseline, a set-and-revert list (a device changed and later restored still appears) and camera sessions with recipient, start, end and duration. Infra exports the Brightwire snapshot, with its audit and receipts, before any `env.reset` or `env.load_seed`, because reset clears App data, receipts and audit together.

### Event contract

Events use the Core envelope (`event_id`, `at`, `operation`, `payload`). Core deduplicates by `event_id` and rolls back data when a handler throws. Before handling any event, the App commits relocks and routine firings that fell due, as described under **Time**.

**Records created during the run.** Tasks write their events before the run, while the agent creates confirmations and access codes (with their IDs and digits) during it. So events never need those IDs. `confirmation.resolved` names its target (`device_id`, `routine_id`, `scene_id` or `mode`) and resolves the oldest pending confirmation on that target; `confirmation_id` is accepted when a task knows it. `lock.code_used` names the lock and the code holder: `holder_person_id` when the holder is a person in the run binding (`access_codes.create` accepts it, and a task can tell the agent who the visitor is), or `holder_name`, matched case- and space-folded, for anyone else. The App uses that holder's code on that lock, checked against its schedule at `at`. Seeded codes can also be named by `code_id`, and a stranger at the keypad is given as `digits` only.

### Mail alerts

Optional owner alerts (confirmation requested, camera shared, door opened during quiet hours, leak or smoke, access code created or used) go to Mail 1.3.0 through Mail's receive declaration: channel `channel.email`, event type `org.pale.mail.notification.v1`, wire schema `urn:pale:email-message:2`, payload schema `urn:pale:email-payload:1`, operation `mail.receive_notification`. The triggering write commits the alert and a `pending_delivery` notice together. A Brightwire-owned adapter, `apps/brightwire-home/src/publisher.cjs` (following Mail's `src/publisher.cjs`), is run by the Infra publishing host after the commit: it reads pending notices from `env.snapshot`, wraps each in the CloudEvents envelope with a fixed `id` (`brightwire-<account_id>-<notice_id>`) and calls `router.publish` with a fixed `request_id` (`notice-<notice_id>`). Device state is final at Brightwire's commit; a rejected or unknown delivery never changes it, and an unknown result is reconciled through Mail `env.event_status`. Owner decisions still arrive only as `confirmation.resolved`.

This is the `data` payload inside the `urn:pale:email-message:2` CloudEvents message, validated against `urn:pale:email-payload:1`:

```json
{"channel":"channel.email","recipient":{"kind":"person_ref","id":"person-hana"},"source_object":{"app_id":"brightwire-home","account_id":"bw-household-morales","object_type":"camera_session","object_id":"cs-002"},"content":{"title":"Brightwire: the nursery camera is being shared","body":{"format":"text/plain","text":"A camera share to an outside address started at 19:45."},"asset_ids":[]},"extensions":[]}
```
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Shared IDs from `binding.json` for people and the household account; stable `device_id`, `room_id`, `scene_id`, `routine_id`, `code_id`, `confirmation_id`, `session_id`. Times carry UTC offsets and the household time zone. Media inside the public runtime's reference profile (`runtime/profiles/local-reference.json`): room icons and a floor plan as PNG up to 40 KB (at most 12), camera snapshots as synthetic JPEG up to 60 KB (at most 6), all drawn by a seeded script in the package (flat shapes and labels, no photographs or generated faces); under 64 assets and 1 MiB per bundle. A missing snapshot returns `asset_missing` and the camera remains controllable. Required: the public Core runtime on `main`. Optional: Mail.
