## 1. App and domains

- **Authors / GitHub handles (required):** Huawei Lin ([@huawei-lin](https://github.com/huawei-lin))
- **Reference app or website / URL (required):** PaleAir, an independently branded synthetic airline website for searching, booking and changing flights. United Airlines (https://www.united.com) is a public interaction reference only; the airline, its partner, airports, schedules, fares and rules are invented, and no real branding or data is used.
- **Primary domain (required):** Travel & commuting
- **Secondary domains (optional):** None

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** PaleAir is a resettable airline booking site in which what a traveler may do, and what it costs, depends on the fare they bought and on the clock. It sells PaleAir's own flights and, like real airline sites, flights operated by a partner airline, some of them under PaleAir flight numbers (codeshares). A ticket can be canceled free within 24 hours of purchase, changed for a fee and a fare difference, or refunded only as travel credit; a booking can wait on the bank before it is ticketed; the airline can move a departure, cancel a flight or offer a rebooking that expires. The App states every rule on the fare and on each preview and then executes what it is told; it never chooses a flight, judges whether a trip or carrier meets anyone's rules, or recommends an option.

Flights anchor most travel tasks: a pickup, a hotel night, a meeting and a connection all hang on one arrival time. Tasks [#47](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/47), [#49](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/49) and [#55](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/55) each need flight booking. Task #49 (plan approved) books its flights in PaleAir and needs the two features added in this revision: a booking held for payment verification, and a non-U.S. partner carrier with operating and marketing carriers on each flight.

Six workflows, each complete end to end:

1. **Search and fare detail.** Search one-way or round-trip itineraries, nonstop or one connection, across a small invented network. Each result shows times, connection length, price, seats left, the marketing and the operating carrier of each flight (with the carrier's country), and three fare brands with their stated rules for changes, refunds, bags and seat selection. Prices and availability move with the simulated clock.
2. **Book.** Preview an itinerary for one or more saved travelers with fare, taxes, total and payment method, then confirm. What happens next depends on the card's bank, preset in the seed: a ticket with a receipt at once, or a hold that is not ticketed until the bank approves or the hold is released. Payment uses a stored payment label; no card number is entered.
3. **Manage a trip.** Choose or change seats, add bags, request or cancel a special service such as wheelchair assistance, and correct a traveler's details within stated limits.
4. **Change and cancel.** Preview a change or cancellation to see the fee, fare difference and whether money returns to the original payment or as travel credit with an expiry; confirm or discard. Split one traveler off a shared booking so that only their ticket changes. Apply a travel credit to a new booking.
5. **Disruptions.** When the airline changes a schedule, delays or cancels a flight, read the rebooking offer and its deadline, accept it, pick another eligible flight at no charge, or take the refund the rules allow.
6. **Check-in and travel day.** Check in when the window opens, retrieve the boarding pass, and read flight status and gate.

Core examples (precondition → action or event → outcome → control case). Tool and event names here omit the `paleair.` prefix.

| Workflow | Example |
| --- | --- |

| 1 | A route has a PaleAir nonstop and a partner flight → `flights.search` → the partner flight appears twice, as `LU402` and as codeshare `PA7402`, each showing operating carrier Lumora Airways (Portugal), its own marketing carrier and the same seats left → a search filtered by an unknown carrier code is rejected, and the App marks no listing as meeting any travel rule. |

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 33 Agent-facing MCP business tools over stdio in the public runtime, registered in `tools.json` as `paleair.<name>`.
  - Reads (15): `airports.list`, `carriers.list`, `flights.search`, `fares.get`, `policies.get`, `travelers.list`, `trips.list`, `trips.get`, `seats.map`, `offers.list`, `credits.list`, `status.get`, `receipts.get`, `itinerary.get`, `boardingpass.get`.
  - Writes (18): `travelers.save`, `travelers.remove`, `bookings.preview`, `bookings.confirm`, `seats.select`, `bags.set`, `services.request`, `services.cancel`, `travelers.correct`, `changes.preview`, `changes.confirm`, `cancel.preview`, `cancel.confirm`, `trips.split`, `offers.accept`, `offers.decline`, `checkin.complete`, `alerts.set`.
  - Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version`. Confirming a booking, change or cancellation also needs a single-use preview ID; as the review notes, this preview-and-confirm step is how a purchase that needs the user's consent is expressed, and a Task can also drop the confirming tools from the Agent's policy `allow` list. No sign-in or card entry exists.
  - Management operations are Infra-only: Core's portable lifecycle operations (`env.load_seed`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.apply_event`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle`) and runtime 1.6.0's `env.advance_time`. An Agent call returns `permission_denied`.
  - An optional read-only renderer with four views is for human reviewers only (`audience: ["reviewer"]` in `gui.json`); it has no controls and is not an Agent computer-use interface.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** One account per instance; every time-based outcome is computed from stored times and the clock; Core's write receipts and reset.
  - *Isolation and seed.* State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one customer account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). Reusable definitions: carriers, each with code, name and country from the seed (one home carrier, PaleAir, which the fixture places in the United States, and one or more partners), airports, routes, fare brands and rules, and a schedule generated from a seeded rule whose start date and length (7–21 days) the Task sets. Per-account seed: saved travelers, payment labels, each with a preset bank behavior (see Payment labels), past bookings and trips (with their own flight details, since past flights fall outside the generated schedule), travel credits, alert settings and `email_notices`. Per-run state: inventory and price changes, bookings, tickets, holds, seats, services, offers, credits, refunds, check-ins, notifications and applied event IDs. History in the fixture is dated from 2026-01-01. Amounts are integer cents in one currency.
  - *Carriers and codeshares.* Each operating flight has one operating carrier and an operating-flight ID (`op-LU402-2027-02-22`). It is sold under one or more listings, each a marketing flight number on a date (`LU402-2027-02-22`, `PA7402-2027-02-22`). Search results, bookings, tickets and offer alternatives name listings, because the marketing carrier matters to some travel rules. Trips, receipts and ICS files show both carriers. Events name the operating flight, so a delay or cancellation changes every listing of it together. All listings of one operating flight share its seat inventory and, in this version, its prices. All flights PaleAir sells use PaleAir's fare brands and rules.
  - *Time-based states: one source.* Every outcome that depends on time is computed when a read or write happens, from stored times and `ctx.virtual_now`; no event or stored status flag decides it, and `env.advance_time` writes no business record (it does advance the App version, so an `expected_version` read earlier becomes stale). Intervals are half-open: at `expires_at` or `release_at` exactly, the item is already expired or released.
    - Price: base fare by days before departure (21+, 7–20, 3–6, 0–2), with the multipliers stated by `policies.get`. `paleair.fare.changed` replaces a base fare.
    - Free cancellation: within 24 hours of ticketing (`ticketed_at`).
    - Offers and travel credits: usable while the clock is before their `expires_at`, `expired` after. `paleair.offer.expired` and `paleair.credit.expiring` are notice-only events (see Events).
    - Check-in window: opens 24 hours and closes 45 minutes before the flight's current departure, delays included.
    - Flight status: `scheduled`, `delayed` or `canceled` from events; `departed` and `arrived` from the clock against current times.
    - Segments: stored ticket states are `booked`, `changed` and `canceled`. Each flight segment of a ticket is judged on its own: after that segment's current departure, a read shows it `flown` if the traveler checked in and `no_show` if not, and writes on that segment are rejected (`departed`), while later segments, such as a return leg, stay open. A segment on a canceled flight is excluded from this rule: it shows `canceled` and stays open to its offer or refund until the offer expires. Nothing is written when a departure passes.
    - Holds: `on_hold` until the bank's approval time or `release_at`, whichever comes first, then `ticketed` or `released` (see Payment labels). An approval at exactly `release_at` is too late, so the trip is `released`.
    - Refunds: a refund to the original payment is recorded, with its amount and destination, when a cancellation is confirmed or a refund is taken on an offer. This version has no processing stage.
  - *Payment labels and the bank.* There is no payments App on the platform, so the card's bank is modeled as a behavior preset on each payment label, the way a payment provider's test cards always approve or always ask for verification. The Task chooses it in the seed; nothing during the run sends a bank decision.
    - `travelers.list` returns the saved travelers and the account's payment labels. Each label shows only its `label_id` and `display` (for example "Visa ending 4417"); no Agent tool returns its `bank` object, so the Agent learns the bank's behavior only from what happens, as a user would.
    - Fields of `bank`, checked by `validateSeed` (`invalid_seed` otherwise):

      | Field | Values | Meaning |
      | --- | --- | --- |
      | `mode` | `approve` (default) or `verify` | What the bank does with a new booking |
      | `approve_after_minutes` | integer 1–4320 | `verify` only: the bank approves this long after confirmation; without it, the bank never answers |
      | `hold_hours` | integer 1–72, default 24 | `verify` only: how long the seats are held while the bank decides |

      Example seed entry: `{"label_id":"pl-mc-2210","display":"Mastercard ending 2210","bank":{"mode":"verify","approve_after_minutes":90,"hold_hours":24}}`.
    - `approve`: `bookings.confirm` issues the tickets and records the charge at once.
    - `verify`: `bookings.confirm` creates the trip `on_hold` at `confirmed_at`.
      - `release_at` is the earlier of `confirmed_at` plus `hold_hours` and 3 hours before the first departure, and it is fixed at confirmation: a later schedule change or delay does not move it. A booking too close to departure for any hold is rejected (`too_close_to_departure`).
      - A held trip has a record locator and held seats, but no ticket, receipt or charge, and `trips.get` shows "ON HOLD – not ticketed". The held fare does not change during the hold.
      - The bank approves at `confirmed_at` plus `approve_after_minutes`. If that is before `release_at`, from then on the trip reads as `ticketed`: the charge is recorded with `ticketed_at` at that time, which starts the 24-hour free-cancellation window, and the receipt is available. Otherwise, or if no approval time is set, it reads as `released` from `release_at`, with the seats back and nothing charged.
      - While a trip is on hold, seat, bag, service, change, split, offer and check-in calls are rejected (`not_ticketed`), and `cancel.confirm` ends the hold at once with nothing charged.
      - Disruptions on a held trip: its times follow the flight, and no offer is opened for it; if a flight on it is canceled, the hold ends at once, as if released.
    - The bank behavior applies to new bookings only; change fees are charged without a hold. No event ticketing or releasing a hold exists, and the clock alone sends no notice.
    - A payments App, or a harness that reacts during the run, could later replace the preset behavior through a new, separately reviewed contract; nothing in this version depends on one.
    - *Stored and derived state together.* Every tool and event handler first derives each trip's status at `ctx.virtual_now`, then acts on that status:
      - A stored end of a hold (`cancel.confirm`, or a canceled flight) takes effect only while the derived status is still `on_hold`. A cancellation before the bank's approval time therefore ends the hold, and the later approval never applies.
      - A `paleair.flight.canceled`, `paleair.schedule.changed` or `paleair.offer.issued` that arrives after an approval time treats the trip as ticketed: it opens offers and allows the refund the rules give.
      - The first write or event that changes a derived-ticketed trip first stores the ticket and charge exactly as reads showed them, with ticket numbers from the record locator, `ticketed_at` set to the approval time, and the `request_id` of the original `bookings.confirm`.
  - *Previews.* A preview freezes the price, fees and schedule it shows. Confirming checks them against current state, and checks that the requested seats are still available. A different price, fee or schedule, seats no longer available, or a hold whose derived status has changed since the preview (for example, a cancellation previewed while on hold, after which the bank approved) gives `preview_stale`, and nothing is charged; more seats becoming available does not.
  - *Writes and evidence.* A committed write returns `status: committed`, the affected trip, ticket, offer or credit with its status, and the App `version` (`epoch`, `revision`). Core's rules apply:
    - an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` returns the stored receipt with `duplicate: true`, with no second effect or audit row;
    - the same ID with different arguments returns `idempotency_conflict`;
    - an old `expected_version` returns `stale_version` with `current_version`.

    Business rejections (`too_close_to_departure`; `preview_stale`; `preview_consumed`; `fare_rule`; `offer_expired`; `not_ticketed`; `departed`; `outside_checkin_window`) and every other failure leave data, version and notifications unchanged, and Core appends a denial audit row. Reads return `status: ok` and never change data.
  - *Reset.* `env.reset` is Core's: it restores this instance's loaded seed and the time loaded with it, starts a new epoch and clears receipts and audit. It touches no other App: messages already delivered to Mail stay there, and resetting Mail belongs to run setup. Infra takes `env.snapshot` before a reset to keep evidence. Reset checks compare business data and clock with the loaded baseline, not epoch or audit bytes.
  - *Evaluator records.* Tickets and charges that come from the bank's preset behavior are derived until a later write stores them, so they may not appear as records in `env.snapshot`. The package exports the pure status function, which evaluators apply to a snapshot and a time to get the same view that reads give. App state holds trips, tickets, holds, charges and refunds, credits, offers, check-ins, notifications and applied event IDs, each with the `request_id` or `event_id` that made it. Core audit has one row per allowed call except replays and `env.snapshot`, one per denial and one per clock advance; rows carry before and after versions and event traces but no business `request_id`. App records join the supplied Agent execution trace on `request_id` and `event_id`, and join audit rows on the version after the write.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Nine events through `env.apply_event`, each in Core's envelope `{event_id, at, operation, payload}`. `at` carries a UTC offset and is when the event takes effect: Core moves the App clock to it, and the handler reads it as `ctx.virtual_now`. The Task chooses what happens and when, scheduled ahead or chosen by the harness in reaction to the Agent; Infra applies it; PaleAir owns the effect. PaleAir has no timers. Each example below stands alone; together they are not one timeline. Flight IDs in events name operating flights.

  No event needs an ID created during the run. Events name flights, offer IDs the Task chooses, or dates. An event whose match depends on what the Agent did (an offer on a flight the Agent may not have booked, a credit it may not have created) commits with no effect when nothing matches, instead of failing the timeline; bookings, payments and refunds made during the run follow from the flights they are on and from the bank behavior preset on the card. When an event's offer reaches several trips, each trip gets its own offer, `<offer_id>/<record locator>`, and `paleair.offer.expired` with the event's `offer_id` covers all of them.

  A replayed event ID returns Core's first receipt with `duplicate: true` and writes no second notification record, so no second notice is sent; the same ID with different content returns `idempotency_conflict`; an event dated before the App clock is rejected (`invalid_event`). PaleAir also rejects, as `invalid_event` with nothing applied: an unknown flight, offer or fare brand; a time that contradicts a stored one, as listed above; a delay or schedule change for a departed or canceled flight.

  *Notifications and Mail.* PaleAir writes one notification record per occurrence inside the write or event that causes it:
  - with files: `booking_ticketed` (when a booking is ticketed at confirmation), and `change_confirmed` for a confirmed change or an accepted offer (receipt PDF and one itinerary ICS per flight segment, since Calendar imports one VEVENT per file); `schedule_changed` (the ICS of each changed segment);
  - text only: `booking_on_hold`, `cancellation_confirmed`, `flight_delayed`, `flight_canceled`, `gate_changed`, `offer_issued`, `offer_expired` and `credit_expiring`.

  The clock alone writes no notice: a hold that the bank approves, or that reaches `release_at`, shows in the App but is not mailed. `email_notices` is a seed setting; `alerts.set` turns the `flight_delayed` and `gate_changed` notices on or off per trip. Each record has an App-local ID (`ntf-<seq>`) and notes whether `email_notices` was on at that moment. Reset restarts that sequence, and an App cannot see the epoch, so the builder below forms the CloudEvents `id` and the `request_id` from the account, the snapshot's `epoch` and the record ID (for example `paleair-ana-e1-ntf-12`); they never repeat after a reset. Messaging directions: publishes `channel.email` and `channel.email.attachments`; receives none.

  Delivery is Infra's job and is not part of PaleAir's commit: a booking, change or cancellation commits whether or not any notice is delivered. After the commit, Infra reads the notification records with its own `env.snapshot` call and passes them to a pure builder exported by the package. The builder turns each record into the wire message, rendering its files with the same functions as `receipts.get` and `itinerary.get`. Infra then publishes through the publisher connection the router grants PaleAir's account. The builder holds no connection, so no PaleAir code gains management access. Mail's own [publisher](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/ae889d2cbbfc72242fc5e70b877f3f2a2f029618/examples/apps/mail/src/publisher.cjs) reads through its Agent connection because it sends Agent-written mail; most PaleAir notices come from events, so Infra does the reading.

  Infra keeps a cursor per epoch (the last delivered notification) in its run directory. It publishes later records in order, advances the cursor only on a committed receipt, and never writes into PaleAir. Before each delivery, Infra brings Mail's clock to the notice's time, as the [time contract](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/ae889d2cbbfc72242fc5e70b877f3f2a2f029618/docs/runtime-time.md) requires. The package ships unmodified copies of Mail's two wire schemas; a changed copy is rejected as `incompatible_contract`. Outcomes (router codes; the lists of codes are examples, not exhaustive):
  - `email_notices` off: no wire message is stored and nothing is sent.
  - Infra has not approved PaleAir as a publisher in the run: no sender runs.
  - Committed: one unread message in the Mail account bound to the user, with its files, and the router's receipt; the cursor advances.
  - Retry with the same `request_id` and message in the same router session: the first receipt with `duplicate: true`; still one inbox record.
  - Rejected before dispatch, for example `recipient_unavailable` (no Mail account bound to the person), `recipient_not_authorized` (no grant for that account and channel), `ambiguous_recipient`, `source_identity_mismatch`, `incompatible_contract` or `idempotency_conflict`: Mail has no record, and the cursor stays.
  - Halted, for example `receiver_rejected` (including a Mail account whose file budget would be exceeded), `receiver_unconfirmed`, `receiver_lifecycle_changed` or `concurrent_dispatch`: the router accepts no further publishes for the run, and Infra reconciles the cursor against Mail's inbox before the next run.

  In every case delivery leaves PaleAir's state unchanged, and nothing in it is rolled back.

- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** The run binding's person ID; stable flight, trip and carrier IDs; files rendered on demand; no package dependencies.
  - *People and accounts.* The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`, so the user has one person ID across Apps. Notices address `{"kind":"person_ref","id":<owner_person_id>}`, and Messaging resolves that to the Mail account bound to the same person; with no such account, delivery is rejected as `recipient_unavailable`. Each notice's `source_object` is `{"app_id":"paleair","account_id":<account_id>,"object_type":"trip","object_id":<trip_id>}`. Saved travelers may carry a `person_id` shared with other Apps' seeds.
  - *Formats.* Stable carrier, airport, flight, fare, trip, ticket, traveler, offer, credit, refund and notification IDs. Local times carry UTC offsets on the simulated clock.
  - *Files and their budget.* PaleAir stores no files: its seed bundle has no assets, and no write or event adds any, so the per-App limit of 64 assets and 1 MiB never applies to it.
    - `receipts.get`, `itinerary.get` and `boardingpass.get` render a PDF or ICS on demand from stored records, with a fixed template and no wall-clock input. The same record always gives the same bytes and hash.
    - They return a file object `{filename, mime_type, base64, sha256}`, which other Apps' attachment tools accept.
    - Sizes: a receipt or boarding pass up to 8 KiB, an ICS up to 2 KiB, one ICS per flight segment.
    - The files that do count are the ones Mail stores. A notice with files carries a receipt and at most 4 ICS (a round trip with a connection each way), so it adds at most 5 files and 16 KiB to the receiving Mail account. A typical task run sends at most 6 such notices: at most 30 files and 96 KiB.
    - If a Mail account's own budget would be exceeded, Mail rejects the delivery and the router halts, as above; PaleAir is unaffected. Task authors keep Mail seeds small or turn `email_notices` off for long runs.
  - *Standalone package.* The App package runs with only the shared runtime and its declared dependencies, and needs no other App or code and files outside the package. `app.json.dependencies` and `contribution.json.dependencies.required_apps` are empty, with `core_api` 1.2.0. Mail is a run-assembly recipient, not a package dependency, so `npm run app:verify -- --app apps/paleair` runs with the shared runtime alone. The Mail flow is checked separately with Mail 1.3.0 supplied.
