# Environment Track proposal

Proposed PR title: `[Environment Proposal] [News & information] Belltower local news and public notices with revisions and corrections`

## 1. App and domains

- **Authors / GitHub handles (required):** Ming Yin (@mingyin1)
- **Reference app or website / URL (required):** Belltower, an independently branded synthetic local-news reader (several local outlets and official notice feeds in one account, with every update, correction and retraction kept as a numbered revision); workflow references: the Atom syndication format for an entry's `published`, `updated` and `source` metadata (https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4287), schema.org's `correction` property for news articles (https://schema.org/correction) and Notify NYC for official notices by area and type (https://a858-nycnotify.nyc.gov/notifynyc/Home/About). No articles, notices, text, branding or feeds are reused.
- **Primary domain (required):** News & information
- **Secondary domains (optional):** None

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Belltower is a resettable local-news reader in which every item has a source, every change to an item is a numbered revision (an update, a correction or a retraction) that stays readable, and every republished copy says which revision of which item it copied, so the right answer depends on which version this person read, saved or passed on. One account follows sources, areas and topics, keeps a reading record and clippings, and is notified at once or in a daily digest; the task supplies items and revisions as events, and every time-based state follows the environment clock. Four workflows are complete in the first version.

**Entities.** Source (an official body, a newsroom or an aggregator), area (a small tree: city, district, neighbourhood), item (an article or a public notice), revision (numbered from 1; type `original`, `update`, `correction` or `retraction`; headline, body, topics, areas, priority, three times, and an optional `based_on` link to one revision of another item), follow rule, read mark, clipping with its history, notification with its release time, account preferences.

**Simplifications.** Content is whatever the task supplies: Belltower has no ranking, no recommendations and no fact-checking, and it never judges which source is right. One App instance holds one account. Topics are a fixed list of twelve (`water`, `power`, `roads`, `transit`, `schools`, `libraries`, `health`, `safety`, `weather`, `government`, `community`, `business`) and priority has three levels (`routine`, `important`, `urgent`). Items are text only. Search is plain word matching. The account reads; it does not publish, comment or share.

- **W1 Following and preferences.** Create a follow rule from any of a list of sources, a list of areas, a list of topics and a minimum priority, with a delivery (`immediate` or `digest`); pause it, change it or remove it; set the time zone, quiet hours, the daily digest time, how corrections are delivered (`as_item` or `immediate`) and email notices. Paths: modify = `follows.update` / `preferences.update`; cancel = `follows.remove`; errors = unknown source or area (`not_found`), a rule identical to an existing one (`already_following`), more than 20 rules (`limit_exceeded`), unknown time zone (`invalid_timezone`), a rule with no source, area or topic, or a digest time inside the quiet hours (`invalid_arguments`). Confirm: not applicable, because nothing here spends money or commits the user to anything and every write can be undone by another write.
- **W2 Evidence (read-only).** List sources and areas; list or search items by source, area, topic, kind, priority and a window over the time their revisions were received; read one item. A read returns the item's latest revision unless a `revision` number is given, and always names what it returned (`revision`, `type`, `is_latest`) with the list of all revisions of that item: number, type, times and, for a correction or a retraction, the publisher's note. Every revision carries three times: `published_at` as its publisher states it, `received_at` when it reached Belltower, and, for a public notice, the `effective` period of what it announces, from which its `effect` (`upcoming`, `in_effect` or `ended`) follows the clock. A copy or a report names its basis exactly as its publisher declared it (`based_on`: item, revision, relation `copy` or `report`), and the item it is based on lists every revision that declares it as a basis (`referenced_by`, each with the revision it used and its own times). Belltower states these links and never draws the conclusion: it does not label a copy as outdated, does not merge copies into one story and does not rank sources. Search matches words in the headline and body of every revision and says which revisions matched, so a claim that was later corrected can still be found and is shown as belonging to an earlier revision. Paths: modify, cancel and confirm are not applicable to reads; errors = unknown item, source or area, or a revision number the item does not have (`not_found`), `invalid_window`; no match returns an empty list, not an error.
- **W3 Reading record and clippings.** Mark an item as read at the revision that was read. Clip an item at its current revision with a note, an optional reference to the plan that relies on it (`linked_object`) and a choice to watch it; later move the clipping to a newer revision or change its note, each change kept in the clipping's history; remove it. Reads show `changed_since_read` for an item and `is_current` for a clipping. Reading an item never marks it: a read mark exists only when `reading.mark` is called or the seed has it, so the record says what the person was shown, not what the Agent looked up. Paths: modify = `clippings.update`; cancel = `clippings.remove`; errors = unknown item or revision (`not_found`), marking a revision that is already marked (`already_read`), clipping an item twice (`already_clipped`), moving a clipping to a revision that is not newer than the one it holds (`invalid_revision`), more than 100 clippings (`limit_exceeded`). Confirm: not applicable, for the same reason as W1.
- **W4 Notifications and the digest.** Notifications are written when an item or a revision arrives. The Agent shapes them through follow rules, watched clippings and preferences, and reads them with `notifications.list`. Paths: modify = change the rule or the preference (later notifications follow it; those already written keep their release time); cancel = pause or remove the rule, which stops discovery through it: from then on that rule brings no notification for a new item or for an item the account was not told about before; or stop watching the clipping, which ends the notifications that depended on the watch alone. Neither withdraws a notification that is already written, and neither stops later revisions of an item the account was already told about, which still notify under "Whoever was told is told again" below. The first version has no opt-out for a single item; turning `email_notices` off stops the Mail copies, while the log is still written. Errors and controls = no notification under a paused rule, below a rule's minimum priority, or for a revision of an item the account was never told about and does not watch. Confirm: not applicable, because the Agent's own writes in this workflow are those of W1 and W3.

Rules:

- Time is the environment's. Belltower has no timers, no scheduler and no clock event: Infra moves `virtual_now` with an event or with Core's `env.advance_time`, which runs no App code. Every time-based state is therefore stored as a due time and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever it is read or checked: a public notice's `effect` from its effective period, a notification's `held` or `released` from its release time. Event handling uses the same derivation (a notification counts as released once the event's time has reached its release time), and so does the Infra-run sender (§3).
- Revisions are numbered in the order their publisher issued them. A revision must carry the next number, a `published_at` that is not earlier than the previous revision's and not later than the time it arrives, and it cannot follow a retraction. The latest revision is the one with the highest number. An item is `retracted` when its latest revision is a retraction, otherwise `corrected` when any of its revisions is a correction, otherwise `current`.
- `based_on` is the publisher's own statement and names one revision of another item. Belltower checks only that this revision exists when the link arrives. A copy that is published after its basis was corrected still names the revision it copied, and nothing in Belltower updates it.
- A follow rule matches a revision when every filter the rule has is met: the item's source is in the rule's sources, one of the revision's topics is in the rule's topics, its priority is at least the rule's minimum, and one of its areas overlaps one of the rule's areas (the same area, or one contains the other). A paused rule matches nothing. A retraction is matched with the topics, areas and priority of the revision before it.
- Whoever was told is told again. When a revision arrives (revision 1 included), the account gets exactly one notification if an active rule matches it, or if the account already holds a notification for that item, or if it has a watched clipping of it; otherwise none. The kind follows the revision: `item.published`, `item.updated`, `item.corrected` or `item.retracted`. The notification records why it was written (`follow`, `earlier_notification`, `watched_clipping`) and which rules match this revision, possibly none: a correction that narrows an area matters most to the people it no longer covers. A read mark or an unwatched clipping alone brings no notification. The second condition does not depend on any rule: after a rule is paused or removed, or a watch is ended, a revision of an item the account already holds a notification for still notifies, with the reason `earlier_notification`, because the account was told the earlier version and stopping a follow must not hide its correction. Stopping a follow or a watch therefore stops discovery, not corrections.
- Delivery is `immediate` when a matching rule is `immediate`, when the account watches a clipping of the item, when the item's previous notification was `immediate`, or when the revision is a correction or a retraction and the account's `corrections` preference is `immediate`; otherwise `digest`.
- An `immediate` notification written inside quiet hours (taken in the account's time zone) gets the end of that quiet period as its release time, except for a revision whose priority is `urgent`, which is never held by quiet hours. A `digest` notification gets the next daily digest time at or after the moment it is written; `urgent` does not turn a `digest` delivery into an immediate one, because the person chose the digest for that rule. Nothing is rewritten when a release time passes.
- A replaced version is not put in front of the person, in the log or in Mail. In the log: when a revision arrives at time T while an earlier notification for the same item is still held (its release time is after T), the earlier one becomes `superseded` and is never released; the new notification names it and takes the stronger of the two deliveries. A notification whose release time is at or before T was released: it is left as it is and is followed by the new one. In Mail: the sender never dispatches a notification whose revision is no longer the item's latest in the snapshot it reads, so a released notification whose Mail copy has not gone out when the correction arrives is skipped, however late the sender runs. The guarantee therefore holds at first dispatch, not only at release. Nothing is deleted or rewritten: every notification stays in the log with its times and its state, and whether it has a Mail copy is read from Mail's records and the sender's report (§3).

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** A correction matters to exactly the people who acted on the earlier version, and only the person's own records say who that is. A narrowed boil-water notice changes nothing for a father whose street is still covered and everything for a sister whose street is not; a library that corrects its opening hours matters to the student whose evening plan was built on the first announcement; five copies of the old announcement with newer timestamps are not five confirmations; and a correction at 23:10 is worth waking one person and not another. Belltower makes these checkable: which items and revisions the agent read and when, what it left in the account (rules, read marks, clippings and their history), and what the account was told, in which version and at what time. Distinct task situations (separate Task proposals): (1) pass a public-safety notice to the relatives it covers, then its narrowed correction and its lifting, each to the right people (Task #115, plan approved, which plans a minimal feed of its own and says it will align with an approved News & information environment); (2) a library-hours announcement the user planned around is corrected while two feeds keep republishing the original (Task #10, which asks for stable source and item IDs, visible revision links and a notification log); (3) a school-bus route notice is updated twice in one week and only one child's stop changes; (4) a newsroom retracts a report that a clinic will close after the user has already asked for an appointment to be moved; (5) the start of a bridge closure is corrected to a day earlier on the evening before the user's early shift, under quiet hours and a digest preference. Fixtures are plain seed data, so each task loads its own sources, areas, items with their history and account record.

**Boundaries and overlap.** Proposal list checked on 2026-11-21: no Environment proposal provides news items or public notices from several sources, a revision history, or copies that name what they copied. In the same domain, #143 (RecallWatch, plan approved) covers product recalls matched to household items by lot, with remedy claims: a recall can appear in Belltower as an item, while lot matching and claims stay with RecallWatch. #66 (Cornerpost) is business listings and reviews. Elsewhere: #16 (CivicDesk) has rule dockets with an optional subscription; #26 (Stoopside) has member posts and organiser announcements inside a group; #168 (Postyard) is the publisher's side, where the account holder writes posts and corrects them, while a Belltower account only reads what local sources publish; #144 (ShelfMark) and #145 (Trailhead) apply a closure to their own holds and bookings; #123 (Rainshadow, by this author) and #126 (Gustline) carry weather alerts with their own update and cancel messages, and a Belltower public notice has no weather data, so a weather task uses those. None of them keeps the published history of a text or records which version a person read. Among the shared Apps, a newsletter in Mail is one message with no revisions and no source links; telling other people stays in Messages or Mail, since Belltower has no sharing; the person's plans and reminders stay in Calendar and Tasks; a long briefing text can live in Notes, with a Belltower clipping holding the item and revision each entry relies on. Tasks #10 and #115 need a feed of this kind, and Task #158 (an erratum to a brief the user sent, built on the shared Apps only) shows that corrections recur as a task theme in this domain. I will ask the authors of #115 and #10 in this PR which fields they need.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 17 Agent-facing MCP operations over stdio (`service.cjs --role agent`), registered in `tools.json` as `belltower.<name>`. Read (9): `sources.list`, `areas.list`, `items.list`, `items.get`, `follows.list`, `reading.list`, `clippings.list`, `notifications.list`, `preferences.get`. Write (8): `follows.create`, `follows.update`, `follows.remove`, `preferences.update`, `reading.mark`, `clippings.add`, `clippings.update`, `clippings.remove`. Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version` and returns the object's `id` with the committed version. The target object is always the argument `id` and a time window is always `start` / `end`; the reference host's audit projection at `61b6371` keeps `id`, `start` and `end` among the few inputs it records, so its audit rows show which item, rule or clipping every call touched, reads included. The Agent acts as the bound account holder set by Infra, never through an argument; a task can narrow tools, objects and fields with `policies.json`. App errors: `not_found`, `already_following`, `already_read`, `already_clipped`, `invalid_revision`, `invalid_window`, `invalid_timezone`, `limit_exceeded`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`; Belltower also answers `invalid_arguments` for what an input schema cannot express (an empty `patch`, a rule with no source, area or topic, a digest time inside the quiet hours). Infra-only operations: `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle`, and the host's clock operation `env.advance_time` of runtime 1.6.0; they are not listed on the Agent connection and Core returns `permission_denied` if the Agent calls them. The first version has no GUI and no reviewer console.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). The seed schema carries world records (sources, areas, items with all their revisions) and account records (preferences, follow rules, read marks, clippings with their history, notifications, activity rows), so a task can load any history, including items that were already corrected and an account that was already told about them. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, revision numbers that do not run from 1 in publication order, a `based_on` that names a missing revision, timestamps without a UTC offset and an account that the binding does not declare (`invalid_seed`). The seed is history: every record in it counts as already published, and new information arrives only through events. Writes follow Core: an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` or `event_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`; the same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`; a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`; a failed call leaves data, version and notifications unchanged and Core appends a denial audit row. Reads never change business data: statuses are computed from stored records and `virtual_now`, and a read is never recorded as a read mark. Reset is Core's: it restores the loaded seed and the time loaded with it (`reset_scope: app_instance`) and touches no other App; messages already delivered to Mail stay there. Evaluator records come from three places: (a) App state in the snapshot: activity rows with sequence, virtual time, actor `agent` or `event`, action, object ID and the `request_id` or `event_id` behind them, one for every committed Agent write, one for every applied event and one for each effect of that event (a notification written, a notification superseded); read marks with their revision and time; each clipping's history; notifications with creation time, release time, the revision they describe, why they were written and what they superseded; (b) Core audit rows with virtual time and the projected `id`, `start` and `end`: one for every allowed call that is not a replay, one for every denied call, and one for every `env.advance_time` with the clock before and after; (c) full arguments and results in the supplied Agent execution trace. They join on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Whether a notification has a Mail copy is read on the receiver's side, from Mail's inbox records (their `thread_id` is the notification ID) and the sender's report, never from Belltower's state. Counting rules: count committed writes and event effects from the activity rows, one row each. Core returns the stored receipt for a repeated `request_id` or `event_id` before it appends an audit row, so a replay appears only in the trace, as a call whose result carries `duplicate: true`: count it as a retry of the original, never as a new action. A rejected attempt writes no activity row: count rejected attempts from the denial audit rows and the trace, one for each attempt, repeats included. Count reads from the allowed audit rows and the trace. Time-driven changes (a notification released, a public notice coming into effect or ending) run no App code and write no row: read them from the stored due times and the clock, and from the audit row of the `env.advance_time` call or the event that moved it. Take the snapshot before a reset, because reset clears the audit.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Four events through `env.apply_event`, in Core's envelope. Belltower has no clock event: Infra moves time with an event or with `env.advance_time`. An event is handled at its own time T, which is `ctx.virtual_now` inside the handler in runtime 1.6.0: T becomes the revision's `received_at`, a held notification whose release time is after T can still be superseded, and a notification written at T is held or released by the quiet hours and the digest time at T. Core rejects an event dated before the App's current time (`invalid_event`); Belltower also rejects an unknown source or area, a duplicate item ID, a revision number that is not the next one, a `published_at` later than the event's `at` or earlier than the previous revision's, a revision of an unknown or retracted item, a `based_on` that names a missing revision, and an `effective` period on an article or one that ends before it starts (`invalid_event`, state unchanged).

  Notifications, each written once per arriving revision: `item.published`, `item.updated`, `item.corrected`, `item.retracted`. Messaging directions: publishes `channel.email` only; receives none. The App writes the notification log and, when `email_notices` is on at that moment, the full wire message with the notification, dated at its release time. An Infra-run sender in the package, an outbound adapter that Infra runs with its own Infra and publisher connections (the pattern of Mail's `src/publisher.cjs`), reads notifications through `env.snapshot` and dispatches a notification when three things hold in that snapshot: its release time has been reached, it is not superseded, and its revision is still the item's latest. It calls `router.publish` once for each such notification, in order of release time, for an account that has a Mail account bound in the run and an Infra grant for `channel.email`, and it returns a report of what it dispatched and what it skipped and why (`held`, `superseded`, `replaced`); it does not write back into Belltower, so no cross-App transaction is assumed. A digest notification written at 10:00 is therefore published after Infra advances the clock past the 18:00 digest time, with no App event, and one that was superseded at 16:30 is never published. Delayed dispatch: a notification is due at 18:00, its item is corrected at 18:05, and the sender first runs at 18:10. The 18:00 notification is `released` in the log, but its revision is no longer the latest, so the sender skips it as `replaced` and Mail receives no copy of the old version. The correction is dispatched when its own release time is reached: in that same run when it is delivered at once, which leaves exactly one inbox record in Mail (its `thread_id` is the correction notification's ID), or after the next digest time when the account keeps corrections in the digest. Had the sender also run at 18:02, Mail would hold two records in order, the notification and then its correction. In every case the log keeps both notifications with their release times. A delivery rejected before dispatch creates no Mail record; an unknown acknowledgement halts the router for reconciliation; neither rolls back Belltower. - **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for sources, areas, items, follow rules, clippings and notifications; a revision is named by its item and number. The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` are the shared values of the run's `binding.json`, so the person has one ID in Belltower and Mail, and notifications address the recipient by `person_ref`. Belltower is the source of truth for its items, revisions, follow rules, reading record, clippings and notifications and keeps no copy of another App's records; a `linked_object` is an opaque reference it never dereferences. Missing data is explicit: an unknown ID returns `not_found`; an article has `effective: null`; an open-ended notice has `effective.end: null`; the latest revision of a retracted item has `body: null` and the publisher's note. All records are JSON; there are no images, audio, video or attachments (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the profile's 64 assets). Runtime needs: the public Core/Messaging runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` commit `61b6371`; Mail is optional; `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; no external service. The package runs on its own: a copy that holds only `apps/belltower/` and the pinned runtime starts the App, and its business tests and example run from the package alone. It imports and reads nothing outside its own folder and uses no other App's files or state; the two real-MCP scripts are given the location of the pinned runtime, and the Mail flow also needs the pinned Mail package. Quiet hours and digest times use IANA time zones through Node's built-in `Intl`, so no package is installed.
