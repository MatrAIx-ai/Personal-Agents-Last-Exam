## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Ribbonwell is a resettable wish-list account that one person shares with a family group and a friends group. Its defining rule is that information is split by who is looking: everybody except the list owner can see who has reserved or bought an item, and the owner sees nothing. The account holder's agent therefore holds things it must not pass on (what the user bought for a partner, whom the user drew in an exchange) and cannot see things it might be asked about (what others bought for the user). Nothing is bought inside the App: a purchase is a recorded state, what other members do arrives as task-supplied events, and every time-based state follows the environment clock. Four workflows are complete in the first version.

**Entities.** Account (the bound member, two notification settings `notify_item_added` and `notify_exchange_activity`, a `draw_salt` that no read returns), member (name, optional `person_id`), group (name, members), list (owner, group, optional `managed_by`, optional occasion name and date), item (list, name, `variant`, `price_cents`, `store`, `note`, `priority`, `quantity_wanted`, stored status `listed` or `removed`), claim (member, item, quantity, stored status `reserved`, `purchased` or `released`, `reserved_until`, a private note, optional `for_exchange_id`, the item fields that changed since the claim), exchange (group, organizer, name, `budget_cents`, `join_by`, `exchange_date`, exclusion pairs, participants, `drawn_at`), assignment (giver and receiver in one exchange), notification, activity row.

**Simplifications.** One App instance holds one account, which belongs to exactly one member. There is no store, cart, price lookup, link fetching, payment, shipping, group chip-in, photo or public registry: an item is text and numbers, and `purchased` means only that a member said so. A group has at most 12 members and an exchange at most 10 participants. A list belongs to one group. A managed list is a list whose owner is a child or other person without an account of their own; its `managed_by` member edits it and, unlike an owner, sees its claims. The notification settings are loaded by the seed and no tool changes them. The Agent acts as the account's member and nobody else.

- **W1 Lists and claims as this member may see them (read-only).** Read the account (with the current environment time as `now`), groups and members, lists (by group, owner or an occasion-date window), one list with its items, one item, a text search across visible items, the member's own claims, exchanges, and notifications. On a list the member owns, items carry `claims_hidden: true` and no claim field of any kind. On every other list in the member's groups, including lists the member manages, each item carries `remaining`, `over_claimed` and its claims with the claimer and read status. Paths: modify, cancel and confirm are not applicable to reads; errors = an unknown ID or a list outside the member's groups (`not_found`), `invalid_window`.
- **W2 The member's own and managed lists.** Add an item, change it, remove it, restore it; set a list's occasion. Paths: modify = `items.update`; cancel = `items.remove`, undone by `items.restore`; errors = editing a list the member neither owns nor manages (`not_list_editor`), a name already used by a listed item on that list (`name_taken`), `invalid_quantity` (outside 1–20), restoring an item that is not removed (`invalid_state`). Confirm: the write is the commitment, and every write here is reversible by another write. On the member's own list no result and no error depends on claims: removing an item that someone has bought returns exactly what removing an unclaimed item returns.
- **W3 Reserving and buying for others.** Reserve a quantity of an item on someone else's list; change the claim (quantity, mark purchased or back to reserved, extend the hold, a private note, tag it for an exchange); release it. A reservation holds for 14 days and then lapses unless extended or marked purchased; a purchase never lapses. Paths: modify = `claims.update`; cancel = `claims.release`; errors = an item on the member's own list (`own_list`), a removed item (`removed_item`), more than is left (`insufficient_quantity`), a second claim on an item the member already has an unreleased claim on (`already_claimed`), `invalid_quantity`, a hold that ends in the past or more than 14 days ahead (`invalid_window`), an exchange tag for an item whose list owner is not the member's giftee (`not_my_giftee`), a change to a released claim (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `items.get` returns `remaining` and the existing claims, which is everything a reservation would be checked against.
- **W4 Gift exchanges.** Read an exchange; join or leave one; as organizer, create an exchange in a group, change it and run the draw. After a draw the member can read only their own giftee. Paths: modify = `exchanges.update` before the draw; cancel = `exchanges.leave` before the draw, not offered for a draw (it cannot be undone inside the run); errors = joining when the exchange is not `open` (`joining_closed`), `already_joined`, `exchange_full`, leaving when not a participant (`not_participant`), an organizer action by a non-organizer (`not_organizer`), a draw with fewer than three participants (`too_few_participants`) or with exclusions that leave no valid assignment (`no_valid_draw`), an exclusion that names someone outside the group or pairs a member with themselves (`invalid_exclusion`), `join_by` not before `exchange_date` (`invalid_window`), a name already used by an exchange in that group (`name_taken`), any change, leave or draw once the exchange reads `drawn`, `past` or `expired` (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `exchanges.get` returns `draw_ready` with the reasons when it is false.

Rules:

- Time is the environment's. Ribbonwell has no timers and no clock event: Infra moves `virtual_now` with an event or with Core's `env.advance_time`, which runs no App handler. Only two things depend on time and both are stored as times and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever they are read or checked: a claim's read status (`lapsed` when the stored status is `reserved` and the time is at or past `reserved_until`) and an exchange's status (`drawn_at` set and before `exchange_date` → `drawn`; set and at or after → `past`; not set and before `join_by` → `open`; not set and from `join_by` up to `exchange_date` → `closed`; not set and at or after `exchange_date` → `expired`). Nothing is rewritten when time passes, so there is no state between a due time and the next event that differs from what a read returns. Writes and event handlers use the same two derivations.
- An active claim is one that reads `purchased` or `reserved`. `remaining` is `quantity_wanted` minus the quantities of active claims, never below zero. A claim that becomes active again (a lapsed one extended or marked purchased) or grows is checked against `remaining` computed without it, exactly like a new reservation. The same rule applies to every member, so a lapsed reservation made by an event frees its quantity too.
- An owner never learns about claims. Reads of the member's own list omit them; `items.update`, `items.remove` and `items.restore` on it succeed or fail on the item alone; no notification is ever written about a claim on the member's own list. The owner may lower `quantity_wanted` below what is claimed: the claims stay and shoppers see `over_claimed: true`. Claims on the member's own list still exist in App state, loaded by the seed or recorded by events, where only the evaluator reads them.
- A removed item keeps its claims. They read with `item_removed: true`, can be released, and count again if the item is restored. A removed item's name is free for reuse; restoring it while a listed item has that name returns `name_taken`.
- “Same item” is the item ID. Within one list, names of listed items are unique after trimming and case-folding. A material change is a change to `name`, `variant`, `price_cents` or `store`; `note`, `priority` and `quantity_wanted` are not material. When a material field of an item changes, or the item is removed, every unreleased claim on it records the changed field names (`changed_fields`, or `["removed"]`); the list is cleared by that claimer's next committed `claims.update` on the claim.
- One member has at most one unreleased claim per item. After a release, a new reservation creates a new claim.
- The draw is reproducible and not predictable from what the Agent can read. Joined participants are ordered by SHA-256 of `draw_salt`, the exchange ID and the member ID; the first stays in place and the orders of the others are tried in lexicographic order of that ranking; each order is read as a ring in which everyone gives to the next and the last gives to the first; the first ring in which no giver and receiver form an excluded pair is stored. An exclusion pair blocks both directions. `my_giftee` is the only assignment any read returns.
- The budget is advice, not a limit. A claim tagged with `for_exchange_id` reads `over_budget: true` when `price_cents` times its quantity exceeds `budget_cents`, and `null` when the item has no price.
- Notifications are written for what other members do, never for the Agent's own writes and never for a change that time alone causes: `item.added` for a new item on a list in the member's groups when `notify_item_added` is on; `claim.item_changed` once per event that materially changes or removes an item, for each unreleased claim the member holds on it; `exchange.invited` when someone else opens an exchange in one of the member's groups; `exchange.member_changed` when someone joins or leaves an exchange the member organizes and `notify_exchange_activity` is on; `exchange.drawn` when someone else's exchange in which the member is a participant is drawn. A notification is written again each time its condition occurs again and is never removed.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** A wish list is a small standing set of secrets between people who talk to each other every day. The agent of one member can see that Grandma already bought the train set, knows the user has reserved a jacket for a partner who shares the same family chat, and holds the name the user drew; it cannot see what anyone bought for the user and has to say so instead of guessing. The right action depends on who is asking, in which conversation, about whose list: answering a child's “did anyone get me the bike?” from the managed list, telling the partner what the user still needs to buy without naming the item bought for the partner, noticing that a size changed after the user reserved, or that a hold lapsed and an uncle took the item. Ribbonwell makes these checkable: which lists and claims the agent read, which it changed and for whom, what it tagged for which exchange, when a hold ended, and whether anything about a hidden claim or an assignment left the App in a message the agent wrote elsewhere. Distinct task situations (separate Task proposals): (1) finish holiday shopping for six relatives without buying anything twice, when two reservations lapsed while the user was travelling; (2) organize the family draw so that couples do not draw each other and the user's own giftee stays out of the shared chat; (3) answer a spouse who asks what is left to buy for the children without revealing what was bought for the spouse; (4) a reserved item's size is changed by its owner three days before the party, and the purchase is already made; (5) the user wants to take a claimed item off their own list, and the agent cannot know whether someone bought it; (6) a relative asks the agent's user to release a reservation so they can give the item instead. The rationale is independent of any one task. Fixtures are plain seed data, so each task loads its own groups, lists, claims and dates.

**Boundaries and overlap.** Proposal list checked on 2026-11-23 (95 Environment proposals): none covers wish lists, gift claims or name-draw exchanges, and a search of pull requests for gift, wishlist, registry and santa returns none. Nearby proposals differ in what they hold: #26 (Stoopside) has public interest groups and event sign-ups; #85 (Kinthread) adds group governance and a care vault to Messages; #50 lends physical items from a shared library; #179 is a meeting poll; #221 (PriceWatch) watches prices of products; #37 and #186 are a grocery store and a secondhand marketplace with orders; #68 (PalePay) and #203 move or split money. Ribbonwell holds no order, price history or money: paying for a gift stays in the shopping and finance Apps, and talking about one stays in Messages and Mail, which Ribbonwell never reads or writes.

## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** None in the first version. All four workflows complete with Ribbonwell alone, and no workflow needs the Agent to call another App's tools. Notifications are an in-App log read with `notifications.list`; no email, chat or calendar message is sent, so there is no messaging declaration (`publishes` and `receives` are empty) and no publisher that something else would have to call. Calendar, Mail, Messages, Contacts, Files, Notes and Tasks: none; Ribbonwell does not copy or shadow any of them.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 24 Agent-facing MCP operations over stdio (`service.cjs --role agent`), registered in `tools.json` as `ribbonwell.<name>`. Read (11): `account.get`, `groups.list`, `groups.get`, `lists.list`, `lists.get`, `items.get`, `items.search`, `claims.list`, `exchanges.list`, `exchanges.get`, `notifications.list`. Write (13): `lists.update`, `items.add`, `items.update`, `items.remove`, `items.restore`, `claims.reserve`, `claims.update`, `claims.release`, `exchanges.create`, `exchanges.update`, `exchanges.join`, `exchanges.leave`, `exchanges.draw`. Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version` and returns the object's ID with the committed version. The Agent acts as the bound member set by Infra, never through an argument, and package tools are exposed to the Agent only: there is no user-only tool and no approval state. Because `policies.json` can check only the argument `id` and the keys of `patch`, every tool names the object it acts on as `id` (the list for `items.add`, the item for `claims.reserve`, the group for `exchanges.create`, otherwise the object itself) and every change to an existing object goes through `patch`. A reservation can only be created as `reserved`; marking it purchased is the `status` key of `claims.update`, so a task can forbid exactly that key, limit claims to named items or forbid additions to a named list. What this does not give: a task cannot list in advance the IDs of items or claims the Agent creates during a run, so narrowing those is by tool and by `patch` key only, and the proposal claims nothing more. App errors: `not_found`, `not_list_editor`, `name_taken`, `invalid_quantity`, `own_list`, `removed_item`, `insufficient_quantity`, `already_claimed`, `not_my_giftee`, `joining_closed`, `already_joined`, `exchange_full`, `not_participant`, `not_organizer`, `too_few_participants`, `no_valid_draw`, `invalid_exclusion`, `invalid_window`, `invalid_state`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Every refusal is an error that leaves data and version unchanged; no workflow has a refused outcome that must be kept as a record, and rejected attempts are counted from Core's audit rows. Infra-only operations are the fixed set of runtime 1.6.0: `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the host's `env.advance_time`; Ribbonwell adds no Infra query, they are not listed on the Agent connection, and Core returns `permission_denied` if the Agent calls them. The first version is MCP-only: no GUI and no reviewer console.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). The seed schema carries the account, members, groups, lists, items (listed and removed), claims in every stored status and on any list including the member's own, exchanges in any state with their participants, exclusions and assignments, notifications and activity rows, so a task can load any history. Seeded history is dated from 2026-01-01 onward, and the package fixtures cover the whole span from 2026-01-01 to the fixture's `virtual_now`; a task loads its own dates. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, an account whose member the binding does not declare, a list whose owner or manager is outside its group, two listed items with the same name on a list, a claim by an item's own list owner, two unreleased claims by one member on one item, an exchange with more than 10 participants, assignments that are not a single valid set over the participants or that break an exclusion, a creation or change time after `virtual_now` or before 2026-01-01, and timestamps without a UTC offset (`invalid_seed`). Writes follow Core: an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` or `event_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`; the same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`; a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`; a failed call leaves data and version unchanged. Reads never change business data. Reset is Core's: it restores the loaded seed and the time loaded with it (`reset_scope: app_instance`) and touches no other App. Evaluator records come from three places: (a) App state in the snapshot, which unlike any Agent read contains claims on the member's own list and every assignment, plus activity rows with sequence, virtual time, actor `agent` or `event`, action, object ID, list or exchange ID, and the `request_id` or `event_id` behind them, one for every committed Agent write and every applied event, with the replaced values for `items.update`, `claims.update` and `exchanges.update` and, for `items.remove`, the number of unreleased claims the item carried (in the activity row only, never in the Agent's result); (b) Core audit rows for allowed and denied calls, reads included, which show which lists, items and exchanges the Agent looked at; (c) full arguments and results in the supplied Agent execution trace. They join on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Count committed writes from the activity rows, rejected attempts from the denial audit rows, and replays (a result carrying `duplicate: true`) as retries of the original. Time-driven changes write no row: read them from the stored times and the clock. Take the snapshot before a reset.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Six events through `env.apply_event`, in Core's envelope. Ribbonwell has no clock event. An event is handled at its own time, which is `ctx.virtual_now` inside the handler, and Core rejects an event dated before the App's current time. An event may name a record the Agent created during the run without knowing its ID: wherever a payload takes `item_id` it may instead take `item_match` (`list_id` and `name`), and wherever it takes `exchange_id` it may take `exchange_match` (`group_id` and `name`). A match compares names after trimming and case-folding, considers listed items only, and can never find two records because those names are unique; no match is `invalid_event`, which Infra reads with `env.event_status`. Ribbonwell also rejects a duplicate ID, an unknown member, list, item or exchange, an actor who is not in the group, an actor who is the bound member (the Agent's tools are the only way the member acts), an item added or changed by someone who neither owns nor manages the list, a claim by the item's list owner, a claimed quantity beyond `remaining`, a join when the exchange is not `open` or is full, and a set of assignments that is not valid for the participants and exclusions (`invalid_event`, state unchanged).

- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for members, groups, lists, items, claims, exchanges and notifications. The account's `account_id` and `member_id` and the member's `person_id` are the shared values of the run's `binding.json`. Every member may carry an optional `person_id`, so a task that has the same people in Contacts or Messages gives them the same IDs in its seeds; Ribbonwell stores a display name, not a copy of a contact. Ribbonwell is the source of truth for its lists, items, claims and exchanges and keeps no copy of another App's records. All records are JSON; there are no images or attachments (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the profile's 64 assets). Runtime needs: the public Core runtime 1.6.0 with Core API 1.2.0 on `main` at commit `a4bce02`; `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; no external service. The package runs on its own: a copy that holds only `apps/ribbonwell/` and the pinned runtime starts the App, and its business tests and example run from the package alone. It imports and reads nothing outside its own folder. Dates use IANA time zones through Node's built-in `Intl` and the draw uses Node's built-in `crypto`, so no package is installed.

Local business tests (workflow in brackets):

- T01 [W1] Groups, members and lists; filters by group, owner and occasion window; pagination; `not_found` for a list outside the member's groups.
- T02 [W1] The three views of a list: own (no claim field anywhere, including `items.get`, `items.search` and `claims.list`), managed, and someone else's; `remaining` and `over_claimed`.
- T03 [W1] `account.get` with `now`; exchanges and notifications reads; an exchange returns only `my_giftee`.
- T04 [W2] `items.add`, `items.update`, `lists.update`; `name_taken` after trimming and case-folding; `not_list_editor`; `invalid_quantity`.
- T05 [W2] `items.remove` and `items.restore`; claims kept and flagged; restore against a reused name; lowering `quantity_wanted` below the claimed quantity.
- T06 [W2] Owner blindness: for every own-list write, the result and errors are identical with and without claims on the item, and the claim count appears only in the activity row.
- T07 [W3] `claims.reserve`: quantity checks, `own_list`, `removed_item`, `already_claimed`; reservation on a managed list.
- T08 [W3] `claims.update`: quantity up and down, `purchased`, back to `reserved`, extending a hold, `invalid_window`, the private note, `claims.release` and a new claim after it.
- T09 [W3] Lapse at the exact `reserved_until`; a lapsed claim frees its quantity; renewing or purchasing a lapsed claim with and without quantity left; the same for a reservation recorded by an event.
- T10 [W3] Material and non-material changes, removal and restore of a claimed item: `changed_fields`, clearing, and one notification per event.
- T11 [W4] `exchanges.create` and `exchanges.update`: `name_taken`, `invalid_window`, `invalid_exclusion`, `not_organizer`; moving `join_by` reopens joining.
- T12 [W4] Join and leave by tool and by event in each status; `joining_closed`, `already_joined`, `exchange_full`, `not_participant`.
- T13 [W4] The draw: determinism, exclusions in both directions, `too_few_participants`, `no_valid_draw`, no change after the draw; `exchange.drawn` validation for someone else's exchange.
- T14 [W4] Status at each boundary (`open`, `closed`, `drawn`, `past`, `expired`); exchange tags, `not_my_giftee` and `over_budget`.
- T15 [all] Events and notifications: each of the six events with its rejections; addressing by ID and by match key, including no match; one notification per occurrence and none for the Agent's own writes, for time alone or for any claim on the member's own list.
- T16 [all] Core contracts and evidence: repeated `request_id` → stored receipt and no second effect; changed input → `idempotency_conflict`; stale version → `stale_version`; the Agent calling `env.apply_event`, `env.reset` or `env.advance_time` → `permission_denied`; seed validation failures; reset restores the loaded seed and time; one activity row for each committed write and each applied event, and none for a replay, a rejected call or a time-driven change.
