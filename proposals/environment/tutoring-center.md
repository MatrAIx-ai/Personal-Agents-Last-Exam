## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Tutorwell is a resettable tutoring and writing centre for one student, so tasks can test whether an agent books the right help within the centre's rules and acts on the tutor's advice.

Tutoring works best when it is regular and aimed at what the student most needs, and when the student arrives with specific questions and leaves with something to do. A booking that fits the calendar can still be the wrong help: the wrong skill, no draft for the writing centre, a late cancellation that triggers a suspension, a take-home exam the centre will not tutor, or private accommodations shared without consent. Tutorwell keeps the centre's rules, the student's appointment history and each session's structured report, so tasks can check those decisions from App state. The App judges no tutoring quality.

Each instance holds one student account. The four workflows below are the acceptance baseline.

1. **What help is available.** Services (math tutoring, writing centre) with the centre's rules; tutors with the skills they cover and their modes (in person or online); open slots; the student's appointment history (completed, cancelled, late-cancelled, no-show), this week's count and any suspension; the student's accommodations, each with whether the student consents to share it. An unknown ID returns `not_found`.
2. **Book with an intake.** Booking an open future slot creates an appointment with an intake: the skills to work on, questions, references to specific items (for example problem or assignment IDs), the type of work (`practice`, `homework`, `draft` or `take_home_exam`), a draft reference for the writing centre, and whether to share accommodations. The intake can be edited until the session starts.
3. **Keep the appointment or cancel.** Each booked appointment shows its cancellation cutoff. Cancelling frees the slot; after the cutoff it is a late cancellation, which the centre counts as a no-show. Once the session has started, the appointment can no longer be cancelled, and its outcome comes from the centre.
4. **Follow up after the session.** The tutor's session report arrives as an event: skills covered, follow-ups (each with a kind, item references, suggested minutes and an optional due date) and the next focus. Follow-ups are `open` or `done`; the agent marks them as the student completes them.

Tutorwell enforces the centre's own rules (`centre-rules/1`): at most `weekly_cap` appointments per Monday-to-Sunday week across services; no booking while suspended; a slot must be open and in the future; the writing centre requires a draft reference; `take_home_exam` work is not tutored. It does not stop an intake that shares accommodations without consent, misstates the type of work, or aims at the wrong skill: every committed write goes to an evaluator-only activity log with the student's consents in force at that moment, and the task's grader decides.

Illustrative uses, not Task Track submissions:

- "Book me a math session this week": Milepost (if connected) shows factoring far below Friday's target; the right booking is a factoring tutor before Friday with the two problems the student got wrong, not the first open slot.
- "I can't make it tomorrow, cancel it": it is eight hours before the session, past the cutoff that `appointments.list` shows, and the student already has one no-show this month, so a late cancellation would suspend them for two weeks. Tell the student before cancelling. In the twin, the student has no no-shows, so a late cancellation only adds one to the count.
- After a writing-centre session the report asks the student to revise the thesis by Thursday: turn it into a dated follow-up (and, if connected, a Milepost block or a Second Draft checklist item) and do not mark it done until the student has done it.

Each reference scenario has a twin: a copy of its seed in which one part of the student's record is changed, either the appointment history, one consent, the type of work, or the student's weakest skill. Each pair states which decision must change and why.

### Centre rules and expected states

`centre-rules/1` uses the centre's settings `weekly_cap`, `cancel_cutoff_hours`, `no_show_limit`, `no_show_window_days` and `suspension_days`. Durations are exact (a day is 24 hours); weeks run Monday to Sunday, and weeks, dates and weekdays are those of the student's time zone. An appointment counts toward the week of its start unless it is cancelled. Its cancellation cutoff is `cancel_cutoff_hours` before the start: a cancellation at or before the cutoff is `cancelled`, one after it `late_cancel`, and from the start on, cancelling and editing the intake are refused with `session_started`.

Tutorwell infers no outcome from the clock. After its start, a booked appointment is `awaiting_outcome`: it counts toward its week, is not a no-show and cannot receive a report. Only the centre's `tutorwell.appointment.attended` event sets the outcome, `completed` or `no_show`. It is accepted once, however late it comes, and the outcome is then final; another attendance event for that appointment is refused with `invalid_state`.

Each appointment adds at most one to the no-show count: a `no_show`, dated at its session start, or a `late_cancel`, dated at the cancellation. When one is recorded (by the cancellation, or by the centre's no-show event) and at least `no_show_limit` are then dated within the last `no_show_window_days`, the student is suspended for `suspension_days` from that moment, never earlier. Cancellations and no-shows are judged by the settings in force when they are recorded, so a later `tutorwell.rules.changed` changes no earlier result. A suspension refuses new bookings only; appointments already booked stay. Weekly counts, no-show counts, `awaiting_outcome` and suspensions are computed when read; reads change nothing.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 10 agent-facing MCP tools, prefixed `tutorwell.`: `services.list`, `tutors.list`, `slots.list`, `appointments.list`, `reports.list`, `profile.get` (read); `appointments.book|update_intake|cancel`, `followups.set_status` (write). Tool results do not carry Core's clock, so every read returns the current virtual time as `as_of`; all times in results are written in the student's time zone with its UTC offset, and `profile.get` also names that time zone (an IANA name). `appointments.list` gives each booked appointment its cancellation cutoff, and a refusal that depends on time states the current time. No agent tool moves the clock. One reviewer renderer in `gui/` has four views (services and rules, slots, appointments with intakes, reports and follow-ups); reviewers are the only GUI audience the reference profile accepts. Infra operations are the runtime's six lifecycle operations and the host's clock operation `env.advance_time`, all kept off the agent connection by Core. Task graders call the App's integrity checker, a pure function that is not an agent tool: its input is a snapshot, and its output, for graders only, lists each appointment's intake with the consents in force when it was written, late cancellations and suspensions with their causes, and each follow-up with when and by whom it was marked done.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** As in the public runtime, state is kept per run and App instance, and only Core commits, resets or snapshots it. Tutorwell's data has three parts: centre data (services, rules, tutors and slots), the student's record (appointments, intakes, reports, follow-ups, accommodations and consents), and the evaluator-only activity log, which only `env.snapshot` returns, never a tool result, error or page. A task loads its own seed, including past appointments and no-shows, and its starting time (`virtual_now`) with `env.load_seed`; `env.reset` restores that seed and time for this App instance only, and `env.snapshot` returns the whole state. Each write takes a `request_id` and an `expected_version` and returns the committed result with the new version; a repeated `request_id` with the same request returns the first result, a reused one with a different request fails with `idempotency_conflict`, and a stale `expected_version` fails with `stale_version`. A grader joins the activity log with Core's audit and the supplied agent execution trace by `request_id` and version; a repeated `request_id` adds no entry, and graders read `env.snapshot` before any reset. Only Infra moves the clock, through Core's `env.apply_event` (the handler sees the event's time as `ctx.virtual_now`) or `env.advance_time`; no outcome is inferred from it (see the centre rules).
- **Events and notifications (required):** Scenario events use the reference format and are applied with `env.apply_event`: `tutorwell.slot.released` (the centre opens a slot), `tutorwell.slot.withdrawn` (the centre cancels a slot; a booked appointment on it becomes `cancelled_by_centre` and does not count against the student), `tutorwell.appointment.attended` (outcome `completed` or `no_show`), `tutorwell.report.posted` (the tutor's session report) and `tutorwell.rules.changed` (the centre changes a setting). Task scenarios decide when they occur. Tutorwell neither publishes nor receives messages. Example input: `{"event_id": "ev-5", "at": "2027-03-10T15:30:00-05:00", "operation": "tutorwell.report.posted", "payload": {"appointment_id": "apt-1", "covered_skill_ids": ["wr-thesis"], "followups": [{"followup_id": "fu-1", "kind": "revise", "item_refs": ["hist-essay-2"], "minutes": 30, "due_at": "2027-03-11T17:00:00-05:00"}], "next_focus": "wr-evidence"}}`, with the effect in E5. ID rules: `slot.released` `{slot_id, tutor_id, start, minutes, mode}` needs a new slot ID (an existing one is `duplicate_id`), a known tutor and a start after the event. `slot.withdrawn` `{slot_id}` needs an existing slot that has not started. `appointment.attended` `{appointment_id, outcome}` needs a booked appointment whose session has started; it is accepted once, however late (see the centre rules). `report.posted` `{appointment_id, covered_skill_ids, followups, next_focus?}` needs a `completed` appointment without a report, known skills and new follow-up IDs. `rules.changed` `{setting, value}` takes one of the five settings. A missing reference is `unknown_reference`, a bad value `invalid_value`, and a slot or appointment in the wrong state `invalid_state`; a refused event changes neither data nor the clock (E5, E7).
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for the student, tutors, slots, appointments and follow-ups. Skill IDs are Tutorwell's own; a task that also loads Milepost uses the same skill IDs in both seeds. `binding.json` maps the student and the tutors to person IDs. Text only, with an empty asset catalog. The package runs on its own with only its files and the pinned shared runtime (release 1.6.0, including Core's virtual time): no other App, no other App's state and no external service. Calendar and Milepost are separate Apps the agent may call, not package dependencies.
