## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Brindle Card is a resettable credit card account, seen from one cardholder's account, in which money that appears on the card is not always money the person keeps: a refund can be partial, the issuer's credit during a dispute is temporary, an offer has conditions, and closing the account loses the points. So a step the account accepts can still be wrong for this person, and a balance that looks settled may not be. The account holds the person's bill, payments, disputes, offer choices and points; the merchants and the issuer's staff act through task-supplied events, and every time-based state follows the environment clock. Four workflows are complete in the first version.

**Entities.** Issuer (time zone and four rules), product (name, annual fee, and the transfer partners with their ratio, smallest amount and step), account (product, credit limit, state, autopay setting), transaction (kind `purchase`, `refund`, `fee`, `interest`, `credit`, `provisional_credit` or `reversal`; a fee is an `annual` fee or another fee; `pending` or `posted`; amount, merchant, its transaction time and, once posted, its posting time; and the transaction, dispute or offer it relates to), statement (closing time, statement balance, minimum due, due time), payment (amount, day, source label, state), dispute (the purchase, the amount, a reason, a note, state and history), offer (kind, terms, the group it belongs to, the time to accept by, state), points entry (earned, redeemed, transferred, corrected or forfeited), notification, account preferences.

**Simplifications.** One App instance holds one cardholder account with one card at one fictional issuer. Merchants and the issuer's staff are not accounts: what they do arrives as events. A purchase arrives already approved: Brindle decides no authorization and has no spend controls. There is no card number, no interest formula and no credit score: interest and fees are amounts the issuer posts. Money does not move: a payment is a record, and the bank account that pays it is a label. A conversation with the issuer's service desk is outside the App: an offer reaches the account as an event. The partner program that receives transferred points is outside the App: a transfer is a record. A day is a calendar day in the issuer's time zone. The issuer's rules are four values: how many days after it posts a purchase can be disputed (`dispute_window_days`), how many days after it posts an annual fee is refunded if the account is closed or moved to a product with no fee (`fee_refund_days`), the value of a point as a statement credit (`redeem_cents_per_point`) and the smallest redemption (`min_redeem_points`).

- **W1 The account, the bill and activity (read-only).** Read the account (`account.get`: product, credit limit, state, current balance, pending total, available credit, the latest statement with what has been paid toward it, the autopay setting, the points balance and the flags); list transactions in a window of transaction time (`transactions.list`: by kind, state or merchant; the list filters and sorts by transaction time only) and read one with everything that relates to it and its recovery summary (`transactions.get`); list and read statements (`statements.list`, `statements.get`); list payments (`payments.list`); list and read disputes (`disputes.list`, `disputes.get`); list and read offers (`offers.list`, `offers.get`); read the points balance, the points entries and the transfer partners (`rewards.get`); preview what closing the account would do now (`account.close_preview`: the points that would be lost, whether an annual fee would be refunded and until when, the offers that would end, the accepted offers whose bonus points have not arrived, and what stays open); list notifications (`notifications.list`); read the account's preferences (`preferences.get`). What the issuer and the merchants post is taken as given, and the flags say where it does not add up. Paths: modify, cancel and confirm are not applicable to reads; errors = unknown object (`not_found`), `invalid_window`.
- **W2 Payments, autopay and preferences.** Schedule a payment for today or a later day, for a fixed amount, the minimum due, the statement balance or the current balance, from a named source (`payments.schedule`); cancel it (`payments.cancel`); set autopay to off, the minimum due or the statement balance (`autopay.set`); set the account's preferences (`preferences.update`: the time zone used in notification text, and `email_notices`). Paths: modify = not offered for a payment, because a payment records what was asked: cancel it and schedule another; `autopay.set` and `preferences.update` can be called again; cancel = `payments.cancel` before the start of the payment's day; errors = an unknown time zone (`invalid_timezone`), a day before today (`invalid_window`), an amount above the current balance minus the payments I have already scheduled, autopay payments left out (`over_balance`), the minimum due or the statement balance asked for when there is no statement (`no_statement`) or when it is already paid (`nothing_due`), a cancellation once the payment's day has come (`too_late`), a cancellation of a payment that is already cancelled or returned, or `autopay.set` on a closed account (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `account.get` shows the bill and what each choice would pay; the scheduling is the commitment.
- **W3 Refunds, disputes and temporary credits.** A merchant's refund posts as a transaction that relates to the purchase. Dispute a posted purchase for an amount, with a reason and a note (`disputes.open`); withdraw the dispute (`disputes.withdraw`). The issuer's temporary credit, its reversal and the end of the case arrive as events. Paths: modify = not offered, because a dispute records what was claimed: withdraw it and open another; cancel = `disputes.withdraw` while the dispute is `open`; errors = a transaction that is pending, is not a purchase or is already refunded in full (`not_disputable`), an amount above what the purchase still costs after its final refunds (`over_net`), a purchase that has an open dispute or a dispute the issuer has ended (`already_disputed`), a dispute later than `dispute_window_days` after the purchase's posting time (`too_late`), a dispute that is not `open` (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `transactions.get` shows what has been refunded for good, what is temporary and what the purchase still costs; opening the dispute is the commitment.
- **W4 The annual fee, offers, points and closing.** The annual fee posts as a transaction. An offer from the issuer arrives as an event: a statement credit, a credit after a spending target, bonus points, or a change to another product. Accept or decline it (`offers.accept`, `offers.decline`). Redeem points as a statement credit (`rewards.redeem`); transfer points to a partner program (`rewards.transfer`); close the account (`account.close`). Paths: modify = not offered: an accepted offer, a redemption, a transfer and a closing cannot be changed; cancel = not offered: none of them can be undone; errors = an offer whose time to accept has passed (`too_late`), an offer that is not `available`, or any of these writes on a closed account (`invalid_state`), an unknown partner (`not_found`), more points than the balance (`insufficient_points`), fewer points than the smallest amount (`below_minimum`), an amount that is not a whole number of steps (`invalid_increment`). Confirm: `offers.get` shows an offer's full terms, `rewards.get` shows the balance and each partner's rule, and `account.close_preview` shows what a closing would do; the write is the commitment.

Rules:

- Time is the environment's. Brindle has no timers, no scheduler and no clock event: Infra moves `virtual_now` with an event or with Core's `env.advance_time`, which runs no App code. Every time-based state is therefore stored as a due time and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever it is read or checked: a payment's `posted` from its day, an offer's `expired` from its `accept_by`, the end of a dispute window and of a fee refund window, a spending target's deadline, and whether a statement's due time has passed. Writes and events use the same derivation: an expired offer cannot be accepted, and a payment that has posted cannot be cancelled. An offer counts as `available` only before its `accept_by`, for every rule, the validation of the seed and of events included. Nothing is rewritten when a due time passes.
- A limit of N days after a moment ends at the end of the calendar day that is N days after the day of that moment, in the issuer's time zone: this holds for `dispute_window_days`, `fee_refund_days` and an offer's spending window. A payment for a day posts at the start of that day; a payment scheduled for today posts at once. A transaction has two times: its transaction time, which lists use, and its posting time, from which the dispute window and the fee refund window count.
- The balance is computed when read: the posted purchases, fees and interest and the reversals, minus the posted refunds, credits and temporary credits, minus the payments that have posted and were not returned. `pending_total` is the sum of the pending purchases. `available_credit` is the credit limit minus the balance and `pending_total`. A pending transaction is in no balance.
- A statement is the issuer's fact. `paid_toward` it is the sum of the payments posted after its closing time and not returned. When the due time has passed and the balance is above zero, the account reads `past_due` if `paid_toward` is below the minimum due, and `statement_unpaid` if it reaches the minimum and is below the statement balance.
- A payment's amount is fixed when it is scheduled: the fixed amount, the current balance, or the part of the minimum due or of the statement balance that `paid_toward` has not covered at that moment. A payment I schedule may not take the total of my scheduled payments above the current balance; autopay payments are left out of that total, and the autopay payment that the handler schedules is not held to it. When a statement is issued while autopay is on, the handler schedules one autopay payment for the day of the statement's due time, for the minimum due or the statement balance of that statement; turning autopay on later schedules nothing for a statement that is already issued. An autopay payment posts for its amount minus what other payments paid toward that statement before the start of its day, and it reads `skipped` if that leaves nothing. That amount is fixed by what had posted before the start of the day: a payment on the same day, or a return after that day, does not change it. A manual payment is never reduced.
- The recovery summary of a purchase is computed when read from the transactions that relate to it. For each dispute, its outstanding credit is its temporary credits minus its reversals, never below zero. `refunded_final` is the merchant's posted refunds plus the outstanding credit of a dispute that ended `resolved_for_me`; `temporary_credit` is the outstanding credit of every other dispute, so the credit of a withdrawn dispute stays temporary until the issuer reverses it; `net_charge` is the purchase amount minus `refunded_final`, and it does not count `temporary_credit`. A dispute can ask for at most `net_charge`.
- The issuer and the merchants are not checked. A posted transaction, a statement, a returned payment, an update to a dispute and an offer are applied as given; only the structure of the data is validated (§3). A purchase can therefore be credited twice for a time, and a flag says so.
- A purchase has at most one open dispute. A dispute ends in one of four ways: I withdraw it; the issuer decides for me, and its temporary credit stays; the issuer decides for the merchant, and it reverses the temporary credit; or the merchant refunds the purchase and the issuer closes the case as matched and reverses the temporary credit. Each reversal and each temporary credit is a posted transaction that relates to the purchase and names the dispute. A temporary credit arrives only with `dispute.updated`. A reversal arrives with `dispute.updated` while the dispute is open and with `transaction.posted` after it has ended; any other path is rejected. A purchase can be disputed again after a withdrawal, and not after the issuer has ended a dispute on it.
- Offers that share a group exclude each other: accepting one makes the other available offers of the group `unavailable`. An offer is accepted once. An accepted statement credit or bonus is paid by the issuer, as a later transaction or points entry that names the offer; Brindle posts nothing itself. An accepted spending offer shows its progress, computed when read: the purchases posted from the acceptance to the deadline, minus the refunds of those purchases that posted by the deadline, against the target. Its condition is `open` before the deadline while the target is not reached, `met` while it is reached, and after the deadline it stays `met` or `missed` by the progress at the deadline; a later refund changes nothing. An accepted product change moves the account to the new product at once.
- `rewards.redeem` takes points off the balance and posts a credit of that many points times `redeem_cents_per_point` at once. `rewards.transfer` takes the points off the balance and records the partner, the member name given and the points the partner will receive by its ratio; it cannot be undone and Brindle does not follow the points further. Points are added or corrected by the issuer through events.
- `account.close` cannot be undone. It forfeits the whole points balance with one points entry, makes available offers `unavailable`, ends the spending offers in progress as `missed`, turns autopay off and leaves scheduled payments in place. It records whether an annual fee is to be refunded: the latest fee of the type `annual`, if the closing is within `fee_refund_days` of its posting time. The same refund rule applies to an accepted change to a product with no annual fee. A fee's refund is recorded as due once: a closing after such a product change records nothing more. Bonus points of an accepted offer that the issuer has not yet added are lost with the closing, because a closed account takes no points; `account.close_preview` lists such offers. A credit the issuer still owes can post after the closing. The refund itself is posted by the issuer, as a `credit` transaction that relates to the fee. After a closing, a spending condition that is not yet met reads `missed`. After the closing, payments and disputes go on as before, and offers, points and autopay accept no write.
- Flags are computed when read and never stored. On the account: `past_due`, `statement_unpaid`, `payment_returned` (a payment toward the latest statement was returned), `credit_balance` (the balance is below zero), `fee_refund_open` (closing today would refund an annual fee). On a purchase: `disputed` (it has an open dispute), `temporary_credit` (its `temporary_credit` is above zero), `double_credited` (`refunded_final` plus `temporary_credit` is more than the purchase amount). On an accepted spending offer: `condition_met`, `condition_missed`.
- Notifications are written for what the issuer and the merchants do, never for my own writes and never for a change that time alone causes (a payment that posts on its day, an offer that expires and a due time that passes write none). One is written for each of these events: a posted refund, fee, interest, credit, temporary credit or reversal (`transaction.posted`; a purchase writes none), `statement.issued`, `payment.returned`, `dispute.updated`, `offer.issued` and `points.adjusted`. A notification is listed from the moment it is written.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** "I see the full amount back on my card. Are we done?" has different true answers on different days, and the account summary alone does not tell them apart. Twenty of sixty came back from the merchant for good, forty is the issuer's temporary credit, and when the merchant pays the rest the card shows more than the purchase until the issuer takes its credit back. An offer of a credit "after you spend 3,000" is not a credit. Closing a card inside the refund window gets the fee back and loses twelve thousand points. A payment of the minimum keeps the account current and leaves the bill unpaid. A pending hotel hold is not a charge yet and cannot be disputed. Because each credit says what it is and which purchase it belongs to, an acceptance, a transfer and a closing cannot be undone, and every window has an end, the order and the timing of what the agent does are part of the answer. Brindle makes these checkable: which transactions, statements, offers and summaries the agent read, which payments it scheduled or cancelled and for what, which disputes it opened, for which amount and when, which offers it accepted or declined, what it redeemed or transferred, and whether it closed the account. Distinct task situations (separate Task proposals): (1) a merchant refunds part of a charge and promises the rest, and the agent must dispute only the unpaid part, at the time the person's standing instruction allows, and must tell a temporary credit from a final one (Task #107 meets this problem and plans task-owned merchant and issuer fixtures for it); (2) the annual fee has posted, the issuer makes three offers, and the person's earlier words allow one of them, forbid a spending target and reserve any closing or change of product (Task #133 meets this problem and plans a task-owned service desk and issuer ledger); (3) the bill is due tomorrow, autopay pays only the minimum, last month's payment was returned, and the person has said when to pay in full; (4) a transfer of points to an airline is allowed by the account and cannot be undone, and the person wanted part of the points kept for another trip; (5) a pending hold is larger than the price the person agreed to, and the right step is to wait until it posts. No Task proposal depends on Brindle, and both tasks can keep their own fixtures; the rationale is independent of any one task. Fixtures are plain seed data, so each task loads its own issuer rules, products, account, statements, transactions, payments, disputes, offers and points.

**Boundaries and overlap.** Proposal list checked on 2026-11-22. #87 (Keelcard) is the nearest, and the two share a part: both have card transactions with pending and settled amounts, refunds tied to a transaction, disputes and a monthly statement, and Brindle uses the same error names where the case is the same (`not_disputable`, `already_disputed`). The difference is the kind of account. Keelcard is a funded virtual card that a person shares with an assistant: it decides each authorization from the owner's spend controls, and its scope leaves out "credit lines and interest, rewards". Brindle is a revolving credit account: a bill with a minimum and a due time, payments and autopay, the issuer's temporary credit during a dispute and its reversal, the annual fee, the issuer's offers, points and closing. Brindle decides no authorization and has no spend controls, cards or delegates. A task can use both: Keelcard for the card an agent pays with, and Brindle for the account the person is billed on. #6 (Personal Finance Workspace) is a bank and brokerage; in Brindle the bank account that pays the card is only a label. #68 (PalePay) moves money between people. #20 (Subscription Exit) cancels one subscription at the merchant. #64 (Larkfield Health) and #46 (Coverline) are insurance. The points side stops at the transfer: award search and booking are outside the App. A message to a merchant or to the issuer's service desk goes through the person's own Apps or a task's own counterpart, not through Brindle.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 25 Agent-facing MCP operations over stdio on the Agent connection of the public host (`node runtime/cli.cjs serve --app apps/brindle --role agent`), registered in `tools.json` as `brindle.<name>`. Read (14): `account.get`, `account.close_preview`, `transactions.list`, `transactions.get`, `statements.list`, `statements.get`, `payments.list`, `disputes.list`, `disputes.get`, `offers.list`, `offers.get`, `rewards.get`, `notifications.list`, `preferences.get`. Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version` and returns the object's `id` with the committed version. The target object is always the argument `id` and a time window is always `start` / `end`. For an operation that creates an object or acts on the account, `id` is the object it is about: the account for `payments.schedule`, `autopay.set`, `rewards.redeem`, `rewards.transfer` and `account.close`, and the purchase for `disputes.open`. The reference host's audit projection at `61b6371` keeps `id`, `start` and `end` among the few inputs it records, so its audit rows show the object and the window of every call that has them, reads included. The Agent acts as the bound account holder set by Infra, never through an argument; a task can restrict the allowed tools with `policies.json`. App errors: `not_found`, `invalid_window`, `invalid_state`, `invalid_timezone`, `too_late`, `over_balance`, `no_statement`, `nothing_due`, `not_disputable`, `over_net`, `already_disputed`, `insufficient_points`, `below_minimum`, `invalid_increment`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`; Brindle also answers `invalid_arguments` for what an input schema cannot express (an amount or a number of points of zero or less, a fixed payment with no amount, a transfer with no member name). Infra-only operations: `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle`, and the host's clock operation `env.advance_time` of runtime 1.6.0; they are not listed on the Agent connection and Core returns `permission_denied` if the Agent calls them. The first version has no GUI, no reviewer console and no console for the issuer or a merchant: other parties act only through events.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). The seed schema carries issuer records (time zone, rules, products with their transfer partners) and account records (product, credit limit, state, autopay setting; statements; transactions with what they relate to; payments; disputes with their history; offers; points entries; preferences, notifications, activity rows), so a task can load any history, including many past statements, a purchase with a partial refund, a dispute in every state, an account that is past due, an offer that has expired and an account that is already closed. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, a refund, temporary credit or reversal that relates to no purchase, a temporary credit or reversal that names no dispute, two open disputes on one purchase, a dispute opened on a purchase after the issuer ended an earlier one, a dispute on a transaction that is not a posted purchase, two enrolled offers in one group, a points balance below zero, a payment with an amount of zero or less, a statement whose due time is before its closing time, timestamps without a UTC offset and an account that the binding does not declare (`invalid_seed`). The seed is history: everything in it has already happened, and new facts arrive only through events. Writes follow Core: an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` or `event_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`, so a repeated request schedules no second payment and opens no second dispute; the same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`; a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`; a failed call leaves data, version and notifications unchanged and Core appends a denial audit row. Reads never change business data: the balance, payment states, the recovery summary, offer progress, windows and flags are computed from stored records and `virtual_now`, and `account.close_preview` writes nothing. Reset is Core's: it restores the loaded seed and the time loaded with it (`reset_scope: app_instance`) and touches no other App; messages already delivered to Mail stay there. Evaluator records come from three places: (a) App state in the snapshot: activity rows with sequence, virtual time, actor `agent` or `event`, action, object ID and the `request_id` or `event_id` behind them, one for every committed Agent write, one for every applied event and one for each effect of that event (an autopay payment scheduled, a dispute ended, a notification written); every transaction with its time and what it relates to; each payment with its amount, its day and its source label; each dispute's history; each offer with its answer and the time; each points entry; notifications with their creation time; (b) Core audit rows with virtual time and the projected `id`, `start` and `end`: one for every allowed call that is not a replay, one for every denied call, and one for every `env.advance_time` with the clock before and after; (c) full arguments and results in the supplied Agent execution trace. They join on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Counting rules: count committed writes and event effects from the activity rows, one row each. Core returns the stored receipt for a repeated `request_id` or `event_id` before it appends an audit row, so a replay appears only in the trace, as a call whose result carries `duplicate: true`: count it as a retry of the original, never as a new action. A rejected attempt writes no activity row: count rejected attempts from the denial audit rows and the trace, one for each attempt, repeats included. Count reads from the allowed audit rows and the trace. Time-driven changes (a payment posted or skipped, an offer expired, a window closed, a statement past due) run no App code and write no row: read them from the stored due times and the clock, and from the audit row of the `env.advance_time` call or the event that moved it. Take the snapshot before a reset, because reset clears the audit.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Seven events through `env.apply_event`, in Core's envelope. Brindle has no clock event: Infra moves time with an event or with `env.advance_time`. An event is handled at its own time T, which is `ctx.virtual_now` inside the handler in runtime 1.6.0. Core rejects an event dated before the App's current time (`invalid_event`), and it validates the data after every event with the same rules as the seed; Brindle also rejects a duplicate ID (a `posted` event for a transaction that is `pending` is the one case that reuses an ID), an unknown transaction, payment, dispute, offer or product, a posted transaction with an amount of zero or less, a refund, temporary credit or reversal that relates to no purchase, a temporary credit or reversal that names no dispute, a temporary credit sent with `transaction.posted`, a reversal sent with `transaction.posted` for a dispute that is still open, a drop of a transaction that is not pending, a returned payment that has not posted, an update to a dispute that is not `open`, a statement whose due time is before its closing time, an offer issued to a closed account or into a group that already has an enrolled offer, and a points change that would take the balance below zero or that adds points to a closed account (`invalid_event`, state unchanged).

- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for products, the account, statements, transactions, payments, disputes, offers, points entries and notifications. The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` are the shared values of the run's `binding.json`, so the person has one ID in Brindle and Mail, and notifications address the recipient by `person_ref`. A merchant, a bank account and a partner program are names on records, not accounts. Brindle is the source of truth for its bill, transactions, payments, disputes, offers and points and keeps no copy of another App's records. Missing data is explicit: an unknown ID returns `not_found`; an account with no statement has `latest_statement: null`, and reads no `past_due`; a purchase with nothing related to it has a `refunded_final` and a `temporary_credit` of zero; a product with no annual fee has `annual_fee: null` and no fee refund; a product with no transfer partners has an empty list; an offer with no group excludes no other offer. All records are JSON; there are no images, audio, video or attachments (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the profile's 64 assets). Runtime needs: the public Core/Messaging runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` commit `61b6371`; Mail is optional; `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; no external service. The package runs on its own: a copy that holds only `apps/brindle/` and the pinned runtime starts the App, and its business tests and example run from the package alone. It imports and reads nothing outside its own folder and uses no other App's files or state; the two real-MCP scripts are given the location of the pinned runtime, and the Mail flow also needs the pinned Mail package. Days use IANA time zones through Node's built-in `Intl`, so no package is installed. Amounts are numbers with two decimals in one currency, kept as integer cents inside the App; points are whole numbers.
