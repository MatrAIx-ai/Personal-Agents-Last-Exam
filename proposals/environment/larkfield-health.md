## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable member portal for one employer health plan, where a household reads claims and explanations of benefits (EOBs), answers the plan's information requests, follows prior authorizations (PAs), and files, supports or withdraws appeals within their deadlines. The App records facts, statuses, amounts and consequences; it does not decide medical necessity, rank options or say what the right action is. No third-party code, brand assets, real member data or proprietary billing codes; services use plain synthetic descriptions.

**Simulated policies and simplifications.** Time follows the environment clock (`ctx.virtual_now`), which Infra keeps in step across the enabled Apps, in one time zone (America/New_York); the local date of a period's starting event is day 0, and the 3-day review, the 45-day request expiry, the 15-, 30- and 60-day due dates and the 180-day filing window each end at 23:59 on that date plus N calendar days; the window-closing notice falls due at 23:59 on the date 14 calendar days before the window's last day; a PA's validity ends at 23:59 on its `valid_until` date; the 72-hour expedite periods count elapsed hours from the request or filing; money is USD in integer cents; accumulators are kept per plan year (January 1 to December 31) by service date.

- One EPO-style plan: in-network care only, except emergency care, which is covered at in-network cost sharing and never needs PA. Embedded deductibles and out-of-pocket maximums (a member stops at the individual amount; the family stops at the family amount), 20% coinsurance, copays by service category; copays count toward the out-of-pocket maximum, not the deductible. Pharmacy claims belong to Mendwell Pharmacy (#63); Larkfield records pharmacy PAs and the plan's decisions on them.
- Each claim is reviewed at its 3-day cutoff. Adjudication is deterministic and runs then or, if that review opens information requests, once they are all answered or expired (below), line by line in a stable order (service date, then the line's one-based position in `lines`): non-emergency out-of-network → `out_of_network`; a PA-listed non-emergency service needs a PA for the same member and service, in `approved` or `expired` status, whose validity covers the service date and whose remaining units cover the whole line, otherwise `prior_auth_missing` with no units used; among several qualifying PAs, units come from the one with the earliest `valid_until`, then the lowest PA ID; the allowed amount is the lesser of billed and fee-schedule amounts, split into copay, or deductible then coinsurance, within the out-of-pocket limits; member coinsurance is rounded to the nearest cent, half up, before the out-of-pocket cap; each paid PA-covered line uses its units.
- Information requests open at the 3-day review and are typed: `other_coverage` on every line when the member's other-coverage status for that plan year is `unknown`, answered only by `coverage.cob.confirm` (`none`, `other_secondary` or `other_primary`); and `accident` on each line that `claim.received` flags `possible_accident`, answered only by `claims.info.confirm` (`not_accident`, `accident` or `work_related`). Opening a request emits `notice.info_requested`. An attestation sets the member's status for that plan year; while it is `other_primary`, later lines are denied `other_party_liable` without a new request. `other_primary` and `work_related` deny the affected lines `other_party_liable`, because two-plan and third-party payment are out of scope; the other answers adjudicate normally. Attestations are per member and plan year: one resolves, in received order, only that member's open other-coverage requests for lines whose service dates fall in that plan year; `coverage.cob.preview` with no open request for that member and year returns `info_not_requested` and no token, as does a confirm that reaches the state check. A claim stays `pending_info` until each request on it is answered or expires 45 days after its notice; expiry denies only the unresolved lines `missing_information` and keeps any `other_party_liable` denial an answer already set. The claim is then adjudicated in one pass, against the accumulators and PA units at that moment; request outcomes apply first, and a line they deny is not checked further. EOB v1 is issued once, when nothing is open.
- Deadlines are app policy modelled on US employer-plan rules: appeals are filed within 180 days of the denial notice; PA decisions are due 15 days after submission; appeal decisions are due 30 days (PA) or 60 days (claim) after filing; an approved expedite moves the due date to the earlier of the existing one and 72 hours after the expedite request. Due dates never pause, a simplification. A missed due date sets `overdue` once, including when an approved expedite moves the due date into the past; the App never decides a PA or an appeal itself.
- Only denied lines are appealable, a simplification (real plans also accept appeals of amounts). An overturned claim appeal re-prices only the lines it names, against the accumulators at that moment and without using PA units; every other line keeps its previous outcome and amounts, and other claims are never re-adjudicated. Each adult's records are private to that adult, which is stricter than the common practice of sending EOBs to the subscriber, so delegation is observable.

**Workflows.**

1. **Household access.** Only an adult member (for their own records) or a seeded guardian of a minor grants or revokes member-wide `view` or `act` (authorized representative) access, with an optional expiry, after a user confirmation. Grants made in another member's session arrive as `grant.changed` events.
2. **Claims and EOBs.** Read claims, lines, versioned EOBs and the caller's permitted accumulators. Answer information requests through `coverage.cob.preview` / `.confirm` and `claims.info.preview` / `.confirm`; both are attestations that need a user confirmation.
3. **Prior authorization.** Providers and pharmacies submit, supplement or withdraw PAs through events; only the plan decides them, from `pending` or `info_requested`. Approvals carry validity dates and units. A member may ask once for expedited review while a PA is undecided; the plan answers through an event.
4. **Appeals.** File against a denied claim (its denied lines) or a denied PA, with a statement; add statements while the appeal is open; withdraw. There is one internal appeal level: once an appeal on a target is `upheld` or `overturned`, filing on it again returns `not_appealable`; a withdrawn appeal may be filed again within the original window. Overturning a claim appeal overrides the lines named in `appeal.decided`; overturning a PA appeal approves the PA with the units and `valid_until` carried by `appeal.decided`, valid from the decision. An expedited PA appeal is due 72 hours after filing, with no separate plan approval step. Approving a PA never re-adjudicates processed claims.

Rules:

- Filing requires authority, a denied target, a date within 180 days of that denial's notice, no open appeal on the target (`duplicate_request`) and no decided appeal on it (`not_appealable`). Expedited filing is allowed only for a denied PA, with an urgency attestation. Checks run in this order and return the first failure: authority (`not_authorized`), an appealable target (`not_appealable`), no open appeal (`duplicate_request`), the window (`appeal_window_closed`), expedite eligibility (`expedite_not_allowed`).
- Re-adjudication creates a new EOB version, posts the accumulator difference and keeps every earlier version; the seed's accumulators must equal the sum of current EOB versions.
- An expedite request is allowed once per PA while it is `pending` or `info_requested`; a second request on such a PA returns `duplicate_request`, and a PA in any other state returns `expedite_not_allowed`. A `pa.submitted` whose dates and units an approved PA fully covers creates no new PA; it adds a `covered_submission` entry, visible in `pa.get`, to the covering PA, with no notice.
- Appeals in `upheld`, `overturned` or `withdrawn` accept no Agent writes; once authority is established, `appeals.add_statement` and a new `appeals.withdraw.preview` return `invalid_state`; a withdrawal confirm follows the check order in §3, so it can stop earlier at authority, approval or freshness; records stay readable.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** Insurance work for a family depends on whose records an agent may see, which denial is still appealable, which PA covers which date, and what the EOB actually charged. The App exposes reusable behavior, not one task's answer. Examples (separate Task proposals): appeal a child's denied imaging claim before the window closes, citing a PA approved for different dates; reconcile a spouse's bill without seeing his records until he grants access.

**Boundaries.** The Northlake Patient Portal (#14) shows provider bills and defers insurer adjudication, which Larkfield owns; in v1, claims arrive as task-supplied `claim.received` events, so no Northlake interface is needed. A link to Mendwell Pharmacy (#63, same author), mirroring prior-authorization requests and decisions between the two Apps, is left for a later version; in this version pharmacy PAs arrive as task-supplied `pa.submitted` events: `service_id` names a drug that the seed lists as a service, `provider_id` a seeded pharmacy or prescriber, and `units` a count the task sets, which Larkfield records and shows in `pa.get` (pharmacy claims, which would draw on them, are outside this App). The Northlake link is optional. Write (7): `grants.change.confirm`, `coverage.cob.confirm`, `claims.info.confirm`, `pa.request_expedite`, `appeals.file.confirm`, `appeals.add_statement`, `appeals.withdraw.confirm`. The Agent acts as the bound account holder set by Infra, never through an argument.

  Unknown IDs return `not_found`; records of a member the caller may not see return `not_authorized`. Every read, list and notice is filtered by member: `members.list`, `grants.list` and `plan.get` show only members the caller may see. `plan.get` returns family accumulator totals only if the caller may view every member enrolled in the family for that plan year, including minors; otherwise it returns only the permitted individual totals. Previews store nothing. `confirmation_invalid` means the approval is expired, already used, or from someone other than the required confirmer; restated arguments or a target version that no longer reproduce the token return `stale_preview`. A preview runs the same authority and state checks as its confirm and returns that error instead of a token; otherwise the token is a digest of the actor, the action, its full arguments, the run ID, the loaded seed's `seed_id` and the target object's own version (an App-maintained counter that changes only when that record changes, so unrelated writes and the approval event itself do not invalidate it). The confirm carries the token, restates the arguments and Core's current `expected_version` like any write; the App finds the approval by the token and compares it with a digest recomputed from current state (targets: the grant, identified by member and grantee, the member's other-coverage record, the information request, the appealed claim or PA, and the appeal). Each of the five confirms needs a `user.confirmed` event naming the token from the bound account holder; it is single-use, expires after 30 simulated minutes, is consumed only by a successful commit. Confirms check authority, then the approval, then preview freshness, then state, and return the first failure. `pa.request_expedite` and `appeals.add_statement` are not gated because they change no coverage; the plan decides. Infra-only operations: `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, reset and snapshot, plus the host's `env.advance_time` on runtime 1.6.0; Core returns `permission_denied` when the Agent calls them. App errors: `not_authorized`, `confirmation_required`, `confirmation_invalid`, `stale_preview`, `duplicate_request`, `appeal_window_closed`, `not_appealable`, `expedite_not_allowed`, `info_not_requested`, `invalid_state`, `not_found`; Core adds `idempotency_conflict`, `stale_version`, `permission_denied`, `invalid_arguments` for input that fails a tool or event schema, and `invalid_event` for an event it cannot apply, such as a fresh one dated before the App's current time.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is per run and account, loaded by `env.load_seed` and validated (unique IDs, grants consistent with members and guardians, accumulators equal to the sum of current EOB versions). Each distinct seed has a required, unique `seed_id`, which preview tokens include; a token whose seed ID differs from the loaded seed's fails the freshness check, and reset restores the loaded seed ID. Within an epoch, Core returns the stored receipt (marked duplicate) for an exact repeat of a committed request or event, and `idempotency_conflict` for the same ID with changed input; a new write or event (a fresh ID) with an old epoch or revision, including one from before a reset, gets `stale_version`. A failed Agent call preserves business data, version and notices; Core appends a denial audit entry. Reset is Core's new epoch: Core restores the loaded seed and the virtual time loaded with it (the task's `virtual_now` in `env.load_seed`); approvals created during the run are discarded (seeds contain none), and timers, being derived from deadlines in App data, return to the seed's. Reset covers this App instance only; notices already delivered to Mail stay there.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Events use Core's envelope via `env.apply_event`: `claim.received`, `pa.submitted`, `pa.info_requested`, `pa.info_provided`, `pa.decided`, `pa.withdrawn`, `pa.expedite_decided`, `appeal.info_requested`, `appeal.decided`, `grant.changed`, `user.confirmed`. Time follows the environment clock, `ctx.virtual_now`: Core sets it at seed load and reset, Infra moves it forward with `env.advance_time` (which calls no App business handler), and an event's `at` sets it while that event is applied. Larkfield defines no clock event and keeps no separate scheduler: each timer (the 3-day claim review, the 45-day request expiry, the end of a PA's validity, the PA and appeal decision due dates that set `overdue`, and the window-closing notice) is a due time stored on its record, and an approved expedite only rewrites a stored due time; if the rewritten due time is at or before that event's `at` and the PA is not already `overdue` once the earlier due timers have run, the `overdue` flag and one `notice.decision_overdue` are effects of the `pa.expedite_decided` event itself, dated at its `at` and addressed by the grants in force then, while a PA that was already overdue keeps its original flag time and notice entries. Likewise, a `pa.decided` approval, or a PA appeal overturned by `appeal.decided`, whose validity cutoff is at or before the event's `at` records the approval and the immediate move to `expired` at that `at`, keeping the approved dates and units. When Core invokes a write or event handler, Larkfield first applies all timers due at or before `ctx.virtual_now`, in a stable order (due time, then object ID, then timer kind), including timers that those timers start, and then applies the action; Core commits the timer effects and the action together. A timer and an action at the same instant resolve in the timer's favour, so a filing at 23:59 on a window's last day, or an answer at the instant its request expires, is too late. If the call then fails, through an App error or a Core rejection, no business data, version or notice is stored (Core keeps only its denial audit row): the timer effects roll back with it, and the next fresh write or event that succeeds persists them. An exact replay of a committed request returns its stored receipt without reaching the handler, so it persists nothing new. Reads and previews compute the same timer effects in memory and report the resulting statuses, EOBs, accumulators, due dates and `overdue` flags without storing them. A seed whose timers are already due at its `virtual_now` supplies its records, including the grants in force, as of immediately before the earliest of those timers, and is caught up from there in the same way. A timer takes effect as of its due time: the record's timeline, any EOB and accumulator posting and any notice it causes carry that time, and the notice is written when the timer is committed, so the committed timer effects match what reads projected at that instant, before the action's own effects. A late event skips its own action: PA events apply only to a PA in a matching state, appeal events only to an open appeal; it is still committed: the clock moves to its `at` (which may equal the current time), and timers due by then are applied before its action, committed with it and may change records. For a schema-valid event with an unseen ID and the current version, Core rejects an `at` earlier than the App's current time as `invalid_event`. Notices, each emitted once per occurrence: `notice.eob_available`, `notice.info_requested`, `notice.claim_denied`, `notice.pa_decision`, `notice.appeal_received`, `notice.appeal_info_requested`, `notice.appeal_decision`, `notice.appeal_window_closing` (14 days before the deadline, only while the target is still appealable), `notice.decision_overdue`. `notice.eob_available` is written for every EOB version; `notice.claim_denied` is written once, with EOB v1, when at least one line is denied, and its date (or that of a denying `notice.pa_decision`) starts the 180-day window whether or not Mail is connected. Recipients are the member, guardians, and `view` or `act` holders for that member; for a timer notice they follow the grants in force at its due time (an expiry is compared with the due time, and no later grant change has been applied yet, because every write and event first applies due timers). Each notice is one log entry per recipient; seeded notice entries are history, and the adapter publishes only entries appended in the current epoch. An Infra-run sender adapter reads the log, rechecks each recipient's access at dispatch and calls `router.publish` for those who have a Mail account bound in the run and an Infra Messaging grant for `channel.email`, using one router session, with its own grant, per recipient Mail account, because a session binds one receiving account; other entries stay in the log. The adapter is package code that Infra runs; it reads the notice log through `env.snapshot`. A delivery rejected before dispatch (for example `incompatible_contract`) creates no Mail record; a rejection by Mail or an unknown acknowledgement halts the router for reconciliation; none of these rolls back Larkfield. Member IDs (`m-1`… in the examples) are the shared `person_id` values of the run's `binding.json`, so a person has the same ID in Larkfield and Mail; the seed's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` name the bound account holder, and notices address recipients by `person_ref`. Larkfield is the source of truth for its claims, EOBs, PAs, appeals, grants and accumulators and keeps no copy of another App's records. A seed with a missing required member, provider, service or fee-schedule reference is rejected as `invalid_seed`; unknown IDs in Agent calls return `not_found`. Notices are plain text, without attachments. Runtime needs: the public Core/Messaging runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` commit `61b6371`, including Infra's `env.advance_time`; Mail and #14 are optional; no external service. The App package runs on its own with only its own files and the shared runtime: `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; business code and business tests import and read nothing outside the package; only the launcher `service.cjs`, the real-MCP script and the sender adapter load the shared runtime, all through `PALE_RUNTIME_ROOT`. The Mail wire schema is a package-local copy of the generated `runtime/contracts/schemas/router-message.schema.json` at `61b6371`; `node runtime/build-contracts.cjs` writes that schema only to its fixed paths, none under `apps/`, so the package's real-MCP script, which runs with the pinned runtime and is not a business test, compares the two; on an otherwise valid publish, Messaging rejects a schema whose canonical digest differs as `incompatible_contract`. The optional Mail flow is a separate integration check run with the pinned Mail package.
