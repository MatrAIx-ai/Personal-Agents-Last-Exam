## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Ridgelog lets a personal agent decide whether a hike is still a good idea and keep the paperwork for it in order. Conditions on a trail come from two kinds of evidence that often disagree. Official alerts are versioned: a closure is issued, updated with a detour, then lifted. Trip reports from other hikers carry two dates, the day they hiked and the day they posted, and describe one stretch of trail rather than the whole route. Ridgelog exposes both, segment by segment, so the agent can tell the user what is true for their route on their date and say where that comes from. The same account holds the household's saved lists, planned hikes and permits. Permits have quotas, group-size caps, alternate leaders, holds, lotteries and refund windows, and a closure can turn a confirmed permit into one that needs a decision.

### Three example requests

The seeded household is Maya (account holder), her partner Theo and their teenager Ivy. Maya's brother Arjun has his own Ridgelog account. All places, people, alerts and fees are fictional.

| User request | What the user and agent encounter | What a reviewer can check |
| --- | --- | --- |

### Workflows

| Workflow | Entities and transitions | Confirm, modify, cancel and error paths |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W1 Areas and trails | Area (managing agency, time zone), trail, segment (from and to junction, distance, elevation gain, high point, aspect), trailhead (access road and parking status), water source and permit rule (which zones and dates need which permit type). | Read-only. An unknown ID returns `not_found`. A trail whose area is closed stays readable and is marked closed. |
| W2 Conditions | An alert has a type (`closure`, `detour`, `advisory`, `fire_restriction` or `road`), the segments, trailheads or zones it covers, an effective window and versions. Its status is `scheduled` → `active` → `lifted` or `expired`, and each update adds a version. A trip report records author, `hiked_on`, `posted_at`, route segments and per-segment condition tags (snow, ice, mud, blowdown, water flowing or dry, bugs, crowding). | `alerts.list` returns the current version by default and earlier versions on request. The agent can post, edit and delete trip reports for the bound member only. A `hiked_on` date in the future or before the trail opened returns `invalid_date`. Editing another member's report returns `forbidden`. |
| W3 Lists and planned hikes | A saved list is a versioned, ordered set of trails. A planned hike has a date, start time, route segments, party (by `person_id`) and turnaround time. Its status is `planned` → `cancelled`, or `past` once its date has passed. | A list is replaced as a whole with `expected_version`. A planned route whose segments do not connect returns `invalid_route`. A planned hike on a closed segment is saved and carries a warning listing the alert, so the agent sees the conflict without being blocked. |
| W4 Permits and lotteries | A permit type is a day-use timed entry (slots with a quota) or an overnight zone permit (a nightly quota and a per-party cap). A permit goes `held` → `confirmed` → `modified` → `cancelled`, `affected` or `past`. A hold lasts 15 minutes of virtual time. A lottery application goes `submitted` → `awarded` or `unsuccessful`. An award goes `accepted` (which creates a confirmed permit), `declined` or `lapsed` after its deadline. Each permit has a leader and up to two alternate leaders. | `permits.preview` quotes fees and refunds without writing. A full slot returns `sold_out`, a party over the cap returns `group_too_large` and a second permit for the same party, zone and night returns `duplicate_party`. Confirming after a hold expires returns `hold_expired`. A zone closed for the requested entry returns `zone_closed`. Changes and cancellations follow the refund rule below, and a change later than two days before entry returns `outside_change_window` unless the permit is `affected`. Only the account holder can confirm, change or cancel. An alternate leader's own account can read the permit and gets `forbidden` on writes. |

**Refund rule.** Every permit carries a $6 reservation fee and a per-person fee. Cancelling two or more days before entry refunds the per-person fee. Cancelling later refunds nothing. On publication, compare the closure’s covered geography and effective interval with the permit’s booked location and entry interval. An overlap makes the permit `affected` even when the closure starts in the future. An advisory or detour alone leaves the permit confirmed. The same overlap rule applies to a new hold for the affected date. It can then be changed to another date or slot at no charge until the original entry time, or cancelled with a full refund including the reservation fee. An alert marked `cancels_permits` cancels covered permits and refunds them in full.

## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** None in the first version. All four workflows complete inside Ridgelog. Ridgelog sends no messages and receives none. A task may separately give the agent Calendar, Mail, Gustline or Rainshadow tools. Ridgelog does not import from them.
- **Other apps and websites (required):** None at runtime. Every area, trail, alert, report and permit is a synthetic record in the app.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** MCP only in the first version, with 35 agent operations. There are 19 read operations: `areas.search`, `areas.get`, `trails.search`, `trails.get`, `trailheads.get`, `alerts.list`, `alerts.get`, `reports.list`, `reports.get`, `lists.list`, `plans.list`, `permits.search`, `permits.rules`, `permits.preview`, `permits.list`, `permits.get`, `lottery.list`, `notices.list` and `account.get`. There are 16 write operations: `reports.create`, `reports.update`, `reports.delete`, `lists.save`, `lists.delete`, `plans.create`, `plans.update`, `plans.cancel`, `permits.hold`, `permits.release`, `permits.confirm`, `permits.modify`, `permits.cancel`, `lottery.apply`, `lottery.withdraw` and `lottery.respond`. The bound account and member come from Infra, never from an argument, so a task can narrow tools and fields with `policies.json`. App errors are `not_found`, `forbidden`, `invalid_date`, `invalid_route`, `sold_out`, `group_too_large`, `duplicate_party`, `hold_expired`, `zone_closed`, `outside_change_window`, `application_closed` and `award_lapsed`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Infra alone uses `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the runtime's `env.advance_time`. They are not on the agent connection, and Core returns `permission_denied` if the agent calls one. There is no reviewer console or GUI.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Areas, trails, segments, trailheads, permit types, fees, quotas and other members' trip reports are App state loaded from the run fixture. The household, its members, saved lists, planned hikes, its own reports, holds, permits, lottery applications and notices are account state bound to one account instance. For the alternate-leader permission test, Infra seeds a separate Arjun-bound instance with a read-only view of Maya’s permit at the same revision. The test compares a read and a denied write against that baseline. Inventory mutations and concurrent holds are tested within the owning instance. The seed schema accepts any history, including a pending lottery award, an `affected` permit or an alert with several versions. Seed validation rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, segments that do not connect to their junctions, reports with `posted_at` before `hiked_on`, permits over quota or over the party cap, and timestamps without a UTC offset (`invalid_seed`). Each hold reserves capacity immediately. Expiry or release frees it exactly once, and confirmation consumes the existing reservation. A modification checks destination capacity and commits the slot swap and fee effect together. A sold-out destination preserves the original booking. Each write returns a receipt with the object ID and new version. Repeating a committed `request_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`. The same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`, and a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`. A failed call leaves data, inventory and version unchanged, and reads never change business data. `permits.modify` and `permits.cancel` return the fee charged or refunded and the inventory they released. Time-based states are stored as due times and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever they are read or checked. These are hold expiry, alert effective windows, lottery and award deadlines, the two-day change window and `past` permits and plans. Infra moves the clock with an event or `env.advance_time`. Reset (`reset_scope: app_instance`) restores the loaded catalog, accounts, inventory and clock without touching other Apps. Evaluator records come from App activity rows, Core audit rows and the supplied agent trace, joined on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Each activity row records the sequence number, virtual time, actor (`agent` or `event`), action, object, member, fee effect and replaced value. There is one row per committed write or applied event, and none for a replay, a rejected call or a time-driven change. Infra takes the snapshot before reset.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Infra applies five scenario events, and the runner controls their timing. `alert.publish` adds an alert or a new version of one. If the published closure overlaps a confirmed booking as defined above, it marks that permit `affected`, or cancels and refunds it when `cancels_permits` is true. `alert.lift` ends an alert early, and an affected permit returns to confirmed once no remaining closure covers its entry. An agency-cancelled permit stays cancelled after a lift. `report.publish` adds another member's trip report. `lottery.draw` resolves the household's applications to `awarded` or `unsuccessful` and sets an award deadline. `inventory.update` changes remaining quota, for example when other parties cancel. An unknown trail, segment, zone or permit type fails the whole event, and replaying an identical `event_id` changes nothing. Ridgelog writes in-app notices, read with `notices.list`, only for what the agency or other people do. These are an alert covering a saved trail, a planned hike or a permit, a lottery result, and a permit becoming `affected` or cancelled by the agency. It writes none for the agent's own writes and none for time passing alone. It sends no cross-app notifications: `publishes` and `receives` are empty. The example below is version 2 of the Mill Creek footbridge alert.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Node.js 18+ and the public Core runtime, reference revision `a4bce02`. The household's `account_id` and each member's `person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`. Party members, leaders and alternate leaders are stored as `person_id` with the display name from the binding. Ridgelog keeps no contact details. Fixtures ship as UTF-8 JSON. The first version has no photos or maps (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the reference profile's 64 assets). Distances, elevations and condition tags are numbers and text. The package runs on its own with only Core: `dependencies.required_apps` is empty, and it reads nothing outside its own folder. Task authors keep grading judgments in the evaluator.
