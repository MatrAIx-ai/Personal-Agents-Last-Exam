[AllTrails Help](https://support.alltrails.com/) · [Recreation.gov Help](https://www.recreation.gov/help-center) · [NPS Data API (alerts)](https://www.nps.gov/subjects/digital/nps-data-api.htm)

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Ridgelog lets a personal agent decide whether a hike is still a good idea and keep the paperwork for it in order. Conditions on a trail come from two kinds of evidence that often disagree. Official alerts are versioned: a closure is issued, updated with a detour, then lifted. Trip reports from other hikers carry two dates, the day they hiked and the day they posted, and describe one stretch of trail rather than the whole route. Ridgelog exposes both, segment by segment, so the agent can tell the user what is true for their route on their date and say where that comes from. The same account holds the household's saved lists, planned hikes and permits. Permits have quotas, group-size caps, alternate leaders, holds, lotteries and refund windows, and a closure can turn a confirmed permit into one that needs a decision.

### Three example requests

The seeded household is Maya (account holder), her partner Theo and their teenager Ivy. Maya's brother Arjun has his own Ridgelog account. All places, people, alerts and fees are fictional.

### Workflows

| Workflow | Entities and transitions | Confirm, modify, cancel and error paths |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W1 Areas and trails | Area (managing agency, time zone), trail, segment (from and to junction, distance, elevation gain, high point, aspect), trailhead (access road and parking status), water source and permit rule (which zones and dates need which permit type). | Read-only. An unknown ID returns `not_found`. A trail whose area is closed stays readable and is marked closed. |
| W2 Conditions | An alert has a type (`closure`, `detour`, `advisory`, `fire_restriction` or `road`), the segments, trailheads or zones it covers, an effective window and versions. Its status is `scheduled` → `active` → `lifted` or `expired`, and each update adds a version. A trip report records author, `hiked_on`, `posted_at`, route segments and per-segment condition tags (snow, ice, mud, blowdown, water flowing or dry, bugs, crowding). | `ridgelog.alerts.list` returns the current version by default and earlier versions on request. The agent can post, edit and delete trip reports for the bound member only. A `hiked_on` date in the future or before the trail opened returns `invalid_date`. Editing another member's report returns `forbidden`. |
| W3 Lists and planned hikes | A saved list is a versioned, ordered set of trails. A planned hike has a date, start time, route segments, party (by `person_id`) and turnaround time. Its status is `planned` → `cancelled`, or `past` once its date has passed. | A list is replaced as a whole with `expected_version`. A planned route whose segments do not connect returns `invalid_route`. A planned hike on a closed segment is saved and carries a warning listing the alert, so the agent sees the conflict without being blocked. |
| W4 Permits and lotteries | A permit type is a day-use timed entry (slots with a quota) or an overnight zone permit (a nightly quota and a per-party cap). A permit goes `held` → `confirmed` → `modified` → `cancelled`, `affected` or `past`. A hold lasts 15 minutes of virtual time. A lottery application goes `submitted` → `awarded` or `unsuccessful`. Each draw leaves a draw record with the outcome for every account it names. An award goes `accepted` (which creates a confirmed permit), `declined` or `lapsed` after its deadline. Each permit has a leader and up to two alternate leaders. | `ridgelog.permits.preview` quotes fees and refunds without writing. A full slot returns `sold_out`, a party over the cap returns `group_too_large` and a second permit for the same party, zone and night returns `duplicate_party`. Confirming after a hold expires returns `hold_expired`. A zone under an active closure returns `zone_closed`. Changes and cancellations follow the refund rule below, and a change later than two days before entry returns `outside_change_window` unless the permit is `affected`. Only the account holder can confirm, change or cancel. An alternate leader's own account can read the permit and gets `forbidden` on writes. |

**Refund rule.** Every permit carries a $6 reservation fee and a per-person fee. Cancelling two or more days before entry refunds the per-person fee. Cancelling later refunds nothing. When an active closure covers a confirmed permit, the permit becomes `affected`. It can then be changed to another date or slot at no charge until the original entry time, or cancelled with a full refund including the reservation fee. An alert marked `cancels_permits` cancels covered permits and refunds them in full.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** MCP only in the first version, with 35 agent operations. There are 19 read operations: `ridgelog.areas.search`, `ridgelog.areas.get`, `ridgelog.trails.search`, `ridgelog.trails.get`, `ridgelog.trailheads.get`, `ridgelog.alerts.list`, `ridgelog.alerts.get`, `ridgelog.reports.list`, `ridgelog.reports.get`, `ridgelog.lists.list`, `ridgelog.plans.list`, `ridgelog.permits.search`, `ridgelog.permits.rules`, `ridgelog.permits.preview`, `ridgelog.permits.list`, `ridgelog.permits.get`, `ridgelog.lottery.list`, `ridgelog.notices.list` and `ridgelog.account.get`. There are 16 write operations: `ridgelog.reports.create`, `ridgelog.reports.update`, `ridgelog.reports.delete`, `ridgelog.lists.save`, `ridgelog.lists.delete`, `ridgelog.plans.create`, `ridgelog.plans.update`, `ridgelog.plans.cancel`, `ridgelog.permits.hold`, `ridgelog.permits.release`, `ridgelog.permits.confirm`, `ridgelog.permits.modify`, `ridgelog.permits.cancel`, `ridgelog.lottery.apply`, `ridgelog.lottery.withdraw` and `ridgelog.lottery.respond`. The acting person is the bound account's `owner_person_id` from `binding.json`, never an argument. Maya's runs bind her account, and a run that tests Arjun's access binds his. Writes that change an existing object take it as `id` and the fields to change as `patch`, so a task can narrow tools, objects and fields with the `constraints` in `policies.json`, which Core checks directly. App errors are `not_found`, `forbidden`, `invalid_date`, `invalid_route`, `sold_out`, `group_too_large`, `duplicate_party`, `hold_expired`, `zone_closed`, `outside_change_window`, `application_closed`, `already_applied` and `award_lapsed`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Infra alone uses `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the runtime's `env.advance_time`. They are not on the agent connection, and Core returns `permission_denied` if the agent calls one. There is no reviewer console or GUI.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Areas, trails, segments, trailheads, permit types, fees, quotas and other members' trip reports are App state loaded from the run fixture. The household, its members, saved lists, planned hikes, its own reports, holds, permits, lottery applications, draw records and notices are account state bound to one account instance. A second account instance (Arjun's) can be loaded in the same run to test alternate-leader access. The seed schema accepts any history, including a pending lottery award, an `affected` permit or an alert with several versions. Records dated after `ctx.virtual_now` do not exist yet for any tool: an alert version before its `issued_at`, a trip report before its `posted_at`, a draw record before its `drawn_at`, a notice before its `created_at`, and the household's lists, planned hikes, reports, permits and lottery applications before the time they were made. They are absent from lists, counts and search, `not_found` by ID, and appear when the clock reaches them, with nothing written. An alert's current version is the latest one issued by the clock, and checks such as `duplicate_party` and `already_applied` look only at records that exist at the clock. Scheduled fields stay visible: a hike date, a permit's entry date and an alert's effective window. A seed that runs past its clock, such as the evaluation dataset, can therefore load at any point in its window. The current state of a record that already exists is stored as of the dataset clock and is not rewound: a saved list's contents, a permit's status and remaining quota. A Task that starts earlier and needs that state supplies its own seed. Seed validation rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, segments that do not connect to their junctions, reports with `posted_at` before `hiked_on`, permits over quota or over the party cap, and timestamps without a UTC offset (`invalid_seed`). Each write returns a receipt with the object ID and new version. Repeating a committed `request_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`. The same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`, and a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`. A failed call leaves data, inventory and version unchanged, and reads never change business data. `ridgelog.permits.modify` and `ridgelog.permits.cancel` return the fee charged or refunded and the inventory they released. Time-based states are stored as due times and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever they are read or checked. These are hold expiry, alert effective windows, lottery and award deadlines, the two-day change window and `past` permits and plans. Infra moves the clock with an event or `env.advance_time`. Reset (`reset_scope: app_instance`) restores the loaded catalog, accounts, inventory and clock without touching other Apps. Evaluator records come from App activity rows, Core audit rows and the supplied agent trace, joined on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Each activity row records the sequence number, virtual time, actor (`agent` or `event`), action, object, member, fee effect and replaced value. There is one row per committed write or applied event, and none for a replay, a rejected call or a time-driven change. Infra takes the snapshot before reset.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Infra applies five scenario events, and the runner controls their timing. `alert.publish` adds an alert or a new version of one. If it covers a confirmed permit it marks that permit `affected`, or cancels and refunds it when `cancels_permits` is true. `alert.lift` ends an alert early, and `affected` permits whose dates are no longer covered return to `confirmed`. `report.publish` adds another member's trip report. `lottery.draw` resolves a lottery for one permit type and entry date. Because the agent submits applications during the run, their IDs are not predictable, so the event lists results by `account_id` (from `binding.json`), not by application ID. Each account's open application for that lottery becomes `awarded`, with an award deadline, or `unsuccessful`. An account can hold at most one open application per lottery, and applying again returns `already_applied`. Each draw is stored as a draw record in account state, with the outcome for every account the event names: `awarded`, `unsuccessful` or `skipped`. If a named account has no open application, the draw records that account under `skipped` and processes the remaining accounts. Core's `env.apply_event` returns only a commit receipt, so the per-account outcome is read from the draw record: `ridgelog.lottery.list` shows it to the agent for the bound account, and the evaluator reads it from the App state snapshot. This makes "the agent never applied" observable. An event that fails, for example on an unknown permit type, stores nothing; `env.apply_event` returns `applied: false` with the code and the audit records a `deny` row. Other events name seeded records by ID. `inventory.update` changes remaining quota, for example when other parties cancel. An unknown trail, segment, zone or permit type fails the whole event, and replaying the `event_id` of a committed event changes nothing. Ridgelog writes in-app notices, read with `ridgelog.notices.list`, only for what the agency or other people do. These are an alert covering a saved trail, a planned hike or a permit, a lottery result, and a permit becoming `affected` or cancelled by the agency. It writes none for the agent's own writes and none for time passing alone. It sends no cross-app notifications: `publishes` and `receives` are empty. The example below is version 2 of the Mill Creek footbridge alert.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Node.js 18+ and the public Core runtime 1.6.0, reference revision `a4bce02` (the runtime is unchanged at `6933c43`). The household's `account_id` and each member's `person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`. Party members, leaders and alternate leaders are stored as `person_id` with the display name from the binding. Ridgelog keeps no contact details. The smoke fixtures and the evaluation dataset ship as UTF-8 JSON. The first version has no photos or maps (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the reference profile's 64 assets). Distances, elevations and condition tags are numbers and text. The package runs on its own with only Core: `dependencies.required_apps` is empty, and it reads nothing outside its own folder. Task authors keep grading judgments in the evaluator. [Runtime](../../runtime/README.md) · [App contribution guide](../../docs/app-contribution.md)
