## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Benchline is a resettable lab compute and results workspace, seen from one researcher's account, in which the lab shares one GPU-hour allocation and a result counts only under the evaluation protocol the lab signed, so a job the cluster accepts can still be wrong for this person's labmates, deadline or paper. The account sees the whole lab's jobs and results and holds the researcher's own jobs, result notes, protocol proposals and results tables; labmates, the lab head and the cluster act through task-supplied events, and every time-based state follows the environment clock. Four workflows are complete in the first version.

**Entities.** Project (time zone, one allocation period with its GPU-hours, partitions with a charge rate and a longest job time, and a policy of four values), person (the account holder, the lab head and labmates, each with an optional share of the allocation), config (a registered, immutable experiment setup: parameters, dataset version, evaluation split and an opaque code reference), protocol version (one version of an evaluation protocol line: dataset version, evaluation split, metrics, the seeds to run, who signed it and when), job (config, seed, partition, GPUs, time limit, an optional protocol version, its times and its history), result (the metric values of a completed job, with tags, a note and a disposition), table version (named rows of results under one protocol version, a draft or a frozen manifest), notification with its release time, account preferences. A run plan is world data, described under Rules.

**Simplifications.** One App instance holds one researcher account in one project on one cluster. Labmates and the lab head are not accounts: what they do arrives as events. The cluster is not simulated: Benchline runs no code, trains nothing and has no scheduler. How long a job waits, how long it runs and what it reports is task data. There are no files, logs, checkpoints or artifacts, so a stopped job cannot resume. There are no job arrays, job dependencies, priorities or fair-share scores. A charge is `gpus × rate × hours`, in GPU-hours. The allocation period has a start and an end date, which the summary shows; it does not close by itself in the first version, and usage does not reset. The policy is four values per project: whether a person's share is a hard limit (`hard_shares`), how many jobs one person may have queued, held or running (`max_active_jobs`), the people who must sign a protocol version (`required_signers`, a list of person IDs) and how long a proposed version waits for them (`signoff_window_days`).

- **W1 Allocation, queue, results and notices (read-only).** Read the project, its policy and its people (`project.get`); read the usage summary (`usage.summary`: allocation, used, reserved, remaining, overdrawn, and the same for each person's share); list and read configs (`configs.list`, `configs.get`); list jobs in a window of submission time (`jobs.list`: mine, a labmate's, by state, config or protocol; the list filters and sorts by submission time only) and read one job with its history and its result (`jobs.get`); list results (`results.list`) and compare two or more (`results.compare`: `comparable`, and every field in which their protocol versions differ); list and read protocol versions with their sign-offs (`protocols.list`, `protocols.get`); list and read tables (`tables.list`, `tables.get`; a frozen table returns its manifest and the same rows as CSV text); list the released notifications (`notifications.list`); read the account's preferences (`preferences.get`); and check in advance whether a job may be submitted (`jobs.estimate`: `allowed`, every rule it would break, the GPU-hours it would reserve, what the project and my share would have left, and the flags the job would carry). Jobs, results and tables carry `flags`, listed under Rules. A labmate's job and a change to the allocation are facts and are never rejected, so a project can be overdrawn, and the summary says by how much. Paths: modify, cancel and confirm are not applicable to reads; errors = unknown object (`not_found`), `invalid_window`.
- **W2 Running jobs, and preferences.** Submit a job for a registered config and seed on a partition with a number of GPUs and a time limit, optionally under a protocol version in any state; put a queued job on hold; release a held job; cancel a job; set the account's preferences (`preferences.update`: the time zone used in notification text, `email_notices`, and `job_end_notices`, which says whether a notice is written when my job ends). Paths: modify = not offered for a job, because a job records what was asked: cancel it and submit again; `preferences.update` for the settings; cancel = `jobs.cancel` while the job is queued, held or running (a running job is charged up to that moment and leaves no result); errors = an unknown time zone (`invalid_timezone`), an unknown config or partition (`not_found`), a time limit above the partition's longest (`time_limit_too_long`), a config or seed that the named protocol version does not cover (`protocol_mismatch`), too many jobs of mine queued, held or running (`too_many_active`), a reservation larger than what the project has left (`over_allocation`) or, where shares are hard, larger than what my share has left (`over_share`), a labmate's job (`not_yours`), a job that is not in the state the operation needs (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `jobs.estimate` previews the same checks without writing; the submission is the commitment.
- **W3 Evaluation protocols and sign-off.** Propose a new version of a protocol line (a changed dataset version, evaluation split, metric list or seed list, with a rationale); sign or decline a version a labmate proposed (`protocols.respond`); withdraw my own proposal. A version is `signed` only when every required signer has signed, and only then does the earlier signed version of that line become `superseded`. Paths: modify = not offered, because a proposal records what was asked: withdraw it and propose again; cancel = `protocols.withdraw` while it is `proposed`; errors = a version identical to the signed one (`no_change`), a line that already has a proposed version (`already_proposed`), answering when I am not a required signer (`not_a_signer`) or have already answered (`already_answered`), a labmate's proposal (`not_yours`), a version that is no longer `proposed` (`invalid_state`). Confirm: the proposal or the answer is the commitment; the other signers' answers arrive as events.
- **W4 Reporting results.** Tag a result, add a note, or exclude it with a reason and bring it back (`results.update`; nothing is ever deleted); create a results table under one protocol version, set its rows, freeze it, revise a frozen table into a new draft version, or discard a draft. Paths: modify = `tables.update` while the table is a draft; a frozen table is never edited, and a correction is a new version (`tables.revise`, which starts the draft with the rows of the frozen version); cancel = `tables.discard` for a draft; errors = a job with no result yet (`not_completed`), a labmate's result (`not_yours`), an exclusion without a reason (`reason_required`), a row that cites a result run under another protocol version or under none (`mixed_protocols`), a result cited twice (`duplicate_result`), and at freeze: a table with no rows (`empty_table`), a protocol version that was never signed (`protocol_not_signed`), a row that does not hold exactly one result of one config for each seed of the protocol (`incomplete_seeds`), a cited result that is excluded (`cites_excluded`) or invalidated (`cites_invalidated`); a change to a table version that is not a draft, or a revision of a table that already has a draft or of a version that is not its latest frozen one (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `tables.get` on the draft shows the flags that would stop a freeze; the freeze is the commitment.

Rules:

- Time is the environment's. Benchline has no timers, no scheduler and no clock event: Infra moves `virtual_now` with an event or with Core's `env.advance_time`, which runs no App code. Every time-based state is therefore stored as a due time and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever it is read or checked: a job's `running` and its end state from its start and end, a protocol version's `expired` from its `respond_by`, a notification's `released` from its release time. Writes and events use the same derivation: a job that has ended cannot be cancelled, and an expired version cannot be signed. Nothing is rewritten when a due time passes.
- The cluster is not simulated. A run plan says, for a config (and optionally one seed and one partition), how many minutes a job waits, how many minutes it runs, and whether it completes with which metric values or fails with which error text. Run plans are world data: they come from the seed or from the `plan.set` event, they stay in App state, and no Agent operation returns them. When a job is submitted or released, Benchline copies the most specific matching plan into the job and stores its start (that moment plus the wait) and its end (the start plus the run time, or plus the time limit if that is shorter, in which case the job ends `timed_out`). A job shows its start only once it has started and its end and result only once it has ended. A job with no matching plan has no start and stays `queued` until a `plan.set` event gives one or the job is cancelled.
- Charging. A queued or running job reserves `gpus × rate × time_limit_hours`. While it runs it is charged `gpus × rate × hours run`; when it ends, what it did not use returns to the allocation. A held job reserves nothing. `remaining` is the allocation minus what is used and reserved, never below zero; anything beyond is `overdrawn_hours`. The same sums are kept for each person against `share_hours`. A partition with rate 0 charges nothing. A labmate's recorded job follows the same rule: it reserves from the time of its event until it starts, is charged while it runs, and returns what it did not use.
- One check is used wherever I ask for compute: by `jobs.estimate`, `jobs.submit` and `jobs.release`. In this order: the config and the partition exist; the time limit is within the partition's longest; if a protocol version is named, the config has its dataset version and evaluation split and the seed is in its seed list; my queued, held and running jobs are fewer than `max_active_jobs`; the reservation fits what the project has left; and, where `hard_shares` is on, it fits what my share has left. In `jobs.release` the job itself is left out of the count of my jobs. The check does not look at the state of the protocol version: a job may run under a version that is not signed, and it is flagged. A write returns the first refusal, while `jobs.estimate` lists every rule that fails. Where shares are soft, a job that takes me past my share is accepted and flagged.
- Labmates, the lab head and the cluster are not held to that check. A recorded job, a changed allocation and an interruption are applied as given, whatever the limits, the allocation and the shares say; only the structure of the data is validated (§3). When `allocation.changed` leaves less than my queued jobs reserve, the handler holds them one by one, the last submitted first, until the rest fit; running jobs go on. A later increase releases nothing by itself: a held job stays held until I release it. A lower share holds nothing: it limits only later submissions and releases.
- Flags are computed when read and never stored. On a job of mine: `over_share` (on the queued and running jobs that take my used and reserved hours past a soft share, counted in order of submission), `unfunded` (held with the reason `allocation`). On a job or a result: `protocol_unsigned` (its protocol version is `proposed`, `declined`, `withdrawn` or `expired`) and `protocol_superseded`; on a result also `invalidated`. On a table: `empty_table`, `incomplete_seeds`, `cites_excluded`, `cites_invalidated`, `protocol_unsigned` and `protocol_superseded`; on a draft, the first five are exactly what would stop a freeze (the flag `protocol_unsigned` stops it with the error `protocol_not_signed`; the other four have the same name as their error).
- A result belongs to one protocol version, the one named when its job was submitted, or to none, and this never changes. Results are comparable only when they name the same protocol version.
- A protocol line has at most one `proposed` version at a time. A proposer who is a required signer has signed by proposing; if no other signature is missing, the version is `signed` at once. A version that is still `proposed` at its `respond_by` (the proposal time plus `signoff_window_days`) is `expired`. It counts as `proposed` only before that moment, for every rule, the validation of the seed and of events included, so a line can take a new proposal once the old one has expired.
- A table can be frozen under a protocol version that is `signed` or `superseded`, because both were agreed by every required signer; this lets a frozen table be corrected under its own version after the lab has moved to a newer one. A table has at most one draft version at a time. A frozen table is a manifest and is never edited: for each row its config, seeds and result IDs, and for each metric the count, the mean and the sample standard deviation, rounded to four decimals, with the protocol version, the freeze time and a content hash. A later invalidation, exclusion or new protocol version only adds a flag. A correction is a new version, and freezing it makes the old one `superseded`.
- Notifications are written for what other people and the cluster do, never for my own writes and never for a change that time alone causes (a version that expires writes none), with one exception. One is written for each change to the allocation or to my share (`allocation.changed`), each interruption of a job of mine (`job.interrupted`), each proposal by a labmate when I am a required signer (`protocol.proposed`), each time a version that I proposed or am a required signer of is answered by someone else, is withdrawn by a labmate or becomes `signed` (`protocol.updated`), and each invalidation of a result that is mine or that a table of mine cites (`result.invalidated`). The exception is the end of my own job (`job.ended`), written only while `job_end_notices` is on: the write or event that fixes the job's end also writes this notice with the end as its release time, and a cancellation, a hold or an interruption removes it. No notification is listed before its release time.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** The same request, "run it", calls for different actions for different people, and the cluster only knows half of each person. A job that fits the project can spend the hours a labmate was promised for her deadline. A cheaper evaluation split can pass every check in the app and still answer a different question from the one the user and her co-authors signed. A rerun can beat the number in the draft, and swapping it in silently changes a table that a co-author already has. A time limit that is too generous blocks hours other people need, and one that is too tight loses the run. Because hours are shared, a result is tied to one protocol version, and a frozen table cannot be edited, the order and the timing of what the agent does are part of the answer. Benchline makes these checkable: which usage, jobs, protocols and results the agent read, which jobs it submitted, held, released or cancelled and what each reserved, which protocol versions it proposed, signed or declined, which results it excluded and why, and which table versions it froze. Distinct task situations (separate Task proposals): (1) the allocation is cut in the middle of the period while three of the user's jobs wait for a deadline, and the agent must keep the jobs her lab head asked for first and must not move to a cheaper protocol that nobody signed (Task #109 meets this problem and plans three task-owned fixtures for it: a signed protocol ledger, a quota job fixture and an artifact vault with frozen manifests); (2) a labmate proposes a protocol version that makes every job cheaper, the app lets the user sign it, and her own earlier written rule says the test subjects stay unseen; (3) a labmate reports a bug in one seed of a frozen table, and the agent must rerun that seed under the same protocol version and publish a new table version, not edit a number; (4) "run the usual three seeds tonight" fits the project but takes the user past her share in the week she promised a labmate not to; (5) a summary must be updated from new runs without mixing results from two protocol versions (Task #34 meets this with a task-owned workspace of run manifests and results). No Task proposal depends on Benchline, and both tasks can keep their own fixtures; the rationale is independent of any one task. Fixtures are plain seed data, so each task loads its own project, people, configs, protocol history, jobs, results, tables and run plans.

- **Operations and access (required):** 29 Agent-facing MCP operations over stdio on the Agent connection of the public host (`node runtime/cli.cjs serve --app apps/benchline --role agent`), registered in `tools.json` as `benchline.<name>`. The first version has no GUI, no reviewer console and no console for the lab head: other people act only through events.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). The seed schema carries project records (allocation period, partitions, policy, people and shares, the history of allocation changes, configs, protocol versions with their sign-offs, the lab's jobs with their history, run plans) and account records (preferences, result tags, notes and dispositions, table versions, notifications, activity rows), so a task can load any history, including past jobs in every state, a project that is already overdrawn and a table frozen under a protocol version that has since been superseded. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, a job that ends before it starts or runs longer than its time limit, a job whose seed or config its protocol version does not cover, an empty `required_signers` list, two versions on one line that are both proposed and not yet expired, two draft versions of one table, a frozen table whose manifest does not match the results it cites, timestamps without a UTC offset and an account that the binding does not declare (`invalid_seed`). The seed is history: everything in it has already happened, and new facts arrive only through events. Writes follow Core: an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` or `event_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`, so a repeated request submits no second job; the same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`; a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`; a failed call leaves data, version and notifications unchanged and Core appends a denial audit row. Reads never change business data: job states, charges, expiry and flags are computed from stored records and `virtual_now`, and `jobs.estimate` and `results.compare` write nothing. Reset is Core's: it restores the loaded seed and the time loaded with it (`reset_scope: app_instance`) and touches no other App; messages already delivered to Mail stay there. Evaluator records come from three places: (a) App state in the snapshot: activity rows with sequence, virtual time, actor `agent` or `event`, action, object ID and the `request_id` or `event_id` behind them, one for every committed Agent write, one for every applied event and one for each effect of that event (a job held, a version signed or superseded, a notification written); each job's history with its reservation, start, end and charge; each protocol version's sign-offs; each table version's rows and manifest; notifications with creation time and release time; (b) Core audit rows with virtual time and the projected `id`, `start` and `end`: one for every allowed call that is not a replay, one for every denied call, and one for every `env.advance_time` with the clock before and after; (c) full arguments and results in the supplied Agent execution trace. They join on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Counting rules: count committed writes and event effects from the activity rows, one row each. Core returns the stored receipt for a repeated `request_id` or `event_id` before it appends an audit row, so a replay appears only in the trace, as a call whose result carries `duplicate: true`: count it as a retry of the original, never as a new action. A rejected attempt writes no activity row: count rejected attempts from the denial audit rows and the trace, one for each attempt, repeats included. Count reads from the allowed audit rows and the trace. Time-driven changes (a job started or ended, a version expired, a notification released) run no App code and write no row: read them from the stored due times and the clock, and from the audit row of the `env.advance_time` call or the event that moved it. Take the snapshot before a reset, because reset clears the audit.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Nine events through `env.apply_event`, in Core's envelope. Benchline has no clock event: Infra moves time with an event or with `env.advance_time`. An event is handled at its own time T, which is `ctx.virtual_now` inside the handler in runtime 1.6.0: a version whose `respond_by` has passed by T is expired and cannot be answered, and a job that has ended by T cannot be interrupted. Core rejects an event dated before the App's current time (`invalid_event`); Benchline also rejects a duplicate job, config or protocol ID, an unknown person, partition, config or protocol version, a recorded job that ends before it starts, runs longer than its time limit or names a protocol version that does not cover its config and seed (Core validates the data after every event with the same rules as the seed), an interruption of a job that is not queued or running, a second proposed version on one line, an answer from a person who is not a required signer or has already answered, an answer or a withdrawal for a version that is not `proposed`, and an invalidation of a job that has no result (`invalid_event`, state unchanged).

- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for partitions, people, configs, protocol versions, jobs, table versions and notifications; a result has the ID of its job. The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` are the shared values of the run's `binding.json`, so the person has one ID in Benchline and Mail, and notifications address the recipient by `person_ref`. Labmates and the lab head are named by `person_id`; a task that has the same people in Contacts or Messages gives them the same IDs in its seeds, and Benchline stores only what a lab workspace knows about them (a display name, a role label and a share), not a copy of a contact. Benchline is the source of truth for its allocation, jobs, results, protocol versions, tables and notifications and keeps no copy of another App's records. Missing data is explicit: an unknown ID returns `not_found`; a job that has not started has `starts_at: null`, and one that has not ended has `ends_at: null` and no result; a job with no run plan stays `queued`; a person without a share has `share_hours: null` and only the project's allocation applies; a job submitted under no protocol has `protocol_id: null` and its result cannot enter a table. All records are JSON; a frozen table's CSV is returned as text in the result of `tables.get`; there are no images, audio, video or attachments (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the profile's 64 assets). Runtime needs: the public Core/Messaging runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` commit `61b6371`; Mail is optional; `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; no external service. The package runs on its own: a copy that holds only `apps/benchline/` and the pinned runtime starts the App, and its business tests and example run from the package alone. It imports and reads nothing outside its own folder and uses no other App's files or state; the two real-MCP scripts are given the location of the pinned runtime, and the Mail flow also needs the pinned Mail package. Dates and the allocation period use IANA time zones through Node's built-in `Intl`, so no package is installed.
