## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Build a resettable browser App and MCP business interface for one fictional domain registrar. The initial version will support five complete workflows:

1. **Search and register a domain:** search deterministic availability across a small set of synthetic top-level domains, compare first-year and renewal prices, inspect registration restrictions, select a registration term, contact profile, privacy option, payment method, and auto-renew setting, then explicitly confirm registration. The resulting domain preserves the accepted price/policy and expiration date. Expected failures include unavailable or reserved names, invalid syntax, unsupported TLDs, expired quotes, ineligible contact profiles, and concurrent registration conflicts.
2. **Renew and manage expiration:** inspect expiration, renewal pricing, auto-renew state, payment eligibility, and lifecycle status; renew an eligible domain for one or more supported years; and enable or disable auto-renew. Deterministic lifecycle processing creates in-App reminders 30, 7, and 1 day before expiration and notices at expiration and grace-period boundaries. Seeded payment failures leave a visible renewal failure and retry path rather than silently extending the domain. Manual and automatic renewals are idempotent and record an itemized synthetic charge and confirmation.
3. **Configure DNS:** inspect the active nameserver mode and hosted DNS zone; switch between Namegrove-hosted DNS and validated custom nameservers; and create, update, or delete A, AAAA, CNAME, MX, TXT, and CAA records with name/value, priority where applicable, and bounded TTL. Writes use zone versions and reject invalid addresses, duplicate/conflicting records, apex/CNAME conflicts, malformed nameservers, stale edits, or attempts to edit a hosted zone while custom nameservers are active. Every successful change records a zone revision and audit-friendly before/after result.
4. **Transfer a domain:** initiate an inbound transfer using a synthetic authorization code, inspect status and fees, and cancel while policy permits; or unlock an eligible Namegrove domain, issue a short-lived synthetic outbound authorization code, and approve or cancel a pending outbound transfer. Task-scheduled registry events move transfers through pending, approved, rejected, cancelled, and completed states. Transfer locks, recently registered/transferred domains, expiration proximity, invalid/expired codes, and duplicate requests produce explicit failures without changing ownership. Authorization codes are never included in ordinary list responses, snapshots intended for reviewers, or Mail notifications.
5. **Respond to expiration risk:** review the in-App notification center, filter domains requiring action, retry a failed auto-renewal with another saved synthetic payment method, or renew during the configured grace period. Expired domains block DNS edits and outbound transfer until restored; seeded redemption and deletion states are read-only in v1 and clearly disclose that they require out-of-scope registrar support. Reminders do not themselves renew, transfer, or delete a domain.

The environment is useful for personal-agent evaluation because safe domain administration combines deadlines, irreversible availability changes, renewal prices, account preferences, DNS correctness, transfer locks, sensitive authorization material, and explicit user authority. It enables tasks such as registering a suitable name within budget, fixing one DNS record without disturbing mail delivery, moving a domain before a deadline, or recovering from a failed renewal reminder.

The GUI will expose the same business state and actions as MCP through product-oriented search/checkout, domain portfolio, domain detail, DNS editor, transfer center, and notification-center views. It will not be a generic state inspector.

## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** Mail 1.3.0 is optional in v1.
  - **Mail 1.3.0, optional:** Namegrove will publish registration and renewal confirmations, transfer-status notices, failed-auto-renew warnings, and expiration reminders through Mail's implemented `channel.email` receive contract in [`examples/apps/mail/contribution/contribution.json`](../../examples/apps/mail/contribution/contribution.json). Connected validation will verify the actual destination Mail record and Messaging receipt. Namegrove remains independently runnable without Mail because every reminder and confirmation is retained in the App. Failed or unavailable delivery is reported separately and does not roll back a domain transaction; v1 does not claim an atomic outbox or automatic queued retry. Messages never contain authorization codes, full contact profiles, or payment credentials.
- **Other apps and websites (required):** None required for v1. Future tasks may coordinate Namegrove with website hosting, business email, certificate, billing, or project-management environments, but no external App is a package or acceptance dependency. Registry, payment, and DNS-provider behavior is represented by deterministic fixtures and task/Infra events rather than live services.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** Agent-facing MCP and GUI operations will cover availability search, quote creation/read, registration, portfolio/domain read, renewal, auto-renew update, notification list/read, DNS zone read, nameserver update, DNS record create/update/delete, inbound transfer create/read/cancel, outbound unlock/auth-code issuance/approval/cancel, and transfer status. Exact names and schemas will be declared in `tools.json`; representative names are `domain.search`, `domain.quote.create`, `domain.register`, `domain.list`, `domain.get`, `domain.renew`, `domain.auto_renew.update`, `domain.notifications.list`, `dns.zone.get`, `dns.nameservers.set`, `dns.record.create`, `dns.record.update`, `dns.record.delete`, `domain.transfer.initiate`, `domain.transfer.get`, `domain.transfer.cancel`, `domain.transfer.unlock`, and `domain.transfer.auth_code.issue`. Infra-only `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.advance_time`, `env.snapshot`, and `env.reset` will load histories, inject registry/payment/lifecycle events, advance deadlines, inspect outcomes, and restore a clean run. Setup, reset, time control, and event injection will not be exposed to the Agent.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Each App instance represents one customer's registrar account and is isolated by Core's `run_id` and `app_id`. Synthetic seed data will contain TLD availability/pricing rules, owned domains, contact profiles, privacy and auto-renew preferences, redacted payment methods, DNS zones/revisions, transfers, notifications, and optional history. Registration quotes and outbound authorization codes expire at declared virtual times. A domain moves through active, expiring, expired-grace, redemption, transferred, or deleted states according to explicit policy and task/Infra lifecycle processing. Writes use idempotency keys and expected state/zone versions. Duplicate successful retries return the prior result, stale concurrent writes fail without partial state changes, and each commit returns object status plus Core epoch/revision. Reset replaces business state, audit/receipts, task-loaded history, and issued synthetic secrets with the fixture baseline; snapshots provide deterministic review evidence but no Agent restore capability.
- **Events and notifications (required):** The App will accept task/Infra events for `domain.lifecycle_processed`, `domain.payment_outcome_recorded`, `domain.transfer_status_changed`, and `domain.registry_status_changed`. Events use stable event/domain/transfer IDs, an effective virtual timestamp, and a bounded outcome/reason; they are idempotent and reject invalid lifecycle transitions. `domain.lifecycle_processed` creates due reminders and applies expiration/grace transitions based on Core virtual time, without choosing whether to renew. Transfer and registry events cannot rewrite DNS or contact data unrelated to their declared transition. Namegrove will optionally publish compatible `channel.email` CloudEvents for the Mail messages listed above. A reminder identifies the Namegrove domain as `source_object` and includes the deadline, current status, and safe next actions, but no secret or raw payment data. Sender commit, receiver commit, and delivery acknowledgement remain separate outcomes. Task scenarios, not the App, choose when external events and lifecycle processing occur.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Shared references include `person_id`, `registrar_account_id`, `contact_profile_id`, `domain_id`, `quote_id`, `zone_id`, `zone_revision_id`, `dns_record_id`, `transfer_id`, `notification_id`, `payment_method_id`, and `event_id`. Contact and payment fixtures are synthetic; normal responses expose only necessary contact labels and redacted card brand/last-four/expiry metadata. Synthetic authorization codes are returned only by the authorized issuance operation, stored as non-reversible verification material, redacted from ordinary reads/log summaries, and invalidated on use, expiry, reset, or relock. The App requires no media attachments. The package will run independently with the repository's pinned public Core/runtime and Node.js 18+ using only built-ins. Mail 1.3.0 is optional in run assembly for notification evidence and is not required for core domain workflows.
