## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Cornerpost is a resettable third-party source of business listings and customer reviews that sits outside the businesses it describes. A listing, its star average, an individual review, the reviewer's history, an owner reply and the business's own website are separate records that can disagree. The App stores what people wrote and when; it never labels a business as risky, summarizes reviews or recommends a choice. The agent decides what the evidence means for this user.

Many personal tasks start with "find me someone to do this" and turn on evidence the provider does not control. Without a shared source, each provider App would embed its own reviews, which makes them first-party and removes the cross-check. Cornerpost supplies that source once for any category a task loads: movers, contractors, clinics, restaurants, repair shops.

Five workflows, each complete end to end:

1. **Search and listing detail.** Search by category, text and area label. A listing shows name, text address, phone, website, hours, open/closed and claimed status, creation date, rating average, review count, count of not-recommended reviews, and an optional external reference to the business's own App or registry record.
2. **Read reviews as evidence.** Sort by most relevant (default; rule in §3), newest, highest or lowest; filter by keyword or stars; page. Each review has rating, text, dates, edit history, optional photos, an optional dated owner reply, and the reviewer's profile: account age, review count and other reviews. Reviews flagged as not recommended appear only in a separate section and do not count toward the average or review count; the listing shows how many exist. The flag comes from the seed or an event, never from the App.
3. **Saved lists and notes.** Create, rename and delete lists; add or remove listings; keep a private note per saved listing, so a shortlist and its reasons persist across sessions.
4. **Contribute and correct.** Post, edit or delete the user's review, with optional photo; report another review, see the report's status, and withdraw it while it is open. One review per account per listing.
5. **Ask the business.** Post and delete a public question; read answers, which arrive as scenario events.

Core examples (precondition → action or event → outcome → control case). Tool names here and in §3 omit the `cornerpost.` prefix.

| Workflow | Example |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Two listings have near-identical names → `places.get` on each → different phone, creation date and external reference → an unknown ID returns `not_found`. |
| 2 | A listing averages 4.6 → `reviews.list` with `sort=newest` → the last eight reviews are 1–2 stars within six weeks → an unsupported sort is rejected. |
| 2 | A listing shows 4.6 from 31 reviews and "14 not recommended" → `reviews.list` with `section=not_recommended` → 14 reviews, 11 with 1–2 stars → the default call never includes them. |
| 3 | `lists.item.add` commits → a repeat with the same `request_id` returns the original result and adds no duplicate → a different add with a stale `expected_version` is rejected. |
| 4 | `reviews.create` → review visible, average recomputed → a second create on the same listing is rejected and points to `reviews.update`. |
| 5 | `cornerpost.question.answered` event → answer visible with its date → replaying the event ID adds nothing. |

Illustrative downstream task: a user wants a car shipped at the lowest price. The cheapest company has a high average, but its newest and not-recommended reviews describe a price increase after dispatch, and a near-identical listing belongs to a different company. A companion Task proposal will define the person, the companies' behavior, the rubric and model trials; this App encodes no task's answer.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 22 Agent-facing MCP business tools over stdio in the public runtime, registered in `tools.json` as `cornerpost.<name>`. Reads (9): `places.search`, `places.get`, `reviews.list` (`section` = `recommended` or `not_recommended`; `sort` = `relevant`, `newest`, `highest` or `lowest`), `reviews.get` (includes `my_report` when the user has reported that review), `reviews.photo.get`, `reviewers.get`, `questions.list`, `lists.list`, `lists.get`. Writes (13): `lists.create|update|delete`, `lists.item.add|remove|note`, `reviews.create|update|delete`, `reviews.report`, `reviews.report.withdraw`, `questions.ask`, `questions.delete`. Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version`. Photo bytes go through Core's resource access: the package declares `assetReferences` from review to photo and proposes `runtime/registrations/cornerpost.json` (`{"app_id":"cornerpost","resource_access":{"review":"cornerpost.reviews.get"}}`) for Infra review. Management operations are Infra-only: Core's portable lifecycle operations (`env.load_seed`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.apply_event`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle`) and runtime 1.6.0's `env.advance_time`; an Agent call returns `permission_denied`. An optional read-only renderer (listing, review section, reviewer profile) is for human reviewers only (`audience: ["reviewer"]` in `gui.json`); it has no write controls and is not an Agent computer-use interface.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** One account per instance; aggregates and ranking derived at read time; Core's write receipts and reset.
  - *Isolation and seed.* State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one user account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). The seed is history: listings, reviewers, reviews with recommendation flags, photos, owner replies, questions and answers, and the user's lists, notes, reviews, reports and settings (`email_notices`). `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, a review dated before its listing or its reviewer's join date, a reply dated before its review, two reviews by one reviewer on one listing, timestamps without a UTC offset, and an account the binding does not declare (`invalid_seed`). New facts arrive only through Agent writes and events.
  - *Derived values.* A listing's average (one decimal, rounded half up) and review count are computed at read time from its visible recommended reviews and never stored.
  - *Ranking rule.* `relevant`, the default, orders reviews by a score computed at read time from stored fields and `ctx.virtual_now`: +2 if the text has at least 400 characters, +1 if it has a photo, +1 if it has an owner reply, plus 3 if posted in the last 90 days, 2 if within a year, 1 otherwise. Ties go to the newer `posted_at`, then the lower review ID. The star rating is not an input, so the default order neither buries nor promotes low ratings. `newest` sorts by `posted_at`; `highest` and `lowest` sort by rating, then newest. Both sections accept all four sorts. Because age comes from the clock, the same call can order differently after Infra advances time; no record changes.
  - *Writes and evidence.* A committed write returns `status: committed`, the affected object with its ID, and the App `version` (`epoch`, `revision`). An exact repeat of a committed `request_id` returns the stored receipt with `duplicate: true`, with no second effect and no new audit row; the same ID with different arguments returns `idempotency_conflict`; an old `expected_version` returns `stale_version` with `current_version`. An unknown `sort` or `section` fails the input schema (`invalid_arguments`). Business rejections (`not_found`, `already_reviewed` with a pointer to `reviews.update`, `not_author`, `report_closed`) and every other failure leave data, version and notifications unchanged, and Core appends a denial audit row. Reads return `status: ok` and never change data; Core rejects a read that tries.
  - *Reset.* `env.reset` is Core's: it restores this instance's loaded seed, its photos and the time loaded with it, starts a new epoch and clears receipts and audit. It touches no other App: messages already delivered to Mail stay there, and resetting Mail belongs to run setup. Infra takes `env.snapshot` before a reset to keep evidence. Reset checks compare business data, resources and clock with the loaded baseline, not epoch or audit bytes.
  - *Evaluator records.* App state holds the user's lists, notes, reviews, reports, questions, notifications and applied event IDs, each with the `request_id` or `event_id` that made it. Core audit has one row per allowed call except replays and `env.snapshot`, one per denial and one per clock advance; rows carry before and after versions and event traces but no business `request_id`. The supplied Agent execution trace has arguments and results, including what each read returned. App records join the trace on `request_id` and `event_id`, and join audit rows on the version after the write.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Eight events through `env.apply_event`, each in Core's envelope `{event_id, at, operation, payload}`. `at` carries a UTC offset and is when the event takes effect: Core moves the App clock to it, and the handler reads it as `ctx.virtual_now`. Business dates in the payload (`posted_at`, `edited_at`) are what readers see; they may be earlier than `at` and default to it. The Task chooses content and timing, including replies that depend on what the Agent wrote; Infra applies them; Cornerpost owns the effect. Cornerpost has no timers, and advancing the clock runs no Cornerpost code.

  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `cornerpost.review.added` | `{"event_id":"ev-cp-014","at":"2027-02-24T07:10:00-08:00","operation":"cornerpost.review.added","payload":{"place_id":"pl-07","review":{"review_id":"rv-311","reviewer_id":"u-88","rating":1,"text":"Quoted one price, charged another after pickup was assigned.","recommended":true}}}` | Adds the review to its section, dated `at`; a recommended review changes the average and count |

  A replayed event ID returns Core's first receipt with `duplicate: true`, and the same ID with different content returns `idempotency_conflict`; an event dated before the App clock is rejected (`invalid_event`). Cornerpost also rejects, as `invalid_event` with nothing applied: an unknown place, review, reviewer, question or report; a second review by one reviewer on one listing; a `posted_at` later than `at`; an edit of, or reply to, a removed review; resolving a report that is withdrawn or already resolved.

  *Notifications and Mail.* Cornerpost writes one notification record per occurrence (`owner_reply`, `question_answered`, `report_resolved`) inside the event that causes it; the Agent's own writes create none. Notification IDs include the epoch (`ntf-e<epoch>-<seq>`), so they never repeat after a reset. When `email_notices` is on at that moment, the record also stores the full wire message, built by a pure function in the package. Messaging directions: publishes `channel.email`; receives none.

  Delivery is Infra's job and is not part of Cornerpost's commit. After the event has committed, Infra runs the package's sender script with two connections: its own Infra connection, to read notification records through `env.snapshot`, and the publisher connection the router grants Cornerpost's account. No Cornerpost code gains management access. Like Mail's App-owned [publisher](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/ae889d2cbbfc72242fc5e70b877f3f2a2f029618/examples/apps/mail/src/publisher.cjs), the script only builds and publishes; unlike it, it reads through Infra, because these messages come from events rather than Agent writes. The script keeps a cursor (the last delivered notification) in Infra's run directory. It publishes later records in order, with the notification ID as `request_id`, advances the cursor only on a committed receipt, and never writes back into Cornerpost. Before each delivery, Infra brings Mail's clock to the event time, as the [time contract](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/ae889d2cbbfc72242fc5e70b877f3f2a2f029618/docs/runtime-time.md) requires; Mail dates the message by its own clock. The package ships an unmodified copy of Mail's `router-message.schema.json`; a changed copy is rejected as `incompatible_contract`. Outcomes (router codes; the lists of codes are examples, not exhaustive):
  - `email_notices` off: no wire message is stored and nothing is sent.
  - Infra has not approved Cornerpost as a publisher in the run: no sender runs.
  - Committed: one unread message in the Mail account bound to the user, with the router's receipt; the cursor advances.
  - Retry with the same `request_id` and message in the same router session: the first receipt with `duplicate: true`; still one inbox record.
  - Rejected before dispatch, for example `recipient_unavailable` (no Mail account bound to the person), `recipient_not_authorized` (no grant for that account), `ambiguous_recipient`, `source_identity_mismatch`, `incompatible_contract` or `idempotency_conflict`: Mail has no record, and the cursor stays.
  - Halted, for example `receiver_rejected`, `receiver_unconfirmed`, `receiver_lifecycle_changed` or `concurrent_dispatch`: the router stops the run, and Infra reconciles the cursor against Mail's inbox before continuing.

  In every case delivery leaves Cornerpost's state unchanged, and nothing in it is rolled back.

  Example `owner_reply` message, which passes Mail's `urn:pale:email-message:2` schema at `ae889d2` under `runtime/contracts/schema-validator.cjs` (a copy without `recipient`, and a copy with one `asset_ids` entry, both fail): `{"specversion":"1.0","id":"ntf-cp-e1-0007","source":"urn:pale:app:cornerpost:account:cornerpost-ana","type":"org.pale.mail.notification.v1","time":"2027-03-01T10:30:00-08:00","datacontenttype":"application/json","dataschema":"urn:pale:email-payload:1","data":{"channel":"channel.email","recipient":{"kind":"person_ref","id":"person-ana"},"source_object":{"app_id":"cornerpost","account_id":"cornerpost-ana","object_type":"review","object_id":"rv-512"},"content":{"title":"Harbor Auto Transport replied to your review","body":{"format":"text/plain","text":"Harbor Auto Transport replied to your 2-star review of March 1: \"We are sorry about the pickup delay. The price we quoted is the price you pay.\" Open Cornerpost to see the reply with your review."},"asset_ids":[]},"extensions":[]}}`. This is a schema check, not an integration test.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** The run binding's person ID, App-local reviewer IDs, stored provider references, PNG photos, and no package dependencies.
  - *People and accounts.* The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`, so the user has one person ID across Apps. Notifications address `{"kind":"person_ref","id":<owner_person_id>}`, and Messaging resolves that to the Mail account bound to the same person; with no such account the delivery is rejected as `recipient_unavailable`. Reviewers, owners and other authors are Cornerpost profiles with App-local IDs (`u-…`). A reviewer carries an optional `person_id` only when a task deliberately puts the same person in another App's seed.
  - *External references.* A listing may carry `external_ref: {app_id, account_id, object_type, object_id}` naming a provider App's record. Cornerpost stores and returns it as-is and never resolves it. The package lists these through `plugin.references`. At startup, Core checks references in the package fixtures and assembly seeds against an enabled App's account and object IDs (not `object_type`); references in a seed loaded later with `env.load_seed`, or to an App not in the run, are stored unchecked. Two listings may not share one reference.
  - *Formats.* Stable IDs for listings, reviewers, reviews, photos, questions, answers, reports, lists and notifications; ISO 8601 timestamps with offset on the simulated clock; UTF-8 text; area as a text label, no coordinates. Photos are PNG within the local reference profile (at most 64 assets and 1 MiB per bundle); no OCR.
  - *Standalone package.* `app.json.dependencies` and `contribution.json.dependencies.required_apps` are empty, with `core_api` 1.2.0. Mail is a run-assembly recipient, not a package dependency, so `npm run app:verify -- --app apps/cornerpost` runs with the shared runtime alone; the Mail flow is checked separately with Mail 1.3.0 supplied.

- **Fixture quality rubric:**
  - *Variation (automated):* no two reviews share more than half of their word trigrams; short (under 200 characters), medium and long (400 or more) reviews each make up at least 20% of the fixture; in each category at least one listing has a high average with a recent run of low ratings and one has many not-recommended reviews, so sorts and sections change what a reader sees.
  - *Consistency (automated):* dates are in order (listing, reviewer join, review, reply or answer); each reviewer's review count equals their reviews in the fixture; aggregates recompute from the recommended section; every photo belongs to one review and its caption matches something the review says; not-recommended reviews follow seeded patterns (new accounts, one-review accounts, near-identical text across listings) documented in the README; nothing in the fixture states which business is trustworthy.
  - *Ranking (business tests):* for three fixture listings, the expected first five reviews under each sort and section are fixed, including a tie broken by date and one broken by ID.
  - *Human review:* 60 reviews sampled across categories, sections and ratings, plus all 20 photos. Each review passes or fails on four questions: (1) it reads like a specific customer's account of this business; (2) the stars match the text; (3) the date, reviewer history and any reply are plausible together; (4) it does not repeat another review in substance. A photo passes if it fits its review and caption. Every failed item is rewritten; if more than 6 of the 60 fail, a fresh sample of 60 is reviewed after the fixes.
