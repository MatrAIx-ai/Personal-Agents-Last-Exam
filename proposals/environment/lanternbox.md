## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Lanternbox is a resettable streaming account shared by one household. The account holder's agent can see and manage every profile, so the same request ("add the new season to our list", "mark what we watched", "drop us to the cheaper plan") has a different right answer depending on who watched what, on which profile, whose history a change would erase and which child's limits it would loosen. Nothing is played by the agent: what people watch arrives as task-supplied events, and every time-based state follows the environment clock. Four workflows are complete in the first version.

**Entities.** Account (plan, `paid_through`, last charge, a pending downgrade or cancellation, the owner profile), plan (three fixed plans: price, `max_profiles`, a quality label), profile (name, kind `adult` or `kids`, `maturity_ceiling`, autoplay, two notification settings), title (a film or a series with seasons and episodes; maturity rating, genres, `available_from`, optional `available_until`), playable (a film or one episode, with its runtime and release time), progress (one row per profile and playable: position, `completed`, when and by whom it was set), history row (one per recorded viewing: profile, device, playable, start time, seconds watched, `hidden`), watchlist entry (per profile), device (name, kind, last use, `signed_in`), charge (period, amount, result), notification.

**Simplifications.** One App instance holds one account. There is no video, artwork, search ranking, recommendation engine, simultaneous-stream limit, downloads, gift cards, payment method or real payment: a charge is a task-supplied event with a result. Maturity is one of five levels (`all`, `7+`, `13+`, `16+`, `18+`); a `kids` profile cannot go above `7+`. Profile locks and PINs are not modelled: the Agent acts as the account holder, who can open every profile. Plans are `basic` (2 profiles), `standard` (4) and `premium` (5).

- **W1 Catalog and viewing state (read-only).** Search the catalog (by text, kind, genre, maturity, availability; optionally as one profile, which leaves out what that profile may not see), read one title with its seasons and episodes and, for a profile, each playable's progress and the `next_up` episode; list a profile's progress (in progress, completed), viewing history and watchlist; read the account, plans, charges, devices and notifications. Each title carries a derived `availability`: `upcoming`, `available`, `leaving_soon` (within 14 days of `available_until`) or `expired`. Paths: modify, cancel and confirm are not applicable to reads; errors = unknown title, playable, profile or device (`not_found`), `invalid_window`.
- **W2 Lists, progress and history, per profile.** Add a title to a profile's watchlist or remove it; set a profile's progress on one playable (a position, completed, or back to the start); hide or unhide one history row. Paths: modify = `progress.set` again; cancel = `watchlist.remove`, `progress.set` to the earlier value, `history.hide` with `hidden: false`; errors = a title above the profile's ceiling (`restricted`), a title that has expired or a playable not yet released (`unavailable`), `already_listed`, `not_listed`, a position below zero or beyond the runtime (`invalid_position`), any write while the account is suspended or cancelled (`account_inactive`). Confirm: reads return what a write would touch; the write is the commitment. Agent edits to progress are marked `set_by: agent` and add no history row, so what was actually watched stays distinguishable from what was marked.
- **W3 Profiles and devices.** Create, edit or delete a profile; sign a device out. Deleting a profile removes its progress, history and watchlist and cannot be undone inside the run. Lowering a ceiling deletes nothing: titles above it become invisible to that profile and return when the ceiling is raised. Paths: modify = `profiles.update`; cancel = not offered for a deletion (the result says how many rows were removed); errors = the plan's profile limit, counting a pending downgrade's limit when there is one (`limit_reached`), deleting the owner profile (`owner_profile`), a `kids` profile above `7+` (`invalid_ceiling`), a duplicate name (`name_taken`), a device already signed out (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `profiles.get` returns the counts that a deletion would remove.
- **W4 Plan and billing.** Read the plans and the charges; change plan; cancel; undo a pending downgrade or cancellation. An upgrade takes effect at once and the next charge carries the new price. A downgrade is stored as pending and takes effect at `paid_through`. A cancellation leaves the account active until `paid_through`. Paths: modify = `plan.change` again (it replaces a pending downgrade); cancel = `plan.resume` while the change is still pending; errors = the same plan (`same_plan`), a downgrade to a plan with fewer profiles than the account has (`too_many_profiles`), nothing pending to undo (`no_pending_change`), cancelling twice or resuming after the account has ended (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `plans.list` returns, for each plan, whether the account could move to it now and what would have to change first.

Rules:

- Time is the environment's. Lanternbox has no timers and no clock event: Infra moves `virtual_now` with an event or with Core's `env.advance_time`. Title availability, the account's status and whether a pending change is due are stored as due times and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever they are read or checked. Writes and events use the same derivation.
- What was watched and what was marked stay apart. Only `playback.recorded` writes history rows. `progress.set` changes one progress row and records who set it and the value it replaced.
- A profile sees a title only if the title's rating is at or below its ceiling. The same check is used by profile-scoped reads, by `watchlist.add`, by `progress.set` and by `playback.recorded`.
- `next_up` for a series and a profile is the first released episode, in order, that the profile has not completed. It is computed when read.
- The profile limit is the current plan's, or the pending downgrade's when one exists, so a household cannot grow past the plan it is about to move to.
- Notifications are written for what other people or the service do, never for the Agent's own writes and never for a change that time alone causes: `episode.released` for each profile that has the series on its watchlist or in progress and has that setting on; `title.leaving` likewise when an end date is set; `payment.failed`; `plan.changed` when a pending downgrade takes effect with a charge; `device.new` for a sign-in from a device the account has not seen.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** The app knows what each profile did, not who was on the sofa. Two people who watch one series partly together and partly apart leave progress that no counter reconciles; a child's profile has a ceiling that an older cousin's visit should not loosen; the cheaper plan fits the budget only if somebody's profile, with a year of history, is deleted; a film on a list expires three days before the evening it was saved for; a cancellation is harmless or not depending on a finale date and on who else uses the account. Lanternbox makes these checkable: which profiles, progress and dates the agent read, which rows it changed and for whom, what it deleted, what plan change it asked for and when, and what the household did afterwards. Distinct task situations (separate Task proposals): (1) resume a shared series from what each viewer actually saw (Task #83 describes this need); (2) move to the cheaper plan before the next charge when the household has one profile too many, and one of them belongs to a relative who still uses it; (3) set up a profile for a visiting child without changing anyone else's; (4) a listed film is leaving before the date the user planned to watch it with someone; (5) a failed charge during a trip, where the right step is to tell the user in time, not to change the plan; (6) hide a surprise: one viewing must not show up in a partner's shared history. The rationale is independent of any one task. Fixtures are plain seed data, so each task loads its own household, catalog, history and billing dates.

**Boundaries and overlap.** Proposal list checked on 2026-11-23: no Environment proposal covers a streaming catalog, per-profile progress and history, or household profiles. In the same domain, #20 (a minimal subscription-cancellation flow), #45 and #62 (live-event and cinema ticketing) and #194 (a personal photo library) do not hold viewing state. #124 (Treefort) and #11 (Beam) cover family settings and purchases on a game platform, with play-time limits and a store; Lanternbox has neither. A plan change here follows the service's own rules; money leaving a card or bank stays in the finance Apps, and Lanternbox never reads or writes them.

## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** None in the first version. All four workflows complete with Lanternbox alone, and no workflow needs the Agent to call another App's tools. Notifications are an in-App log read with `notifications.list`; no email, chat or calendar message is sent, so there is no messaging declaration (`publishes` and `receives` are empty). Calendar, Mail, Messages, Contacts, Files, Notes and Tasks: none.
- **Other apps and websites (required):** None required. A task that also uses a card, bank or family-chat App seeds consistent data in each; there is no App-to-App channel. No external website, catalog or payment service is called at run time.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 23 Agent-facing MCP operations over stdio (`service.cjs --role agent`), registered in `tools.json` as `lanternbox.<name>`. Read (12): `account.get`, `plans.list`, `billing.history`, `profiles.list`, `profiles.get`, `catalog.search`, `titles.get`, `progress.list`, `history.list`, `watchlist.list`, `devices.list`, `notifications.list`. Write (11): `watchlist.add`, `watchlist.remove`, `progress.set`, `history.hide`, `profiles.create`, `profiles.update`, `profiles.delete`, `devices.sign_out`, `plan.change`, `plan.cancel`, `plan.resume`. Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version` and returns the object's `id` with the committed version. The Agent acts as the bound account holder set by Infra, never through an argument; the profile a call concerns is always the argument `profile_id`, so a task can narrow tools, profiles and fields with `policies.json` (for example, read-only access to other people's profiles). App errors: `not_found`, `restricted`, `unavailable`, `already_listed`, `not_listed`, `invalid_position`, `limit_reached`, `owner_profile`, `invalid_ceiling`, `name_taken`, `same_plan`, `too_many_profiles`, `no_pending_change`, `account_inactive`, `invalid_state`, `invalid_window`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Infra-only operations: `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the host's `env.advance_time` of runtime 1.6.0; they are not listed on the Agent connection and Core returns `permission_denied` if the Agent calls them. The first version is MCP-only: no GUI, no reviewer console and no playback surface.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). The seed schema carries catalog records (titles, seasons, episodes, the three plans) and account records (account, profiles, progress, history rows, watchlist entries, devices, charges, notifications, activity rows), so a task can load any household and any history, including an account that is already past due or has a pending downgrade. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, more profiles than the plan allows, a `kids` profile above `7+`, progress beyond a playable's runtime, history for a title above the profile's ceiling, timestamps without a UTC offset and an account that the binding does not declare (`invalid_seed`). Writes follow Core: an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` or `event_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`; the same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`; a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`; a failed call leaves data and version unchanged. Reads never change business data: availability, status and `next_up` are computed from stored records and `virtual_now`. Reset is Core's: it restores the loaded seed and the time loaded with it (`reset_scope: app_instance`) and touches no other App. Evaluator records come from three places: (a) App state in the snapshot: activity rows with sequence, virtual time, actor `agent` or `event`, action, object ID, `profile_id` and the `request_id` or `event_id` behind them, one for every committed Agent write and every applied event, with the replaced value for `progress.set` and the removed counts for `profiles.delete`; (b) Core audit rows for allowed and denied calls, reads included; (c) full arguments and results in the supplied Agent execution trace. They join on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Count committed writes from the activity rows, rejected attempts from the denial audit rows, and replays (a result carrying `duplicate: true`) as retries of the original. Time-driven changes write no row: read them from the stored due times and the clock. Take the snapshot before a reset.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Six events through `env.apply_event`, in Core's envelope. Lanternbox has no clock event. An event is handled at its own time, which is `ctx.virtual_now` inside the handler. Core rejects an event dated before the App's current time; Lanternbox also rejects a duplicate title, playable or device ID, an unknown profile, device, title or playable, a viewing of a title that is restricted for the profile or not available at that time, a position beyond the runtime, a viewing while the account is suspended or cancelled, an end date earlier than the event, and a charge whose period does not start at the account's `paid_through` (`invalid_event`, state unchanged).

Notifications, each written once per occurrence: `episode.released`, `title.leaving`, `payment.failed`, `plan.changed`, `device.new`. Messaging directions: publishes none; receives none.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for plans, profiles, titles, playables, history rows, devices, charges and notifications. The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` are the shared values of the run's `binding.json`. A profile may carry an optional `person_id`, so a task that has the same people in Contacts or Messages gives them the same IDs in its seeds; Lanternbox stores a profile name and settings, not a copy of a contact. Lanternbox is the source of truth for its catalog, profiles, progress, history, lists, devices and charges and keeps no copy of another App's records. Missing data is explicit: an unknown ID returns `not_found`; a title with no end date has `available_until: null`; a playable a profile never opened has no progress row; a series with every released episode completed has `next_up: null`. All records are JSON; there are no images, audio, video or attachments (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the profile's 64 assets). Runtime needs: the public Core runtime 1.6.0 on `main` at commit `a4bce02`; `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; no external service. The package runs on its own: a copy that holds only `apps/lanternbox/` and the pinned runtime starts the App, and its business tests and example run from the package alone. It imports and reads nothing outside its own folder and uses no other App's files or state. Dates use IANA time zones through Node's built-in `Intl`, so no package is installed.
