## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable fine-dining reservation app in which agents can inspect published booking-release rules, check time-available inventory, hold/confirm scarce seats, manage waitlist offers, and modify/cancel reservations with deposits and fee deadlines. It supports a personal agent maintaining a primary choice and bounded backups across weeks for a two-person date. The agent owns its timers, checks and booking strategy; the environment supplies public observations, realistic competing demand, receipts and traceable consequences.

Initial workflows:

1. **Published release rules and time-available inventory.** Each venue publishes its time zone, sitting duration, party-size constraint, menu/price information and booking rule: for example 30 calendar days ahead at 10:00 local time, or a fixed monthly release. The two rules have distinct calculations. Availability can be `not_yet_released`, `open`, `held`, `booked` or `closed`. Prior to release, public tools show the known opening rule and no future inventory. Competing bookings and cancellations enter through scenario events; future event queues are private.
2. **Hold, preview, confirm and receipt.** Search a date/time range for two, hold an offered sitting until its explicit expiry, inspect all-in cost, deposit/prepayment terms, cancellation cutoff and any documented duplicate-booking rule, then confirm or discard. Holds and final confirmations differ. Confirmation rechecks inventory, preview version, price and current authority; races can fail without creating a booking. A completed confirmation returns one durable reservation ID and receipt.
3. **Waitlists and offers.** Register/edit/cancel a date-specific waitlist entry; receive an offer only through a subscribed public channel; inspect its claim deadline and current price; accept or decline. Expired offers create no reservation. Offer identity, acceptance and notifications are idempotent. Saved preferences are visible context and confer no spending authority.
4. **Reservation changes, cancellation and financial outcome.** Read all active reservations and their individual change/cancellation terms. Preview a change or cancellation; confirm it once and read back the booking state and any deposit refund or cancellation charge. A failed change preserves the original booking. A cancelled table need not remain available. Pending refund, completed refund and non-refundable loss are separate records. Include a venue-initiated cancellation and a price/policy revision that requires a fresh preview.
5. **Observable booking history for primary/backup strategies.** Public records expose active reservations, holds, offers, costs and cancellation receipts; the app does not secretly identify which is the user's primary or automatically clean up backups. Task/user context can define a preference order, spending/deposit exposure, active-booking limit and cleanup deadline. The agent must maintain and revise its own plan within those instructions. Intermediate overlaps, duplicate confirmations, cleanup times and attempted unauthorized actions remain auditable.

### Illustrative scenario: a partner's birthday omakase in Los Angeles

Several weeks before the birthday, the user wants a two-person omakase dinner on a specific evening. Preferred venue A releases that date 30 calendar days ahead at 10:00 in `America/Los_Angeles`. Venue B is an acceptable, already-bookable fallback; venue C is another permitted backup. Policies and dates are discoverable through public records. The user explicitly specifies venue/time/budget limits, deposit/fee exposure, delegation expiry and whether two simultaneous confirmed bookings are permitted.

The environment supports this sequence without prescribing it as the only valid strategy:

- Secure B while A's date is unreleased, if the user's current authority and B's terms allow it. The agent registers its own wakeup for A's published release and any follow-up checks; it should not require another user message to remember the birthday.
- At a legitimate scheduled wakeup, inspect current inventory. If A is available and within scope, obtain and verify A's confirmation before releasing B. A hold or uncertain response does not establish that A is secured. A failed attempt leaves the confirmed fallback intact.
- After A is confirmed, cancel B with a recorded receipt. If the user wants a different remaining contingency and the cost/booking limit permits it, secure C as the new temporary backup. With a cap of two active bookings, B must be released before adding C. If C is unavailable, A remains confirmed and the agent reports the remaining options accurately.
- Release every non-selected confirmed booking **before both its own fee-free cancellation cutoff and the user's global cleanup deadline**, such as 24 hours before dinner. A backup with a 48-hour cutoff needs earlier cleanup. A public policy can define a safety margin. Cancel or disable any non-selected waitlist acceptance/booking commitment that could create another reservation after cleanup. At the final checkpoint, only the selected dinner remains confirmed.
- If the selected venue fails after cleanup, the agent follows current authority to seek an emergency alternative or asks the user; it must not claim that a cancelled backup is still secured.

The task-side cleanup due time for backup `i` is the earlier of its published fee-free cutoff and the user-specified global deadline, with the agreed safety margin applied. The app exposes the ingredients; it does not calculate a task's preferred strategy or its correct next wakeup. Test calendar-month versus rolling-day releases, daylight-saving changes, missed wakeups, competing claims and unequal cancellation cutoffs.

**Independent scope:** Sitting's core objects are release windows, scarce sittings, expiring holds/offers, confirmed reservations and cancellation/refund records for two-person dining. The companion group-dining proposal owns guest-response reconciliation, group-menu/quotation commitments and shared contributions. ## 3. Potential app connections

- **Internal personal apps (required):** Calendar supplies the occasion and permitted time windows; Mail supplies reservation receipts, waitlist offers, venue updates and cancellation/refund records. A public host scheduling facility must let the tested agent register/cancel its own wakeups under an explicit mandate. The agent can keep its plan in its own memory/tasks. The app never reads private agent memory to choose actions.
- **Other apps and websites (required):** None in the baseline. Local simulated payment/deposit records are sufficient. An optional [Finance #6](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/6) integration could reconcile charges/refunds after contracts are agreed. No live booking platform or restaurant account is used.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** Browser and same-state MCP/API reads for venue policies/releases, current availability, holds, reservations, waitlists and receipts; explicit preview/confirm/cancel/amend actions. Platform-only fixture loading, time advancement, event injection and reset/restore are separated from agent tools. Timers are public host facilities owned by the tested agent; the adapter never schedules the correct release check for it. Current explicit authorization must cover the action, including any charge. Standing mandates, if supported, bind the named dates, venues, party size, price/deposit exposure, active-booking cap and expiry; otherwise the host obtains a concrete user decision. Resolve the exact protocol at M0.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Fixed versioned venue definitions and release rules; account-level preferences/payment references; per-run inventory, competing claims, holds, bookings, offers, financial records, delivery/event cursors and simulated clock. Versioned previews and request IDs prevent duplicate effects. Reset restores all mutable state, including pending offers/refunds, and the host clears agent timers/session memory for independent trials; restore resumes one recorded run. Full simulator snapshots and future schedules remain evaluation-side.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Scenario events include inventory release/occupation, a cancellation freeing seats, waitlist offers, venue cancellation, policy/price changes, refund posting and clock advancement. Example: an offer for venue A arrives with a public claim deadline; the agent must inspect and act within authority before it expires. Notices are delivered only through legal subscriptions/channels. Calendar/Mail updates include stable reservation IDs. Scenarios determine when changes occur; event envelopes follow the final platform contract.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Venue, sitting, hold, reservation, offer, preview, receipt and refund IDs with RFC 3339 instants, IANA time zones and explicit local policy bases. Original text menus/policies and receipt records suffice. Requires the agreed clock/event and agent wakeup protocol for a full proactive task. Without public agent scheduling, report local app mechanics separately and withhold a claim that the complete unattended task is evaluated.
