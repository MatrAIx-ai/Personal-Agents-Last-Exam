## 2. What you plan to build

**Your answer (required):** A reservation and assistance module for personal travel: free holds, paid confirmations, cancellations, equipment verification, provider assistance replies, designated journeys and actual physical travel outcomes. A booking, staff confirmation and successful boarding are distinct facts.

Workflows: Inspect routes/windows -> hold a feasible option -> request current equipment verification and route-specific assistance -> confirm/designate a journey -> adapt after service/readiness changes -> cancel unused paid bookings -> reconcile actual travel and ledger.

Entities: route version, capacity dimensions, explicit reservation/confirmation/cancellation windows, booking, measured equipment request, provider reply, selected physical itinerary, actual trip outcome, wallet and append-only spending ledger.

Business rules: A held slot is not paid or confirmed assistance. Provider confirmation describes the declared dimensions; it cannot magically know a user is using stale measurements. Actual travel checks current equipment, service, readiness and matching assistance at departure. Budget authority is a task/user rule, separate from technical balance. Cancellation preserves financial history. Late outbound reservations cannot be manufactured after an outage.

Reusable value: Airport assistance with changed baggage, school transport after a vehicle substitution, and time-sensitive accessible appointments can reuse the reservation/physical outcome model. Route IDs and event schedules are fixture data, not business-code answers.

Related work: [Hopline #171](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/171) covers route/facility lookup and a fare card; TransitWallet covers per-departure reservations, equipment-specific assistance and performed travel. Its reservation wallet is a separate synthetic account for reservation charges, not a second representation of Hopline's fare-card balance. There is no balance synchronization, transfer or required Hopline dependency in v1. A future combined assembly must designate one owner for each actual balance and separately review any financial integration. Approval is non-exclusive. Companion [Task #152](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/152) depends on validated delivery; the App does not depend on its grader or actor script.

### V1 state, money and modification rules

These decisions address review items 2 and 4 before scope approval; the platform/time mapping also resolves the intended behavior for items 1 and 3.

| Object | States and transition producer | Preconditions / observable failure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Booking | Agent hold creates `held`; successful confirm makes `confirmed`; explicit cancel makes `cancelled`; a departure attempt consumes a confirmed ticket (`consumed`), including a failed physical boarding. An unconfirmed hold may be effectively `expired` without a stored-state edit. | Hold requires the dated service's open reservation window and free capacity. Confirm requires an unexpired hold, open confirmation window and sufficient wallet funds. One live booking per account/dated service; group tickets are out of v1. Failures include `window_closed`, `capacity_exceeded`, `booking_exists`, `hold_expired`, `insufficient_funds`, `invalid_state`. |
| Verification request | Agent creates `pending` for a known equipment ID; Infra replies `performed` or `refused`; Agent may cancel a pending request. Performed measurements remain immutable observations when later equipment changes. | At most one pending request for the same equipment/kind. Reply identifies the exact attempt and source actor. `request_exists`, `request_not_pending`, `wrong_subject`, `stale_generation`. No request or acknowledgement counts as a measurement. |
| Assistance request | Agent requests `pending` against an exact booking generation, service and declared equipment snapshot; Infra makes it `confirmed` or `refused`. Agent may cancel pending/confirmed assistance before departure; cancellation retains prior replies. | At most one active request for that booking; re-request after cancellation/refusal is a new attempt. A refusal changes only the request, not the paid booking or wallet. Old-dimension confirmation stays historically true for that snapshot but does not establish fit for changed equipment. |
| Journey | Agent designates one `planned` journey per booking; Agent may cancel it before departure. Infra departure phase makes it `in_transit` or `failed`; arrival phase makes an in-transit journey `completed`. | Departure checks confirmed ticket, actual current equipment dimensions, service state, readiness and matching confirmed assistance at that instant. A physical incompatibility is a recorded failed outcome with reasons, not a successful booking or a tool fault. Cancelled/consumed journeys cannot be reused; arrival without departure is rejected. |
| Wallet / ledger | Seed includes an opening credit. Confirmation appends one debit; eligible paid cancellation appends one refund. Balance is derived from those ordered immutable entries. | Amounts are non-negative integer minor units in one fixture currency; each financial row references its booking, action and originating Core request/event ID. No Agent credit/edit/delete operation and no silent correction of past debits. |

**Prices, refunds and authority.** A free hold reserves capacity but creates no debit or financial authorization. Confirmation charges the locked quoted fare. Each offer explicitly supplies `fare_cents`, `cancel_before` and `cancel_fee_cents` (0 through the paid fare); paid cancellation before its cutoff returns fare minus that retained fee. A non-refundable offer explicitly has fee equal to fare. Free holds, including effectively expired ones, can be cancelled without a fee. At/after a paid cancellation cutoff, or after a departure attempt, cancellation returns `cancellation_closed` with no state or ledger change. Fixture fees are synthetic, not a real operator policy.

The read-only booking preview shows fare, retained fee, refund, resulting technical balance and cutoff; a financial confirm/cancel call must carry its matching preview digest and fresh Core version. The digest covers the booking/offer generation, quoted monetary terms and relevant deadline state, and is recomputed before commit; changed terms return `quote_changed`. This is two-step business confirmation, not a native platform user-consent mechanism. User spending authority remains a Task/user rule; wallet capacity and an Agent's confirmation do not grant it.

**Included modification/cancellation paths.** V1 rescheduling is explicit cancel-and-rebook: obtain a free hold on the new dated service, cancel the old booking under its quoted refund policy, then confirm the new booking and designate a new journey. Each step has its own visible receipt. Old-booking cancellation also cancels its active assistance/designation, preserving every prior reply; equipment verification observations remain available. This sequence is not an atomic exchange: if the later confirm fails, the earlier cancellation/refund remains and the Agent must reconcile. A new hold may expire or be refused without altering the old booking. In-place edits of a paid route/date/party, transferred assistance clearance, automatic operator compensation after travel failure, multi-passenger bookings and cross-App wallet transfers are excluded; explicit independent records avoid silently reusing stale assistance or rewriting financial history.

### Event matching, examples and time

**No guessed run-time IDs.** Seeded dated-service IDs and equipment IDs are stable. The App assigns ordinary object IDs; its creation receipts also expose predictable, monotonically increasing business generations:
- Booking selector: `{service_id, booking_attempt}`, with attempt incremented per bound account/service for each new hold, including holds later cancelled/expired.
- Verification selector: `{equipment_id, kind, request_attempt}`; assistance adds `service_id, booking_attempt` instead of equipment as its parent, while preserving its declared equipment snapshot.
- Journey selector: `{service_id, booking_attempt, journey_attempt}`. Only one currently planned journey may be bound to a booking.

Counters never restart on cancel/rebook or re-request within a run. A response's selector must match that historical generation, its state and the expected subject/source, not merely the newest request. Exact duplicate event IDs/payloads replay through Core; changed replay conflicts. Zero matches, cancelled/non-pending targets, wrong equipment/service or stale generations are rejected with no accepted business effect or clock change; Core denial audit remains. More than one match is `ambiguous_target`, a seed/invariant failure, never a broadcast or arbitrary selection.

Selectors are scoped to the bound account/run and the Core epoch captured with the target snapshot. The Infra adapter sends that snapshot's outer `expected_version`; after reset/seed replacement it discards prepared targets rather than attaching a fresh version to an old event. This uses Core's existing version boundary, not an invented App context epoch field. A Task can prewrite first-attempt selectors. For branching actions, its trusted actor driver reads the public Infra `env.snapshot`, resolves the scripted business intent to one matching object and copies its returned selector into the event. This driver adapter must be delivered with the App examples/tests; no inaccessible private service is assumed. If there is no applicable request/selection, the driver records that in its own trace and emits no reply/travel event. It does not retarget an old reply to a new generation. Invalid direct injections remain negative App tests, not model-difficulty evidence.

Each line below is a planned JSON event example; prerequisite holds/requests/selection are created first in the runnable example, not fabricated by the event. Actor/source IDs are seeded and validated for the expected kind.
```jsonl
{"event_id":"tw-note-1","at":"2027-02-08T16:00:00-06:00","operation":"record.add","payload":{"id":"provider-notice-1","source_actor_id":"provider-1","body":"Morning lift status will be confirmed tomorrow."}}
{"event_id":"tw-measure-1","at":"2027-02-08T16:05:00-06:00","operation":"verification.reply","payload":{"selector":{"equipment_id":"chair-1","kind":"measurement","request_attempt":1},"source_actor_id":"user-1","status":"performed","observation":{"width_mm":740,"equipment_revision":2}}}
{"event_id":"tw-route-1","at":"2027-02-09T06:10:00-06:00","operation":"route.update","payload":{"service_id":"rail-out-20270209","source_actor_id":"provider-1","status":"lift_unavailable"}}
{"event_id":"tw-assist-1","at":"2027-02-09T06:10:00-06:00","operation":"assistance.reply","payload":{"selector":{"service_id":"wide-out-20270209","booking_attempt":1,"request_attempt":1},"source_actor_id":"provider-1","status":"confirmed","equipment_revision":2}}
{"event_id":"tw-depart-1","at":"2027-02-09T07:30:00-06:00","operation":"journey.perform","payload":{"selector":{"service_id":"wide-out-20270209","booking_attempt":1,"journey_attempt":1},"phase":"departure","source_actor_id":"provider-1","actual_equipment":{"width_mm":740,"equipment_revision":2},"user_ready":true}}
{"event_id":"tw-arrive-1","at":"2027-02-09T08:30:00-06:00","operation":"journey.perform","payload":{"selector":{"service_id":"wide-out-20270209","booking_attempt":1,"journey_attempt":1},"phase":"arrival","source_actor_id":"provider-1"}}
```
Effects: `record.add` appends context; `route.update` appends provider service-status/physical-capacity history, without editing locked fares or refund terms; measurement replies append performed observations; assistance replies preserve the requested equipment snapshot; departure records physical success/failure and consumes the ticket; arrival records actual completion only after a successful departure. Events never imply a future arrival at departure time. Route-policy/price rewrites, unsolicited financial credits and spontaneous matching to an unrequested journey are not supported in v1.

**Deadline effects are computed, not scheduled.** All gates compare explicit timestamps with `ctx.virtual_now`; opening is inclusive, closing/expiry is exclusive. A held booking is effectively expired when now reaches the earlier of its hold expiry or confirmation cutoff, and no longer consumes capacity. Reads return both stored and effective status with the cause; reads do not normalize/mutate stored records. Every write re-evaluates those same predicates before changing state. A cancelled or consumed booking also stops contributing future reservable capacity. No background release job is needed. Paid confirmation, cancellation and service reservation windows close by those predicates even if no event arrives; a completed financial effect is never inferred from clock advancement.

Infra's `env.advance_time` moves only the Core clock; it neither invokes these events nor changes business records. Actual replies, route changes, departures and arrivals require explicit scheduled Infra events at their own times. The caller processes them in chronological order before advancing beyond them, with explicit tie ordering. The App keeps no second clock, timer or scheduler. Invalid late/backdated events cannot be repaired by silently moving time backward.
