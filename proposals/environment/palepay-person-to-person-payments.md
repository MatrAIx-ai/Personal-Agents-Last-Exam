# Environment Track proposal

**PR title:** `[Environment Proposal] [Money & insurance + Social & communities] PalePay person-to-person payments (Venmo-inspired)`

## 1. App and domains

- **Authors / GitHub handles (required):** Huawei Lin ([@huawei-lin](https://github.com/huawei-lin))
- **Reference app or website / URL (required):** PalePay, an independently branded synthetic app for paying, requesting and splitting money between people. Venmo (https://venmo.com) is the public interaction reference; the transfer and bill-splitting features of Alipay are comparable. No third-party branding, data or assets are used.
- **Primary domain (required):** Money & insurance
- **Secondary domains (optional):** Social & communities

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** PalePay is a resettable app for money between people: pay a friend, ask to be paid back, split a shared cost and see who still owes what. Every payment carries a note and an audience (public, friends or private), a completed payment cannot be recalled, and two accounts can have nearly the same name. The App records and executes what it is told; it never decides who should pay, how a cost should be divided or whether a request is legitimate.

Shared life produces shared costs: a cabin, a dinner, a gift, rent, a ride. Tasks that involve other people usually end with someone owed money, and the right amount depends on what the group agreed and what changed. Without a payments App, each task would have to invent its own ledger.

Six workflows, each complete end to end:

1. **Find people.** Search by name, handle or shared person ID; a profile shows handle, display name, join date and number of mutual friends. Send a friend request or remove a friend; block or unblock an account.
2. **Pay.** Preview a payment with recipient, amount, note, audience and funding source, then send it. Sending completes at once and cannot be undone; the only remedy is a request to the recipient.
3. **Request.** Ask a person for money, remind them, or cancel the request. Accept or decline a request received from someone else; accepting pays it through a preview, like workflow 2.
4. **Split.** Divide one expense among several people by equal shares or stated amounts, which creates one request per other person. See who has paid, change or cancel unpaid shares; paid shares are fixed.
5. **Activity and privacy.** List past payments and requests, change the audience of a past payment, and set the default audience. Who can see each payment is readable from state.
6. **Balance.** View the PalePay balance and the linked bank account, move balance to the bank by standard transfer (free, completes later) or instant transfer (fee, completes at once), and cancel a standard transfer while it is pending.

Core examples (precondition → action or event → outcome → control case). Tool names here and in §3 omit the `palepay.` prefix.

| Workflow | Example |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Two accounts are both displayed as "Owen Park" → `people.get` on each → different handle, join date and mutual-friend count → an unknown profile ID returns `not_found`. |
| 2 | A preview shows recipient, $390.00, note and audience → `payments.send` with that preview ID → payment completed, balance reduced → a send with a new `request_id` and the same preview is rejected (`preview_consumed`) and nothing moves; a retry with the original `request_id` returns the first receipt. |
| 3 | An outgoing request is unpaid → `requests.cancel` → the request is closed and can no longer be paid → canceling a request that was already paid is rejected. |
| 4 | A $1,950.00 expense is split among five other people → five requests of $390.00 → one is paid, then `splits.update` lowers the total to $1,700.00 → the paid share stays $390.00 and the four unpaid requests become $327.50. |
| 5 | A payment was sent with a public note → `activity.visibility.set` to private → accounts other than the two parties no longer see it → changing a payment the user is not party to is rejected. |
| 6 | A standard transfer is pending → `transfers.cancel` → funds return to the balance → canceling after the `palepay.transfer.completed` event is rejected. |

Illustrative downstream task: six friends paid for a ski cabin, one drops out, and the group is rebooked into a smaller place. The agent must work out each person's share under the rule the group agreed, refund the right person the right amount, and keep the reason for dropping out off every note that others can see. A companion Task proposal will define the people, the agreement, the rubric and model trials; this App encodes no task's answer.

**Distinct scope:** By our reading of the proposal list on 2026-11-24: Finance [#6](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/6) covers transfers between the user's own accounts and brokerage orders, with no other people, requests, splits or audience settings. Ledgerleaf [#203](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/203) (plan approved) is a long-lived group expense ledger with several payers, split rules, debt simplification and records of settlements made elsewhere; it moves no money and has no payment requests, audiences or bank balance. PalePay moves money between people, and its split is a single expense that creates payment requests. The two are complementary, as #203 also notes: a task could work out an obligation in Ledgerleaf and pay it in PalePay, and neither depends on the other. Provider Apps that charge for group activities, such as group dining [#40](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/40), could be followed by a PalePay split without either App knowing about the other.

- **Other apps and websites (required):** None at run time. The linked bank account is a balance held inside PalePay, not a connection to another App. A payment may carry an optional external reference `{app_id, account_id, object_type, object_id}` to an order in another App; it is stored and returned, never resolved.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 30 Agent-facing MCP business tools over stdio in the public runtime, registered in `tools.json` as `palepay.<name>`. Reads (12): `people.search`, `people.get`, `friends.list`, `activity.list`, `activity.get`, `requests.list`, `splits.list`, `splits.get`, `balance.get`, `transfers.list`, `settings.get`, `approvals.list`. Writes (18): `friends.add`, `friends.remove`, `people.block`, `people.unblock`, `payments.preview`, `payments.send`, `requests.create`, `requests.remind`, `requests.cancel`, `requests.respond`, `splits.create`, `splits.update`, `splits.cancel`, `activity.visibility.set`, `settings.privacy.set`, `transfers.preview`, `transfers.create`, `transfers.cancel`. Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version`. No sign-in, card number or bank credential flow exists. Management operations are Infra-only: Core's portable lifecycle operations (`env.load_seed`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.apply_event`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle`) and runtime 1.6.0's `env.advance_time`; an Agent call returns `permission_denied`. An optional read-only renderer (activity, person, split) is for human reviewers only (`audience: ["reviewer"]` in `gui.json`); it has no controls and is not an Agent computer-use interface.
  - *Previews.* `payments.preview` (for a new payment, or with `for_request` to accept an incoming request) and `transfers.preview` store a preview that freezes the exact action: kind, counterparty profile, amount, fee, note, audience, funding source and, for acceptance, the request. No money moves. The preview ID is the only input that says what `payments.send`, `requests.respond` with `decision: accept`, and `transfers.create` do. Declining needs no preview. A preview is single-use and has no time-based expiry; it stops being usable only when consumed or when what it froze no longer holds.
  - *Writes, replay and preview reuse.* A committed write returns `status: committed`, the affected object (payment, request, split, transfer or preview) with its status, and the App `version` (`epoch`, `revision`). Cases, each with its own test:
    - Response lost after a committed send: a retry with the same `request_id` and arguments returns the stored receipt with `duplicate: true`; no second payment and no new audit row (Core).
    - Same `request_id`, different arguments: `idempotency_conflict` (Core).
    - New `request_id`, preview already used: `preview_consumed`; nothing moves.
    - Preview no longer holds at send time (insufficient funds, request closed, counterparty blocked by either side): `preview_stale`; nothing moves; the Agent makes a new preview.
    - Old `expected_version`: `stale_version` with `current_version` (Core). A preview stays usable across later versions as long as its frozen facts hold; the Agent sends it with the current version.

    Every rejection leaves data, version and notifications unchanged, and Core appends a denial audit row. Reads return `status: ok` and never change data.
  - *User approval.* PalePay does not decide whether the user approves; a trusted caller does, and PalePay checks that the approval matches the exact action.
    - The seed sets `approval_mode`: `none` (default) or `required`. The Agent can read it with `settings.get` but has no tool to change it.
    - New approvals arrive only as the Infra event `palepay.approval.recorded`. The user harness applies it when the simulated user answers, or the Task schedules it.
    - An approval binds either to one `preview_id` or to a `match` on the action:
      - `kind`: `payment`, `request_accept` or `transfer`;
      - `amount_cents`: for a transfer, the amount taken from the balance, before the fee;
      - `counterparty_profile_id` for payments and acceptances, or `speed` for transfers;
      - optionally `note`, `audience` and `funding_source`.

      A `match` lets a scripted approval be recorded before the preview exists. A field the match leaves out is not covered: a grant for "$390.00 to Theo" that does not pin the note also allows a public note, so a Task that grades the note should pin it or bind to the preview. An approval is single-use and is consumed with the preview it matches.
    - With `approval_mode: required`, a money-moving write looks at the decisions that match its preview, and the latest one wins:
      - an unused grant lets the write proceed and is consumed;
      - a denial rejects it (`approval_denied`) and voids any earlier grant it matches;
      - no matching decision gives `approval_required`.

      A grant for $390.00 does not cover a preview for $400.00. `approvals.list` shows recorded approvals and whether each is used.
    - Core's policy profile remains available: a Task can drop the money-moving tools from the Agent's `allow` list so that the Agent cannot move money at all.
    - Missing bridge: the live path from a user's chat reply to `env.apply_event` belongs to the planned Runner and user harness (see [environment architecture](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/ae889d2cbbfc72242fc5e70b877f3f2a2f029618/docs/environment-architecture.md)). Huawei Lin owns the App-side event and checks. Until the bridge exists, Tasks schedule approval events by exact match, or run with `approval_mode: none` and grade from the trace whether the Agent asked first.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** One account per instance; balances derived from a ledger with opening entries; author-owned fee, split and visibility rules; Core's reset.
  - *Isolation and seed.* State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one user account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). Other people are profiles; what they do arrives only as events. The seed is history: profiles, the friendship graph (including friendships between other profiles, for mutual-friend counts), blocks, settings (`default_audience`, `email_notices`, `approval_mode`), past payments, requests and transfers, open splits, recorded approvals, and the ledger. Notes are at most 280 characters, and the serialized seed stays under 256 KiB. Because Core reruns `validateSeed` on the state after every write and event, the tool input schemas enforce the same note limit. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, split shares that do not sum to their total, timestamps without a UTC offset, a record dated after the loaded clock (`ctx.virtual_now`), amounts that are not integer cents, and an account the binding does not declare (`invalid_seed`). The package ships `fixtures/clock.json` for its own fixture.
  - *Ledger.* Amounts are integer cents in one currency (USD). The ledger is the only source of balances: the PalePay balance and the linked bank balance are each the sum of their entries and are never stored. A seed has exactly one `opening` entry per balance, dated before every historical item; each historical payment, accepted request and transfer then has its own entry, so every initial balance is counted once. `validateSeed` also rejects a money-moving record without its entry (or an entry without its record), a history item dated before the opening entries, and a running balance below zero. A fixture helper derives the opening amounts from the balances a task author wants at load time.
  - *Policies.*
    - Instant transfer fee: 1.5% of the amount, rounded half up to the cent, minimum $0.25, maximum $15.00, deducted from the amount transferred. An instant transfer must be at least $1.00 (`amount_below_minimum`). Standard transfers and payments are free.
    - Equal split: each share is the total divided by the number of participants, rounded down to the cent; the leftover cents go one each to the first participants in the order given. The user's own share, if included, is part of the split but creates no request.
    - Updating a split: paid shares are fixed. For an equal split, the rest of the new total is divided equally among the unpaid shares (including the user's own), with the same rounding. For a split by stated amounts, the update must give new unpaid amounts that sum to the new total minus what is already paid (`amounts_mismatch` otherwise). A new total below the sum already paid is rejected (`total_below_paid`); a refund is a separate payment.
    - Visibility: `public` means every profile in the instance; `friends` means the two parties and their friends; `private` means the two parties only. The two parties always see their own payment; anyone else blocked by either party is always excluded. `activity.get` returns the current viewer set, computed at read time from the audience, the current friendships and the current blocks. It also returns `viewers_at_send`, stored once when the payment completes. A later friendship or block change alters the first, not the second. Requests are visible only to their two parties.
  - *Reset.* `env.reset` is Core's: it restores this instance's loaded seed and the time loaded with it, starts a new epoch and clears receipts and audit. It touches no other App: messages already delivered to Mail stay there, and resetting Mail belongs to run setup. Infra takes `env.snapshot` before a reset to keep evidence. Reset checks compare business data and clock with the loaded baseline, not epoch or audit bytes.
  - *Evaluator records.* App state holds the ledger, payments with both viewer sets, requests, splits, transfers, previews, approvals, notifications and applied event IDs, each with the `request_id` or `event_id` that made it. Core audit has one row per allowed call except replays and `env.snapshot`, one per denial and one per clock advance; rows carry before and after versions and event traces but no business `request_id`. App records join the supplied Agent execution trace on `request_id` and `event_id`, and join audit rows on the version after the write.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Ten events through `env.apply_event`, each in Core's envelope `{event_id, at, operation, payload}`. `at` carries a UTC offset and is when the event takes effect: Core moves the App clock to it, and the handler reads it as `ctx.virtual_now` and dates the resulting records with it. People are named by stable profile IDs, never by handle, and payment requests by `payment_request_id`, so that `request_id` always means Core's idempotency key. The Task chooses what other people do and when, scheduled ahead or chosen by the harness in reaction to what the Agent sent; Infra applies it; PalePay owns the effect and contains no responder logic. PalePay has no timers, and advancing the clock runs no PalePay code.

  *Transfer timing.* A standard transfer starts `pending`, with an `estimated_by` date (3 days after creation) that is shown only; it completes or fails only when the Task schedules `palepay.transfer.completed` or `palepay.transfer.failed`, and the clock passing `estimated_by` changes nothing. `transfers.cancel` succeeds only while the transfer is pending; after either event it is rejected (`not_cancelable`). An event for a transfer that is canceled, already settled or instant is rejected. An instant transfer completes inside `transfers.create`, with no event, and cannot be canceled or fail. Simultaneous events are applied in the order the Task gives. A scheduled event that the Agent's actions have overtaken, such as a completion after a cancel or a `palepay.request.paid` after a split update changed the amount, is rejected and changes nothing; a Task that depends on such ordering should expect the rejection or let the harness choose the event in reaction to the Agent.

  A replayed event ID returns Core's first receipt with `duplicate: true`, and the same ID with different content returns `idempotency_conflict`; an event dated before the App clock is rejected (`invalid_event`). PalePay also rejects, as `invalid_event` with nothing applied: an unknown profile, request, transfer or preview; a request event for a request that is not open; a payer or amount that differs from the request; a payment from a profile the user has blocked; an approval whose `by_person_id` is not the account's owner, or whose `preview_id` is unknown or already consumed.

  *Notifications and Mail.* PalePay writes one notification record per occurrence (`payment_received`, `request_received`, `request_paid`, `request_declined`, `transfer_failed`) inside the event that causes it; the Agent's own writes create none. Each record has an App-local ID (`ntf-<seq>`) and notes whether `email_notices` was on at that moment. Reset restarts that sequence, and an App cannot see the epoch, so the builder below forms the CloudEvents `id` and the `request_id` from the account, the snapshot's `epoch` and the record ID (for example `palepay-ana-e1-ntf-31`); they never repeat after a reset. Messaging directions: publishes `channel.email`; receives none.

  Delivery is Infra's job and is not part of PalePay's commit. After the event has committed, Infra reads the notification records with its own `env.snapshot` call and passes them to a pure builder exported by the package, which turns each record into the wire message. Infra then publishes through the publisher connection the router grants PalePay's account. The builder holds no connection, so no PalePay code gains management access. Mail's own [publisher](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/ae889d2cbbfc72242fc5e70b877f3f2a2f029618/examples/apps/mail/src/publisher.cjs) reads through its Agent connection because it sends Agent-written mail; PalePay's notices come from events, so Infra does the reading. Infra keeps a cursor per epoch (the last delivered notification) in its run directory. It publishes later records in order, advances the cursor only on a committed receipt, and never writes into PalePay, so no payment, request or transfer depends on delivery. Before each delivery, Infra brings Mail's clock to the event time, as the [time contract](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/ae889d2cbbfc72242fc5e70b877f3f2a2f029618/docs/runtime-time.md) requires; Mail dates the message by its own clock. The package ships an unmodified copy of Mail's `router-message.schema.json`; a changed copy is rejected as `incompatible_contract`. Outcomes (router codes; the lists of codes are examples, not exhaustive):
  - `email_notices` off: no wire message is stored and nothing is sent.
  - Infra has not approved PalePay as a publisher in the run: no sender runs.
  - Committed: one unread message in the Mail account bound to the user, with the router's receipt; the cursor advances.
  - Retry with the same `request_id` and message in the same router session: the first receipt with `duplicate: true`; still one inbox record.
  - Rejected before dispatch, for example `recipient_unavailable` (no Mail account bound to the person), `recipient_not_authorized` (no grant for that account), `ambiguous_recipient`, `source_identity_mismatch`, `incompatible_contract` or `idempotency_conflict`: Mail has no record, and the cursor stays.
  - Halted, for example `receiver_rejected`, `receiver_unconfirmed`, `receiver_lifecycle_changed` or `concurrent_dispatch`: the router accepts no further publishes for the run, and Infra reconciles the cursor against Mail's inbox before the next run.

  In every case delivery leaves PalePay's state unchanged, and nothing in it is rolled back.

  Example `request_paid` message, which passes Mail's `urn:pale:email-message:2` schema at `ae889d2` under `runtime/contracts/schema-validator.cjs` (a copy without `recipient`, and a copy with one `asset_ids` entry, both fail): `{"specversion":"1.0","id":"palepay-ana-e1-ntf-31","source":"urn:pale:app:palepay:account:palepay-ana","type":"org.pale.mail.notification.v1","time":"2027-02-11T09:40:00-08:00","datacontenttype":"application/json","dataschema":"urn:pale:email-payload:1","data":{"channel":"channel.email","recipient":{"kind":"person_ref","id":"person-ana"},"source_object":{"app_id":"palepay","account_id":"palepay-ana","object_type":"request","object_id":"rq-107"},"content":{"title":"Theo Lund paid your request for $390.00","body":{"format":"text/plain","text":"Theo Lund (@theo-lund) paid your request for $390.00 (Cabin weekend, share 2 of 5). Your PalePay balance is now $1,204.50. Open PalePay to see the split."},"asset_ids":[]},"extensions":[]}}`. This is a schema check, not an integration test. Messages go only to the account owner, so a note's audience setting does not apply to them.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** The run binding's person ID, stable profile IDs, text-only records, and no package dependencies.
  - *People and accounts.* The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`, so the user has one person ID across Apps. Notifications address `{"kind":"person_ref","id":<owner_person_id>}`, and Messaging resolves that to the Mail account bound to the same person; with no such account the delivery is rejected as `recipient_unavailable`.   - *Formats.* Stable profile, payment, request, split, transfer, preview, approval and notification IDs; ISO 8601 timestamps with offset on the simulated clock; UTF-8 notes, emoji included. No images or other files in the first version.
  - *Standalone package.* `app.json.dependencies` and `contribution.json.dependencies.required_apps` are empty, with `core_api` 1.2.0. Mail is a run-assembly recipient, not a package dependency, and Contacts is not a dependency at all, so `npm run app:verify -- --app apps/palepay` runs with the shared runtime alone; the Mail flow is checked separately with Mail 1.3.0 supplied.
