## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Pegboard is a resettable staff-scheduling app, seen from one hourly employee's account, in which a shift stays that employee's responsibility until a coworker has accepted it and the manager has approved, and every request has a cut-off, so a change the app accepts can still be wrong for this person's classes, childcare or pay. The account sees the posted shifts of the whole team and holds the employee's availability, time off and requests to hand a shift over, trade it, release it or pick one up; managers and coworkers act through task-supplied events, and every time-based state follows the environment clock. Four workflows are complete in the first version.

**Entities.** Workplace (time zone, week start, locations, positions with an hourly rate, and a policy of five values), person (the account holder and coworkers, each with the positions they hold and an optional personal hours cap), week (when it was posted), shift (location, position, start, end, assignee or open, and a history of changes), availability (weekly blocks when the employee cannot work), time-off entry, handoff (one of four kinds: `cover`, `trade`, `release`, `pickup`; the people and shifts it names, its cut-off and its history), notification with its release time, account preferences.

**Simplifications.** One App instance holds one employee account at one workplace with one or more locations; a second job stays in Calendar. Managers and coworkers are not accounts: what they do arrives as events. There is no time clock, timesheet, payroll, tips, breaks or overtime pay: "estimated pay" is scheduled hours times the position's rate and is labelled an estimate. The policy is five values per workplace: the minimum rest between working days (`min_rest_hours`), the weekly hours cap (`max_weekly_hours`; a person may have a lower cap of their own), the cut-off for handoffs (`handoff_cutoff_hours` before the shift starts), the notice for time off (`time_off_notice_days`) and whether cover, trades and pickups need the manager (`manager_approval`). A shift counts in the week and on the day in which it starts, in the workplace's time zone.

- **W1 Schedule and hours (read-only).** List shifts in a window (mine, a coworker's, open ones, by position or location), read one shift with its history, list the team, read the workplace policy, read a weekly summary (scheduled hours, the cap that applies, hours left, days worked, estimated pay) and check in advance whether someone may take a shift (`eligibility.check`: `eligible`, every rule it would break, the weekly hours and the rest gaps that would result, and the cut-off). Each shift of mine carries `flags`: `overlap`, `short_rest`, `over_hours`, `outside_availability`, `during_time_off`. A manager's schedule is a fact and is never rejected, so a posted week can break the policy, and the flags say where. Paths: modify, cancel and confirm are not applicable to reads; errors = unknown shift or person (`not_found`), `invalid_window`.
- **W2 Availability, time off and preferences.** Set the weekly blocks when I cannot work; ask for time off for a range of dates with a reason; cancel it; set the account's time zone, quiet hours, email notices and open-shift notices. Paths: modify = `availability.update` (it replaces the pattern; flags follow at the next read and nothing is rescheduled) / `preferences.update`; cancel = `time_off.cancel`, while pending or, once approved, before its first day; errors = less notice than the policy asks (`too_late`), a date on which I hold a shift (`shift_assigned`: get it covered first), dates that overlap another pending or approved entry (`conflict`), a range that has started, ends before it starts or is longer than 30 days (`invalid_window`), cancelling an entry that is closed (`invalid_state`), an unknown time zone (`invalid_timezone`). Confirm: the request is the commitment; the decision is the manager's and arrives as an event.
- **W3 Getting a shift covered, and answering coworkers.** Ask one named coworker to take my shift (`cover`) or to exchange it for one of theirs (`trade`), or ask the manager to release it to the open shifts (`release`). The shift stays mine until the last approval. Answer a coworker's cover or trade request to me (`handoffs.respond`: accept or decline). Paths: modify = not offered, because a handoff records what was asked: cancel it and ask again; cancel = `handoffs.cancel` while it is pending; errors = a shift that is not mine (`not_yours`) or, in a trade, not the coworker's (`not_theirs`); a shift that has started or whose cut-off has passed (`too_late`); the taker does not hold the position (`not_qualified`), would work two shifts at once (`overlap`), would have less than the minimum rest (`short_rest`) or would pass the weekly cap (`over_hours`), checked for both people in a trade; a shift that already has a pending handoff (`already_requested`); answering or cancelling a handoff that is not waiting for me (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `eligibility.check` previews the same checks without writing; the request or the acceptance is the commitment.
- **W4 Open shifts.** List open shifts, check eligibility and pick one up (`handoffs.pickup`): approved at once where the workplace does not ask for the manager, otherwise pending the manager's decision; withdraw while pending. Paths: modify = not applicable; cancel = `handoffs.cancel`; errors = the shift is not open (`not_open`), my own pickup of it is already pending (`already_requested`), the cut-off has passed (`too_late`), and the same eligibility errors as W3. Confirm: as in W3, `eligibility.check` previews and the pickup is the commitment.

Rules:

- Time is the environment's. Pegboard has no timers, no scheduler and no clock event: Infra moves `virtual_now` with an event or with Core's `env.advance_time`, which runs no App code. Every time-based state is therefore stored as a due time and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever it is read or checked: a shift's `phase` from its start and end, a handoff's or a time-off entry's `expired` from its cut-off or first day, a notification's `held` or `released` from its release time. Writes and events use the same derivation: an expired handoff cannot be answered, decided or cancelled, and a shift that has started cannot be handed over. The Infra-run sender uses it too (§3).
- Responsibility does not move while anything is pending. A shift belongs to its assignee until a handoff that names it is `approved`; a pending, declined, denied, cancelled, void or expired handoff changes no shift.
- One eligibility check is used everywhere the employee side acts: by `eligibility.check`, by every handoff I ask for (for the coworker who would take my shift, and for me in a trade or a pickup) and when I accept a coworker's request. A person may take a shift when they hold its position, it overlaps none of their assigned shifts, it leaves at least `min_rest_hours` between the last shift of one day and the first shift of a later day, and their hours in that week stay within the cap (the lower of the workplace's and their own). In a trade each person is checked with the shift they give up left out. A handoff write checks, in this order, that the shift is mine, the coworker's or open as the kind requires, that it has not started and its cut-off has not passed, that it has no pending handoff, and then these four rules; it returns the first refusal, while `eligibility.check` lists every rule that fails.
- Managers are not checked. A posted schedule, a change and a decision are applied as given, and the resulting shifts of mine carry flags. `over_hours` is put on the shifts that take the week past the cap, counted in order of start time; `outside_availability` and `during_time_off` compare a shift with my own blocks and approved time off. Flags are computed when read and never stored.
- A shift can be named by only one pending handoff at a time: a second one from me is refused (`already_requested`), and a coworker's request that names such a shift is rejected as an event.
- Cut-offs. A handoff must be asked for before `handoff_cutoff_hours` ahead of the earliest shift it names, and it expires at that same moment if it is still pending. Time off must start at least `time_off_notice_days` after the day of the request, and a pending entry expires when its first day begins.
- A `void` handoff is one whose subject no longer exists as it was agreed: any manager's change to a shift it names voids it, whatever the change.
- Notifications are written for what other people do, never for my own writes and never for a change that time alone causes: one for each posting that has a shift of mine (`schedule.published`), one for each manager's change that touches a shift of mine before or after it (`shift.changed`, saying whether it was changed, assigned to me, taken from me or cancelled), one for each shift for a position I hold that becomes open after its week was posted, whether it is added open or a coworker's shift loses its assignee, if `open_shift_notices` is on (`open_shift.posted`), one for each request a coworker sends me (`handoff.received`), one for each answer, decision, withdrawal or voiding of a handoff I am part of (`handoff.updated`) and one for each decision on my time off (`time_off.decided`).
- A notification written inside quiet hours (taken in the account's time zone) gets the end of that quiet period as its release time, unless it is about a shift that starts less than 12 hours after it is written, which is never held. Nothing is rewritten when a release time passes.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** The same posted week calls for different actions for different people, and the app only knows half of each person. A trade the app allows can land on a seminar that exists only in Calendar; giving up Saturday can take a student below the hours that pay the rent, or keep another under a cap that matters for a visa; a manager's late change can leave nine hours between a close and an open on the morning of the school run; and a coworker's request for cover can arrive on the day of a recital. Because a shift stays the employee's until a coworker and the manager have both said yes, and every step has a cut-off, the order and timing of what the agent does are part of the answer. Pegboard makes these checkable: which shifts, policy and hours the agent read, which handoffs and time off it asked for, answered or cancelled and how long before each cut-off, what coworkers and the manager decided, and what the account was told. Distinct task situations (separate Task proposals): (1) a manager moves Thursday's shift onto a class, and the agent must get it covered before the cut-off by someone who holds the position and would not pass their own cap; (2) a coworker proposes a trade that passes every check but leaves the user short of the hours she said she needs this month; (3) an open shift appears in a week when the user is four hours short, and picking it up is allowed only after a shorter shift has been released and approved; (4) exam week: time off for the days that are still inside the notice period, and cover for the one that is not; (5) one evening's cover must not turn into a standing change (Task #154 meets the same problem with a volunteer rota kept in Calendar and Notes). No Task proposal depends on a scheduling App yet; the rationale is independent of any one task. Fixtures are plain seed data, so each task loads its own workplace, policy, team, posted weeks and history of handoffs and time off.

**Boundaries and overlap.** Proposal list checked on 2026-11-21: no Environment proposal covers employer-posted shifts, handoffs between coworkers, a manager's approval or checks against a staffing policy. In the same domain, #131 (the shared Files, Messages, Notes, Tasks and Contacts, accepted), #22 (Beacon, a job board), #42 (Claimline, employee expenses), #33 (personal records) and #88 (Forkyard, code hosting) do not schedule work. Calendar 1.3.0 holds the person's own events: it has no approvals, no policy and no cut-offs, and an entry there is not a commitment that an employer recognises. Pegboard never writes it, and an agent that wants a shift in Calendar adds it with Calendar's own tools. Booking Apps such as #15 (Cadence, studio classes), #161 (Tutorwell), #14 (a patient portal) and #5 (handyman slots) are customer-side reservations in which nobody else has to agree. #26 (Stoopside) has sign-up sheets whose slots members claim and release inside a community event, with no positions, hours caps, rest rule or manager. A coworker's chat stays in Messages; a handoff carries only its own short `message`.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** 22 Agent-facing MCP operations over stdio (`service.cjs --role agent`), registered in `tools.json` as `pegboard.<name>`. Read (12): `workplace.get`, `team.list`, `shifts.list`, `shifts.get`, `hours.summary`, `eligibility.check`, `availability.get`, `time_off.list`, `handoffs.list`, `handoffs.get`, `notifications.list`, `preferences.get`. Write (10): `availability.update`, `time_off.request`, `time_off.cancel`, `handoffs.cover`, `handoffs.trade`, `handoffs.release`, `handoffs.pickup`, `handoffs.respond`, `handoffs.cancel`, `preferences.update`. Every write takes Core's `request_id` and `expected_version` and returns the object's `id` with the committed version. The target object is always the argument `id` (for a new handoff, the shift it is about) and a time window is always `start` / `end`; the reference host's audit projection at `61b6371` keeps `id`, `start` and `end` among the few inputs it records, so its audit rows show the shift or handoff and the window of every call, reads included. The objects are called handoffs and time-off entries, with `handoff_id` and `time_off_id` in events, so nothing can be confused with Core's `request_id`. The Agent acts as the bound account holder set by Infra, never through an argument; a task can narrow tools, objects and fields with `policies.json`. App errors: `not_found`, `not_yours`, `not_theirs`, `not_open`, `too_late`, `not_qualified`, `overlap`, `short_rest`, `over_hours`, `already_requested`, `shift_assigned`, `conflict`, `invalid_window`, `invalid_state`, `invalid_timezone`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`; Pegboard also answers `invalid_arguments` for what an input schema cannot express (an empty `patch`, availability blocks that overlap, a handoff to myself). Infra-only operations: `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle`, and the host's clock operation `env.advance_time` of runtime 1.6.0; they are not listed on the Agent connection and Core returns `permission_denied` if the Agent calls them. The first version has no GUI and no reviewer console, and no manager's console: managers act only through events.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). The seed schema carries workplace records (policy, locations, positions, people, posted weeks, shifts with their history) and account records (preferences, availability, time-off entries, handoffs with their history, notifications, activity rows), so a task can load any history, including past weeks, handoffs in every state and a schedule that already breaks the policy. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, a shift that does not end after it starts, a handoff whose state does not fit the shifts it names (an approved cover whose shift is still mine), two pending handoffs on one shift, timestamps without a UTC offset and an account that the binding does not declare (`invalid_seed`). The seed is history: everything in it has already happened, and new facts arrive only through events. Writes follow Core: an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` or `event_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`, so a repeated request creates no second handoff; the same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`; a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`; a failed call leaves data, version and notifications unchanged and Core appends a denial audit row. Reads never change business data: phases, expiry and flags are computed from stored records and `virtual_now`, and `eligibility.check` writes nothing. Reset is Core's: it restores the loaded seed and the time loaded with it (`reset_scope: app_instance`) and touches no other App; messages already delivered to Mail stay there. Evaluator records come from three places: (a) App state in the snapshot: activity rows with sequence, virtual time, actor `agent` or `event`, action, object ID and the `request_id` or `event_id` behind them, one for every committed Agent write, one for every applied event and one for each effect of that event (a shift reassigned, a handoff voided, a notification written); each shift's history; each handoff's and time-off entry's history with its state after every step and its cut-off; notifications with creation time and release time; (b) Core audit rows with virtual time and the projected `id`, `start` and `end`: one for every allowed call that is not a replay, one for every denied call, and one for every `env.advance_time` with the clock before and after; (c) full arguments and results in the supplied Agent execution trace. They join on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Counting rules: count committed writes and event effects from the activity rows, one row each. Core returns the stored receipt for a repeated `request_id` or `event_id` before it appends an audit row, so a replay appears only in the trace, as a call whose result carries `duplicate: true`: count it as a retry of the original, never as a new action. A rejected attempt writes no activity row: count rejected attempts from the denial audit rows and the trace, one for each attempt, repeats included. Count reads from the allowed audit rows and the trace. Time-driven changes (a handoff or a time-off entry expired, a shift started or ended, a notification released) run no App code and write no row: read them from the stored due times and the clock, and from the audit row of the `env.advance_time` call or the event that moved it. Take the snapshot before a reset, because reset clears the audit.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Eight events through `env.apply_event`, in Core's envelope. Pegboard has no clock event: Infra moves time with an event or with `env.advance_time`. An event is handled at its own time T, which is `ctx.virtual_now` inside the handler in runtime 1.6.0: a handoff whose cut-off has passed by T is expired and cannot be answered, decided or withdrawn, and a notification written at T is held or released by the quiet hours at T. Core rejects an event dated before the App's current time (`invalid_event`); Pegboard also rejects a duplicate shift or handoff ID, an unknown location, position or person, a shift that does not end after it starts or is longer than 16 hours, a change or cancellation of an unknown, cancelled or ended shift, a `handoff.received` that names a shift not assigned as it states, already started, past its cut-off or already in a pending handoff, and an answer, decision or withdrawal for a handoff or time-off entry that is not waiting for it (`invalid_event`, state unchanged).

  Notifications, each written once per occurrence: `schedule.published`, `shift.changed`, `open_shift.posted`, `handoff.received`, `handoff.updated`, `time_off.decided`. Messaging directions: publishes `channel.email` only; receives none. The App writes the notification log and, when `email_notices` is on at that moment, the full wire message with the notification, dated at its release time. An Infra-run sender in the package, an outbound adapter that Infra runs with its own Infra and publisher connections (the pattern of Mail's `src/publisher.cjs`), reads notifications through `env.snapshot`, treats one as released once the snapshot's `virtual_now` has reached its release time, and calls `router.publish` for an account that has a Mail account bound in the run and an Infra grant for `channel.email`; it does not write back into Pegboard, so no cross-App transaction is assumed. A notification held at 23:10 is therefore published after Infra advances the clock past 07:00, with no App event. A delivery rejected before dispatch creates no Mail record; an unknown acknowledgement halts the router for reconciliation; neither rolls back Pegboard. - **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for locations, positions, people, shifts, handoffs, time-off entries and notifications. The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` are the shared values of the run's `binding.json`, so the person has one ID in Pegboard and Mail, and notifications address the recipient by `person_ref`. Coworkers and managers are named by `person_id`; a task that has the same people in Contacts or Messages gives them the same IDs in its seeds, and Pegboard stores only what a staffing app knows about them (a display name, positions and an hours cap), not a copy of a contact. Pegboard is the source of truth for its shifts, handoffs, time off, availability and notifications and keeps no copy of another App's records. Missing data is explicit: an unknown ID returns `not_found`; an open shift has `assignee: null`; a person without a cap of their own has `max_weekly_hours: null` and the workplace's applies; a week that was never posted has no shifts and no record. All records are JSON; there are no images, audio, video or attachments (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the profile's 64 assets). Runtime needs: the public Core/Messaging runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` commit `61b6371`; Mail is optional; `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; no external service. The package runs on its own: a copy that holds only `apps/pegboard/` and the pinned runtime starts the App, and its business tests and example run from the package alone. It imports and reads nothing outside its own folder and uses no other App's files or state; the two real-MCP scripts are given the location of the pinned runtime, and the Mail flow also needs the pinned Mail package. Weeks, days and quiet hours use IANA time zones through Node's built-in `Intl`, so no package is installed.
