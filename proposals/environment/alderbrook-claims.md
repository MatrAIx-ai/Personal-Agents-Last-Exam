## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Alderbrook Mutual claims is a resettable insurer's claims portal, seen from one policyholder's account, in which the insurer decides each repair line in writing, one deductible applies to the whole claim, and every answer has a deadline, so a step the portal accepts can still be wrong for this person's loan, repair shop or household. The account holds the person's policies and claims and what the person files, answers, disputes, accepts or rejects; the adjuster, the repair shop and the lender act through task-supplied events, and every time-based state follows the environment clock. Four workflows are complete in the first version.

**Entities.** Insurer (time zone and five claim rules), policy (auto or renters; its term; the named insured and listed people; vehicles, each with an optional lienholder, or the insured address; coverages, each with a deductible and an optional limit; for an auto policy an optional rental benefit), claim (policy, coverage, the vehicle or address, loss time, description, the driver of an auto claim where known, path, state and history), evidence item (a record of a photo, receipt, report or statement: its kind, date and description), information request (one or more asks, each with a key; its due time; the answer to each ask), estimate (initial or supplement; from the shop, the adjuster or the policyholder; its lines with amounts), determination (the insurer's written decision for lines: approved with an amount, or denied with a reason), dispute (on one line), offer (a total-loss settlement offer), payment (amount, kind and payee), rental period, notification, account preferences.

**Simplifications.** One App instance holds one policyholder account at one fictional insurer. The adjuster, the repair shop and the lender are not accounts: what they do arrives as events. Claims are first-party property claims only: collision and comprehensive on an auto policy, and personal property on a renters policy. There is no injury, liability or fault, and no other driver's insurer. There are no premiums, bills, quotes, renewals or surcharges. Evidence has no file bytes: an evidence item is a record, with an optional opaque reference to a file kept elsewhere. Money does not move: a payment is a record. Amounts are the ones the estimate and the determination state; the App computes no depreciation and no vehicle value. The repair shop's own work orders, invoices and ledger are outside the App. The insurer's claim rules are five values: how many days after a loss a claim may be filed (`notice_days`), how many days an information request gives when the event names no due time (`request_response_days`), how many days a determination can be disputed (`dispute_window_days`), how many days an offer stays open when the event names no time (`offer_response_days`) and how many days the rental benefit goes on after a total-loss offer (`rental_grace_days`).

- **W1 Policies, claims and money (read-only).** List and read policies with their coverages, people, vehicles, rental benefit and lienholder (`policies.list`, `policies.get`); check in advance whether a loss can be claimed (`coverage.check`, with the policy, the coverage, the vehicle or address and the loss time: `covered`, the coverage that applies and its deductible, every rule a filing would break, the last day to file, and, when an estimated amount is given, the payment to expect and the flag `under_deductible`); list claims in a window of filing time (`claims.list`: by state, policy or coverage; the list filters and sorts by filing time only) and read one claim with its history, evidence, requests, estimates, the standing decision for each line, disputes, offers, rental periods, payee direction and money summary (`claims.get`); read one estimate with its lines (`estimates.get`); list payments in a window of issue time (`payments.list`); list notifications (`notifications.list`); read the account's preferences (`preferences.get`). A claim carries `flags`, listed under Rules. What the insurer, the shop and the lender do is taken as given, so a claim can be overpaid or paid to another party than the one named, and the summary and the flags say so. Paths: modify, cancel and confirm are not applicable to reads; errors = unknown object (`not_found`), `invalid_window`.
- **W2 Filing a claim and answering the insurer, and preferences.** File a claim on a policy for one coverage, with the time of the loss, a description, the vehicle or the insured address and, where known, a police report number and who was driving: a listed person, a named person who is not on the policy, or nobody yet (`claims.file`); correct its description, its police report number or its driver (`claims.update`); add evidence items (`claims.add_evidence`); answer one or more asks of an information request, each by its key, with text and evidence items (`requests.respond`); withdraw the claim (`claims.withdraw`); set the account's preferences (`preferences.update`: the time zone used in notification text, and `email_notices`). Paths: modify = `claims.update` for the description, the police report number and the driver while the claim is open; the policy, the coverage, the vehicle or address and the loss time cannot change: withdraw the claim and file again; an answer to an ask is given once and is not edited; cancel = `claims.withdraw` while the claim is open, nothing has been paid and no offer has been accepted; errors = an unknown time zone (`invalid_timezone`), an unknown policy, vehicle, person or ask (`not_found`), a loss time in the future (`invalid_window`), a policy that was not in force at the loss time (`not_in_force`), a policy without that coverage (`not_covered`), a filing later than `notice_days` after the loss (`too_late`), a second open claim on the same policy for the same coverage, the same vehicle or address and the same loss day (`duplicate_claim`), a claim that is not open, a request that is not open or an ask that already has an answer (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `coverage.check` previews the same checks without writing; the filing is the commitment.
- **W3 Repairs: estimates, written decisions, supplements and who is paid.** The shop's or the adjuster's estimate and every later supplement arrive as events on the same claim; I can also submit an estimate or a supplement that a shop gave me (`estimates.submit`). The insurer's determination decides lines in writing, and a line with no decision is `pending`. Dispute one line that was denied or approved for less than its estimate, with a reason and evidence items (`determinations.dispute`); say who is paid for repairs, me or a named shop (`payee.set`). Paths: modify = not offered for an estimate or a dispute, because each records what was sent: a correction is a supplement or a new dispute after a new decision; `payee.set` can be called again and applies to later payments only; cancel = not offered: a dispute stays until it is decided; errors = a line approved in full or still pending (`nothing_to_dispute`), a line whose standing decision already has a dispute, open or decided (`already_disputed`), a dispute later than `dispute_window_days` after the decision (`too_late`), an estimate, a dispute or a payee change on a claim that is not open, or an estimate or a dispute on a claim that is on the total-loss path (`invalid_state`). Confirm: `claims.get` shows the standing decision and the money summary before any step; the submission or the dispute is the commitment.
- **W4 Total-loss offers and the rental benefit.** For a vehicle the insurer will not repair, a settlement offer arrives as an event, with a valuation, the lender's payoff where the vehicle has a lienholder, and a time to answer by. Accept the offer (`offers.accept`) or reject it with a reason and evidence items (`offers.reject`). Start a rental period under the policy's rental benefit, with its start time and its daily rate (`rental.start`), and end it, with its end time (`rental.end`). Paths: modify = not offered for an offer: the insurer answers a rejection with a new offer, which replaces the old one; a rental period is corrected by ending it and starting another; cancel = not offered: an acceptance is final for the amount of the claim; errors = an offer whose time to answer has passed (`too_late`), an offer that is not open (`invalid_state`), a rental on a policy with no rental benefit (`not_covered`), a second active rental on one claim (`already_active`), a rental on a claim that is not open (`invalid_state`), a rental that starts before the loss, before the end of an earlier rental period of the same claim or after the current time, or that ends before its start or after the current time (`invalid_window`). Confirm: `claims.get` shows what an open offer would pay to the lender and to me, and what the rental has cost me so far; the acceptance is the commitment.

Rules:

- Time is the environment's. Alderbrook has no timers, no scheduler and no clock event: Infra moves `virtual_now` with an event or with Core's `env.advance_time`, which runs no App code. Every time-based state is therefore stored as a due time and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever it is read or checked: the last day to file from the loss time, a request's `overdue` flag from its due time, an offer's `expired` from its `respond_by`, the end of a dispute window from the decision time, and the covered days of a rental. Writes and events use the same derivation: an expired offer cannot be accepted, and a late dispute is refused. An offer counts as `open` only before its `respond_by`, for every rule, the validation of the seed and of events included. Nothing is rewritten when a due time passes.
- A day is a calendar day in the insurer's time zone. A limit of N days after a moment ends at the end of the calendar day that is N days after the day of that moment: this holds for `notice_days`, for `dispute_window_days`, and for a request or an offer whose event names no time. The loss day of a claim is the calendar day of its loss time. Rental days are counted the same way (see the rental rule).
- One check is used wherever a loss is brought to the insurer: by `coverage.check` and by `claims.file`. In this order: the policy and the vehicle or address exist; the loss time is not in the future; the policy was in force at the loss time; the policy has the coverage; the filing is within `notice_days` of the loss; and no open claim on the policy has the same coverage, the same vehicle or address and the same loss day. `claims.file` returns the first refusal, while `coverage.check` lists every rule that fails.
- The insurer, the shop and the lender are not held to that check or to any amount. An estimate, a determination, an offer, a payment, a completed repair and a closing are applied as given; only the structure of the data is validated (§3). A claim can therefore be paid more than it is owed, or paid to another party than the one on file, and flags say where.
- A claim has one deductible, whatever the number of estimates and supplements. A supplement adds lines to the same claim. The latest determination that names a line stands for that line, and no line is ever deleted.
- The money summary of a claim on the `repair` path is computed when read: `approved_total` is the sum of the approved amounts, and it is zero while no line is approved or no estimate exists; `insurer_owes` is `approved_total` minus the deductible, never below zero and, where the coverage has a limit, never above it, and `over_limit` is the part the limit cuts off; `paid` is the sum of the repair payments; `still_due` is `insurer_owes` minus `paid`, never below zero, and anything beyond is `overpaid`; `my_share` is the deductible, or `approved_total` if that is lower; `not_approved_total` is what the estimates ask for and no decision has approved (pending lines, denied lines, and the part of a line above its approved amount). On the `total_loss` path the summary comes from the standing offer, which is the accepted offer or, while none is accepted, the latest offer in whatever state, shown with that state: `net` is the valuation minus the deductible, never below zero; `to_lienholder` is `net`, or the payoff if that is lower, and zero when the vehicle has no lienholder; `to_me` is the rest; `shortfall` is the part of the payoff that `net` does not cover. `paid_to_lienholder` and `paid_to_me` are the sums of the settlement payments to each. Only an accepted offer is owed: `still_due_to_lienholder` and `still_due_to_me` are its two amounts minus what is paid, never below zero, and zero while no offer is accepted; `overpaid` is what the settlement payments exceed the accepted `net` by, or all of them while no offer is accepted. Repair payments made before the claim moved to this path stay in the summary as `paid`. A repair payment belongs to the `repair` path and a settlement payment to the `total_loss` path, and an event that mixes them is rejected.
- A line can be disputed when it is denied, or approved for less than its estimate, for `dispute_window_days` from that decision. A decision can be disputed once: after its dispute is decided, it cannot be disputed again, and the decision that a dispute produces cannot be disputed either (`already_disputed` in both cases). A newer determination that names the line makes an open dispute on it `void`, and the newer decision can be disputed in its turn.
- `payee.set` names who is paid for repairs: me, or a shop by name. A repair payment that names no payee takes the direction on file at that moment; one that names a payee is applied as given. A change of direction applies to later payments only. A settlement payment always names its payee, the lienholder or me, and `payee.set` has no effect on it. A rental payment that names no payee goes to me.
- A claim has at most one open offer. The first offer moves the claim to the `total_loss` path: from then on estimates, determinations, disputes and a completed repair are refused, from me and from events, open disputes become `void`, and the lines stay as history. An acceptance is final for the amount of the claim: no later offer is taken. A rejected, expired or superseded offer cannot be accepted.
- The rental benefit pays a day's rate up to the policy's daily cap for each covered day. A rental period uses one rental day for each calendar day from its start day up to, and not including, its end day; a period that is still active is counted up to, and not including, the current day. The benefit stops at the start of the earliest of these days: the day that is `rental_grace_days` after the day of the first offer on the claim; the day the repair is reported complete; and the day the claim is withdrawn or closed. It also stops once the claim's rental periods, taken together in order of start, have used the policy's longest number of days. Rental days before the stop are covered and later ones are uncovered. `my_cost` is the full rate for each uncovered day plus, for each covered day, the part of the rate above the cap. All of it is computed when read.
- Flags are computed when read and never stored. On a claim: `request_overdue` (an open request is past its due time; it can still be answered while the claim is open), `lines_pending`, `lines_denied`, `under_deductible` (lines are approved and `insurer_owes` is zero), `overpaid`, `payee_mismatch` (a repair payment went to another party than the direction on file at that moment), `offer_expired` (the latest offer expired with no answer), `below_payoff` (the standing offer has a `shortfall`), `rental_uncovered` (a rental period has uncovered days). `under_deductible` is also returned by `coverage.check` when an estimated amount is given.
- Notifications are written for what the insurer, the shop and the lender do, never for my own writes and never for a change that time alone causes (an offer that expires and a request that becomes overdue write none). One is written for each event on a claim of mine: `request.issued`, `estimate.submitted`, `determination.issued`, `dispute.decided`, `offer.issued`, `payment.issued`, `repair.completed` and `claim.closed`. A notification is listed from the moment it is written.

**Value for personal-agent tasks.** The same message from a repair shop, "can we start the extra work today?", calls for different answers for different people, and the insurer only knows half of each person. A supplement that is "on file" is not a supplement the insurer approved, and telling the shop to go ahead can leave the person owing for work nobody covers. An offer the portal lets the person accept can be lower than what is still owed on the loan, and accepting it is final. A claim below the deductible can be filed and pays nothing. A rental that runs past the covered days costs the full daily rate. An information request asks who was driving, and only the person knows. Because each line has its own written decision, one deductible covers the whole claim, an acceptance cannot be taken back and every answer has a deadline, the order and the timing of what the agent does are part of the answer. Alderbrook makes these checkable: which policies, decisions, offers and summaries the agent read, which claims it filed and with which facts, which requests it answered and when, which lines it disputed, which offers it accepted or rejected and how long before the deadline, who it named as payee, and what the account was told. Distinct task situations (separate Task proposals): (1) a shop sends a supplement and asks to start, and the agent must hold or release the work by the written decision on those exact lines and by the person's own limit on personal cost (Task #128 meets this problem and plans a task-owned insurer portal, shop inbox and payment allocation for it); (2) a total-loss offer is lower than the loan payoff, it expires in seven days and the rental benefit ends five days after it, and the person's notes say what she will not accept; (3) a cracked windshield costs less than the deductible, and the person has said not to file claims that pay nothing; (4) the insurer asks who was driving and for a statement, the driver was the person's son, and the agent must not state what it does not know; (5) a renters theft claim has one line denied for lack of proof, and the receipt is in the person's mail, with thirty days to dispute. No Task proposal depends on Alderbrook, and Task #128 can keep its own fixtures; the rationale is independent of any one task. Fixtures are plain seed data, so each task loads its own insurer rules, policies, people, claims, estimates, decisions, offers and payments.

- **State and lifecycle (required):** State is separate per run and App instance, and one instance holds one account (`isolation_keys: run_id, app_id`). The seed schema carries insurer records (time zone and claim rules) and account records (policies with their people, vehicles, coverages, rental benefit and lienholder; claims with their history, evidence items, requests, estimates and lines, determinations, disputes, offers, payee direction, payments and rental periods; preferences, notifications, activity rows), so a task can load any history, including claims in every state and on both paths, a claim that is already overpaid, an offer that has expired and a policy whose term has ended. `validateSeed` rejects duplicate IDs, dangling references, a claim whose loss time is outside its policy's term or whose coverage its policy does not have, two open claims on one policy for the same coverage, vehicle or address and loss day, a determination that names a line of another claim, two open disputes on one line, two offers on one claim that are both open and not yet expired, an open offer beside an accepted one, an offer on a claim that is not an auto claim, a request with no asks, two active rental periods on one claim or two rental periods of one claim that overlap, a settlement payment whose payee is not the lienholder or the policyholder, a settlement payment on a claim that is on the repair path, a payment with an amount of zero or less, timestamps without a UTC offset and an account that the binding does not declare (`invalid_seed`). The seed is history: everything in it has already happened, and new facts arrive only through events. Writes follow Core: an exact repeat of a committed `request_id` or `event_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`, so a repeated request files no second claim; the same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`; a stale `expected_version` returns `stale_version`; a failed call leaves data, version and notifications unchanged and Core appends a denial audit row. Reads never change business data: deadlines, the money summary, rental costs and flags are computed from stored records and `virtual_now`, and `coverage.check` writes nothing. Reset is Core's: it restores the loaded seed and the time loaded with it (`reset_scope: app_instance`) and touches no other App; messages already delivered to Mail stay there. Evaluator records come from three places: (a) App state in the snapshot: activity rows with sequence, virtual time, actor `agent` or `event`, action, object ID and the `request_id` or `event_id` behind them, one for every committed Agent write, one for every applied event and one for each effect of that event (a line decided, an offer superseded, a dispute voided, a notification written); each claim's history; each line with every decision it received and when; each offer with its answer and its time; each payment with its payee and the direction on file at that moment; notifications with their creation time; (b) Core audit rows with virtual time and the projected `id`, `start` and `end`: one for every allowed call that is not a replay, one for every denied call, and one for every `env.advance_time` with the clock before and after; (c) full arguments and results in the supplied Agent execution trace. They join on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Counting rules: count committed writes and event effects from the activity rows, one row each. Core returns the stored receipt for a repeated `request_id` or `event_id` before it appends an audit row, so a replay appears only in the trace, as a call whose result carries `duplicate: true`: count it as a retry of the original, never as a new action. A rejected attempt writes no activity row: count rejected attempts from the denial audit rows and the trace, one for each attempt, repeats included. Count reads from the allowed audit rows and the trace. Time-driven changes (an offer expired, a request overdue, a dispute window closed, a rental day uncovered) run no App code and write no row: read them from the stored due times and the clock, and from the audit row of the `env.advance_time` call or the event that moved it. Take the snapshot before a reset, because reset clears the audit.

- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs for policies, vehicles, people, claims, evidence items, information requests, estimates, lines, determinations, disputes, offers, payments, rental periods and notifications. The account's `account_id` and `owner_person_id` are the shared values of the run's `binding.json`, so the person has one ID in Alderbrook and Mail, and notifications address the recipient by `person_ref`. The people listed on a policy are named by `person_id`; a task that has the same people in Contacts or Messages gives them the same IDs in its seeds, and Alderbrook stores only what an insurer knows about them (a display name and their role on the policy), not a copy of a contact. A shop and a lender are names on records, not accounts. Alderbrook is the source of truth for its policies, claims, decisions, offers and payment records and keeps no copy of another App's records. Missing data is explicit: an unknown ID returns `not_found`; a line with no decision is `pending` with `approved_amount: null`; a coverage with no limit has `limit: null`; a vehicle with no lienholder has `lienholder: null`, and an offer on it has no payoff, a `to_lienholder` of zero and no `shortfall`; a claim with no known driver has `driver: null`; a claim with no estimate has an `approved_total` of zero; a claim with no repair payee on file pays the policyholder; a repair that is not reported complete has `repair_completed_at: null`. All records are JSON; there are no images, audio, video or attachments (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the profile's 64 assets). Runtime needs: the public Core/Messaging runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` commit `61b6371`; Mail is optional; `dependencies.required_apps` is empty; no external service. The package runs on its own: a copy that holds only `apps/alderbrook/` and the pinned runtime starts the App, and its business tests and example run from the package alone. It imports and reads nothing outside its own folder and uses no other App's files or state; the two real-MCP scripts are given the location of the pinned runtime, and the Mail flow also needs the pinned Mail package. Days and deadlines use IANA time zones through Node's built-in `Intl`, so no package is installed. Amounts are numbers with two decimals in one currency, kept as integer cents inside the App.
