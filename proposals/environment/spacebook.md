# Environment Track proposal

**Proposed PR title:** `[Environment Proposal] [Work & business] SpaceBook`

SpaceBook is an independently branded synthetic workplace room and equipment reservation App. This document proposes scope for review; implementation and validation have not yet been performed. It targets public runtime 1.6.0 / Core API 1.2.0, with a business Web GUI and MCP interface over the same state.

## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A resettable reservation portal for one employee at one fictional office. The agent can inspect room capacity, accessibility, built-in facilities and current availability; hold one room together with optional portable equipment; confirm a booking; replace or cancel it; and recover after a facility outage. A room being free does not establish that it suits the user's meeting. Tasks supply the user's needs and preferences; the App exposes facts and enforces resource ownership and scheduling rules.

The first version includes six rooms and four individually identified portable equipment units, such as conference cameras and projectors. Each booking covers exactly one room and zero to two equipment units for one shared interval. Five business views cover resource search, resource details/availability, hold review and confirmation, my bookings with change/cancel controls, and booking activity/notices. Equipment has explicit room-compatibility data. Unknown capability values remain unknown rather than being treated as positive matches.

| Workflow | Preconditions and action | Observable result and failure/control |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Inspect and compare | Employee searches a future interval and opens resources | Results expose capacity, known facilities, compatibility, maintenance blocks and busy intervals. Search is read-only and does not reserve anything. Other employees' titles, identities and booking IDs are hidden. |
| Hold a bundle | Employee selects a room and up to two compatible units | One five-minute hold reserves the entire bundle, or nothing is held. An occupied unit, duplicate unit ID or incompatible room rejects the whole request. A hold is visibly distinct from a confirmed booking. |
| Confirm or release | Owner confirms an unexpired hold, or releases it | Confirmation consumes the hold and creates one booking with history and a local notice. Confirmation at or after expiry fails. Release frees all held resources; repeating release has no further business effect. |
| Replace a booking | Owner selects a new bundle/interval for a future confirmed booking, or for a disrupted booking whose meeting has not started | One App-local transaction validates the replacement, supersedes the old booking and creates the new linked booking. Unavailable replacement leaves the original record and allocation unchanged. Resources shared with the old booking do not conflict with that same booking. |
| Cancel and inspect | Owner cancels a future confirmed or disrupted booking | Cancellation releases any allocation, retains history and records one notice. Repeated cancellation adds no notice. Another employee's booking cannot be changed by guessing its ID. |
| Recover from an outage | Infra blocks a resource for an interval that overlaps holds or future bookings | Affected holds become invalidated; affected confirmed bookings become disrupted and release their entire bundles. Their original details and outage reason remain visible. Agent may replace or cancel its disrupted booking; removing the block never silently reinstates it. |

**Time and allocation rules.** Inputs are offset-qualified timestamps, stored as UTC instants and displayed in the office's declared IANA time zone. Booking intervals are half-open `[start, end)`, so adjacent bookings are allowed. Duration is 15–240 minutes and the full interval must fit within seeded office opening hours. New holds, confirmations and replacements require a future start. A hold expires at the earlier of five minutes after creation and the booking start; equality means expired. Expired holds never block availability, even if no expiry event has run. Reads derive effective status without changing business data. Explicit Infra expiry events can persist expiry history/notices. No automatic reminder or scheduler is implied by clock advancement.

Active holds and confirmed bookings prevent overlapping allocation of any resource in their bundles. All availability checks and allocation writes occur under the existing Core transaction/version mechanism. At the booking end the interval no longer occupies resources and the GUI displays the booking as past; historical records remain. V1 outage events concern reservations that have not started, and reject intervals beginning before the event time or intersecting an in-progress confirmed booking. Handling evacuation or an interrupted live meeting is outside scope.

**Personal-agent value.** Independent tasks can ask the agent to preserve a meeting time while finding a larger room, retain step-free access after an outage, choose built-in video facilities when portable cameras are occupied, or abandon an expired hold and reconsider current alternatives. For example, an eight-person meeting needs video and step-free access; an outage leaves a small accessible room and a larger room needing the last available camera. Success requires a suitable confirmed bundle and no abandoned active allocation. The App does not hard-code this example's answer, infer private preferences or automatically select an alternative. Capacity and requested feature filters support selection; task-specific suitability remains an evaluation concern.

**Overlap and scope.** The [proposal index](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/blob/proposal-index/proposals/proposal-list.md) was checked on November 27, 2026. Its summaries identify these adjacent scopes:

- [Meeting Poll #179](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/179) coordinates people's availability before choosing a meeting time. SpaceBook allocates physical resources for a chosen interval and collects no participant votes.
- [Community borrowing #50](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/50) covers object loans, holds/queues and check-in/out. SpaceBook's equipment is available only as part of a room bundle; it has no lending queue, pickup, return, late fees or standalone equipment loans.
- [Pegboard #173](https://github.com/MatrAIx-ai/Personal-Agents-Last-Exam/pull/173) covers employee shifts and approval. SpaceBook does not assign staff or transfer work responsibilities.

This is preliminary comparison against index summaries, not an exhaustive review of the related PRs or a claim of exclusive scope. The assigned reviewer should confirm the boundary during design review.

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** Plan 12 Agent business tools: `spacebook.context`, `resources.search`, `resources.get`, `bookings.list`, `bookings.get`, `holds.create`, `holds.release`, `holds.confirm`, `bookings.replace`, `bookings.cancel`, `notifications.list` and `notifications.read`, all under the `spacebook` namespace. Search includes availability; booking details include revision/activity history. The GUI calls the same tools through the public `pale.web` v1 bridge. Infra alone loads seeds, resets, snapshots, advances time and injects events. Resource/catalog administration and another employee's actions are not Agent tools. Core's trusted account binding determines the caller; no Agent-controlled actor override is accepted.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Core isolates state by run/App and supplies persistence, policy checks, locking, epochs/revisions and idempotent receipts. Each run binds one employee account. Holds transition from active to consumed, released, expired or invalidated. Bookings transition from confirmed to superseded, cancelled or disrupted; disrupted bookings may become superseded or cancelled. No terminal record is silently reactivated. Writes carry the observed Core version and retry-stable request ID; a stale request fails without allocation changes. Retrying a committed confirmation/replacement returns its original receipt and creates no duplicate. Inspecting that receipt alone does not establish that the booking remains active after later events: the current booking read is authoritative. Reset restores the loaded baseline and advances the epoch; snapshot exports state for review, without promising an unsupported restore API. Other employees' records are available only to Infra; Agent availability output shows anonymous busy intervals.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Planned Infra operations are `spacebook.resource_block`, `spacebook.resource_unblock`, `spacebook.actor_action` and `spacebook.expire_holds`. Block inputs identify a stable block ID, resource ID, interval and reason; removal identifies the block ID. Actor actions use seeded bound identities and the same booking rules as Agent actions, allowing a scripted competing reservation. An example block payload is `{ "block_id": "block-1", "resource_id": "room-cedar", "start_at": "2026-12-08T14:00:00-08:00", "end_at": "2026-12-08T16:00:00-08:00", "reason": "Projector maintenance" }`, applied at `2026-12-08T13:00:00-08:00`. This is planned App event content, not a validated Core envelope or a messaging contract; implementation will provide a schema-valid full `examples/scenario.json`. Task/Infra owns scheduling. Handlers use `event.at`; ordinary operations use `ctx.virtual_now`. Blocking, invalidation/disruption and local notices commit atomically. Stable event IDs prevent duplicate effects on replay. Notices contain recipient, booking/hold reference, type, reason, timestamp and explicit read status, visible only to their recipient. Rejected actions emit no business notice. `messaging.publishes` and `messaging.receives` remain empty; local notices are not email, push or delivery receipts.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Stable IDs link rooms, equipment units, holds, booking revisions, replacement chains, blocks and notices. Seed data includes the office time zone/opening hours, resources, account bindings and historical allocations. All fixture content is original JSON/text, with zero media or attachments. Required dependency: public runtime 1.6.0, Core API 1.2.0, contribution contract 0.4.0 and public Web Host. Use Node 18+ with Node 22 for the documented reproduction run; browser validation uses the repository-pinned Playwright. Proposed package location is `apps/spacebook/`, with a package-local registry. Business tests run from the App directory alone; MCP/GUI checks use only the declared public runtime. No neighboring App source, private service or new Core API is required.
