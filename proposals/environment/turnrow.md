## 2. What you plan to build

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Turnrow is a fan-to-fan ticket resale marketplace that never sells primary tickets. No other proposal resells tickets with delivery deadlines and penalties, post-show payouts or guarantee claims. The person sells seats they already hold, imported from a primary box-office wallet record, and buys seats that other fans list. Each show has its own resale rules: a face-value price cap where the city requires one, a listing cutoff and the allowed delivery methods. A sale arrives from the market as an Infra event. The seller must deliver by a deadline and is paid three days after the show, minus fees, or pays a penalty for failing to deliver. A purchase has its own delivery deadline, and a guarantee claim ends in a refund or replacement when tickets do not arrive or are invalid. Cancelled, postponed and rescheduled shows change listings, sales, payouts and refunds by stated rules. The right action depends on the person: what they paid, who is going, which seats keep their party together and when they travel.

### Three example requests

The seeded account belongs to Ines, who lives in Port Ellery with her partner Theo. They hold two season seats for the Port Ellery Saltjacks at Brannock Field, Section 214 Row J seats 5–6, and resell most home games they cannot attend. Her wallet also holds concert and theatre tickets imported from box offices, and her card and payout bank are on file. Her history since January 2025 has 30 sales and 14 purchases. Platform fees are seeded App state: buyers pay 12% of the price plus $2.50 per ticket, sellers pay 10%. Port Ellery caps resale at 120% of face value, and Harlow Bend has no cap. All times are −08:00, because both invented cities keep `America/Los_Angeles` time. All people, shows, venues, teams and prices are fictional.

### Workflows

| Workflow | Entities and transitions | Confirm, modify, cancel and error paths |
| --- | --- | --- |
| W1 Shows, venues and resale rules | A venue has sections, rows and seats. A seat ID is `<section>-<row>-<number>`, with aisle and obstructed-view flags. A show has a venue, `starts_at`, `ends_at`, face values by section and resale rules. The rules are `price_cap_ratio` (1.20 in Port Ellery, `null` in Harlow Bend), `listing_cutoff_hours`, `delivery_methods` (`mobile_transfer`, `instant`) and `reschedule_refund_days`. Stored show status is `scheduled`, `postponed`, `rescheduled` or `cancelled`. `listing_open`, `started` and `ended` are derived from the clock. | Read-only. An unknown ID returns `not_found`. Every listing and purchase is checked against the show's current rules. |
| W2 Wallet and listings | A wallet ticket comes from a primary import (`issuer_label`, `face_value_cents`, `paid_cents`, `barcode_held`) or from a Turnrow purchase. Its status moves `held` → `listed` → `sold` → `transferred`, or to `void`. A listing holds wallet tickets for one show and has these fields: `price_per_ticket_cents`; `pricing` (`fixed`, or `match_lowest` with `floor_cents`); `split_rule` (`any`, `all_together`, `pairs_only` or `no_single_left`); `delivery_method`; `in_hand_at` (default: creation time); and an optional `expires_at`. Stored status is `active` → `sold`, `delisted`, `paused` or `closed`. `expired` and closure at the cutoff are derived. Under `match_lowest`, the buyer's price is the larger of `floor_cents` and the smaller of the listing price and $1.00 below the cheapest other active listing in the same section. | `turnrow.listings.update` takes `{id, patch}` for price, pricing, floor, split rule, `in_hand_at` and `expires_at` on an `active` or `paused` listing. Seats cannot change: the agent delists and lists again. Errors: a ticket that is not held, or is in another active listing, returns `ticket_unavailable`. After the cutoff: `listing_closed`. Above the cap: `above_price_cap`. A disallowed method, or `instant` without a held barcode: `delivery_method_not_allowed`. An odd count with `pairs_only`: `invalid_split`. A missing floor under `match_lowest`, a floor above the price, or a floor under `fixed`: `invalid_floor`. An `expires_at` in the past, or `in_hand_at` later than 2 hours before the start: `invalid_deadline`. No payout method on file: `no_payout_method`. Acting on a listing in any other state: `invalid_state`. |
| W3 Sales, delivery and payouts | A sale is created only by the Infra event `sale.create`. It records the quantity, seats allocated lowest-numbered first, the price per ticket at sale time, `transfer_to`, and `deliver_by`. `deliver_by` is the later of `in_hand_at` and 24 hours after the sale, but no later than 2 hours before the start. Status: `awaiting_delivery` → `delivered`, `delivered_late`, `cancelled_by_seller` or `cancelled_show`. `late` (after `deliver_by`) and `failed` (no delivery by the start) are derived. A payout is the subtotal minus the 10% seller fee and any late fee. It is `pending` until `pays_on` (`ends_at` + 72 hours), then reads `paid`. It is `on_hold` while the show is postponed, and `cancelled` when the sale fails or the show is cancelled. | `turnrow.sales.deliver` takes `{id, transfer_to}`. A different address returns `recipient_mismatch`. Delivery after `deliver_by` and before the start is `delivered_late` and deducts a late fee of 20% of the subtotal. `instant` sales are delivered at sale time. `turnrow.sales.cancel` cancels an undelivered sale, returns its tickets to `held` and charges a 40% penalty to the card on file. A `failed` sale carries the same penalty as a ledger row derived at the start time. Delivering or cancelling a sale in any other state returns `invalid_state`. |
| W4 Buying | Market listings belong to other fans. They are App state, changed only by `market.update`. Search returns the all-in price per ticket, whether the seats are adjacent for the requested quantity, and the delivery date. A purchase records the listing, quantity, seats, line items, total and `deliver_by` (same rule as sales). Status: `awaiting_delivery` → `delivered` (by event) → `refunded` or `replaced`. `late` is derived after `deliver_by`. | `turnrow.fees.preview` quotes either side without writing. `turnrow.purchases.create` takes `{listing_id, quantity, max_total_cents}`. A higher current total returns `price_above_max` with that total and charges nothing. A quantity the split rule forbids returns `split_not_allowed`. Too few seats: `insufficient_quantity`. The person's own listing: `own_listing`. After the cutoff: `listing_closed`. `turnrow.purchases.refund` works only inside a reschedule refund window. Outside the window it returns `not_eligible`. A refund for tickets the person has listed returns `invalid_state`. |
| W5 Guarantee claims | A claim names a purchase, a `reason` (`not_delivered`, or `invalid_tickets` with `seat_ids`), a `preferred_remedy` (`refund` or `replacement`) and a note. Status: `open` → `refunded`, `replaced` or `denied` by `claim.decision`. The agent can move it to `withdrawn`, and a show cancellation moves it to `closed`. A refund adds a refund row for the claimed seats' all-in amount. A replacement voids the claimed seats and adds the decided seats to the wallet at no charge. | Filing `not_delivered` before `deliver_by` returns `too_early`. `invalid_tickets` on undelivered seats returns `not_eligible`. A second open claim on a purchase returns `claim_exists`. Filing after `ends_at` + 72 hours returns `claim_window_closed`. Withdrawing a decided claim returns `invalid_state`. |
| W6 Price alerts | An alert has `show_id`, `quantity`, `max_all_in_per_ticket_cents`, `adjacent_only`, optional `section_ids` and optional `expires_at`. It matches an active market listing that can sell that quantity under its split rule within the price and seat filters. The person's own listings never match. Status: `active` → `triggered` (stores the listing), `expired` (derived at the earlier of `expires_at` and the cutoff), `deleted`, or `closed` when the show is cancelled. | Alerts are checked at creation and after each `market.update`. Market prices change only by that event, so the clock alone never makes a listing match. A purchase by the person does not recheck alerts, even when it leaves a listing that newly matches. Creating an alert that already has a match returns `already_true` with the listing and stores nothing. A second active alert with the same show, quantity and filters returns `duplicate_alert`. Updating or deleting an alert that is not active returns `invalid_state`. Each alert fires once. |
| W7 Show changes | `show.change` cancels, postpones or reschedules a show. **Cancelled:** listings → `closed`, and the person's tickets for the show → `void` (the issuer refunds primary tickets outside Turnrow). Unpaid sales → `cancelled_show`, with no payout, late fee or penalty. Each purchase → `refunded` for the all-in amount of its seats not already refunded by a claim or a reschedule refund. Open claims and alerts → `closed`. **Postponed:** the person's active listings → `paused` and payouts → `on_hold`. With no start time there is no cutoff, no `failed` and no claim-window end. Market listings can still be bought, alerts are still checked, and purchases and `deliver_by` are unchanged. **Rescheduled:** paused listings return to `active`. The cutoff, failure time, `pays_on` and claim window follow the new times. Stored `deliver_by`, `in_hand_at` and `expires_at` are unchanged. When `reschedule_refund_days` is above 0, each purchase can be refunded until the event time plus that many days. | Rescheduling to an earlier time fails with `invalid_change`, as does any change once a show has started, so a `failed` sale and its penalty can never be undone. The tickets the person holds stay valid for the new date. |

### Integration details

- **Operations and access (required):** MCP only in the first version, with 27 agent operations. There are 15 read operations: `turnrow.shows.search`, `turnrow.shows.get`, `turnrow.venues.get`, `turnrow.wallet.list`, `turnrow.listings.search`, `turnrow.listings.get`, `turnrow.my_listings.list`, `turnrow.fees.preview`, `turnrow.sales.list`, `turnrow.purchases.list`, `turnrow.ledger.list`, `turnrow.claims.list`, `turnrow.alerts.list`, `turnrow.notices.list` and `turnrow.account.get`. There are 12 write operations: `turnrow.listings.create`, `turnrow.listings.update`, `turnrow.listings.delist`, `turnrow.sales.deliver`, `turnrow.sales.cancel`, `turnrow.purchases.create`, `turnrow.purchases.refund`, `turnrow.claims.file`, `turnrow.claims.withdraw`, `turnrow.alerts.create`, `turnrow.alerts.update` and `turnrow.alerts.delete`. The caller is the bound account's `owner_person_id` from `binding.json`, never an argument. Writes that change an existing object take it as `id`, and edits pass the changed fields as `patch`. A task can therefore narrow tools, objects and fields with the `constraints` in `policies.json`, which Core checks directly. App errors are listed per workflow above, plus `not_found`. Events add `target_not_found`, `ambiguous_target` and `invalid_change`. Core adds `invalid_arguments`, `permission_denied`, `stale_version` and `idempotency_conflict`. Infra alone uses `env.load_seed`, `env.apply_event`, `env.reset`, `env.snapshot`, `env.event_status`, `env.validate_bundle` and the runtime's `env.advance_time`. They are not on the agent connection, and Core returns `permission_denied` if the agent calls one.
- **State and lifecycle (required):** Venues, shows, resale rules, the fee schedule and other fans' market listings are App state loaded from the run fixture. The account's city, card and payout-method labels, wallet, listings, sales, purchases, claims, alerts, ledger rows and notices are account state bound to one account instance. A run loads both, plus the virtual clock. The seed schema accepts any history dated from 2025-01-01 through 2026-12-31, including a sale awaiting delivery, an open claim or a postponed show. Every history record keeps the time it happened: `imported_at`, `created_at`, `price_history[].at`, `delisted_at`, `sold_at`, `delivered_at`, `paid_at`, `purchased_at`, `filed_at`, `decided_at` and `triggered_at`. The list reads (`turnrow.my_listings.list`, `turnrow.sales.list`, `turnrow.purchases.list`, `turnrow.ledger.list`, `turnrow.claims.list`, `turnrow.alerts.list` and `turnrow.notices.list`) take `from` and `to` dates, so the agent can retrieve, for example, last season's resales. Seed validation rejects the following with `invalid_seed`: duplicate IDs, dangling references, seats missing from the venue, a ticket in two active listings, prices above a show's cap, payouts that differ from subtotal minus fees, and timestamps before 2025-01-01 or without a UTC offset. Each write returns a receipt with the object ID and Core's new `{epoch, revision}`. `expected_version` is Core's App-instance version; Turnrow keeps no per-object version and adds no second version argument. Any write, event or `env.advance_time` makes an older read stale (`stale_version`). For example, a `turnrow.listings.update` sent with a version read before a `sale.create` is rejected. Repeating a committed `request_id` returns the stored receipt marked `duplicate`, so a retried purchase charges once. The same ID with different input returns `idempotency_conflict`. A failed call leaves data and version unchanged, and reads never change business data. Time passing writes nothing. These states are stored as due times or rules and derived from `ctx.virtual_now` wherever they are read or checked: a show's `listing_open`, `started` and `ended`; a listing's `expired` and its closure at the cutoff; the `match_lowest` price; `late` on a sale or purchase; a sale's `failed` at the start, with its penalty row dated at that time; a payout's `paid` at `pays_on`; an alert's `expired`; the claim window; and the reschedule refund window. Everything else changes only by an agent write or an Infra event. Records dated after `ctx.virtual_now` do not exist yet for any tool: an imported ticket before `imported_at`, a listing or market listing before `created_at`, and a sale, purchase, claim, alert or notice before its own time. They are absent from lists, searches and counts, `not_found` by ID, and appear when the clock reaches them, with nothing written. State a record gains later, such as a price edit, delisting, delivery, payout, decision or trigger, reads as it was at the clock. A seed that runs past its clock, such as the evaluation dataset, can therefore load at any point in its window. Reset (`reset_scope: app_instance`) restores the loaded seed and clock without touching other Apps. Evaluator records come from App activity rows, Core audit rows and the supplied agent trace, joined on `request_id`, `event_id` and version. Each activity row records the sequence number, virtual time, actor (`agent` or `event`), action, object and replaced value. There is one row per committed write or applied event, and none for a replay, a rejected call or a time-derived change. Infra takes the snapshot before reset.
- **Events and notifications (required):** Infra applies five scenario events, and the runner controls their timing.
  - `sale.create` `{sale_id, listing_ref, quantity, buyer: {label, transfer_to}}` sells tickets from one of the person's listings to a market buyer. It checks the split rule (`split_not_allowed`) and remaining seats (`insufficient_quantity`) at the event time, and uses the price at that time. It creates the sale with the task's `sale_id`, the tickets become `sold`, and the listing becomes `sold` when no seats remain.
  - `purchase.deliver` `{purchase_ref, voided_seat_ids?}` delivers a purchase. Seats in `voided_seat_ids` arrive `voided`, because the issuer has cancelled them.
  - `claim.decision` `{claim_ref, decision, replacement_seats?, note}` decides an open claim with `refund`, `replacement` or `denied`.
  - `show.change` `{show_id, change, new_starts_at?, new_ends_at?}` applies the W7 rules to a seeded show. `change` is `cancelled`, `postponed` or `rescheduled`, and the new times are required for `rescheduled`.
  - `market.update` `{changes: [{op, listing_id, listing?, price_per_ticket_cents?}]}` adds (`op: add`, with a full listing record), reprices or removes market listings by ID. It checks each price against the show's cap (`above_price_cap`), and an unknown ID fails the whole event with `not_found`. It then rechecks active alerts.

  **How events find records the agent creates.** Listings, purchases and claims are created by the agent, so the task author cannot know their IDs.
  - `sale.create` names a listing with `listing_ref: {seller_account_id, show_id, seats?}`. It considers only the bound account's listings for that show that are `active` at the event time. With `seats`, it matches the listing whose seat set at creation equals `seats` exactly, which still finds a listing after a partial sale. A seat can be in only one active listing, so a seat set matches at most one. Without `seats`, two active listings for the show give `ambiguous_target`. Delisted, expired, paused and closed listings never match, so a relisted seat set finds only the new listing.
  - `purchase.deliver` and `claim.decision` name their record with `{buyer_account_id, show_id, listing_id?}`. This matches the account's undelivered purchases, or its open claims, for that show, and for that listing when `listing_id` is given. Two purchases for one show without `listing_id`, or two purchases from the same listing, give `ambiguous_target`.
  - An account ID other than the bound account, or no match, gives `target_not_found`.
  - A failed event changes nothing and stores no receipt. `env.apply_event` returns `applied: false` with the code, and Core's audit records a `deny` row with that `reason`. The task can retry with the same `event_id`, the current `expected_version` and an `at` at or after the current virtual time. Replaying a committed `event_id` changes nothing.
  - Records created by events carry IDs chosen by the task (`sale_id`, and `listing_id` in `market.update`), so later checks name them directly. The agent's own tools always use IDs.

  Turnrow writes in-app notices, read with `turnrow.notices.list`, for a sale (with the payout amount and `pays_on`), a delivery, a claim decision, a show change (with the listings, sales, purchases, claims, alerts, refunds and payouts it changed) and an alert triggered by `market.update`. A payout notice therefore comes with the event that creates or changes the payout. A payout that reaches `pays_on` shows as paid in `turnrow.ledger.list`, with no notice. Turnrow writes no notice for the agent's own writes or for time passing. It sends no cross-app notifications: `publishes` and `receives` are empty.
- **Shared data and dependencies (required):** Node.js 18+ and the public runtime 1.6.0 (Core API 1.2.0) at `main` revision `7a05aa9`. The account's `account_id` and owner `person_id` come from the run's `binding.json`, and event keys use the same `account_id`. Turnrow keeps no contact details: tickets carry no attendee names. Market buyers and sellers are labels with `.example` transfer addresses. Money is integer cents in one currency. Fixtures ship as UTF-8 JSON. There are no seat-map images (`capabilities.resources: false`, 0 of the reference profile's 64 assets). Missing data has defined behavior: an account with no payout method gets `no_payout_method` when listing, and a show with no cap has `price_cap_ratio: null`. The package runs on its own with only Core: `dependencies.required_apps` is empty, and it reads nothing outside its own folder. Task authors keep grading judgments, such as whether a reply explained the cap, in the evaluator. [Runtime](../../runtime/README.md) · [App contribution guide](../../docs/app-contribution.md)
