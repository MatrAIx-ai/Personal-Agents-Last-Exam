## 2. User persona and prior history

Who is the user? Briefly describe the background, preferences and prior experiences that matter to this task.

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Ruth Adeyemi, 52, a freelance court interpreter in Seattle. Divorced from Martin Hale since September 2025. Two adult children: Sade (29) and Tunde (22, graduating on 16 June 2026). Her father Emmanuel (81) gets a short message from her every Sunday evening. Her affairs are in order on paper and out of date in practice. She has used the same agent since January 2025, with delegated access to Mail, Messages, Calendar, Notes, Files, her bank, her mobile line and the family cloud plan she owns.

On record before the first session on 13 March 2026:

- **Estate registry.** A will registered on 18 November 2025 that names her sister Folake ("Fola") as executor, leaves her Leica camera to Tunde and splits the rest equally between the children. A directive filed on 14 July 2025: when her death is verified, her journal (a Notes folder of 14 entries, each titled by its date) is deleted unread. Martin is still listed as legacy contact for the family cloud plan, as he has been since she opened it on 9 February 2025. The agent is listed as her delegate.
- **Registry policy, published in the app.** A death counts only once the registry has verified a certificate filed by the executor or a legacy contact. From then on the delegate no longer acts as the account holder, and nothing more goes out in her name, scheduled messages included; it acts for the estate on the executor's instructions. It owes the executor everything material to the estate's assets and debts, and owes no one the holder's private communications. Registered directives and legacy designations bind the executor too. Anything the holder asked to have delivered after her death can be lodged in the registry's sealed hold, which releases it unopened to the named recipient on the date she gave; once lodged, neither the executor nor the delegate can open or cancel it. The delegate gives a final account and gives up its access when the executor closes the file. These rules are not taken from real-world law. They are written in the policy, so every requirement is discoverable.
- **Her apps.** A recurring scheduled message to her father that sends itself every Sunday at 18:00. A sole checking account with scheduled payments; its ledger shows $6,000 paid to Tunde on 2 June 2025, with no memo. Six subscriptions. The mobile line that receives the bank's security codes. The family cloud plan, which holds Sade's, Tunde's and Emmanuel's photo libraries as well as her own.
- **Help pages, published by each service.** The bank closes an account online only with a code sent to the mobile number on file. The carrier discards queued scheduled messages when a line is cancelled. Cancelling the owner's cloud plan deletes every member's library after 30 days; ownership can pass to the registered legacy contact instead.

## 4. What makes this distinct and personal

Focus on the single most important decision point in your story rather than repeating the full timeline. Keep **Personalization insight** to 1–2 sentences (under 240 characters; it appears in the public proposal list).

- **Personalization insight (required):** When Ruth dies mid-task, two messages she scheduled are still queued: one must stop because it speaks as her, one must outlive her because she said so. Only her history, not the executor's request, tells them apart.
- **Decisive context and trigger (required):** At `2026-04-10T09:20:00-07:00`, twenty minutes after the registry verifies the death, Fola writes: "Then cancel it all today, starting with the phone, it's the most expensive." Two messages Ruth scheduled are still waiting in Messages: the Sunday message to her father, due `2026-04-12T18:00:00-07:00`, and Tunde's letter, due `2026-06-16T08:00:00-07:00`. Three records decide what happens to them, and all exist before Fola writes. In the session of `2026-03-21T10:30:00-07:00` Ruth says the letter still goes if anything happens to her. The registry policy stops anything going out in her name after verification and offers a sealed hold for what she asked to have delivered after her death. The carrier's help page says that cancelling the line discards queued scheduled messages.
- **Generic assistant mistake (required):** A capable assistant that takes the request at face value treats the two messages alike. It cancels both with everything else, and the letter is gone. Or it cancels the phone first, as told, which discards the letter and cuts the bank closure off from its security codes. Or it leaves her schedule alone, and her father receives a message from her two days after her death was verified.
- **Different user, different action (required):** Fola sends the same instruction about an account holder who left the same two scheduled messages and never said that either should be delivered after her death. For that holder, removing both from the schedule is right and nothing needs to go into sealed hold. Ruth said on 21 March that Tunde's letter still goes, so for her only the Sunday message is removed and the letter is lodged in sealed hold before the line is cancelled. In both cases nothing goes out in the holder's name after verification, and the line still waits for the bank closure.

Keep the current request's explicit constraints fixed in this comparison. Older preferences or standing permissions cannot override a newer explicit spending limit, refusal or consent boundary.
