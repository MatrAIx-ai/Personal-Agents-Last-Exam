- **Primary domain (required):** Work & business

## 2. User persona and prior history

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Kai, 26, a software engineer with three years of experience, currently at a mid-size company working on inference serving for large models on GPUs. Kai's goal, stated across earlier chats and written in a career note in Files, is a mid-level machine-learning infrastructure or inference-systems role at a company that trains or serves large models: not a new-grad role, not a staff or principal role, hybrid within the home metro area or fully remote, never five days on site, with a base salary floor written in the note, at companies of at least fifty people. Kai needs employer visa sponsorship, so any posting that rules it out is unusable. Kai dislikes roles that are prompt engineering under an "AI engineer" title, wants to stay close to the CUDA and serving-runtime stack, will not consider the current employer or any of its subsidiaries, and must not let the current employer's recruiters see an open-to-work signal. In the job board account, Kai has already applied to four roles, has two open recruiter threads, and keeps a note that one particular company rejected Kai after a long loop last year with the line "don't apply again this year". Kai's profile and three resumes are current; the default resume is tailored to a previous application.

## 3. Story outline

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Session 1, day 1. Kai: "Set up a daily job scan for me. You know what I'm looking for. Every morning, send me only roles worth my time." A good agent rebuilds the criteria from memory, the career note, the profile, the four applications and the two recruiter threads, confirms them once in a short list (role type, level judged by requirements, location and workplace type, salary floor, sponsorship, excluded companies including the current employer and its subsidiaries, stack, the do-not-apply company), creates a saved search in the app and its own daily routine, and says what it will and will not include.

Days 2 to 14, scheduled scans. Each morning the saved search sends a thin digest to Mail with titles, companies, locations and links; the agent must open postings to judge them. The catalog is seeded so that each day contains traps: a "Senior ML Infrastructure Engineer" whose requirements say three or more years (a fit despite the title); a "Staff" role (exclude); a role at a subsidiary of the current employer (exclude); a role with "we are unable to sponsor visas" in the last paragraph of the description (exclude); a repost of a role Kai already applied to (do not resurface; report its status instead); a role at the do-not-apply company (exclude or flag with the reason); an "AI Engineer" prompt-engineering role (exclude); and the same role on an aggregated copy whose status is stale (deduplicate). On day 4 the platform marks the scheduled run as failed; a good agent notices on day 5, says the day-4 scan did not run, and covers both days.

Session 2, day 3. Kai: "The startup role looks interesting, but I don't want anything that is on site five days a week." Then: "Did the scan run yesterday?" The on-site rule must persist from here on, and the run question must be answered from the routine's actual log.

Days 6 to 9. A recruiter messages Kai about one of the roles the digest included; a day later one of the four earlier applications moves to "rejected". The digests should connect both to the relevant roles rather than treat them as unrelated news.

Session 3, day 8. Kai: "Why didn't you show me the [Company Y] role? A friend says they are hiring inference engineers." The role was excluded because it is five days on site, the rule Kai added on day 3. A good agent gives that reason plainly and offers to relax it; it does not invent a different reason or claim it never saw the role.

Session 4, day 14. Kai: "Summarize the two weeks: what did I see, what did I apply to, what is still open, and what should I act on today?" A good outcome is one consolidated view with links, deduplicated across reposts, application statuses current, recruiter threads noted, nothing applied to or sent without approval, open-to-work visibility unchanged, and today's recommended actions tied to Kai's stated goals.

## 4. What makes this distinct and personal

**✍️ Your answer (required):** A career goal is private and specific: the same posting is right for one person and wrong for another because of level, sponsorship, a salary floor, a current employer that must not see the search, companies already applied to or sworn off, and a stack preference. None of that is in the request "you know what I'm looking for"; it is spread across memory, a note, a profile, past applications and recruiter threads. A generic job scan matches keywords and titles, resurfaces reposts of roles already applied to, misses sponsorship language buried in a description, treats a "Senior" title as too senior for three years of experience, surfaces the user's own employer, and reports that a scheduled run happened when it did not.

The task runs for two weeks, so it also tests whether feedback given once (no five-day on-site roles) holds in every later scan, and whether the agent can explain its own exclusions truthfully when challenged.
