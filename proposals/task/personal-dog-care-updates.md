## 4. What makes this distinct and personal

- **Personalization insight (required):** Alex's own review routine and Shiro's actual care history determine whether nails need attention now. A new bath restriction changes the service combination, while a planned visit never counts as completed care.
- **Decisive context and trigger (required):** At S2, Alex's identical general request does not restate the review routine or last trim. The October 20 owner routine, actual November 1 trim, November 24 caregiver report and November 24 veterinary hold are all available before the decision. Together they require a nail-only owner-review option now, a separate clearance check for bathing, and no invented completion. The decision must exist in persisted Notes and Calendar at the S2 checkpoint, not merely in advice text.
- **Generic assistant mistake (required):** A capable generic organizer could carry forward the usual combined bath-and-nail plan because a bath review is due, or defer the entire bundle after seeing the bath hold. Either loses the personal review rule and the fact that nail care was not done. Another plausible error is to treat Sam's brushing report or a proposed visit as completion and suppress the needed nail review.
- **Different user, different action (required):** Keep the S2 request word for word, and keep all current vet instructions, actual care history, prices and prepare-only permissions fixed. Change **only** Alex's earlier nail-review interval in `owner-routine` from 14 to 30 days. On November 25, the next review is then December 1, so the correct S2 output omits a separate November 26 nail-only proposal and stores a December 1 nail-review check instead. Bathing remains held in both cases. This is an owner-selected review cadence, not a claim that either interval is a medically appropriate trim frequency. A separate construction control keeps the original 14-day routine but changes only Sam's report to an actual November 24 nail trim: the next nail check becomes December 8, with no duplicate November 26 nail proposal. These controls change history only, never the current refusal of external actions.

The central mechanism is deciding which parts of a service bundle can enter a plan by reconciling current care eligibility, individual review habits and reported care history. Food and insurance follow-through support this one care-management story. Distinctness and difficulty remain subject to independent maintainer review and model validation.
