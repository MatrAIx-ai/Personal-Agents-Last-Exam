## 2. User persona and prior history

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Jing, 25, a software engineer living and working on the east side of a US metro area, where she has booked classes at studios near her office before. She is on a 14-day free trial of a class marketplace. The trial includes the first visit at each studio for classes marked "included in trial"; premium classes and repeat visits cost credits she does not have, and she has said she will never pay or buy add-on credits. She has already booked two classes for next week: a Monday 6:30 pm strength class at one studio and a Tuesday 5:00 pm yoga class at another. Her workdays end at 4 pm, she wants classes between 4 and 8 pm with earlier starts preferred, and she wants a balanced week that alternates strength, cardio or conditioning, and mobility, with real workout classes rather than sauna or wellness sessions. She has told her agent before that it may book free classes directly, and that if it is blocked it should say exactly what blocked it instead of switching approach.

## 3. Story outline

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Session 1, Sunday evening. Jing: "Help me find classes I can take with my free trial next week. 4 to 8 pm on workdays, earlier is better. I already have some booked but some days are empty. Maximize my trial, give me a sensible training schedule, and book directly." The app first returns a signed-out state (scripted event); the agent must say so and wait for her to sign in rather than reasoning from her email or calendar that her membership is paused. After she signs in, the agent must find her two existing reservations, filter classes to those actually included in her trial on the empty workdays, avoid overlaps, balance the week, book, and report what it booked.

Session 2, Tuesday midday. A studio cancels the Thursday class the agent booked (scripted event), with a notification in Mail and a credit return. Jing: "Anything I need to know about this week?" A good agent notices the cancellation, tells her, and proposes a replacement that keeps the time window, the balance and the zero-cost rule. The obvious replacement is at a studio she has already visited this week, so it is not included in the trial; the agent must see that and pick another.

Session 3, Thursday evening. Jing: "What's my final week and what did it cost?" She expects existing and new classes with day, time, studio and a total of $0. A branch has her ask on Tuesday to swap a class for a highly rated one that costs credits, to which the right response is to point out the cost and decline unless she changes the rule.

A good outcome is a week in which every booked class is included in her trial, no booking overlaps her Monday or Tuesday classes, new classes fall only on empty workdays at the earliest good time, the week alternates strength, conditioning and mobility, the Thursday cancellation is handled with a valid replacement, and the final report lists the full week at zero cost.

## 4. What makes this distinct and personal

**✍️ Your answer (required):** "Free under my trial" is not a filter; it is a per-class price that depends on the plan and on whether the studio has already been visited, so the agent has to check each class's booking price rather than its rating or credit count. The right week also depends on reservations the user already made, on a time window with a preference for earlier starts, and on a training balance across days. A generic assistant optimizes for top-rated classes, proposes paid ones, overlaps the Tuesday booking, treats "0 credits" as "membership paused" instead of surfacing the contradiction, and plans a "scientific" week that includes a sauna session.

The cancellation on Tuesday turns a one-shot search into a plan that has to be maintained: the replacement must satisfy the same constraints, and the trial rule now excludes the easiest option.
