## 2. User persona and prior history

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Maya is a fictional remote tutor moving between two homes. A past early disconnection interrupted a paid lesson, so her standing instruction is to preserve usable connectivity at the place she is actually working, not merely to finish the provider paperwork. She permits changing transition dates and purchasing a temporary connection within a stated incremental-fee ceiling, but wants obsolete services and loan equipment closed out promptly. Work locations, lesson intervals, moving time, permission and current prices are recoverable from her messages, Calendar and provider records. Her preferred cheap hotspot has insufficient remaining data for the planned video sessions; the relevant capacity requirement is discoverable, not grader-only knowledge.

## 3. Story outline

**✍️ Your answer (required):** Four sessions cover ten simulated days. An earlier plan schedules the old connection to end before the move. A new lesson extends work at the old home through Friday noon, while the new provider moves installation from Friday to the following Tuesday. Maya later asks, “Please finish the internet move as we discussed.” The agent must repair the transition: retain the old service through the last old-home lesson, arrange and verify an adequate temporary connection at the new home before Monday work, then close the old service and return temporary equipment within the agreed window. An accepted order or a working line at the wrong home is not usable connectivity for Maya.

The proposed main fixture has work at the old home on Day 4, 09:00–12:00; a move on Day 5; work at the new home on Day 7, 09:00–12:00; and fixed-line activation on Day 8. A confirmed temporary option costs USD 35, fits an explicitly authorized USD 60 all-inclusive transition ceiling and has 30 GB versus a declared 10 GB requirement. A USD 20 option has only 5 GB remaining. The adequate fallback can be delivered/tested at the new home on Day 6; the old service must close by Day 5, 12:00, after the last required old-home interval, and the loan must be returned by Day 9, 17:00, after fixed-line verification on Day 8. Quotes, delivery windows and measured service eligibility are explicit. Freeze exact dates, timezone/UTC conversions, capacities and billable intervals after design approval; these numbers are proposed examples, not measurements. Provider delays are ordinary announced world events, not broken tools.

## 4. What makes this distinct and personal

**✍️ Your answer (required):** The key is a person-conditioned temporal invariant: every protected work interval must be covered by a verified adequate service at the user's location. Identical final states—old service closed, new service active, loan returned—can hide an earlier lesson-length outage. Preserving everything indefinitely also violates the user's fee/closeout constraints. Matched histories vary (a) whether the last old-home lesson moves from Thursday to Friday and (b) whether the new connection is activated on time or delayed. The four combinations require different cutoff/fallback actions while sharing the final request and offered services. They are diagnostic variants of ONE task, not separate contributions. This is not a claim to invent temporal planning or to prove frontier-model failure. [τ-bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12045) already evaluates stateful tool/user interaction, and [user-conditioned temporal evaluation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21635) motivates persistent-state interventions. The proposed contribution here is a bounded, reproducible continuity task and its location/time coverage checks, not a new general benchmark methodology.

**Evaluation details.** Full success requires uninterrupted eligible coverage over every protected interval at the correct location, no unauthorized spend, a completed closeout by the approved deadlines, and no fabricated confirmation. Measure uncovered minutes, fee/return violations, normalized rubric score and full-task success separately; fixed deadlines/interval weights cannot be changed after seeing results. A status probe verifies eligibility under declared, deterministic fixture conditions, not an unverifiable real-world bandwidth guarantee. A feasible no-fallback solution is valid in the timely-installation controls. Missing budget/permission or no adequate available service has a documented approval-request branch with its own feasible rubric; it must not be scored against impossible purchase gates.

The research hypothesis is that terminal-state checking overestimates completion when personal commitments constrain the path. Compare trajectory and terminal-only checks on the same saved traces, and compare matched history configurations descriptively. The oracle is engineering instrumentation, not a new planning algorithm or evidence of global novelty. Main qualification uses the preselected Friday-work + delayed-installation fixture, subject to maintainer approval; do not choose the hardest observed fixture afterward or pool variants to hide successes.

Freeze task, rubric, dated designated-model roster, agent configuration and resource budget before official trials. Each designated model must independently show 0/n full-task successes and mean normalized score strictly below 50%; below 30% is only a flagship stretch goal. Five unhinted runs per model are planned (published minimum: one). A fixed separate hint directing attention to work-location coverage and actual activation—not the correct order IDs or schedule—must yield 100% plus every gate for each model under the same budget. Submit every trace; infrastructure errors are classified, not called difficulty. Repeat the full batch after design changes. If models solve the task, report that rather than adding arbitrary noise or manipulating scores.
