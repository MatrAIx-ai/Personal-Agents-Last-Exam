# Plateway Web App

`index.html` and `src/app.js` own Plateway's customer-facing business interface: restaurant search, menus, dish options, the cart, checkout, orders, cancellation, problem reports and ratings. The shared public `runtime/web/client.js` provides the session, tool discovery, text-only rendering and the shell; `runtime/web/server.cjs` serves this page and forwards only the Agent tools the current policy allows. `src/view.js` remains the compatible reviewer renderer.

From the repository root:

```sh
node runtime/web/server.cjs --app plateway --run web-demo --profile sandbox
```

Open the access link printed by the Host. A new run starts from the fixture at the shared registry's start instant (Wednesday 18 November 2026, 12:00 in New York); starting against existing state never resets it or changes its policy. Restaurant and courier actions (acceptance, pickup, delays, delivery, price and availability changes, pauses) are Infra events sent through a separate Infra MCP process; press Refresh to see them. The page has no route for them.

## What the page does

- **Restaurants.** Choose the delivery address (the page says how many restaurants deliver there), search by restaurant, dish or cuisine name, or filter by cuisine; each restaurant shows its status at the App's current time, fee, estimate and rating. A restaurant says when it closes, or when it next opens and that delivery can be scheduled, and lists its weekly hours; its menu shows declared allergens and sold-out dishes; a dish shows its option groups as radio buttons or checkboxes with their limits, a quantity and an optional receipt label with a 40-character counter. When the cart holds another restaurant's dishes, a dish (or a past order) from a different restaurant shows that a cart holds one restaurant's dishes and offers Clear cart in place of Add (or Reorder); Clear cart is offered only when the dish (or at least the page's view of the order) can actually be added; it rereads the cart first and removes nothing if the cart is already compatible or differs in any way from what the page showed (other dishes, another restaurant, or a reset or reload), rechecks the dish's availability (for a reorder, whether any dish is available with its recorded options) and leaves the cart as it is if nothing could be added; after clearing, add the dish or reorder.
- **Cart and checkout.** Change quantities with − and + (− at one removes the line), edit or remove lines, and undo a removal; orders under $12.00 are told about the small-order fee; choose the address, card (an expired card is marked and cannot be chosen), as soon as possible or a scheduled date and time (entered and shown in the account's time zone, not the browser's, with the restaurant's hours for that day; choosing “Schedule for later” suggests the first time the App accepts: at least 45 minutes ahead, within seven local days, with the order sent to the restaurant after now and while it is open), and a tip of up to $1,000; when checkout is refused because the restaurant is closed or the time is unavailable, the page says when it opens or what the time must allow; after an order, checkout starts again from as soon as possible with no tip; Preview total shows the address, card, time, itemised receipt and total; Place order sends exactly those previewed arguments with the preview token. Changing any checkout detail removes the total on screen, and a preview that returns after a detail changed is discarded. A scheduled time that does not exist in the account's zone (the hour skipped when clocks go forward) is refused in the form; a time that occurs twice (when clocks go back) means the first occurrence, and the zone abbreviation is shown.
- **Orders.** Active (with the expected arrival), scheduled and past orders with status, timeline, receipt, refunds, net charge, cancellation and rating, all in plain words (dish names instead of line IDs, readable reasons). Cancel order shows the refund and the amount kept before confirming; Report a problem picks delivered lines as missing or wrong and is offered only within 48 hours of delivery; Rate order takes 1 to 5 stars and an optional comment within 30 days; when a window has passed, even with the form open, the form closes on the next refresh and the order says when it closed. Reorder puts a past order's dishes, options and labels back in the cart, keeps its delivery address and says which dishes are no longer available. Messages stay on the screen where they happened.
- **Account.** The holder, addresses and masked cards.

Every value shown comes from an Agent read made at the last refresh; nothing from the App is rendered as HTML. Each write carries the Core version read at the last refresh and a new request ID. If the connection drops, or the Host answers with an error that carries no App status (for example a timeout), the page shows a “Waiting for Plateway” banner holding that exact request: Retry sends it again unchanged, so it takes effect at most once, and the page then says whether it had already gone through, was not applied, or (after a reset or reload of Plateway in between) cannot be confirmed; a refusal by the Host or the policy keeps the banner; Dismiss drops it. Until it is settled, other changes wait. A request ID is never reused for a new click. Operations made of several writes (Reorder, Clear cart) run as a plan that carries its own version from write to write: after a dropped connection, Retry finishes the interrupted write (or skips it when the App refuses a dish as unavailable) and then the rest of the plan; a definite refusal part-way says so, and a change by anyone else stops the plan rather than writing over it. Neither removes and adds in one go, so there is never a cart emptied for nothing. If the refresh after a change fails, the page shows the App's answer for the cart and refuses further changes until Refresh succeeds. Refusals are shown where they happen: `stale_version` (Plateway changed since the page last read it) apart from `stale_preview` (the business details behind a preview changed; preview again), and the App's own errors such as `payment_failed` and `window_closed`. While an operation runs, the page's buttons are disabled; unsent dish, line, report and rating choices survive a Refresh. Controls for tools the current policy does not allow are not shown, views whose reads it does not allow say so, and a forced call is refused by the Host. The page needs `plateway.account.get`.

## Why the page draws its own views

Plateway's reads take `restaurant_id`, `item_id` and `order_id` rather than `id`, and checkout's `when` (as soon as possible or scheduled), required option groups and report lines are not expressible as the shared generic forms. The controller therefore draws its own views through the configuration that `runtime/web/client.js` `mount` accepts and the other Apps use: the list call is `plateway.account.get` with no records (so nothing is auto-selected), `load` fetches the views, `renderList` and `emptyView` draw them, and reads, previews and writes go through `ui.call`. It also reads the shared toolbar's search box as the restaurant search and hides the shell's record-count badge, which would always read 0. Styling uses the shared classes plus element styles, as the Host's content security policy allows; the shared Host, client and stylesheet are unchanged.

## Browser acceptance

From the repository root, after `npm ci` and `npx playwright install chromium`:

```sh
node apps/plateway/tests/web.browser.cjs
```

It starts the shared Host on a fresh run and state directory with `apps.registry.json` (`runWebAcceptance` also takes another registry), opens Chromium in a different time zone from the account's, and drives search, a required option, add, an add whose response is dropped after the commit (a new add waits; Retry reports it had already gone through; one line), edit (including a refused 41-character label and an HTML-like label shown as text), remove, a preview compared line by line with the App's own preview, a tip change followed by one click on Preview, an address change while a preview is pending (discarded), place, reload, an Infra acceptance seen after Refresh, a cancel preview that keeps the order, a confirmed cancellation, a cancellation confirmed after the courier's pickup (refused as `stale_version`, nothing refunded), a scheduled checkout with the App's delivery and release times, a declining card, `stale_preview` and `stale_version`, a placement lost before it reached Plateway and retried after another change (not applied, nothing placed), a placement response dropped after the commit and retried after Refresh (one order, receipt marked duplicate), an order delivered through Infra then reported and rated, a report after its 48-hour window, a refused direct management request and four views at 390 px; then, on a second run, that loading a policy without `plateway.orders.rate` and pressing Refresh removes an open rating form and the Rate control, and that a forced call is refused. Each step is checked against App state read through Infra; JavaScript, console and CSP errors other than the induced network failures and refusals fail the run. The report (with runtime, Core API, Node, Playwright and browser versions and the receipts) and screenshots go to `.artifacts/web/plateway/`. CI's shared browser job is fixed to the seven personal Apps and does not run this file. A browser test is not a model-agent evaluation.
